# Literature search 2026-10: the physical variables the model does not carry

Run 2026-10-28. The kinetic core is a closed, well-mixed pot whose temperature is a series of isothermal segments. Real cooking
of plant-based meat analogues (high-moisture extrusion, grilling, pan-frying, roasting, stewing) also moves water, heat and
volatiles. This note searches the literature for **quantitative** data on seven of those variables (V1 to V7) and says, for each,
whether a kinetic model could use what exists. It records the search only: nothing was added to `data/`, no PDF was downloaded.
Context: `docs/guides/PH_BUFFERS_AND_WATER.md` section 7 (water activity) and `tasks/data_restructure_plan.md`
("Condition-modelling programme", B53 buffer catalysis, B54 water activity, and the backlog item on volatile stripping).

## Method

- **Keyword search, OpenAlex** (`api.openalex.org`, anonymous, no key or e-mail sent): 52 queries, 4 to 11 phrasings per
  variable, top 30 results each (`/works?search=`). Query list: V1 heat and mass transfer + Maillard/acrylamide/crust in frying,
  grilling, roasting; V2 core/surface temperature of patties and meat analogues; V3 GAB isotherms and Tg vs moisture of pea, soy
  and gluten; V4 air/water, oil/water partition, protein binding of MFT, FFT, methional, hexanal, pyrazines, extrusion flash-off;
  V5 high-moisture extrusion (HME) residence time, temperature, pressure and Maillard volatiles; V6 phosphate/citrate catalysis
  vs temperature; V7 H2S and thiol Henry's constants vs temperature, sealed-vessel and pressure effects.
- **Keyword search, Crossref** (`api.crossref.org`, anonymous): the same 52 queries (top 30), run first because the shared
  OpenAlex daily budget for this IP was exhausted at the start of the session; plus about 45 targeted author/title lookups for
  papers known to the domain (van der Sman, Datta, Kondjoyan, Feyissa, Kokini, Buttery, Suleimenov, Fernández-Prini...).
- **One-hop chaining** from 33 seed papers (the best 3 to 7 hits per variable, listed in the scratch script). The OpenAlex
  budget ran out again after the keyword pass, so list queries were not available: backward links came from each seed's
  `referenced_works` resolved one by one with OpenAlex single-work lookups (these are not charged), forward links from the
  Semantic Scholar citation lists. Candidates were kept when title or abstract matched a per-variable keyword filter, and ranked
  by the number of seeds linked. Chained pool after filtering: V1 243, V2 60, V3 178, V4 379, V5 256, V6 29, V7 320.
- **Citation contexts**: Semantic Scholar `/paper/DOI:.../citations?fields=contexts,intents` for 136 shortlisted DOIs (anonymous).
  Each paper is classed from how others cite it:
  **Q** = cited for a quantitative result a kinetic model could use (a constant, a profile, a rate, a partition coefficient);
  **M** = cited for a method or a model formulation; **B** = background only; **?** = no citation contexts available.
  The class describes the citing literature, not the paper; several Q papers are Q only for one number.
- **Exclusion**: every DOI in `data/keys/papers.yml`, every PDF in `data/articles/` (first-page DOIs from the round-3 scan), and
  dossier-header DOIs. Corpus papers met on the way are named in the per-variable notes, not in the tables.
- **Limits**: titles and abstracts were read, not full texts; many Elsevier abstracts are missing from OpenAlex, so some
  classifications rest on title and citation contexts only. The OpenAlex budget cut forward chaining to what Semantic Scholar
  holds (its coverage of pre-1990 and non-English papers is thin). Recall is therefore moderate: I would put roughly 60-70 %
  probability that a dedicated full-text search would turn up at least one more DOWNLOAD-grade paper per variable for V2, V5 and V6,
  and 30-40 % for V3, V4 and V7, where the classic sources are well cited and were found from several directions.

## Verdict per variable

| var | does usable quantitative data exist? | confidence |
|---|---|---|
| V1 water loss coupled to Maillard | **Partly.** Coupled heat-and-mass-transfer models of meat cooking are mature (van der Sman, Feyissa, Datta) and give surface temperature and moisture vs time with measured validation; coupling to a reaction exists for HAAs in beef (Kondjoyan, Sprague) and acrylamide in bread/biscuits/chips (Ahrné, Schouten, Warning). **Nothing couples transport to sulfur volatiles, Strecker aldehydes or hexanal, and nothing does it for a meat analogue.** Usable as heating/drying programmes for a surface segment, not as validation of our chemistry. | moderate-high |
| V2 core/surface temperature histories | **For meat patties yes, for meat analogues essentially no.** Contact-heating patties have measured centre and surface temperatures and contact heat-transfer coefficients (Pan 2000, Houšová 1985, Zorrilla 2002, Ou 2006, Alhambra 2023/24). No paper found gives time-temperature histories for cooking a plant-based patty or HME strip; Brouwer 2025 cooks PBMA patties to stated core temperatures only. A meat-patty profile with analogue thermal properties is the defensible proxy. | moderate |
| V3 sorption isotherms and Tg vs moisture | **Soy and gluten yes; pea no.** Tg vs moisture: soy 7S/11S (Morales & Kokini 1997/1999), gluten (Kalichevsky 1992, Noel 1995, Pouplin 1999), soy and gluten together (Bengoechea 2007). Sorption: soy protein isolate at 5/15/25 °C (Hermansson 1977), SPI vs a_w (Martins & Netto 2006). **No GAB isotherm or Tg-vs-moisture curve was found for pea protein isolate/concentrate or any HME extrudate**; nearest proxies are cowpea protein isolate isotherms (Ayranci & Duman 2005) and common-bean state diagrams (Kyomugasho 2020). B54's gate can be built for soy and gluten now and for pea only with a declared proxy. | moderate-high |
| V4 partitioning, binding, stripping | **Mixed.** Oil/water and air/water partition for aldehydes (hexanal) and some pyrazines exist (Buttery 1969/1971/1973; Kutsuna 2021 for hexanal vs T); H2S octanol/water K = 2.1 (Cuevasanta 2012) replaces the assumed `Kfat: 1.0`. Protein binding constants exist for carbonyls and pyrazines on soy and pea (O'Keefe 1991 with temperature; Guo 2023 pyrazines on pea; corpus has Damodaran, Wang & Arntfield, Bi 2022, Snel 2023, Sun 2025). **No measured air/water or oil/water partition coefficient for MFT or FFT was found** (the `henry_constants.yml` values stay estimates), and **no binding constant of MFT, FFT, methional or 2-acetylthiazole to a plant protein** beyond the corpus's Sun 2025 (qualitative/spectroscopic). Extrusion flash-off: one recovery range (Palkert 1980, 4-22 % for pre-added volatiles) and reviews; no compound-specific stripping constants. | moderate |
| V5 inside the HME extruder | **Process conditions yes, chemistry no.** Barrel/die temperature, pressure, SME and residence time for pea and soy HME are reported (Osen 2014, Wittek 2021, Sandoval Murillo 2019, Ellwanger 2023 simulated local residence time). Maillard volatiles vs barrel temperature, moisture and residence time exist only for **low-moisture cereal extrusion** (Bredie 1998 maize, Bredie 2002 wheat with pH, Parker 2000 oat, Rausch 2009 starch + sugar + amino acid, Lipinski 2025 acrylamide/furans). For HME of plant proteins, product volatiles are reported but no time-resolved formation inside the barrel; a citing context reports **no Maillard products** in faba-bean HME (Tuccillo 2022). | moderate-high |
| V6 buffer catalysis at more than one temperature | **Essentially no single multi-temperature series.** Each buffer study works at one temperature: Bell 1997/1998 at 25 °C, Davidek 2002 at 90 °C, Potman 1989, Rizzi 2004, Pepa 2023 and Mikami 2015 at 100 °C, Saunders & Jervis 1966 at 121 °C (and reflux). Huang 2011 heats phosphate-buffered sugars at 80/100/120 °C but compares with unbuffered only at 100 °C. The temperature dependence of κ has to be assembled across studies and systems, as B53 already plans, with that transfer as a declared risk. | moderate (about 30 % that a true series exists that this search missed, most likely in pre-1980 carbohydrate chemistry) |
| V7 sealed vessel: H2S and thiol Henry vs T, pressure | **H2S: yes, excellent.** Fernández-Prini et al. 2003 give an evaluated kH(T) for H2S from 0 °C to near-critical (about 5 % uncertainty); Suleimenov & Krupp 1994 measured 20-320 °C; Lee & Mather 1977 10-180 °C. The repo's constant-ΔH van 't Hoff extrapolation from 25 °C (`delta_H_sol_kj_mol: -17.5`) should be checked against these at 100-150 °C. **Thiols: methanethiol has 25 °C data plus a ΔH; MFT and FFT have no measured Henry's constant at any temperature.** Pressure effects on rates are negligible at sealed-tube pressures: Isaacs & Coulson 1996 measure activation volumes at MPa-GPa, and any plausible magnitude (up to ~30 cm³/mol) changes k by < 1 % at 5 bar; the only sealed-vessel effect worth modelling is headspace partitioning, which needs each pot's headspace volume. | high |

## What the search says about the programme

- **B54 (water activity)** can proceed for soy and gluten matrices with measured Tg-vs-moisture curves (ranks 2-4) and an SPI
  isotherm (Hermansson 1977); for pea it needs either a declared proxy (cowpea isolate, bean fractions) or a measurement. That
  missing pea curve is a cheap DSC + DVS experiment and is worth asking WUR for.
- **Volatile stripping (backlog)** is limited by MFT/FFT volatility, which no one has measured. H2S is fully covered (ranks 1, 5, 6).
  An open-pot model would combine Kaw(T) with a gas-liquid mass-transfer coefficient (Marin 1999 class), and its predictions for
  the thiols would inherit the estimated `Kaw_25c` bands.
- **Heat and mass transfer (V1, V2)** data are for meat. Using them for analogues means swapping in analogue thermal properties
  and water-holding behaviour (Cornet 2020 is the one analogue-specific water-release study), stated as a transfer assumption.
- **HME chemistry (V5)** is the weakest: process conditions are well reported, formation chemistry inside the barrel is not. The
  most promising untraced lead is the HME xylose-cysteine work that a Sun 2025 citing context mentions (MFT/FFT formed in situ in
  textured soy).
- **B53 (buffer catalysis)** will have to assemble temperature dependence across studies; ranks 10-11 add a 121 °C point and a
  phosphate series at 80-120 °C, neither a clean multi-temperature κ.
