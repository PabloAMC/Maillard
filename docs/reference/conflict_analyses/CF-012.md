## 4. Tests outside the conflict

**Outside data (buffered, free-sugar pots, two further laboratories):**
- Meynier 1995 (Reading, 140 °C, pyrophosphate, pH measured): MFT falls **>15×** from pH 4.5 to 6.0, monotonic; norfuraneol (the MFT precursor) also falls 4× (10.6 → 2.6).
- Cerny 2007 (Firmenich, 145 °C, phosphate): pentose-derived MFT pH 4 > pH 5 (×2.1); FFT falls 431 → 0 from pH 4 to 7, as Hofmann's.
- So **three laboratories agree at 140-145 °C in buffer**: MFT and FFT highest at the lowest pH tested. No buffered free-sugar pot on disk shows a near-neutral optimum. Zhou is the only "pH 7 optimum", and it is unbuffered, Amadori-fed and labelled by initial pH.

**No-refit probe (shipped engine).** MFT, µg per 100 mL pot (FFT in brackets):

| pot | pH 3 | pH 5 | pH 7 | measured MFT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hofmann, 145 °C 20 min | 2.5 (152) | 9.4 (35) | 0.0005 (0.017) | 55.3 / 19.8 / 2.5 |
| same pot, 120 °C 60 min | 0.77 | 8.1 | 0.0002 | — |
| same pot, 100 °C 360 min | 0.28 | 3.5 | 5e-10 | — |

- The engine's optimum is pH 5 at **all three temperatures** (it has no temperature-dependent pH law), and its pH 3 / pH 5 ratio *falls* as temperature falls (0.26 → 0.10 → 0.08).
- Why pH 3 is low in the engine: at pH 3 the pentose goes to the 3-deoxyosone (TDP 25 mM) and norfuraneol is 0.15 mM, **55× below pH 5** (8.2 mM; Hofmann Table 5 measures 4.8 mM NF at pH 5, so pH 5 is right). At pH 7 the engine is H2S-starved (H2S peak 0.0002 mM, cysteine gone). Meynier's norfuraneol falls with pH from 4.5, the engine's peaks at 5.5 (Meynier pot probe: NF 0.39 / 0.55 / 0.07 mM at pH 4.5 / 5.5 / 6.5; MFT 0.88 / 0.51 / 0.06 µM, the measured direction but 14× instead of >150×).
- Zhou's pots in the same engine (species start ARP 20 + Cys 20, unbuffered): MFT 171 / 170 / 78 µg/L at initial pH 6 / 7 / 8, final pH 3.38 / 4.25 / 5.12 (measured 3.22 / 3.42 / 5.07). **The engine reproduces Zhou's pH 8 drop and roughly the 6 ≈ 7 plateau with a law whose buffered optimum is pH 5 and no temperature term.** E3 + E2 suffice for Zhou; E1 is not needed.

**Model test still to run:** fed ribose + H2S at pH 3 / 5 / 7 (engine: MFT 7.4 / 6.3 / 0.44 µg per 50 mL, measured only at pH 5: 15.1). A measured amine-free pH series would show whether the pH 3 MFT comes from the sugar side (E2) or from H2S supply.

**Experiment.** Designed protocol core (pH 5 / 6 / 7 label at 25 °C, 100 / 120 / 140 °C, 0.2 M phosphate, pH logged after cooling at every point, norfuraneol and H2S measured) tests E1 directly (pH × T interaction) and E4 (norfuraneol vs pH). **It does not reach pH 3-4**, where the model fails; add one pH 4 cell at 140 °C (6 vials) to the core.

## 5. Verdict

**Explained (as a source conflict); the remaining misfit is the model's.** The sources do not disagree on the
pH law once the pots are described by their real conditions: three buffered free-sugar laboratories agree that
MFT falls with pH above 4-5 at 140-145 °C; Zhou's unbuffered Amadori pots end at pH 3.2-5.1 and are reproduced
in direction by the shipped engine without any temperature-dependent law (probe). Probabilities for the
Hofmann-vs-Zhou difference:

| explanation | P |
|---|---|
| E3 + E2 (unbuffered collapse and Amadori feed) | **0.70** |
| E1 temperature-dependent pH law | 0.15 |
| other (method: SIDA vs HS-SPME external calibration) | 0.15 |

The real open item is **E4, the model's low-pH structure**: norfuraneol at pH 3 is 55× below pH 5 in the engine,
against Meynier's norfuraneol rising toward low pH. **Single most informative test:** a norfuraneol (and H2S) pH
series at 140 °C, pH 3-7, buffered, which the protocol core gives if a pH 4 cell is added. The register's
`li2011` / `hou2017` sources should be replaced by the leads' real identities (or dropped).
