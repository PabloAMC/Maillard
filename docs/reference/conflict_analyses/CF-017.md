## 2. Conditions that differ (between the slices)

Only hold time (40 / 80 / 120 / 180 min) and, possibly, the run: the 120-min column (and at 100 °C the 40 and 80 min
columns) come from the *Food Chem* experiment; the 180-min column (and the 40 / 80 min columns at 120 and 140 °C) appear
only in the JAFC paper. Temperature, pot, method are otherwise identical.

## 3. The dossier's explanation, checked

Dossier claim: "A fixed-time slice across a family of curves whose maxima move left as temperature rises ... the
140 °C curve passes its maximum inside the window."

| leg at 120 min | measured | what makes it | does the dossier's mechanism explain it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| high leg 120 → 140 °C: MFT ×4.26, FFT ×2.79 | 140 °C MFT at its peak (5.91 at 120 min), FFT just past it (12.44 at 80 → 11.44); 120 °C still rising | partly: the slice catches 140 °C at its maximum and 120 °C mid-rise | **yes** |
| low leg 100 → 120 °C: MFT ×1.12, FFT ×1.10 | the **100 °C** curve reaches a plateau at ~120 min (*Food Chem* 20-min grid: MFT 0.98 / 1.24 / 1.18 at 100 / 120 / 140 min; FFT 3.02 / 3.73 / 3.03) while 120 °C keeps rising (to 2.48 / 4.28 at 180 min) | the 100 °C plateau, not the 140 °C maximum | **no**: the moving-maximum picture predicts the 100 °C curve peaks *latest*; here it flattens before the 120 °C curve. The low leg is flat because the 100 °C curve saturates at a lower level. |

Corrections to the dossier's sec. 8.3 wording: (i) "the 'flat low leg then jump' appears at 120 min and nowhere else"
is right for the flat low leg, but the high leg exceeds the low leg at 80 and 120 min for MFT and at 40, 80 and 120 min
for FFT (FFT legs 1.59 / 4.50, 2.50 / 4.12, 1.10 / 2.79, 1.37 / 1.15). A larger step from 120 to 140 °C than from 100 to
120 °C is the majority pattern for FFT, not a one-slice event; only its 120-min size is slice-made. (ii) The 100 °C
"turnover" at 180 min (MFT 1.24 → 0.92) is a cross-run comparison (sec. 1) and a 25 % change in a cell without an SD.

## 4. Candidate explanations and tests outside the slice

| # | explanation | predicts | test |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Fixed-time slice of curves with different shapes (peak and plateau times move with T) | the folds change with hold time | the four-time grid: they do (MFT high leg 2.61 / 3.25 / 4.26 / 1.62) ✓ |
| E2 | A genuine super-Arrhenius step between 120 and 140 °C (a channel with a high barrier taking over, e.g. cysteine breakdown to H2S: free-cysteine loss has Ea 121 kJ/mol in Li 2011's glucose + cysteine pot; or TTCA ring opening) | high leg > low leg at every hold time, and with a co-substrate | FFT: 3 of 4 times ✓; MFT: 2 of 4 ✗; **+xylose arm (*Food Chem*, same 120-min slice): MFT 1.44 / 1.30, FFT 1.35 / 0.96: no switch-on** ✗ |
| E3 | Run offset between the *Food Chem* and JAFC-only cells | discontinuities at the column joins | the 100 °C 120 → 180 min drop and the 140 °C FFT 120 → 180 min fall (11.44 → 4.91) both cross a join; untestable without the raw runs |

**Slice-free outside test (proposed, measured here).** Thiol level is monotone in temperature at every hold time in the
measured grid: 120 °C ≥ 100 °C and 140 °C ≥ 120 °C in **16 of 16** comparisons (2 thiols × 4 times × 2 legs). This
ordering does not depend on where a slice sits relative to a peak.

**No-refit probe (shipped engine, TTCA 10 mM, water, pH 7, species start):**

| | 40 min | 80 min | 120 min | 180 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MFT 120/100, 140/120 | 11.5, 1.75 | 3.85, 0.83 | 2.26, **0.49** | 1.45, 0.26 |
| FFT 120/100, 140/120 | 33, 0.30 | 4.0, 0.052 | 1.41, **0.009** | 0.62, 0.0007 |

The engine's thiols peak before 40 min at 140 °C and collapse (FFT ×600 down by 180 min); it gets the 140 °C ≥ 120 °C
ordering in **1 of 8** cases against 8 of 8 measured, and its 120-min "switch-on" folds are 0.49 / 0.009 against 4.26 /
2.79. Whatever the slice, the engine's high-temperature sink is far too strong. The B2.3 hold-out rows score this
through the single fold; the ordering test and the four-time grid score it through the shape.

**Experiment.** The designed protocol core (100 / 120 / 140 °C, 7 times to 160 min, SIDA for MFT and FFT, free-sugar pot)
**covers E1 vs E2** with one run, one method and liquid-phase (not headspace) values, and removes E3. A TTCA-fed arm is
not needed for this question.

## 5. Verdict

**Explained (the analysis holds, with a correction).** The 120-min magnitudes (MFT ×1.12 then ×4.26; FFT ×1.10 then
×2.79) are made by the slice: the high leg by the 140 °C maximum (the dossier's mechanism), the flat low leg by the
100 °C plateau (not the dossier's mechanism). Supported outside the slice by the *Food Chem* 20-min grid at 100 °C and
by the +xylose arm.

| explanation | P (for the 120-min "switch-on" as printed) |
|---|---|
| E1 slice artefact (both legs) | **0.75** |
| E2 a real 120-140 °C step that the slice exaggerates | 0.15 |
| E3 run offset | 0.10 |

A weaker, real-looking feature remains for FFT (larger 120 → 140 than 100 → 120 step at three of four times; P ~0.4
that it is chemistry rather than noise). The register's remedy stands: retire `kang_switch_on_*` and B10's T4 gate as
scored rows; replace by the four-time grid, counted once (the 100 °C 40-120 min cells are *Food Chem* Fig. 1a, which
has finer resolution, and must not be counted twice), plus the slice-free ordering test above, which the engine
currently fails 7 of 8. **Single most informative test:** the ordering test on the existing grid (no new data); then
the protocol core.
