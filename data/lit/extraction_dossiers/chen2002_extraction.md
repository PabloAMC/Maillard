# Chen & Ho 2002 — EXTRACTION
### *"Effects of Carnosine on Volatile Generation from Maillard Reaction of Ribose and Cysteine"*

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Same pot and heating as Chen 2000 (180 °C, 2 h, Hoke cylinder, unbuffered, pH 5.0 or 8.5), with carnosine
(β-alanyl-L-histidine) instead of urea. **This paper does report MFT**: 0.083 mg/g ribose at pH 5 and 0.040
at pH 8.5, undetectable with carnosine at both pHs; its disulfide 0.015 only at pH 5 without carnosine.
FFT: 0.895 (pH 5), 0.318 (pH 8.5); carnosine cuts it to 0.256 at pH 5 and to undetectable at pH 8.5.
Carnosine raises pyrazines (pH 8.5 only) and some thiazoles; ribose + carnosine alone gives furfural
33.2 mg/g ribose at pH 5. The authors propose carnosine as an NH3 donor and a pH stabiliser (final pH
6.92 vs 4.96 at initial 8.5).

## 4. DERIVED [Z]

Molar yields per ribose (MW ribose 150.13, MFT/FFT 114.17; 1 g ribose = 6.66 mmol):

| quantity | RC pH 5 (→ 4.01) | RC pH 8.5 (→ 4.96) |
|---|---:|---:|
| MFT, mol/mol ribose | **1.09 × 10⁻⁴** (0.011 mol %) | 5.3 × 10⁻⁵ |
| FFT, mol/mol ribose | **1.18 × 10⁻³** (0.118 mol %) | 4.2 × 10⁻⁴ |
| MFT/FFT (mass) | 0.093 | 0.13 |
| pH 5 / pH 8.5 | MFT 2.1×, FFT 2.8× | |

Compared with Hofmann 1998 aqueous 145 °C/20 min pH 5 (MFT 1.73 × 10⁻⁵, FFT 1.06 × 10⁻⁵ mol/mol ribose,
MFT/FFT 1.64): Chen 180 °C/2 h gives MFT ~6× and FFT ~110× higher, and inverts MFT/FFT by ~18×. The
inversion is the robust feature (it does not depend on absolute calibration, only on equal response of two
C5H6OS isomers): **at 180 °C / 2 h FFT dominates MFT by an order of magnitude**, while at 140–145 °C
MFT ≥ FFT (Hofmann; `xu2008` 1:1 MFT/FFT 0.75; `meynier1995` pH 4.5 1.8).

Ribose + carnosine pH 5: furfural 33.2 mg/g ribose = 0.346 mmol/6.66 mmol = **5.2 mol % of ribose** (and only
0.495 mg/g with cysteine present → cysteine/H2S consumes ≥ 98 % of the furfural that would otherwise
accumulate, if carnosine is assumed inert at pH 5) [Z, assumption flagged].

Chen 2000 vs 2002 control pots: FFT pH 5 1.55 vs 0.895 (×1.73); pH 8.5 0.22 vs 0.318; final pH 3.86 vs 4.01
and 6.05 vs 4.96.

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

- **MFT and FFT at 180 °C / 2 h, two starting pHs with measured endpoints:** the corpus's only paired
  MFT + FFT at 180 °C in bulk water. **Candidate HOLD-OUT extrapolation rows (temperature), not benchmark
  quality for absolute level**: unbuffered, no response factors, solvent extraction + K-D concentration
  (the workup Schieberle 1996 shows can lose MFT completely), no replicates stated, ×1.7 between-paper
  spread for FFT. Use as (i) the **MFT/FFT ratio ≈ 0.1** at 180 °C (more robust), (ii) the pH-5/pH-8.5
  ratios (2.1× MFT, 2.8× FFT), with the printed final pH as the `ph_state` check. Score levels as
  order-of-magnitude only (I would put a ×5 band on them, ~70 % confidence).
- **Carnosine:** an imidazole dipeptide (meat 1–50 mM) that removes MFT and FFT and adds pyrazines; the
  engine has no carnosine species. Qualitative: a meat-matrix amine competing for H2S-derived chemistry.
- **Furfural sink (Table 3 vs Table 2):** supports a fast furfural + H2S → FFT channel at 180 °C; directional.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **"0.01 mol of L-cysteine and D-ribose"** each or total — unstated.
2. Unbuffered; pH drifts (8.5 → 4.96 without carnosine).
3. **No replicates, no SD, no response factors.**
4. Column length "86.3 m" is unusual (probably a cut 100 m column); RTs in Table 2 are not monotone with
   listing order within classes (fine — sorted by class).
5. "5-methylthieno[2,3-d]thiophene" is not a valid fused-ring name (thieno[2,3-b] or [3,2-b] intended).
6. Text says FFT at pH 8.5 is "less than half" of pH 5 (0.318/0.895 = 0.36 ✓) and MFT/FFT "undetectable" with
   carnosine at pH 8.5 (✓ blank cells).
