# Obata & Mizutani 1960 — EXTRACTION (Part IV: H2S released from cystine, free cysteine, djenkolic acid, four cysteine mercaptals and two cysteine mercaptoles, 32 mg S in 100 mL water, heated 1 / 3 / 6 h in a 120 °C oil bath under a CO2 sweep; cumulative H2S by methylene blue)

## 1. WHY IT MATTERS

Three facts for the sulfur lane:
1. **A second, more dilute free-cysteine H2S time series** (10 mM S vs Part III's 100 mM), same
   apparatus. Dilution by 10× raised cysteine's H2S by 1.1-1.5× (the authors: "the amounts of
   hydrogen sulfide from cystine and cysteine were increased considerably"). A purely first-order
   source gives the same % at both dilutions.
2. **Cystine as an H2S source**: from 3 h on, cystine releases more H2S per S than cysteine; it is not
   only a sink. The authors' summary: "a small amount of hydrogen sulfide was liberated from
   L-cystine on one hour heating, but its amount increased suddenly after three hours".
3. **Cysteine bound as a dithioacetal (mercaptal, 2 Cys : 1 aldehyde)** does not withhold sulfide:
   0.53-2.6× free cysteine; the furfural mercaptal is "most unstable".

## 2. SYSTEM AND CONDITIONS [M], quoted

- **H2S determination (sec. 3, p. 564), in full:** "Hydrogen sulfide released from cysteine mercaptals
  and mercaptoles in heated aqueous solutions was determined in the same manner as described in the
  previous paper. However, although in the previous paper to a sample containing 32 mg of sulfur were
  added 10 ml of water, in the present paper 100 ml of water were added in order to dissolve cystine
  completely. As the result of it the amounts of hydrogen sulfide from cystine and cysteine were
  increased considerably as compared with the values in the previous paper. After dissolving in
  100 ml of water, hydrochlorides were neutralized with 6 N sodium hydroxide. Carbon dioxide gas
  current was used as a carrier of hydrogen sulfide. After heating for one, three and six hours
  respectively on the oil bath (120°C), to the reaction mixture were added 20 ml of 6 N hydrochloric
  acid and carbon dioxide was passed through the solution furthermore thirty minutes. Hydrogen
  sulfide absorbed in 2% zinc acetate was derived into methylene blue and the absorbancy at 745 mµ
  was determined spectrophotometrically."
- **Concentration [Z]:** 32 mg S = 0.998 mmol S in 100 mL → **10 mM S** (cysteine 10 mM; cystine and
  the 2:1 mercaptals 5 mM molecule).
- **Vessel:** "in the same manner as described in the previous paper" — Part III's flask, CO2 bubbled
  through, outlet to zinc-acetate traps (Part III Fig. 2). Open, gas-swept.
- **Temperature:** 120 °C is the **oil bath**; the liquid in a gas-swept open flask at ~1 atm cannot
  exceed ~100 °C (Part III dossier's reading, ~85 % confidence; applies identically here).
- **pH:** not measured. Hydrochlorides (acetaldehyde, n-butyraldehyde mercaptals; pyruvic and
  levulinic mercaptoles; djenkolic acid as monohydrochloride) "were neutralized with 6 N sodium
  hydroxide" (end point not stated). Free cysteine and cystine: no adjustment stated → unbuffered,
  near their pI, under a CO2 sweep (**[Z]** pH ~4.5-5.5).
- **Quench / workup:** 20 mL 6 N HCl added after heating, CO2 30 min more (acid-labile sulfide
  formed during heating is included in every number).
- **Sweep rate:** not stated.
- **Substances (sec. 2):** L-cysteine "prepared by the reduction of L-cystine, which was separated
  from human hair, with tin-hydrochloric acid"; mercaptals/mercaptoles by L-cysteine 16.6 mmol + carbonyl
  8.3 mmol in 4 mL 6 N HCl (or H2SO4 for furfural); yields 44-59 %; N, S (and Cl) analyses printed
  for each (e.g. furfural mercaptal found N 8.24, S 20.16 %; calcd. for C11H16N2O5S2 N 8.75, S 20.02 %).
  The pyruvic mercaptole "gave a positive test for the sulfhydryl group when treated with sodium
  nitroprusside" — i.e. it is not a clean dithioacetal.

## 3. ANALYTICAL METHOD [M]

Methylene blue at 745 mµ after Zn-acetate trapping; calibration and recovery "in the same manner as
described in the previous paper" (Part III: "Recovery of hydrogen sulfide of this apparatus was
95±5%"). **No replicates, no errors, no LOD** in this paper.

### 4c. Against the engine [Z]

Apparent first-order k for free cysteine, −ln(1 − f)/t: **2.55e-5 (1 h), 3.05e-5 (3 h), 3.70e-5 (6 h)
min⁻¹**. Engine `cysteine_thermolysis_k`: 2.80e-5 min⁻¹ at 100 °C, pH 5 (Zheng & Ho Table I measured
2.65e-5) → predicted 0.17 / 0.50 / 1.00 % → measured/predicted **0.91 / 1.09 / 1.32**. At a literal
120 °C, pH 5: k = 2.47e-4 min⁻¹ → 1.47 / 4.35 / 8.5 % → measured/predicted 0.10 / 0.13 / 0.16. As in
Part III, the paper is consistent with Zheng & Ho only if the liquid is at ~100 °C.

The rising apparent k (2.55 → 3.70e-5) is an acceleration, not a deceleration: no sign of an H2S
sink in a swept vessel (H2S is removed as it forms, so liquid-phase sinks have little time to act).

## 5. WHAT THE PAPER DOES NOT STATE THAT A KINETIC MODEL NEEDS

1. **Liquid temperature** (only the bath, 120 °C); evaporation losses over 6 h under a sweep.
2. **pH** (none measured; neutralisation end point not stated; CO2-saturated).
3. **Sweep gas flow rate** and hence the H2S stripping rate.
4. **Replicates, errors, calibration curve** (deferred to Part III).
5. **Residual cysteine / cystine / mercaptal** after heating; no product other than H2S.
6. **Dissolved O2**: CO2 sweep presumably excludes air but purging before heating is not described.
7. Whether the 1, 3, 6 h points are separate runs (Part III's were) — implied, not stated.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **[!] Oil bath 120 °C ≠ liquid temperature** (open, swept, aqueous → ≤ ~100 °C).
2. **[!] Open, gas-swept system**: H2S leaves the liquid continuously; cumulative trapped H2S is a
   release measurement, not a liquid concentration.
3. **[!] Dilution raised cysteine H2S by 1.1-1.5×** between Parts III and IV: either a bimolecular
   H2S/precursor sink at 0.1 M, or run-to-run scatter (single determinations); cannot be separated.
4. Cystine's 1-h lag then overtaking is reproduced from Part III (0.5-1 h lag there).
5. Mercaptals here are 2:1 dithioacetals, not Part III's 1:1 thiazolidines.
6. The acid workup releases acid-labile sulfide (e.g. persulfide, metal sulfide) as H2S.
7. Registry: paper absent from `data/keys/papers.yml` (Part III, `mizutani1960`, is registered).

## Use in the model

- **Hold-out candidate for `k_cys_h2s` at ~100 °C, pH ~5, 10 mM** (three points), with the same
  declared liquid temperature (100 °C, band 95-100 °C) as `mizutani1960`. Passes at 0.9-1.3×.
- **Cystine → H2S**: the engine has no cystine species and no cystine H2S channel. Cystine releases
  H2S per S at 0.40× cysteine in the first hour and 1.3-1.5× by 3-6 h at ~100 °C, pH ~5 — a
  sign-level fact that a cystine pool (e.g. from cysteine autoxidation in air headspaces) feeds,
  rather than only consumes, sulfide at long times.
- **Dithioacetals of aldehydes** release 0.5-2.6× the H2S of free cysteine; furfural's is the
  largest (2.0-2.6×), agreeing in sign with Part III's furfural thiazolidine.
