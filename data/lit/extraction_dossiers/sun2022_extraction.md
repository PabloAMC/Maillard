## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

The review argues that Maillard-reacted peptides (MRPs: food protein hydrolysates or peptides heated
with reducing sugars, usually with added cysteine, sometimes thiamine and fat) are a natural route to
meat flavourings for plant-based meat analogues. It summarises meat-flavour precursors (Table 1), the
six reaction families (amino-acid/peptide thermolysis, sugar degradation, thiamine degradation, lipid
oxidation, Maillard, Maillard × lipid), MRP preparation (dry, wet, organic-solvent, ionic-liquid) and
analysis, and lists ~30 process-flavour recipes with conditions (Tables 3-4). **It reports no
concentration of MFT, FFT or any other thiol.** Its only numbers are literature "relative contents"
of meat volatiles in µg/g (Table 2) and the recipes' temperatures, times, pH and ingredient masses.
Several mechanistic claims are attributed to the wrong reference or are stated without support (section
4). For the model it is a **reading list**, not an anchor.

### 3.1 Table 1 — precursors (summary)

Lipids (oleic, linoleic, linolenic acid; lipid oxidation); sugars (ribose, glucose, xylose, starch, mannose,
fructose, maltose, mannose 6-phosphate; caramelisation, Maillard); thiamine (thermal degradation → furan,
furan mercaptan, thiophene, thiazole, aliphatic sulfur); amino acids and peptides (glycine, lysine,
alanine, valine, leucine, threonine, glutathione, "inosine, inosine monophosphate, inosine 5'
monophosphate"; Maillard, Strecker); sulfur compounds. References as printed include "Salter et al.,
2010" and "Mottram & Edwards, 1983", "Dwivedi & Arnold, 1973".

## 4. CITATION PROBLEMS IN THE REVIEW [!]

Checked against the reference list as printed (and against Crossref where noted):
- "Salter, Mottram & Whitfield 2010", JSFA **46**, 227-242, title "...involving cysteine, ribose and
  phospholipid": JSFA vol. 46 is 1988, and that title is Farmer, Mottram & Whitfield 1989 (JSFA 49:347).
  Salter et al. 1988 is the **glycine**/ribose/phospholipid paper.
- "Mottram 2010", JSFA **36**, 377-382 (pork heterocycles): vol. 36 is 1985.
- "Jousse et al. 2010", J. Food Sci. **67**: vol. 67 is 2002. "Guth & Grosch 2010", Flavour Fragr. J. **8**:
  1993. "Xu, Gao, Liu & Jian 2010", JSFA **88**:328-335: Crossref gives 10.1002/jsfa.3093, issued 2007
  (vol. 88 = 2008). This is **not** the corpus's `xu2010` (Xu, He, Liu & Gao, Czech J. Food Sci. 28:192,
  2010, same group and topic, pressure series); see candidate 15 in section 6.
- "Liu et al. 2015b" is cited for temperature thresholds of pyrazine/thiazole formation, but the listed
  Liu 2015b is a kokumi-peptide (yeast extract) paper; the claim fits Liu 2015a (LWT 64:316).
- Claim 10 (thiols mainly from thiamine) cites a roasted-duck paper (Liu 2020b).
These are recorded so that nobody imports a claim from this review without going to the primary paper.

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Nothing numerical.** No thiol concentration, no rate, no yield. Table 2's µg/g "relative contents" mix
cooking methods and quantification schemes (and carry formula errors); Tables 3-4 give recipes without
outcomes. The qualitative claims that touch the sulfur lane (lipid × sulfur gives alkylthiophenes and
thiols; GSH vs Cys; low pH favours meaty volatiles; thiamine as a thiol source) are all better taken from
the primary studies, several of which are already dossiers (`whitfield1988`, `farmer1990`, `hou2017`,
`cerny2007b`, `cerny2008`, `ames2001`, `wang2022`, `liu2021`, `zhai2021`; `xu2010` is a sibling of a
cited paper).

**Use: a reading list.** Section 6 lists the primary studies cited by the review that likely report
**quantified** MFT and/or FFT under stated conditions and are **not in the corpus**.
