## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**Tables 1–4 are complete and legible, but they contain no rate constants at all.** The
paper's 36 browning rate constants (3 water contents × 6 temperatures × 2 wavelengths) exist
**only in Figures 4A and 4B**, and their Arrhenius fits **only in Figure 5**. Rendering
Figure 5 at 300 dpi recovers **four activation energies with ± and R²: 117.8 ± 6.7,
155.8 ± 12.1, 128.6 ± 11.3 and 183.6 ± 4.9 kJ/mol** — of which the running text prints only
**117.8** and **155.8**, quotes the range endpoint **183.6** without attributing it, and
**never mentions 128.6 at all** (§5). Figure 5 also reveals that **only two of the three
water contents were ever Arrhenius-fitted**: the 5.85 g/100 g model appears in Figure 4 and
Table 4 but **not in Figure 5**, so **no activation energy exists for it** (§5.1). The
requested "all k and Ea per water content" is therefore delivered as: **4 of a possible 6
activation energies (printed, high confidence), 18 of 36 rate constants (digitised at
70–90 °C, ±20 %), and 18 marked unreadable** (§6). The finding the wave brief asked for —
**Ea depends on water content** — is confirmed and now fully quantified: **at 280 nm,
155.8 → 117.8 kJ/mol as water rises 3.78 → 6.86 g/100 g solids; at 420 nm, 183.6 → 128.6
over the same range.** ⚠️ It is **directly contradicted** by `pereyragonzales2010_extraction.md`
(this wave), which finds Ea independent of a_w; §7 of that dossier proposes the reconciliation.

### 5.3 THE WATER-CONTENT EFFECT ON Ea, fully quantified [Z]

| wavelength | Ea at **3.78 g/100 g** | Ea at **6.86 g/100 g** | **change** | **ΔEa per g water/100 g solids** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **280 nm** | **155.8 ± 12.1** | **117.8 ± 6.7** | **−38.0 kJ/mol (−24.4 %)** | **−12.3 kJ/mol per g/100 g** |
| **420 nm** | **183.6 ± 4.9** | **128.6 ± 11.3** | **−55.0 kJ/mol (−30.0 %)** | **−17.9 kJ/mol per g/100 g** |

**Both effects are far outside their own confidence limits** (the 420 nm pair are separated
by 55.0 against a combined uncertainty of ~12.3). **The finding is real within this system.**

The paper's own mechanism (p. 5255): *"a higher amount of water changed the physical state of
the model and affected the diffusion rate and solute mobility in the system, thus **lowering
the NEB activation energy**"* [C] Duckworth 1981; Sherwin et al. 2002.
And the convergence: *"higher water contents seemed to **decrease the temperature dependence**
of NEB rate. At the higher temperatures, the NEB rate increased, but **the difference of the
ln k between different water content models became smaller**. This suggested that at the high
temperatures the NEB rates were high and became **less water content dependent**."*

**⚠️ Also note the 420 nm Ea exceeds the 280 nm Ea at BOTH water contents, by 27.8 and
10.8 kJ/mol.** Brown-pigment formation is **more** temperature-sensitive than early-stage
yellow-product formation. The paper does not remark on this. **It is a consequence the repo's
Maillard cascade should be able to reproduce: a later step in the sequence carries a higher
apparent activation energy.**

## 7. TABLE 4 — THE WLF COEFFICIENTS. **Anchor: Table 4, p. 5255.** [F]

Title as printed: *"WLF Coefficients of the Model Food System at **280 nm**"*.
Linear forms used, eqs. (2) and (3) as printed [C] Nelson & Labuza 1994:
```
(2)  log[(k_ref/k) − 1] = −C2 / (C1·(T − T_ref))  −  1/C1
(3)  log[(k_g /k) − 1] = −C2′/(C1′·(T − Tg))      −  C1′/1     [as printed]
```
*"where k_ref, k_g, and k are reaction rate constants at reference temperature T_ref, glass
transition temperature Tg, and observation temperature T ... **The experimental temperature
closest to the predicted glass transition temperature was chosen as the reference
temperature, and the reaction rate at glass transition temperature, k_g, was derived using
the Arrhenius equation.** A plot of ln k versus 1/T gave the k₀ and Ea value ... Then, from
the predicted Tg, the k_g was obtained."*

   7034–7041 — *"Nonenzymatic browning in food models in the vicinity of glass transition:
   effects of fructose, glucose, and xylose as a reducing sugar."* **This is the third paper
   of the Roos-group trilogy** (the other two are in this wave) and it is the **only one that

   the foundational `T − Tg` browning paper that both this study and Lievonen & Roos 2002
   compare themselves to, and the source of the "10–40 °C above Tg" rule. **Cited by four of
   the eight papers in wave K4b.** The corpus should hold it directly.
3. **Roos, Y. H., Jouppila, K. & Zielasko, B. (1996)**, *J. Therm. Anal.* **47**, 1437–1450 —
