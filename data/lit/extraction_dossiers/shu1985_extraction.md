## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

0.05 mol L-cysteine·HCl·H2O in 500 g water (≈ 0.1 mol/kg), titrated with 10 % Na2CO3 to pH 2.2, 5.1 or
7.1 (no buffer), heated **½ h at 160 °C** in a sealed 2-L Parr bomb. The vacuum-steam-distilled,
solvent-extracted volatiles weighed **48 mg (pH 2.2), 202 mg (pH 5.1), 18 mg (pH 7.1)**. Composition
(GC-FID area %, Table I): at pH 2.2, **1,2,3-trithia-5-cycloheptene 34.4 %** and **2-thiophenethiol
14.1 %**; at pH 5.1, **acetone 55.8 %** and the two **3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes 13.1 + 3.0 %**; at
pH 7.1, the trithiolanes **14.1 + 14.2 %**, **2-thiazolyl ethyl ketone 8.0 %**, **3-methyl-1,2,4-trithiane
6.3 %**. **H2S, NH3, CO2, acetaldehyde and cysteine itself were not measured at any pH.** The paper
therefore says nothing quantitative about how fast cysteine degrades or how much sulfide it
releases; its pH maximum at pH 5.1 is a maximum in *recovered organic volatiles*.

### 3.4 Mechanisms stated [Q]
- Cysteine → **mercaptoacetaldehyde** (decarboxylation + deamination) and → **acetaldehyde**
  (rearrangement + decarboxylation); their interaction gives 1,2,3-trithia-5-cycloheptene (Fig. 5).
- Mercaptoacetaldehyde self-aldol + hemithioacetal → 2,4-dihydroxy-3-mercaptotetrahydrothiophene →
  2- and 3-thiophenethiol by dehydration and **loss of H2S** (Fig. 6).
- Trithiolane formation "occurred more readily at pH = pI than at pH < pI", the **opposite** of the
  cystine result; attributed to disulfide stability vs pH.

## 4. DERIVED QUANTITIES [Z] (mine, never printed)

**Concentration.** 0.05 mol in 500 g ≈ **0.10 mol/kg** (≈ 0.1 M) — the same nominal concentration as
Zheng & Ho 1994's thermolysis runs. Cysteine (free base, 121.16 g/mol) = 6.06 g.

**Volatile yield as a fraction of the cysteine charge (mass basis):** pH 2.2 **0.79 %**, pH 5.1
**3.3 %**, pH 7.1 **0.30 %**. Ratio pH 5.1 : 2.2 : 7.1 = **11.2 : 2.7 : 1**.

**Sulfur recovered in the main polysulfides** (area % ≈ mass %, an FID assumption the authors do not
make; S atoms from the formula):

| pH | compound(s) | mg [Z] | mmol S [Z] | % of the 50 mmol cysteine S |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.2 | 1,2,3-trithia-5-cycloheptene (C4H6S3, 150) | 16.5 | 0.33 | **0.66 %** |
| 2.2 | 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes (C4H8S3, 152) | 2.6 | 0.052 | 0.10 % |
| 5.1 | 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes | 32.5 | 0.64 | **1.3 %** |
| 7.1 | 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes | 5.1 | 0.10 | 0.20 % |

So **≤ ~2 % of the cysteine sulfur is accounted for at any pH**; the fate of the other ≥ 98 % (H2S
lost to the 1.5-L headspace and discarded, cystine, non-volatile products, unreacted cysteine) is not
measured.

**Acetone at pH 5.1:** 55.8 % × 202 mg ≈ 113 mg ≈ **1.9 mmol ≈ 3.9 mol % of the cysteine**, if area %
is mass %. No pathway is offered for a C3 ketone from cysteine at this level; see Flags.

**What the model's measured thermolysis set says about the same pots (arithmetic, not a model run).**
`parameters_sulfur.ZHENG_CYSTEINE_THERMOLYSIS` (Zheng & Ho 1994, fitted 80–110 °C) evaluated at
160 °C, 30 min, first order, using the code's pH rule (pH-3.0 pair below pH 5, interpolation 5–9):

| Shu pH | Zheng pair used | k (s⁻¹) at 160 °C | fraction of cysteine → H2S in 30 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.2 | pH 3.0 pair | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | **≈ 23 %** |
| 5.1 | ≈ pH 5.0 pair | ≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁴ | **≈ 27–28 %** |
| 7.1 | ≈ pH 7.0 pair | ≈ 1.1 × 10⁻³ | **≈ 86 %** |

This is a **50 °C extrapolation above Zheng's fitted range** and is shown only to make the
comparison explicit: the measured-kinetics set says sulfide release is **largest at pH 7.1**, while
Shu's recovered organic volatiles are **smallest at pH 7.1** (11× below pH 5.1). The two are not in
contradiction, because Shu never measured H2S; they say the pH maximum of the *organic* sulfur
volatiles is set downstream of sulfide release (carbonyl supply — acetaldehyde, mercaptoacetaldehyde
— and the condensation chemistry), not by cysteine thermolysis.

## 5. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**The request.** "H2S / volatile yields vs pH — test of the model's cysteine thermolysis pH
dependence."

**Verdict: it cannot test the thermolysis step. It can supply qualitative pH-shape tests of
downstream cysteine-only chemistry.**

- **(a) Cysteine → H2S pH law: NOT TESTABLE.** No H2S, no cysteine remaining, no NH3, no time course,
  one temperature (160 °C, outside Zheng's 80–110 °C), unbuffered with the post-heating pH unknown.
  The authors' "vigorous at pI / very mild at pH 7.1" is inferred from extract mass and must **not** be
  read as a cysteine degradation rate. Any claim in the repository that "Shu 1985 shows cysteine
  degrades fastest at its pI" (Madruga & Mottram 1995 and Xu 2008 both cite it in roughly that sense)
  overstates the paper.
- **(b) Usable as a directional shape, weakly:**
  1. Total organic volatiles from cysteine alone, 160 °C/30 min: **pH 5.1 > pH 2.2 > pH 7.1, 11 : 2.7 : 1.**
  2. **3,5-Dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes** (H2S + acetaldehyde condensation products): ~2.6 mg (pH 2.2),
     ~32 mg (pH 5.1), ~5 mg (pH 7.1) [Z]: maximum near the pI.
  3. **Mercaptoacetaldehyde-derived ring products** (1,2,3-trithia-5-cycloheptene, 2-/3-thiophenethiol)
     dominate only at pH 2.2 and are absent/trace at pH 5.1.
  4. **Thiazoles / 2-thiazolyl ethyl ketone / 2-acetylthiazole** appear mainly at pH 7.1 (8.0 % + T).
  These are within-study, cysteine-only (no sugar) pH directions. They bear on the sulfur lane only
  if the model carries acetaldehyde/mercaptoacetaldehyde chemistry from cysteine, which it does not
  (H2S is the only cysteine thermolysis product, `k_cys_h2s`). So today they are **not directly
  scorable** against any model species.
- **(c) A real, if indirect, consistency note for the thermolysis parameterisation.** If the Zheng set
  is right at 160 °C, 23–86 % of the cysteine releases H2S in these pots, yet ≤ 2 % of the cysteine
  sulfur turns up in organic volatiles. That is consistent with H2S being mostly lost to headspace or
  non-volatiles, and gives no support for, or against, the model's pH-increasing k_cys_h2s.

**Not usable:** any absolute level (area % only, no response factors, no internal standard); any
rate; the pH 7.1 "mild degradation" as a kinetic statement.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **No H2S, no cysteine assay, no kinetics.** The only quantities are extract mass and GC area %.
2. **Unbuffered, pH measured cold before heating only.** Cysteine thermolysis releases NH3 (and the
   Na2CO3 titrant leaves carbonate); the pH during 30 min at 160 °C is unknown and could drift by
   units (Madruga & Mottram 1995 make exactly this criticism of unbuffered systems).
3. **Temperature appears only in the Introduction** ("160 °C"); the Experimental says only "heated for
   ½ h" and defers to the cystine paper. 160 °C is consistent with the companion DMHF paper on the
   same PDF ("160 °C for 30 min in a closed system at ... 2.2, 5.1, and 7.1").
4. **pH 2.1 vs 2.2** — two labels for one condition (Section 2).
5. **Table I title says "Cystine".** Misprint; content is the cysteine experiment.
6. **Area % is not mass %.** FID response varies by compound class; area % across pH columns compares
   compositions of extracts of different total mass (48 vs 202 vs 18 mg), so a compound's area % must
   be multiplied by the extract mass before comparing pH values (done in Section 4, with the FID caveat).
7. **Acetone at 55.8 % of the pH 5.1 extract (~4 mol % of cysteine)** has no mechanism in the paper.
   It is a common laboratory solvent; the authors state the pH 5.1 run was repeated and confirmed,
   but the source is unexplained. Treat the pH 5.1 total (202 mg) as possibly inflated by it: without
   acetone the pH 5.1 extract is ~89 mg, still ~2× pH 2.2 and ~5× pH 7.1 [Z].
8. **2-L bomb with 500 g charge**: ~1.5 L headspace; H2S and other low boilers partition there and are
   not part of the vacuum-steam distillate. The extract under-represents the most volatile products.
9. **One replicate (pH 5.1 repeated once), no dispersion.**
10. **Peak-number columns are retention order, not identities**; several compounds are on one column
    only (e.g. at pH 7.1, 2-acetylthiazole, 5-methylthiophene-2-carboxaldehyde and the dithiazine
    are seen on CWX only).
    Identification is by MS library match only, except peak 20 (MS + NMR). Nothing confirmed against
    an authentic standard's retention index.
