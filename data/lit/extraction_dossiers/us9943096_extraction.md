## 1. Methods

**Reaction system used in Examples 4-9 and 14 (PDF pp. 24-26, 33; cols 21-26, 39-40).** LegH
(soy leghemoglobin) at 1 % ("pre-cooked w/w" in Example 4) mixed with precursors in phosphate buffer at
pH 6, heated to 150 °C for 3 minutes. Precursors per example: cysteine 10 mM with glucose 20 mM or
ribose 20 mM (Example 4); the same plus thiamine 1 mM, with or without glutamic acid 10 mM (Example 5);
cysteine 10 mM and ribose 10 mM with four different heme proteins at 1 % w/w (Example 9; note ribose is
10 mM here, 20 mM in Example 4); glucose 20, ribose 20, cysteine 10, thiamine 1, glutamic acid 1 mM
(Example 14, "run in duplicate"). Volatiles taken from the headspace and identified by GC-MS.

**Magic Mix (Example 11, PDF pp. 29-30, cols 32-33; Table 13).** A mixture of amino acids, sugars and
small molecules at "estimated concentrations ... in beef based on their values reported in literature";
the sulfur and sugar entries are cysteine 0.8, methionine 0.7, thiamine 0.5, glucose 5.6, ribose 6.7,
maltodextrin 5.0 mM, plus glutamic acid 3.4, IMP 0.6, GMP 0.24, lactic acid 1.0, creatine 1.0, NaCl 10,
KCl 10, Kphos pH 6.0 10 (all mM as printed). For GC-MS: Magic Mix with 1 % w/v LegH at pH 6.0, cooked
with shaking at 150 °C for 3 minutes, then SPME for 12 minutes at 50 °C on the headspace; peaks named by
"a search algorithm" on retention time and mass spectrum. For sensory: Magic Mix and 1 % w/v LegH added
to the meat replica (pH 6.0, Table 4 matrix) and baked in a convection oven for 7 minutes at 160 °C;
control = replica with 1 % w/v LegH only.

**Matrix (Table 4, PDF p. 23, cols 19-20).** Replica burger, % pre-cooked w/w: pea vicilin 3.86, soy
protein concentrate 2.52, bamboo fibre 0.34, NaCl 0.54, pea legumin 2, soy protein isolate 4.68, wheat
gluten 4.68, coconut oil 15, soy lecithin 0.1, k-carrageenan 1, LegH 1. Water to balance. Burgers in
Examples 1-3 cooked 5 minutes at 150 °C.

**Other heme sources.** Example 12 (PDF p. 31): ferrous chlorin made from spinach chlorophyll and
FeCl2, 0.35 %, with Magic Mix, 150 °C / 3 min, against LegH 0.35 % (Table 16, presence only). Example 13
(PDF p. 32): equine myoglobin 0.35 % in solution versus bovine hemin coupled to a CM Sepharose resin,
each with Magic Mix, 150 °C / 3 min (Table 17). Example 10 (PDF p. 27): a "Maillard reaction mix" of
50 mM potassium phosphate pH 6.0, 5 mM cysteine, 10 mM glucose, 0.1 mM thiamine, 0.1 % w/v LegH, with
lipids, 150 °C / 3 min, cooled to 50 °C, SPME headspace (lipid results not used here).

**Sensory.** Trained panels; Example 1 on a 7-point hedonic scale (1 = dislike extremely, 7 = like
extremely), Examples 2-3 on a 5-point scale; Example 11 on a 7-point attribute scale with 7 = "a standard
80:20 ground beef", five tasters. Panel size is not printed for Examples 1-9.

## 3. What it means for the model and for formulation

**(a) The thiamine row** (`results/validation/cultivated_tissue_invariance_prereg.md` sections 10-11:
restoring thiamine comparable to cysteine as an MFT source at 100 °C, close to nothing at 140 °C). This
patent cannot test it. Every thiamine comparison is at 150 °C for 3 minutes, one temperature, so it says
nothing about the 100 °C versus 140 °C contrast. What it does say (Example 5, text only): at 150 °C, adding
1 mM thiamine to a cysteine-sugar-heme system produced thiazoles (4-methylthiazole,
4-methyl-5-thiazoleethanol) and the MFT disulfide that the thiamine-free mixture did not. That is
qualitative support for thiamine being an MFT/MFT-dimer source even with 10 mM cysteine present at
150 °C, which leans mildly against "negligible at 140 °C", but at millimolar thiamine (about 250 to 2,300 times
the beef range the box uses) and with no MFT measured, it does not move the 0.3 probability in section 10.

**(b) Cysteine and ribose as MFT/FFT sources.** The engine's claim is that pentose with cysteine is the
main MFT/FFT route and that ribose beats glucose (section 11: ribose over glucose is the dominant reversal).
Table 8 agrees in direction only weakly: ribose adds 15 peaks over glucose, among them thiophenes and
2-methyl-thiazole, but neither MFT nor FFT appears under either sugar, so the table does not show the
specific thiols the engine scores. Table 14 shows MFT and its dimer at "M" in a ribose + glucose +
cysteine + thiamine system; it cannot attribute them to a precursor. No table in this patent ranks
cysteine against thiamine against ribose by omission with MFT measured.

**(c) Heme.** The engine has no heme or iron catalysis. `grep -i "heme\|hemin\|leghemoglobin\|iron"
src/kinetic_core/*.py` returns 19 lines, all of them the substring "heme" inside "Scheme" (Martins'
scheme, Nedvidek 1992 Scheme 2/3, De Vleeschouwer Scheme 4, Frankel Scheme 1); a whole-word grep for
heme, hemin, leghemoglobin, iron, haem, myoglobin, ferrous or ferric returns nothing. The only metal
handling is in the sulfur lane: thiol dimerisation (MFT and FFT to their disulfides) is first order in an
explicit oxidant pool, and `parameters_sulfur.py` records that the module "cannot predict dimerisation in
a metal-containing matrix it is not told about"; copper appears only as a cited condition in
`trunk_conditions.py`. So a heme-containing plant-based patty is outside the engine twice over: no
catalysis of MFT formation, and no iron-driven MFT-to-disulfide oxidation.

What the patent's heme evidence is worth: every heme-versus-no-heme comparison is either sensory
(Table 5: beefiness 3.20 vs 1.30 on 7 points, n and test not printed) or a presence list (Table 18,
Example 4 text). The heme protein arm always adds 1 % protein as well as heme, and no example runs an
equal-protein, heme-free control (apo-globin, albumin), so heme catalysis is confounded with added amino
acids, peptides and co-purified lipid. Example 13 (hemin on resin) removes the globin but has no
resin-only control. Directionally, iron porphyrins are plausible catalysts of thiol oxidation (the MFT
dimer appears wherever MFT does here), and the literature on iron and Maillard flavour points both ways;
this patent does not measure a rate or a yield. Probability that heme materially raises free MFT yield
(beyond what the same protein mass without heme gives) in a plant-based patty cooked like Table 4:
about 0.5, low confidence; the patent moves it little because the decisive control is absent.

**Formulation.** For a plant-based product the patent's practical recipe is: 1 % heme protein plus
cysteine (~10 mM), a sugar (glucose and/or ribose, ~20 mM) and thiamine (~1 mM), pH 6, cooked hot and
fast (150 °C, minutes). Taken with the model, ribose is the lever the engine and Table 8 both favour,
thiamine is cheap insurance that at least adds thiazoles and the MFT dimer, and whether heme adds
anything beyond protein remains unshown by this document.

## What it does not give

- No quantification: no ng/g, no internal standard, no OAV, no peak areas, no class thresholds for
  0/S/M/L or low/medium/high/very high, no replicate spread for any GC-MS table.
- No MFT or FFT in Tables 8, 9 or 18; FFT nowhere in the data tables at all.
- No no-heme arm except Table 18 and unprinted Example 4/5/6/7/8 controls; no equal-protein heme-free
  control anywhere.
- No temperature or time series; every model-system reaction is 150 °C / 3 min (Example 10 also).
- No GC column, SPME fibre, extraction details beyond "12 minutes at 50 °C" (Example 11) and "SPME"
  (Examples 6, 10).
- No panel size for Examples 1-9, no statistics beyond means and SDs in Tables 5-6.
