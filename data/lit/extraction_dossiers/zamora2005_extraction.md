# Zamora & Hidalgo 2005 — EXTRACTION (REVIEW)
### *"Coordinate Contribution of Lipid Oxidation and Maillard Reaction to the Nonenzymatic Food Browning"*
### An 11-page review, mostly about **browning**, of how lipid oxidation and the Maillard reaction feed each other. It covers Maillard products as pro- **and** antioxidants, lipid oxidation products modifying the Maillard pathway, shared intermediates (glyoxal, methylglyoxal, N-substituted hydroxyalkylpyrroles) and shared polymerisation (aldol, polypyrrole). **Secondary evidence: a mechanism pointer, not a measurement for fitting. It prints no table and no volatile-level number.** It does not cite farmer1992 or elmore2002. Read 2026-11-15 for the L1 lipid-lever scoping.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

The review argues that lipid oxidation and the Maillard reaction run in parallel: a key first intermediate
(hydroperoxide / Amadori product), then fragments, then carbonyl–amine or aldol polymers. It concludes that they should
be treated as one pathway that either lipids or sugars can start. For the L1 levers it gives four points.
(1) **Maillard products act in both directions on lipid oxidation.** Glycated phosphatidylethanolamine (an Amadori
product) **increases** TBARS (Breitling-Utzmann 2001; Bucala 1993), and Amadori compounds catalyse linoleic acid
oxidation (Farag 1982). Browned proteins, whether from ribose or from oxidised lipid, are **antioxidant**, and the
activity tracks browning and lysine loss (Alaiz 1997b, 1999). (2) **Lipid products modify the Maillard reaction**,
mostly through aldehydes taking H2S away from sulfur-heterocycle formation (Mottram's cysteine/methionine + ribose +
phospholipid systems). The effect is stronger with more PUFA (MacLeod & Seyyedain-Ardebili 1981; Meynier & Gandemer 1994),
and oil unsaturation moves pyrazines in xylose- or glucose-lysine (Negroni 2001). (3) **Carbonyl–amine trapping by
lysine** of 4,5-epoxy-2-alkenals gives N-substituted pyrroles; this is the authors' own chemistry. (4) **The direction
depends on temperature.** Oxidised lipids brown BSA more than ribose at 25–50 °C; ribose browns it more at 80–120 °C
(Hidalgo 1999). Glyoxal and methylglyoxal from sugar are negligible at acidic pH and **rise with pH**
(Hayashi & Namiki 1986). **No hexanal, nonanal, 2-pentylfuran or decadienal number appears, and no cysteine or thiamine
comparison.**

### 2.4 Common polymerisation (browning; outside the volatile question)

- Aldol condensation of Schiff bases from lipid carbonyls and protein amines (Mohammad et al. 1949; Montgomery &
  Day 1965; Gardner 1979). Lipid carbonyls are consumed into polymers, and the polymers can release volatiles again on
  scission. This is the same amine-catalysed aldehyde sink that Whitfield 1992 §2.3 cites.
- Maillard melanoidins by aldol-type condensation of sugar fragments (Cämmerer et al. 2002; Yaylayan & Kaminsky 1998;
  Cämmerer & Kroh 1995).
- Polypyrrole formation from either source (Hidalgo & Zamora 1993b; Tressl et al. 1998b; Zamora et al. 2000).

## 3. NUMBERS [M]

The review has no tables. The printed numbers are:

- Sugar–amine melanoidin fractions of ~7000 Da and above make up **> 95 % by weight** (Tressl et al. 1998a).
- N-substituted pyrroles found in **more than 20** fresh foods (Zamora et al. 1999).
- Linear methylene-bridged polypyrroles of **up to 12** pyrrole units; up to **four** conjugated pyrroles from the
  hexamer onward (Tressl et al. 1998b).
- Temperature range **25–120 °C**, five temperatures (Hidalgo et al. 1999); BSA + ribose **24 h** (Alaiz et al. 1999).

None of these is a lipid-volatile quantity. Nothing here can set a band.

## 4. WHERE THE REVIEW SAYS THE DIRECTION DEPENDS ON CONDITIONS

1. **MRP effect on lipid oxidation:** pro-oxidant (Amadori-PE, Amadori-catalysed linoleate oxidation) vs antioxidant
   (browned proteins). The sign depends on the stage and species of the product: early Amadori product vs late
   browned protein.
2. **Temperature:** lipid-driven browning dominates at 25–50 °C; sugar-driven at 80–120 °C (Hidalgo 1999). A 120 °C
   pot sits in the sugar-dominated regime.
3. **Lipid unsaturation:** more PUFA means stronger quenching of Maillard sulfur products (meat studies); oil unsaturation
   reorders pyrazines in both xylose and glucose systems (Negroni 2001).
4. **pH:** sugar fragmentation to glyoxal / methylglyoxal rises with pH (Hayashi & Namiki 1986). Lipid-side pH
   dependence is not discussed in the text.

or for setting a band (there are no measurements).

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Browning focus.** The review is about colour and polymers. Its volatile content is the single §2.2 paragraph and
   Negroni 2001. Do not read it as a lipid-volatile review.
2. **Farag 1982** sits in the antioxidant list in the text although its title reports catalysis of linoleic acid
   oxidation by Amadori compounds. Read the primary before assigning a sign.
3. The H2S-trapping conclusion is attributed to "Mottram and colleagues" with **no specific citation**; Mottram &
   Edwards 1983 and Gandemer 1999 are cited in the previous sentence.
4. "Cobn and Day (1965)" in the text is **Cobb & Day 1965** in the reference list (typo). "Chuten" in the reference
   list is Chuyen.
5. Hidalgo 1999 and Alaiz 1999 have **pH** in their titles, but the review reports only their temperature results.
6. The cover and the first page are a 2014 download stamp; the article layout is the 2005 print.
