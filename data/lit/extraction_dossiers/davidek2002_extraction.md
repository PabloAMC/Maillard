## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

DFG (0.1 mol/L) was heated at 90 °C for up to 7 h at **constant pH 5, 6, 7 or 8, held by a pH-stat
(3 M NaOH) whose electrode measured at 90 °C**, either in pure water or in **100 mmol/L phosphate**
(equimolar with DFG). DFG loss rises with pH and is accelerated by phosphate, most at pH 5–7. The
paper prints **no rate constants**. Its only printed kinetic numbers are the times to 25 % loss in water:
**6 h (pH 5), 135, 40 and 20 min (pH 6, 7, 8)**, plus "about 70 %" remaining after 7 h at pH 5 in water.
Fitted to my Fig. 2 digitisation, first-order constants (h⁻¹, 90 °C) are **water 0.047–0.055 / 0.144–0.180 /
0.521–0.525 / 1.03–1.06** and **phosphate 0.131–0.171 / 0.52–0.60 / 1.01–1.13 / 1.31–1.35** at pH 5/6/7/8.
The band runs across three fitting methods. It comes from departure from first-order, not from read error (§4.3). pH 5–6 curves have a **0.5–0.8 h
induction period**, so a single first-order k is a compromise there. In the authors' words, phosphate
"showed a similar effect … as increasing the pH of the unbuffered solution by one unit", and the digitised k
values confirm it. Products per mol DFG decomposed: **glycine 0.46–0.88**, falling as the reaction advances;
**glucose + mannose 0.02–0.07 in water and 0.03–0.18 in phosphate** (highest at pH 5 phosphate; Glc:Man ≈ 7:3);
**acetic acid up to 0.64 and formic up to 0.14** (sum up to 0.76). **1-DG, 3-DG and methylglyoxal were not measured.**

- **Read-error contribution:** Monte Carlo with σ = 0.3 mmol/L on every point gives SD(k) of 0.0003–0.0065 h⁻¹
  (≤ 0.6 %). **The honest uncertainty is the spread across methods (a)–(d)**, which reflects departure
  from first-order. Recommended band per cell = min–max of (a)–(c):
  water **0.047–0.055 / 0.144–0.180 / 0.521–0.525 / 1.03–1.06**; phosphate **0.131–0.171 / 0.52–0.60 /
  1.01–1.13 / 1.31–1.35**. Point value = (a).
- **Shape [!]:** pH 5 and 6, in both media, are not first-order from t = 0. Local k over 0–1 h is
  0.4–0.6 × the later value. NLS residuals run +2…+8 mmol/L early and −2…−5 late. After ~1 h the decay is
  log-linear within the read error. pH 7–8 are first-order to within ±10 % from the first interval on
  (the 0–0.5 h interval is ~10–20 % slower everywhere). The paper does not define t = 0 or state the heat-up
  time of 150 mL to 90 °C, so **a heat-up artefact cannot be separated from a genuine induction
  (autocatalysis)**. The lag is longest at pH 5 phosphate (0.8 h), which is hard to explain by heat-up alone.
- [Z] pH slopes, water, d log10 k / dpH: **0.49–0.51 (5→6), 0.46–0.56 (6→7), 0.30–0.31 (7→8)**. Phosphate:
  0.55–0.60, 0.23–0.34, 0.06–0.11. Far shallower than unit order in OH⁻. The water series flattens
  between pH 7 and 8; the phosphate series flattens already above pH 6.

### 4.4 Phosphate effect [Z]

| pH | k_P / k_W, NLS (a) | k_P / k_W, post-lag (c) | κ_tot = (k_P/k_W − 1)/0.1 M (M⁻¹) | Δk = k_P − k_W (h⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2.79 | 3.11 | **18–21** | 0.08–0.12 |
| 6 | 3.62 | 3.35 | **24–26** | 0.38–0.42 |
| 7 | 2.17 | 1.94 | **9.4–11.7** | 0.49–0.61 |
| 8 | 1.27 | 1.24 | **2.4–2.7** | 0.25–0.28 |

**Speciation check [Z; it assumes a phosphate pKa2 the paper does not give].** Assume pKa2(H2PO4⁻) at
90 °C and I ≈ 0.2 M lies in **6.8–7.2**. Dividing κ_tot by the H2PO4⁻ fraction then gives a **near-constant
κ' ≈ 15–30 M⁻¹ (per M H2PO4⁻) across pH 5–8** (pKa2 7.0: 18–29; pKa2 7.2: 15–28). Dividing by the HPO4²⁻
fraction instead spans 3 → > 1000 M⁻¹. **The relative phosphate enhancement is therefore consistent with
a multiplicative term proportional to [H2PO4⁻], k_P = k_W(pH)·(1 + κ'[H2PO4⁻]), and inconsistent with
HPO4²⁻ catalysis.** That matches the species the authors cite from Potman & van Wijk. The pH 8 cell rests
on a ratio of 1.24–1.27 and on the assumed pKa2, so it is uncertain by about ±30 %. With one phosphate
concentration, **linearity in [phosphate] is not tested here**.

## 6. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**(a) pH-speciation law for Amadori (DFG) degradation — usable, one temperature.**
- Use the four water k values (§4.3, band per cell) at 90 °C, pH measured hot, 0.1 M DFG, no added salt.
  They span pH 5–8 with d log k/dpH ≈ 0.5 at pH 5–7 and ≈ 0.3 at pH 7–8. A pure k·α law with one
  ionisable group cannot be steeper than slope 1 or flatten this early unless the relevant pKa at 90 °C
  is near 7–7.5, or a pH-independent k0 floor exists. The data constrain the combination, not each
  parameter separately; the pKa must come from elsewhere (Mossine 1994 gives 8.18 at 25 °C only).
- Because pH was measured at 90 °C, **no hot-pH correction is needed for this dataset**. That is rare,
  and is the main reason the series is valuable. Electrode calibration details are absent (§7).
- One temperature only: no activation energy, and no temperature dependence of any pKa, can be taken
  from this paper.

**(b) Phosphate catalysis term — usable at one phosphate concentration.**
- κ_tot(pH) = 18–21 / 24–26 / 9.4–11.7 / 2.4–2.7 M⁻¹ at pH 5/6/7/8 (relative form, total phosphate
  0.1 M). Equivalently, one κ' ≈ 15–30 M⁻¹ on [H2PO4⁻] if pKa2(90 °C) ≈ 7.0–7.2 [Z, §4.4].
- **Not anchored:** the dependence on phosphate concentration (only 0.1 M tested; linear vs saturating
  is open), the counter-ion, and ionic-strength effects separated from catalysis. Water and phosphate
  runs differ in ionic strength as well as in phosphate (§7).
- **Hold-out hygiene [!]:** if the phosphate series is fitted to set κ (B53), it cannot also be a
  pre-registered hold-out for the speciation law (B52), and vice versa. The water/phosphate "+1 pH unit"
  coincidence (§4.2) has been seen by anyone who has opened Fig. 2.

**(c) Product branching per mol DFG decomposed — usable as yields, not as rate constants.**
- Glycine release 0.74–0.88 at 5 % conversion, falling with conversion (Table 2) → a glycine sink
  downstream.
- Parent sugars (reverse Amadori or glucosone reduction; not distinguished): 3–7 % in water, 5–18 % in
  phosphate, Glc:Man ≈ 0.68:0.32 everywhere.
- Acetic 0–0.64 and formic 0–0.14, rising with pH; acetic formation stops when DFG is exhausted.
- **No 1-DG, 3-DG, methylglyoxal or other dicarbonyl data.** This paper cannot split 1,2- vs
  2,3-enolisation directly. Acetic acid is the only 2,3-route proxy.
- NaOH uptake (Table 5) is a total-acid observable at constant pH. At pH 5–7 it exceeds acetic + formic,
  so unidentified acids are formed.

**Cannot anchor:** temperature dependence; food-matrix behaviour; anything at DFG ≪ 0.1 M (all runs are
0.1 M, so second-order side reactions of DFG and its products are at their most favoured);
replicate-to-replicate scatter.

## 7. CAVEATS

1. **No rate constants are printed.** Every k above is mine, from a digitised figure. The 25 %-loss times
   (§3) are the only printed kinetic numbers and agree with the digitisation within 2–6 min.
2. **Single reaction run per condition, apparently.** Only analytical duplicates are mentioned. Fig. 2
   CV < 10 % above 1.5 mmol/L is analytical.
3. **pH-stat details missing:** electrode calibration (temperature, buffers) and temperature
   compensation are not stated. A 90 °C reading from an electrode calibrated at 25 °C without proper
   compensation can be off by a few tenths of a unit. Treat the stated pH as ±0.1–0.2.
4. **Phosphate buffer composition unstated** (Na⁺ or K⁺ salt, how its pH was set). The pH-stat can only
   add base, so the buffer must have started at or below each set-point at 90 °C.
5. **Ionic strength uncontrolled and different between media** [Z, my estimate; nothing is printed]:
   water runs reach roughly 0.05–0.1 M from DFG⁻/Na⁺ at the higher pH. Phosphate runs sit at ~0.15–0.3 M
   depending on pH. Part of the "phosphate" effect could be
   a salt effect. Not separable here.
6. **Induction period at pH 5–6 (0.5–0.8 h)** with t = 0 and heat-up not defined. First-order k differs
   by up to ×1.3 between methods there. Use the band, not a point.
7. **Low-concentration points (< 3 mmol/L) are not individually resolvable** (overlapping symbols on the
   axis). They were excluded from fits. pH 8 and phosphate pH 7 fits rest on 4–6 points within the first 2.5–3 h.
8. **Concentration level:** 0.1 M DFG plus 0.1 M phosphate is far above food levels. Reverse-Amadori
   sugar yields at 0.1 M may not scale to mM.
9. Parent-sugar mechanism unresolved by the authors: reverse Amadori vs glucosone reduction. No labelling.
