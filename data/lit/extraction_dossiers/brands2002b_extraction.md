## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH VERDICT

**This is a MELANOIDIN EXTINCTION-COEFFICIENT paper, NOT a rate-constant paper.** It contains
**zero rate constants, zero activation energies, and a single temperature (120 °C)**. There is no
kinetic model, no Arrhenius analysis, no rate law and no fitted rate parameter anywhere in the six
pages. What it *does* give — and this is genuinely valuable and unique — is the **average molar
extinction coefficient of protein-bound (casein) melanoidins, measured by ¹⁴C radiolabel**, i.e. the
conversion factor that turns a spectrophotometric A₄₂₀ into a melanoidin *concentration* expressed as
mol of incorporated sugar per litre: **ε = 477 (± 50) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ for glucose–casein and
527 (± 35) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ for fructose–casein**, the difference being non-significant at the 5 % level.
Crucially, both values are measured on the **dialysed >12 kDa retentate** (§4.3), so ε here is the
extinction coefficient of the *high-molecular-weight* fraction, not of the whole reaction mixture —
a distinction that matters because in this system ~70 % of the colour is retained in that fraction
whereas in sugar–amino-acid systems only ~10 % is. Secondary deliverables: the browning time course at
120 °C before and after dialysis (Figs. 1 and 2); the ¹⁴C-incorporated-sugar, Amadori-compound and
melanoidin time courses (Fig. 3); a microanalysis table of sugar molecules incorporated per casein
molecule and per lysine residue (Table 1); and the loss of available lysine residues (Fig. 5). The
paper's ε is the natural companion to Martins 2003c (glucose/glycine ε, being extracted by a sibling
agent) and to the Knol 2005 ε = 282 already declared as a Module-4 hold-out — see §9.

### 2.2 Dialysis (p. 1179)

Verbatim: *"To separate unreacted radiolabeled substances and low molecular weight products from the
high molecular weight products, the samples were dialyzed. An aliquot (10 mL) of each reaction mixture
was dialyzed in Visking tubing with a cutoff value of 12000 Da. The samples were dialyzed against
distilled water for 4 or 5 days (eight water replacements). After dialysis, the retentate was removed
to a volumetric flask containing 20 mL of 20 % (w/w) sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS) solution and made up
to 100 mL with distilled water. SDS was added to dissolve any flocculated protein."*

→ **MWCO = 12 000 Da; 4–5 days; 8 water changes; retentate diluted 10× into 4 % (w/w) final SDS.**

### 2.3 Scintillation counting (p. 1179)

Verbatim: *"Aliquots (1 mL) of the diluted dialyzed fraction were pipetted into a scintillation vial
containing 10 mL of scintillation fluid. The vial was shaken vigorously and counted for 1 min using a
Packard Tri-Carb 1900TR scintillation counter. The count due to ¹⁴C was corrected for quenching by the
internal standard method. The specific activity of [¹⁴C]glucose or [¹⁴C]fructose in the reaction
mixture was calculated from the counts obtained from 1 mL of a 50-fold diluted unheated reaction
mixture and was expressed as number of disintegrations per minute (dpm) per mole of glucose or
fructose. … Once the quench-corrected number of counts for a certain sample was known, the
concentration of [U-¹⁴C]sugar incorporated into the high molecular weight fraction could be calculated
by dividing the number of counts per minute by the specific activity of the sugar."*

> **Internal standard: YES for the radiochemistry** — quench correction by the internal standard
> method. This is the one place in Wave K4a where an internal standard is properly used.

> ⚠️ **The furosine route is glucose-specific.** Furosine is formed on acid hydrolysis of the *Amadori*
> product (fructosyl-lysine). Fructose gives a *Heyns* product (glucosyl-lysine), which yields furosine
> at a different — and much lower — molar rate. Fig. 3B accordingly shows the fructose "Amadori" trace
> pinned at ≈0 for all times. Whether that is chemistry or method blindness is **not discussed by the
> authors**. Consequence: for fructose, "melanoidin = incorporated sugar − Amadori" reduces to
> "melanoidin = incorporated sugar", i.e. **the fructose ε is uncorrected for early-stage bound
> product.** This is the single most important methodological caveat on the 527 value.

## 3. THE HEADLINE RESULT — the extinction coefficients

**Anchor: Results and Discussion, "Extinction Coefficient of Melanoidins", journal p. 1181 (PDF page
4), left column; and the Abstract, p. 1178.** Also restated in the Conclusions, p. 1182.

Verbatim (p. 1181): *"In the glucose−casein system the average molar extinction coefficient (±
standard deviation) was calculated to be 477 (± 50) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹. In the fructose−casein system it was
527 (± 35) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹. This difference between the extinction coefficients of melanoidins formed in
glucose−casein and fructose−casein reactions was not significant at the 5 % level. This indicates that
in the glucose−casein and fructose−casein systems similar reaction intermediates might be formed,
giving rise to melanoidins with the same average extinction coefficient."*

Verbatim (Abstract): *"The extinction coefficient of the melanoidins remained constant during the
observation period as the absorbance at 420 nm increased to ≈8 units, and it was calculated to be
477 (± 50) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ in the glucose-casein reaction and 527 (± 35) L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ in the
fructose-casein reaction. This difference is not significant."*

| quantity | value | units as printed | wavelength | sugar | temperature | heating time range | error bar | provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ε, melanoidins | **477 (± 50)** | L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ | 420 nm | glucose | 120 °C | 0–90 min (regression over the 10–90 min points) | ± 1 SD | [F] |
| ε, melanoidins | **527 (± 35)** | L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ | 420 nm | fructose | 120 °C | 0–90 min (regression over the 10–90 min points) | ± 1 SD | [F] |
| difference glucose vs fructose | not significant at the 5 % level | — | 420 nm | — | 120 °C | — | — | [F] |
| ε constancy over time | *"the extinction coefficient of the melanoidins remains constant"* over the observation period, up to A₄₂₀ ≈ 8 | — | 420 nm | both | 120 °C | 0–90 min | — | [M] |

**Basis of the concentration axis — read this before using ε.** The "melanoidin concentration" against
which ε is defined is **mol of sugar incorporated into the >12 kDa fraction, minus the Amadori
compound, per litre of original reaction mixture** — not a mass concentration and not a molar
concentration of any identified species. Verbatim (p. 1179): *"A benefit of this approach is that the
molar extinction coefficient of melanoidins can be expressed simply in terms of the concentration of
glucose molecules converted into melanoidins, even though the molecular weights of melanoidins are
expected to span a very wide range of values."* And (p. 1180): *"the melanoidin concentration was
obtained by subtracting the concentration of Amadori compound from the concentration of sugar
incorporated into the high molecular weight fraction."*

**Fraction basis.** The A₄₂₀ regressed in Fig. 4 is the **dialysed retentate** absorbance, not the
whole mixture: Fig. 4A's largest point is A₄₂₀ ≈ 4.9 at 9.8 mM, which matches the *after-dialysis*
glucose curve of Fig. 2A (4.85 at 90 min), not the before-dialysis curve (6.65). Same for fructose
(Fig. 4B max 6.16 vs Fig. 2B after-dialysis 6.1 and before-dialysis 7.85). `[Z]`
→ **ε = 477 / 527 are HMW-fraction extinction coefficients.**

### 3.1 Comparison the authors make to sugar–amino-acid ε values

Verbatim (p. 1181): *"Leong (13) estimated values of ε at 470 nm to range from 695 L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹ for

melanoidins obtained from glucose−amino acid systems were calculated to be ∼2.5−3.5 times higher than
the extinction coefficient of glucose−casein melanoidins. This means that, in sugar−amino acid
reactions, fewer glucose molecules (or glucose fragments) have to be incorporated into the melanoidins
than in sugar−casein systems to increase the absorbance by one unit."*

### 3.2 The structural interpretation (relevant to any ε-invariance assumption)

Verbatim (p. 1181): *"The results indicate that, over the observation period, the extinction

### 5.2 Glycation-site accounting (p. 1182)

Verbatim: *"From the results we can conclude that after 60 min of heating, about three-fourths of the
lysine residues present in the glucose−casein system have reacted with glucose (or glucose equivalents
with six carbon atoms) and all lysine residues present in the fructose−casein system."*

*"Except for the ε-amino group of lysine residues, other potential glycation sites of casein are the
N-terminal α-amino group and the guanidino group of arginine residues (19). During heating, more
reactive reaction products, such as various dicarbonyl compounds, will be generated in the reaction
system. These compounds have a higher affinity for the guanidino groups of arginine residues and hence
increase the involvement of arginine residues in the glycation reaction (21). One molecule of casein
contains five arginine residues on average (20). If we take the reaction of sugar degradation compounds
with arginine residues into account, the number of sugar equivalents per amino group will decrease."*

> **Internal tension the authors flag themselves.** Table 1's a/b′ = 0.77 (glucose) and 1.02 (fructose)
> at 60 min say three-quarters and all of the lysines have reacted; Fig. 5 says only 53 % and 58 % of
> available lysine has been lost at 60 min. Both are quoted above. The authors resolve it by invoking
> arginine and the N-terminal α-amino group as additional sinks, i.e. the microanalysis a/b′ is an
> over-estimate because it attributes all incorporated sugar to lysine. **This matters for any model
> that uses ε together with an amine-pool balance.**

## PROPOSED FIT / HOLD-OUT ROLE — DRAFT FOR ORCHESTRATOR

> These sources are not yet in `docs/reference/FIT_HOLDOUT_DECLARATION.md`. A declaration
> amendment is required before any wave may fit them. This section is a proposal only.

There is **no temperature axis** in this paper, so the classical fit/hold-out cut on T is unavailable.
The two axes that *are* available and orthogonal are **sugar identity (glucose vs fructose, an
aldose/ketose cut)** and **molecular-weight fraction (whole mixture vs >12 kDa retentate)**. A third,
weaker axis is **method (radiochemistry vs elemental microanalysis)**, which the authors themselves
show disagree by 1.4–2.6×.

**Circularity risks, explicit.**
1. ε is *derived from* the Fig. 3 melanoidin concentrations and the Fig. 2 after-dialysis absorbances.
   **Fitting ε AND scoring against Fig. 2A/3A is circular.** The proposal above fits both because both
   are on the same (glucose) side of the cut and both are *inputs* to the observable model; the scoring
   happens on the fructose arm and on Fig. 5. If any wave wants to *score* glucose browning, ε must be
   demoted to a free parameter for that run.
2. The existing declaration has **Martins 2005 browning (step 9, ε 0.64)** as a ★ HOLD-OUT and the
   Martins-thesis ε as a FIT input. Adding Brands' ε as a FIT input does not disturb that — but the
   **Knol 2005 ε = 282 hold-out** exists precisely to test amine specificity, and Brands' glucose–casein
   477 is a *third* amine. Verify with the orchestrator that fitting 477 does not leak information into
   the Knol hold-out; if the model's ε term is a function of amine identity, it might. Recommend
   ingesting 477 with `amine: casein` as an explicit categorical, not as a global ε.
3. Do **not** use the fructose ε (527) to argue that ε is amine- or sugar-independent and then score
   the Knol or Martins ε hold-outs against that generalisation — that would convert a hold-out into a
   fit by transitivity.
