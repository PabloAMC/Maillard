## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Browning solutions from 80 sugar × amino acid pairs were added to an aqueous linoleic acid emulsion at
50 °C and the oxygen-uptake induction period compared with linoleic acid alone (22 h; batches gave
22–26 h). Almost every product lengthened the induction period (relative stability > 1, antioxidant);
the strongest were alanine/xylose (1.90) and proline/xylose (1.82). **Cysteine-HCl was the exception**: its
products were slightly pro-oxidant with xylose (0.98), arabinose (0.97) and fructose (0.90) and only
slightly antioxidant with glucose (1.04) and galactose (1.06). The authors connect this to SH-group
chemistry (thiols are destroyed by, and promote, linoleate oxidation; their earlier finding that cysteine
catalyses hydroperoxide formation) and advise against SH compounds as additives. Browning (A490) did not
predict antioxidant activity. Glucose and xylose products were, on average, the most antioxidant;
galactose and fructose the least.

**Cysteine-HCl vs the other 15 amino acids, per sugar:**

| sugar | Cys-HCl | median of other 15 | range of other 15 | Cys − median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D-xylose | −0.009 | +0.114 | +0.029 to +0.279 | **−0.12** |
| L-arabinose | −0.013 | +0.090 | 0.000 to +0.185 | **−0.10** |
| D-fructose | −0.046 | +0.090 | +0.009 to +0.238 | **−0.14** |
| D-glucose | +0.017 | +0.140 | +0.049 to +0.215 | **−0.12** |
| D-galactose | +0.025 | +0.079 | +0.013 to +0.114 | −0.05 |

**Sugar identity, glucose vs xylose, same amino acid:** median +0.025, mean +0.008, SD 0.10, range
**−0.23 (alanine) to +0.15 (threonine)** dex; for cysteine-HCl +0.026. Glucose vs arabinose: median +0.03,
range −0.10 to +0.18.

**Resolution.** The replicate rule (> 5 % deviation triggers a repeat) means about ±0.02 dex per value.
Cysteine's own values (−0.05 to +0.03 dex) are therefore **not distinguishable from "no effect"**; what
is resolved is that cysteine products lack the +0.08 to +0.14 dex antioxidant effect the other amino
acids' products show.

## 5. USE IN THIS REPO

**Question it was downloaded for (L1 scoping):** are MRPs antioxidant or pro-oxidant for linoleate
oxidation in water, and do the sugar or the sulfur amino acid change that?

- **Sign of the MRP channel: mostly antioxidant, small.** Median effect on the induction period
  +0.08 to +0.14 dex across sugars. On a fixed-time aldehyde readout this would mean somewhat less
  oxidation where more MRP forms.
- **Sulfur lever (cysteine): the opposite sign to thiol trapping, and small.** Cysteine browning
  products are ~0.12 dex less protective than other amino acids' products, and with pentoses or fructose
  marginally pro-oxidant. This is a **second, opposing cysteine channel**: thiol-catalysed initiation
  (more hydroperoxide, so more aldehyde) against H2S/thiol carbonyl trapping (less aldehyde, `mottram1989`,
  `farmer1992`). Here it is ≤ 0.14 dex on an onset time and is **confounded by pH**: the cysteine-HCl
  solutions had pH 1.9–2.0 against 3–7 for the rest (Table 1), diluted 20× into an unbuffered emulsion.
  Thiamine is not tested.
- **Sugar lever: no systematic glucose vs pentose effect** (median +0.03 dex, but ±0.1 dex scatter
  between amino acids, so any single pair can show ±0.2). No ribose; xylose and arabinose are the
  pentose proxies. Consistent with the L1 note's small sugar probability.
- **pH lever:** not varied; pH is an uncontrolled covariate.
- **Distance from the P3 pot: far.** MRPs made at 80 °C / 2 h / 25 % moisture then dosed at ~10 µM
  into a 10 mM linoleate emulsion at 50 °C for ~1 day; P3 forms MRPs and oxidises lipid together in
  20 min at 140 °C with 15 % pea isolate. IP is a time to onset, not an aldehyde ratio.
- **Not usable for a declared band.** Useful as (i) the sign and a ceiling (~0.1–0.2 dex on onset time)
  of a free-amino-acid MRP antioxidant effect in water, and (ii) a pointer to a cysteine pro-oxidant
  channel that a thiol-sink case would need as its rival.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Concentration statements are inconsistent.** "Sugars (0.005 M) and amino acids (0.005 M)" mixed to
   25 % moisture, then "diluted to 25 mL", under a heading "(2.4 × 10⁻⁴ M)", dosed to "10⁻⁵ M". If
   0.005 mol each in 25 mL (0.2 M), 0.1 mL in ~2.1 mL is ~10 mM, not 10⁻⁵ M. The true MRP dose is
   uncertain by up to ~3 orders of magnitude.
2. **"Amadori substances" is a label**, not an identification: the solutions are whole browning mixtures
   (A490 up to ~1.7).
3. **Cysteine-HCl pH ~1.9–2.0** (and lysine-HCl ~3.0–4.1): the HCl salt, not cysteine chemistry, may
   drive the low values. The text (p. 175–176) says the brown colour "was faded at pH lower than three", citing cysteine-HCl and aspartic acid.
4. **No buffer**; emulsion pH measured but not reported. Control IP varied 22–26 h between linoleic acid
   batches (~±0.04 dex), larger than the ±0.02 dex replicate tolerance; whether each arm was run against
   its own-batch control is not stated.
5. **A490 ceiling** at ~1.7 (§3.1).
6. **Figures** show O2-uptake curves for a few amino acids per sugar; no new numbers.
7. **Registry:** not in `data/keys/papers.yml` before this dossier.
