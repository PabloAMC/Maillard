# Schutte & Koenders 1972 — EXTRACTION (1-methylthio-ethanethiol from ethanal + methanethiol + H2S; the methional → methanethiol elimination as the weak link, catalyst screen at pH 6, boiling)

## 1. Why it matters

Programme 6 puts methional → methanethiol on the sulfur lane. This paper is the original statement, from
a beef-flavour precursor study, that this β-elimination is "the weakest link" of the methionine chain
under broth conditions, and it screens what catalyses it. The result is a pH and catalyst pattern, not a
rate: at pH 6 the uncatalysed elimination gives only traces in 15 min of boiling; a base (pyridine) does
nothing; pH 9 (borax) gives moderate release; the α-dicarbonyl/triketone Strecker agents (ninhydrin ≫
alloxan ≈ pyruvaldehyde) and 2-hydroxypyridine do catalyse it at pH 6. Pyruvaldehyde (methylglyoxal) is
the one in the list that a Maillard pot contains. The paper also shows that ethanal + MeSH + H2S in hot
water make DMDS and the dimethyltrithiolane alongside MTET — the same H2S-plus-MeSH sulfur pool the
trisulfide draws on in Chin 1994 and Zhang 2023.

## 2. Methods as they matter to a model

- **Exp. 1 — immediate precursors under broth conditions.** 1 g ethanal (23 mmol), 1 g methanethiol
  (21 mmol), 6 g Na2S·9H2O (25 mmol) in 1 L water, phosphate buffer **pH 5.8** (Fig. 1a caption says 5.9);
  flask in oil bath at **95 °C**; headspace swept with N2 through a condenser, a −10 °C trap and a
  −196 °C trap; **1 h**. Trap contents warmed and run on GC (3 m × 2 mm glass, 10 % Carbowax 20M, 50 →
  200 °C at 4 °C/min); peaks collected, identified by MS and IR. Largest peak MTET, **estimated 10–20 mg
  (0.5–1 %)** in the −196 °C trap (10–20 mg / 108.2 = 0.09–0.18 mmol = 0.4–0.8 % of 23 mmol — the
  printed 0.5–1 % is on this basis, my check). Also present: cis/trans 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolane
  (double peak) and **dimethyl disulfide** — identified, not quantified.
- **Exp. 2 — vapour-phase control.** Vapours of ethanal (0 °C), MeSH (−10 °C), NaHS solution and water
  led separately or together through a reaction chamber (50 °C) into a −196 °C trap: only traces of
  MTET in any combination → not formed in the gas phase or the trap.
- **Exp. 3 — exchange control.** 40 mg MTET + 20 mg 1,2-¹⁴C-ethanal (0.5 µCi) in 1 L pH 5.8 phosphate,
  treated as the broth: no radioactivity in the MTET GC fraction (background ~60 cpm; ethanal fraction
  330 cpm) → MTET does not dissociate and re-form during isolation. About **15 mg (38 %)** of the MTET
  reached the −196 °C trap — the transfer efficiency of the apparatus for a compound of this volatility.
- **Exp. 4 — two-phase pH series (Table I).** 1 mL CH2Cl2 + 2 mL buffer (pH 1.0–10.6) + 50 µL ethanal +
  50 µL MeSH + 300 mg Na2S·9H2O ("about 1 mmol of each"; 300 mg = 1.25 mmol), room temperature, shaken;
  CH2Cl2 layer by GC; "very slowly, except in highly acidic media", ~24 h before change detectable.
- **Exp. 5 — amino acids with a Strecker agent.** Alanine 0.5 g (5.5 mmol), methionine 0.5 g (3.4 mmol),
  cysteine·HCl 0.75 g (4.8 mmol), 200 mL water, phosphate **pH 6**, **ninhydrin 1.5 g** (8.4 mmol; MW
  178.14), hardened fat 2 g, **boiled 1 h**, Brinkman apparatus: **~0.05 mg MTET (0.01 %)** in the trap
  (0.05 mg / 108.2 = 0.46 µmol = 0.014 % of the 3.4 mmol methionine; the printed 0.01 % is of this order).
  With degrading agents other than ninhydrin: only traces of MTET → subsidiary experiments located the
  bottleneck at methional → MeSH.
- **Exp. 6 — the methional step (Table II).** Three pear-shaped 100 mL three-necked flasks, each
  **100 mg methional (0.960 mmol; MW 104.15) in 20 mL phosphate buffer = 48 mmol/L**, pH 6 unless stated,
  in one oil bath at **110 °C** (the aqueous contents boil, i.e. ~100 °C); one blank, two with **~50 mg
  candidate catalyst** (ninhydrin 0.28 mmol = 14 mM; alloxan MW 142.07, 0.35 mmol = 18 mM;
  pyruvaldehyde MW 72.06, 0.69 mmol = 35 mM — so "catalyst" loadings are 0.3–0.7 of the methional, not
  catalytic amounts). N2 swept through each solution, via its own reflux condenser, into **4 % Hg(CN)2**;
  the **rate of precipitation** (mercuric methylmercaptide) is the measure. **15 min**. Scored +++ / ++ /
  + / − / − − (legend below). A pyridine artefact (precipitate not due to MeSH) was checked with the
  N,N-dimethyl-p-phenylenediamine / FeCl3 methylene-blue-type test.
- Same protocol with **methionine** instead of methional: ninhydrin "by far the most effective", then
  alloxan and pyruvaldehyde. **S-methylcysteine**: only ninhydrin effective.
- No quantification of MeSH mass, no time resolution within the 15 min, no replicates stated.

### Discussion claims with mechanism (Schemes 3–4)

The elimination "proceeds readily at pH 9 but not at all at pH 6 in the presence of a base like
pyridine" → specific base catalysis (methional enolate/anion loses CH3S−, giving acrolein, Scheme 3).
At pH 6 the active catalysts share "a basic and an acidic site adjacent to each other" (2-hydroxypyridine)
or "strongly polarized carbonyl groups that are easily hydrated" (ninhydrin, alloxan, pyruvaldehyde) →
concerted hemiketal-assisted elimination (hypothesis B preferred over catalysed enolisation A). Of the
active agents only pyruvaldehyde is a known cooked-beef component (Yamato 1970).

Scheme 5 (MTET formation): ethanal + MeSH ⇌ hemithioacetal CH3CH(OH)SCH3, equilibrium constant "about
30" (Jencks), specific-base and general-acid catalysed; then SH− displaces OH−. SH− out-competes CH3S−
between pH 5 and 7 because the pKa of H2S is ~3 units below that of CH3SH.

## 5. Flags

1. **Entirely semi-quantitative.** The MeSH measure is "rate of precipitation" of Hg mercaptide on a
   five-step ordinal scale over 15 min; no mass, no time course, no replicate. Hg(CN)2 also traps H2S and
   any other thiol, but the methional flasks contain no other sulfur source.
2. **"Boiled" / "oil bath 110 °C"**: the solution temperature is the boiling point (~100 °C); the N2 sweep
   strips MeSH continuously (so what is scored is release, and the reverse reaction is suppressed) but
   also strips methional itself (bp 165 °C, steam-volatile) at an uncontrolled rate — a methional loss
   channel the design does not account for.
3. **Catalyst loadings are stoichiometric-scale** (14–35 mM against 48 mM methional), so "+" for
   pyruvaldehyde is at a dicarbonyl:methional ratio of ~0.7, far above what a Maillard pot holds relative
   to its methional. The ordering (ninhydrin ≫ alloxan ≈ pyruvaldehyde ≈ 2-hydroxypyridine > nothing)
   is the transferable content, not any level.
4. **Dehydroascorbic acid does NOT catalyse the elimination** here, while Cheng 2020 finds ascorbic acid
   the strongest accelerant of MeSH from methionine at 100 °C. The two are compatible only if ascorbate
   acts on the Strecker step (methionine → methional) and/or as a pro-oxidant (Chin 1994), not on the
   elimination.
5. **pH 6 vs pH 9**: the specific-base reading implies the uncatalysed rate rises with [OH−]; the paper
   gives two points on an ordinal scale, no slope.
6. The trithiolane and DMDS in Exp. 1 show that a hot aqueous pot holding MeSH and H2S together makes
   MeSH-derived disulfide and mixed S3 rings even at 95 °C with a nitrogen sweep (i.e. little O2) —
   the oxidant there is not identified.
7. Exp. 5's 0.01 % MTET yield needed 8.4 mmol ninhydrin for 3.4 mmol methionine; with food-relevant
   dicarbonyls only traces — a reminder that methionine → MeSH via methional is a low-yield chain at
   100 °C / pH 6 on a 1-hour scale.
