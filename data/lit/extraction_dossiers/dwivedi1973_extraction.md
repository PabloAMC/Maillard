## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

The review surveys thiamine degradation by heat/pH, redox agents, sulfite and other bases,
thiaminases, proteins and amino acids, aldehydes and amines, carbohydrates, metals, radiation and
ultrasound. It contains **no rate constant, no activation energy and no yield for H2S**. Its only
H2S number-bearing statement points back to Dwivedi & Arnold 1971 (see `arnold1971_extraction.md`):
thiamine is "a relatively insignificant source of hydrogen sulfide in most food systems". The one
mechanistic claim the model can use is from the authors' ³⁵S-thiamine study (Dwivedi & Arnold
1972, *J. Food Sci.*, then in press). **At pH ≤ 6.0 thiamine cleaves at the methylene bridge, and
the principal sulfur product is the intact 4-methyl-5-(β-hydroxyethyl)thiazole. At pH ≥ 7.0 H2S
"appeared to be a major product"**, which they attribute to the pseudobase and thiol forms present
above pH 6, with a "substantially lower" Ea for thiazole-ring breakdown than for bridge cleavage
(not quantified). Other relevant qualitative points: the thiol form reacts with disulfides such as
cystine to give mixed disulfides; thiols (cysteine, glutathione) reduce thiamine disulfide back to
thiamine; SH compounds and proteins protect thiamine; and elemental sulfur, a major product in
heated milk-like systems, is "likely formed by the oxidation of hydrogen sulfide".

### Qualitative claims bearing on THI → H2S [Q]

- Clarke & Gurin 1935: H2S from heated **alkaline** thiamine (p. 55).
- Hirano 1957: dethiothiamine (1-IV) forms by loss of H2S when the sodium salt of thiamine is
  warmed in water (p. 55).
- Figure 1 scheme (p. 55): thiamine (1-I) → [OH⁻, slow] → thiol form (1-III) → H2S + dethiothiamine
  (1-IV); a branch H2S → [O2] → H2O + S. The scheme draws H2S as coming from the open thiol form,
  not from free thiamine.
- Zima & Williams 1940: thiamine disulfide heated with base yields H2S and thiamine (p. 56).
- "Sulfur is likely formed by the oxidation of hydrogen sulfide, which is also produced from
  thiamine in alkaline solutions" (p. 56).
- Thiamine disulfide is reduced back to thiamine by thiols "such as cysteine or glutathione"
  (p. 56). The thiol form reacts with disulfides such as cystine to give mixed disulfides (3-I).
- Protection: SH groups of albumin protect thiamine (Wada & Suzuki 1965); glutathione minimises
  irradiation loss; cysteine protects under ultrasound (p. 57–58).
- If dissolved O2 is expelled, thiamine is "relatively stable" in alkaline media containing
  "thermostable factors" (p. 56). This refers to oxidation to the disulfide, not heat cleavage.
- "Hydrogen sulfide, a major thermal degradation product under neutral or alkaline conditions"
  (p. 59), followed by the 1971 conclusion that thiamine is an insignificant H2S source in foods.

## 4. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**The model's need.** The sulfur lane makes H2S only from cysteine (`k_cys_h2s`, Zheng & Ho 1994,
pH-resolved first order) and from glutathione. THI feeds MFT and MeSH (`r_thi_hmp`, `r_thi_mesh`)
but releases no H2S, so a pentose + thiamine pot cannot form 2-furfurylthiol. The question is
whether a measured `THI -> H2S` step can be built.

**Verdict: (c), qualitative only.** No rate constant, no Ea, no yield. Use it as directional
support for the numbers in `arnold1971_extraction.md`:
- **pH switch:** H2S from thiamine is a pH ≥ 7 phenomenon. At pH ≤ 6 the sulfur stays in the
  intact thiazole (4-methyl-5-(β-hydroxyethyl)thiazole). This supports a strongly pH-dependent
  branching fraction, and it is consistent with the 1971 slope (d log k/d pH ≈ 2 between 6.5 and
  7.0).
- **Mechanistic placement:** H2S is drawn as coming from the ring-opened thiol form (1-III), the
  same open form that leads to HMP and MFT. A `THI -> H2S` step is therefore a competitor of
  `r_thi_hmp` for the same intermediate, not an independent channel.
- **Contradiction 1:** the model sends all thiamine sulfur to HMP or MeSH. At pH ≤ 6 the review's
  principal sulfur product is the **intact thiazole**, which is neither. Unless HMP is reached
  through it, thiamine's sulfur at pH ~6 is largely sequestered and unavailable to MFT. Whether
  `k_thi_hmp` was fitted at a pH where that matters should be checked.
- **Contradiction 2:** thiols (cysteine, glutathione) protect thiamine or regenerate it from its
  disulfide. The model has no such interaction; Cys + THI pots will over-degrade THI. This agrees
  with the 1971 cysteine result.
- **Oxidation sink for H2S:** elemental sulfur as a major product in heated milk-like systems
  (121 °C, 50 min) is attributed to H2S oxidation. The lane has no H2S → S⁰ sink, so it would
  overstate free H2S from any source under air.
- **For an actual number, the paper to obtain** is Dwivedi & Arnold 1972, *J. Food Sci.* (³⁵S
  distribution vs pH). It is not in `data/articles/`.
