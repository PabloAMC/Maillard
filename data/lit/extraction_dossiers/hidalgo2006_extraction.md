## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

In a Rancimat at 110 °C under an air stream, refined olive oil (control IP ≈ 19.9 h) was protected by
**PE** (IP rises with dose, r = 0.98; **+18 % at 400 ppm**) and by **free lysine** (r = 0.98; **+22 % at
400 ppm**, significantly more than BHT's +12 %), but **not by PC** (IP falls with dose, r = −0.92;
**−4 % at 400 ppm**). Mixing an amine with a phospholipid was **synergistic**: PE/Lys 300/100 ppm gave
**+9.7 h** against an additive expectation of 3.4 h; PC/Lys 300/100 gave **+7.5 h** against 0.6 h. PC/PE
mixtures were **not** synergistic (≤ additive). The authors' reading: carbonyls from the oxidising
lipid react in situ with primary amines (PE's ethanolamine, lysine's α/ε-NH2) to give **pyrroles that
are themselves antioxidants** — confirmed by Ehrlich adducts (515–525 and ~570 nm) and by GC-MS of
N-(4-methoxyphenyl)-pyrroles after acid ring exchange — and the hydrophilic (lysine-bound) pyrroles
protect bulk oil better than lipophilic (PE-bound) ones. IP correlates with yellowness of the spent
oil (r = 0.84, BHT excepted).

## 5. USE IN THIS REPO

**Question it was downloaded for (L1 scoping):** do amines (PE, amino acids) act as antioxidants on
lipid oxidation, and could that move hexanal / nonanal / 2-pentylfuran / decadienal across the P3
levers?

- **Mechanism pointer: yes.** Primary amines + oxidising lipid → carbonyl–amine pyrroles → antioxidant
  feedback (this paper and the Zamora/Hidalgo series, e.g. `zamora2006`–`zamora2020`,
  `hidalgo2004`–`hidalgo2016` dossiers). PC without an amine is not protective; amine + oxidisable
  lipid is synergistic. That supports a **second amine coupling besides the inert lysine
  `COVALENT_SINK`**: amines delaying LOOH formation, i.e. acting on the declared `LOOH_FORMATION_GAP`
  term, not on aldehyde removal.
- **Measurement usable for a declared band: no.**
  - Readout is a Rancimat IP in **bulk olive oil at 110 °C with air sparging** (≈ 20 h to onset); the
    P3 pot is a **20 min, 140 °C aqueous protein pot** where the lipid is dispersed in a 15 % pea
    isolate. No aldehyde, no headspace, no aqueous phase beyond 400 µL of carrier water that boils
    off.
  - Doses are 100–400 ppm in oil (≤ 2.7 mmol Lys/kg). The P3 pot already carries ~0.07 M protein
    lysine residues [Z] (15 % isolate × ~7 g Lys/100 g protein) plus free amines; the amine pool is
    saturated relative to these doses, so **none of the P3 levers (sugar identity, cysteine vs
    thiamine, pH 6 vs 7) changes the amine supply** that this paper varies. Cysteine is an amine too,
    but its thiol chemistry is not tested here.
  - pH, sugar and sulfur source are not variables. Temperature (110 °C) and time (≈ 20–30 h) are far
    from 140 °C / 20 min.
- **Sign information for the scoping note:** if MRP/amine antioxidant capacity mattered in P3 it would
  **lower** aldehydes in the arm with more reactive amine/MRP formation; the magnitude here (+0.07 to
  +0.17 dex on onset time in bulk oil) is small and does not translate into a concentration ratio at
  20 min. Not a basis for the ±0.3 dex bands.
- **PE/PC fatty-acid profiles** (soy PE 61.6 % 18:2 / 7.8 % 18:3; soy PC 75.9 % / 9.9 %) are measured
  values for soybean phospholipids, the right order for a legume lipid, but they are phospholipid
  fractions, not a pea-isolate total-lipid profile; they do not validate the pea carrier's declared
  50 / 12 / 22.
- **Hold-out discipline [!].** `farmer1992` and `elmore2002` are designated hold-outs; do not pool them
  with this paper into any proposed band. (Different lab: Seville, CSIC.)

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Bars, not tables.** §3.2–3.3 are my readings; the printed text increments agree to ≤ 0.1 h, so
   readings are reliable to ~±0.1 h for Fig. 1 and ~±0.2 h for Figs. 2–4.
2. **BHT +12 % printed vs +13 % read** (22.4/19.9); within reading error of the bar.
3. **PC/Lys 200/200 additive** not printed; 2.3 h back-calculated from "+174 %".
4. **"phosphatidylamine"** (p. 5465) is a typo for phosphatidylethanolamine.
5. **Water carrier.** Each oil got 400 µL of 1 % Tween water per 2.5 g (~14 % w/w); it boils off in
   the 110 °C air stream. Tween (a surfactant with peroxide impurities) is in every oil incl. control.
6. **n = "at least two"** experiments; SD from two runs is weak, and SNK letter groups overlap widely
   (most 200–400 ppm arms share "c,d").
7. **Pyrrole identifications** require a derivatisation at pH 3 / 110 °C / 20 h that itself generates
   2-pentylfuran from 2-pentylpyrroles (Scheme 2); 2-pentylfuran in Fig. 6 is therefore an analytical
   product, not an oil volatile.
