# Mottram & Salter 1989 — EXTRACTION
### *"Flavor Formation in Meat-Related Maillard Systems Containing Phospholipids"*
### Book chapter (ACS Symp. Ser. 409, *Thermal Generation of Aromas*, ch. 41) reviewing the Reading model systems: amino acid (glycine or cysteine) + ribose ± phospholipid in phosphate buffer, **initial pH 5.7, 140 °C / 1 h, sealed ampoules**, headspace on Tenax, GC-MS; concentrations "approximating mammalian muscle" (not given). One table (Table I, unpublished Salter & Mottram data): **relative** amounts of three Maillard thiols (no lipid = 1) and four lipid–Maillard adducts (beef PL = 1) in cysteine + ribose with beef triglyceride, beef phospholipid, egg lecithin or egg cephalin. **Lipid lowers the H2S-derived thiols (−0.14 to −1.52 dex) and forms 2-alkylthiophenes and 2-pentylpyridine instead; phospholipids do this, triglycerides barely.** Cysteine + ribose + lecithin gives **> 10× the 2-pentylpyridine of glycine + ribose + lecithin**. No hexanal or other lipid aldehyde is quantified. Read 2026-11-15.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

A review-style chapter. (1) Extracting all lipids (chloroform–methanol) from lean meat, not just the
triglycerides (hexane), turned the cooked aroma from meaty to roasted/biscuit and raised the
alkylpyrazines: the lipids "inhibit the formation of certain heterocyclic compounds" (Mottram & Edwards
1983, cited). (2) In aqueous glycine or cysteine + ribose at pH 5.7, 140 °C / 1 h, adding egg lecithin
**lowered most Maillard heterocyclics by 40–50 %, and 2-thiophenethiol and 2-methyl-3-furanthiol by
> 65 %**, and produced lipid–Maillard adducts: 2-pentylpyridine (glycine and cysteine) and, with
cysteine, a C3–C8 series of 2-alkylthiophenes, 2-pentyl-2H-thiapyran (tentative), 2-hexenylthiophene,
1-heptanethiol and 1-octanethiol. The authors attribute the thiol losses to **competition for H2S by lipid
degradation products**, and the higher 2-pentylpyridine with cysteine to NH3 released with H2S in the
Strecker degradation of cysteine. (3) Beef phospholipid and egg phospholipids increased the meaty aroma;
beef triglyceride did not.

### 3.2 Text numbers [M]
- Lecithin addition: most major Maillard volatiles **−40 to −50 %**; "thiophenethiol and methyl-furanthiol"
  **> −65 %** (p. 447).
- Cysteine + ribose + lecithin gives **"over 10 times more 2-pentylpyridine"** than glycine + ribose +
  lecithin (p. 447).
- Glycine + ribose without lipid: 74 compounds, furfurals and furyl ketones dominant; cysteine + ribose:
  118 compounds, furfurals reduced "to trace amounts" (p. 446).
- "Large concentrations of 2,4-decadienal" in the volatiles of lecithin heated alone or in the Maillard
  mixtures (p. 447); **not quantified**.

### 3.3 Sensory [Q]
Glycine + ribose: caramel, sweet, burnt sugar; + lecithin adds oily, chicken-like. Cysteine + ribose:
sulfurous, rubbery, underlying meaty; much more "cooked meat / beef" with phospholipid; GC-odour found at
least six meaty regions with lecithin, one without (2-methyl-3-thiophenethiol). The alkylthiophenes
themselves are not meaty.

## 5. USE IN THIS REPO

**Question it was downloaded for (L1 scoping):** does cysteine (its H2S and NH3) trap lipid aldehydes in
a heated pot, i.e. is there a thiol / H2S carbonyl sink specific to the sulfur source?

- **Mechanism pointer: yes, both directions of the coupling.** Lipid carbonyls (dienals, enals) and the
  Maillard H2S / NH3 / mercaptoacetaldehyde pool consume each other: the H2S-derived thiols fall by
  0.2–1.5 dex when phospholipid is present, and 2-alkylthiophenes, alkanethiols and 2-pentylpyridine
  appear. That is the H2S sink of the L1 note's proposed case, seen from the sulfur side.
- **Cysteine-specific NH3.** 2-Pentylpyridine (from 2,4-decadienal + NH3) is ≥ 10× higher with cysteine
  than with glycine. Thiamine also releases H2S on heating but its NH3 yield is not addressed here.
- **No aldehyde number.** The chapter never reports hexanal, nonanal, 2-pentylfuran or decadienal with vs
  without cysteine; the lipid side is only "large concentrations of 2,4-decadienal". It **cannot set a
  ratio** for the sulfur lever on hexanal. The quantified version of this design is `farmer1992`
  (designated hold-out) and `elmore2002` (hold-out).
- **Size of the sink, order of magnitude only [Z].** If lipid consumes > 65 % of the H2S that would
  otherwise make thiophenethiol, and the alkylthiophene adducts are among "the most abundant components",
  then in a cysteine pot an appreciable fraction of the dienal pool is diverted. This agrees with the
  dienal-specific depletion recorded in the L1 note (2,4-decadienal −0.23 to −1.30 dex in `farmer1992`;
  2-enals and dienals 5× to > 250× in `elmore2002`) and with saturated aldehydes moving much less.
- **P3 levers.** Sulfur source: cysteine vs glycine only, not vs thiamine. Sugar: ribose only. pH: 5.7
  only. Lipid: egg / beef phospholipid, not a legume lipid.
- **Hold-out discipline [!].** Same Reading laboratory, same pot design (pH 5.7 phosphate, 140 °C / 1 h,
  Tenax) and in part the same unpublished experiments that became `farmer1989` / `farmer1992`. **Treat it
  as inside the hold-out fold:** do not use its Table I to set a band that `farmer1992` or `elmore2002`
  would then test.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Secondary / review chapter.** Most claims summarise refs. 11, 20–22 (Mottram & Edwards 1983;
   Whitfield et al. 1987; Salter et al. 1988, 1989); Table I is unpublished data with no n, no SD, no
   units.
2. **Normalisation differs by block** (thiols to no lipid, adducts to beef PL), so the two blocks cannot
   be compared with each other.
3. **Lipid doses not stated** for the TG / PL / LEC / CEPH comparison, so the lipid-type contrast may
   partly be a dose contrast.
4. **2-Pentylthiapyran** is a tentative identification.
