### *"Changes in the initial stages of a glucose-proline Maillard reaction model system influences dairy product quality during thermal processing"*
### Two experiments, both at **110 and 130 °C, 0-60 min, initial pH 5.8 (25 °C)**: (1) glucose + L-proline (very concentrated, ~2.5 M glucose) in water or phosphate / acetate / citrate at 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 M — glucose loss (Fig. 1); (2) the purified proline Amadori compound DFP heated in water or 0.1 M phosphate / acetate / citrate — DFP, glucose, mannose, HMF, formic acid (Fig. 5). **Phosphate speeds DFP loss ×~4 (110 °C) and ×~2.5 (130 °C) against acetate, citrate and water.** No O2 control, no metals or chelators, no labels, no rate constants, pH not measured after heating; axis units in Fig. 5 are not credible as absolute concentrations.

**Answer.** Guan 2012 shows, at 110-130 °C, initial pH 5.8, that **0.1 M phosphate accelerates the loss of a
(secondary-amine) Amadori compound several-fold against acetate, citrate and water**, and with it the reversion to
glucose + mannose (×1.3-1.5 [M]), HMF (×2.7 / ×14 vs acetate / citrate at 130 °C, 60 min [M]) and formic acid [Q].
That makes it a **third laboratory (South China University of Technology)** after Nestlé (davidek2002, 90 °C) and
Baynes (zyzak1995, 37 °C) for "phosphate speeds ARP decay", and the first at process temperature. Confidence in the
direction ~0.85; in the size (×4 at 110 °C, ×2.5 at 130 °C over 60 min) ~0.5, because pH drift, buffer capacity and the
absolute scale are uncontrolled and the figures carry legend and unit errors. The mechanism Guan proposes for the
sugar-loss step (bifunctional H2PO4⁻ / HPO4²⁻ proton shuttle to the glycosylamine, Fig. 2, after Bell 1997) is
**[P]**; the "confirmation" offered is only that total phosphate is unchanged by IC [Q].

acetate 46.7 / 23.9; citrate 40.7 / 19.6; water ~51 / ~24 [F]. "30 min ... to degrade about 77 %" in phosphate at

Phosphate / water rate ratio, 0-10 min, first-order equivalent [Z]: ×3.8 (110 °C: 0.107 vs 0.028 min⁻¹), ×1.5
(130 °C: 0.141 vs 0.091 min⁻¹); 60-min averages: phosphate 0.057 / 0.063 min⁻¹, acetate 0.013 / 0.024, citrate
0.015 / 0.027 [Z]. Acetate, citrate and water are indistinguishable (±15 %) [Z].

**Fig. 5B-E, 130 °C phosphate at 60 min** [F]: glucose ~0.00023, mannose ~0.00085, HMF ~0.00047, formic acid ~0.0007
("mmol/L"). Printed ratios [M]: glucose + mannose in phosphate ×1.3 (vs citrate) and ×1.5 (vs acetate) at 130 °C,

HMF) ≈ 0.0016 of 0.0040 lost, ~40 % [Z]; formic acid exceeds HMF at every time, which the authors note contradicts a

**Fig. 1, glucose (g/L), glucose + Pro** [F]: 110 °C at 60 min, 0.3 M phosphate ~199 (from ~460), 0.2 M ~283, the
open-diamond curve (0.1 M phosphate OR water) ~305, acetate and citrate ~320-405. 130 °C at 60 min: 0.3 M phosphate ~10,
0.2 M ~24, open diamond ~76, others ~290-320. Use only the 0.2 / 0.3 M phosphate curves.

## 4. Mechanism evidence and its kind (Yu laboratory, South China University of Technology)

- **Within-study factor (kind 6):** buffer type at one nominal initial pH and two temperatures (DFP); phosphate
  concentration (glucose + Pro). No pH-stat, no pH measurement after heating.
- **Fed intermediate (kind 2):** DFP heated alone in each buffer: products glucose, mannose (reversion), HMF and formic
  acid. Reversion is faster in phosphate, i.e. phosphate accelerates both directions of the early equilibrium [M/Z].
- **Proposed, not shown [P]:** bifunctional phosphate catalysis of glycosylamine formation; citrate favouring a
  "Strecker-type" DFP degradation over 1,2-enolisation; formic acid from C1-C2 cleavage of 3-DG.
- No deoxyosone was measured.

## 6. Disagreements found

1. Internal [!]: Fig. 1's legend lists "citrate buffer, 0.2 M" twice (0.3 M missing) and gives phosphate 0.1 M and
   water the same open-diamond symbol; only one diamond curve is distinguishable. The 0.1 M phosphate and the water arms
   of Fig. 1 cannot be assigned.
2. Internal [!]: Fig. 5's ordinate values (DFP 0.0041 "mmol/L", formic acid ≤ 0.0007, HMF ≤ 0.0005) lie 2-3 orders
   below the stated calibration ranges (formic acid 0.3-10 mmol/L, HMF 0.1-1.6 mmol/L) and HMF was measured on
   freeze-dried powder redissolved at 1 mg/mL. The axes cannot be absolute concentrations in the pot; only ratios within
   a panel are usable.
3. With davidek2002 (Nestlé, glycine ARP, phosphate vs water, 90 °C): direction agrees (phosphate faster, reversion
   favoured). Size not compared here (davidek2002's numbers not re-read for this dossier).
