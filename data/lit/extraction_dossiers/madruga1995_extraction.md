# Madruga & Mottram 1995 — EXTRACTION
### *"The Effect of pH on the Formation of Maillard-Derived Aroma Volatiles Using a Cooked Meat System"*
### Minced beef psoas acidified with HCl to pH 5.6 / 5.0 / 4.5 / 4.0, autoclaved 140 °C / 30 min, dynamic-headspace GC-MS. 2-Methyl-3-furanthiol rises from trace to 130 (≥ 65×) as pH falls; 2-furanylmethanethiol does not follow; pyrazines vanish at pH 4.0; thiazoles fall with acid. The only pH series in a real meat matrix in the corpus. Its concentration unit is almost certainly misprinted by 10⁶. Read 2026-10-27.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

100 g portions of minced beef *M. psoas major* (natural pH 5.6) were blended with 12 mL of water or
water + 1 M HCl (3.0, 7.2, 12 mL HCl → pH 5.0, 4.5, 4.0), held overnight at 4 °C, autoclaved in glass
bottles at **140 °C for 30 min**, and their headspace was swept at 60 °C for 2 h onto Tenax and run by
GC-MS against 65 ng dichlorobenzene. The meat buffered well: **pH after cooking 5.8 / 5.4 / 4.9 / 4.4**
(+0.2 to +0.4). Of 137 volatiles, the sulfur-substituted furans and mercaptoketones **increase as pH
falls**: **2-methyl-3-furanthiol Tr → 30 → 60 → 130**, 3-mercapto-2-butanone Tr → 50, 2-mercapto-3-
pentanone Tr → 70 (pH 5.6 → 4.0). **2-Furanylmethanethiol does not** (1420 at pH 5.6, 580–720 at
4.5–5.0, 3100 at 4.0), and its disulfides appear **only at pH 5.6**. Pyrazines fall to ND at pH 4.0;
4,5-dimethylthiazole and 2-acetylthiazole fall 2–3× with acid. Furfural rises 13× (500 → 6650).
The authors attribute more sulfur volatiles at low pH to "increased breakdown of cysteine (to give
hydrogen sulphide) which occurs at acidic pH", citing Shu 1985 — a citation that paper does not
support (see `shu1985_extraction.md`). **No H2S, cysteine, ribose or other precursor was measured.**

## 2. METHODS [M] (pp. 306)

| item | as printed |
|---|---|
| Matrix | beef *M. psoas major*, local supplier, 100 g samples, chopped, blended in a laboratory blender |
| Acidification | "12 ml of solutions containing water and hydrochloric acid (1 M)". pH 5.0, 4.5, 4.0 required **3.0, 7.2 and 12 mL of 1 M HCl** respectively; untreated pH **5.6**; blanks = 12 mL water, no pH adjustment |
| Equilibration | overnight in a refrigerator |
| Cook | **glass bottles in an autoclave at 140 °C for 30 min** (come-up time not stated) |
| Headspace | meat transferred to a 250 mL conical flask with Dreschel head; **O2-free N2 at 40 mL/min for 2 h, flask at 60 °C**; Tenax GC trap (115 mm × 0.75 mm i.d., glass-lined steel); trap then dried 5 min with N2 |
| Internal standard | **1,2-dichlorobenzene, 65 ng in 1 µL ethanol**, added to the trap just before GC-MS |
| Replication | "Usually no less than three headspace collections were performed for each sample"; Table 2 gives mean (SD) |
| GC-MS | HP 5988A MS / HP 5890 GC; SGE Unijector thermal desorption at 250 °C; **DB-5, 30 m × 0.32 mm**; oven 0 °C (cryofocus) → 60 °C in 1 min, 5 min hold, **4 °C/min to 250 °C**; He 2 mL/min; EI 70 eV, source 250 °C, 20–400 amu, 1 s/scan; LRI from C6–C22 n-alkanes (subscripts illegible in the scan) |
| Quantification | peak area in the **total ion chromatogram** vs the dichlorobenzene area, response factor implicitly 1; "approximate concentrations" |
| Odour | four experienced assessors sniffed the cooked meat in foil-wrapped flasks (descriptors only) |

### 3.3 Text statements [Q]
- 85 aliphatic compounds + 52 heterocyclics; "Quantitatively more volatiles were formed overall as the
  pH dropped." Hydrocarbons, ketones, alcohols largely unaffected; alkanals/alkenals/alkadienals and
  long-chain alkylfurans **fall** as pH rises (attributed to aldehyde capture by free amino groups at
  higher pH).
- Mechanism offered for MFT: "pentose dehydration products react with hydrogen sulphide"; HMF
  (4-hydroxy-5-methyl-3(2H)-furanone) + H2S "appears to be favoured by acidic conditions" (citing
  Mottram & Whitfield 1994). FFT "probably involves other routes, such as ... hydrogen sulphide with
  furanmethanol or furfural ... which appear to be unaffected by pH."
- Mercaptoketones from dicarbonyls + H2S, "which appears to occur more readily at lower pH".
- Pyrazines and thiazoles favoured at higher pH because of more unprotonated amino groups.

## 4. THE UNIT — almost certainly ng kg⁻¹, not mg kg⁻¹ [Z] [!]

Printed: "mg kg⁻¹ meat", trace "2 mg kg⁻¹". Three independent checks say this is a misprint for
**ng kg⁻¹** (factor 10⁶):
1. **Mass in the trap.** The internal standard is 65 ng; the sample is 0.1 kg. At response factor 1,
   an analyte peak equal to the IS corresponds to 650 (unit)/kg in the printed scale only if the unit
   is **ng/kg**. Nonanal at "53 110" is then 5.3 µg on the trap (82× the IS) — plausible. As mg/kg it
   would be 5.3 g/kg (0.5 % of the meat) and 5.3 mg on a 115-mm Tenax trap — impossible.
2. **Trace level.** "Tr, 2" in ng/kg = 0.2 ng on the trap = 0.3 % of the IS peak — a sensible trace
   threshold for TIC.
3. **The sister paper.** `madruga1997_extraction.md` (same author, same beef, autoclave, Tenax trap and
   65 ng dichlorobenzene) prints its unit as **ng/100 g meat** with trace **"0.2 ng/100 g"** = **2 ng/kg**
   — exactly this paper's trace value. Its cysteine-added beef gives MFT **3 ng/100 g = 30 ng/kg**, the
   same order as this paper's pH 5.0 value (30).

Even in ng/kg, the numbers are **amounts stripped into a dynamic headspace at 60 °C in 2 h per kg of
meat**, not concentrations in the meat, with response factor 1 on the TIC. **Never use any level from
Table 2 as an absolute quantity.**

## 6. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**The request.** "pH series in a real meat system; quantities."

- **(a) Levels: NONE.** Unit misprinted (Section 4), TIC with response factor 1, dynamic-headspace
  amounts, SDs of 15–50 % (FFT 3100 ± 730, furfural 2480 ± 1300). No precursor contents of the beef
  (cysteine, ribose, IMP, thiamine, glucose) are given, so a model pot cannot even be specified.
- **(b) Within-study pH shapes in a real matrix: YES, directional.** Same meat, same cook, only HCl
  varies (water volume held constant at 12 mL). The robust shapes, all with the post-cook pH
  (4.4 / 4.9 / 5.4 / 5.8) as the x-axis:
  1. **MFT rises ≥ 65× as pH falls 5.8 → 4.4**, monotone. A candidate directional claim for the
     matrix side of the panel; it agrees in sign with Meynier 1995 Table 3 and with the
     thiolate-mediated sink hypothesis (more thiolate, more loss, at higher pH). It does **not**
     discriminate between "formation favoured at low pH" (the authors' reading) and "loss favoured at
     high pH": both give the same sign.
  2. **FFT does not follow MFT** (U-shaped, 2.2× net), and FFT's disulfides appear **only at the
     highest pH**. Any single thiol-class pH factor that moves MFT ≥ 65× must leave FFT within ~2–5×;
     a mechanism acting through the thiolate would have to account for FFT's different response
     (its oxidation to the disulfide at pH 5.8 is consistent with more thiolate there).
  3. **Pyrazines → ND at pH 4.0–4.5; thiazoles fall 2–3× with acid; furfural rises 13× with acid.**
     Same signs as Meynier 1995; usable as trunk-side directional checks.
- **(c) Cysteine thermolysis pH law: NOT TESTABLE.** No H2S measured. The authors' statement that
  cysteine breakdown to H2S "occurs at acidic pH" rests on Shu 1985, which measured no H2S; Zheng &
  Ho's measured constants run the other way (faster at higher pH). This paper's higher sulfur
  volatiles at low pH must therefore come from downstream steps, which is how the model treats it.
- **(d) Meat buffering:** β ≈ 50–75 mmol kg⁻¹ pH⁻¹ for psoas over pH 4.0–5.6, and a +0.2 to +0.4
  pH rise on cooking. Usable as a sanity check on a meat-matrix pH state; not as a fitted value.

## 7. FLAGS

1. **Concentration unit misprint** (mg/kg printed; ng/kg by three independent checks). Section 4.
2. **"pH did not vary" (abstract) vs Table 1's +0.2 to +0.4.** Use the post-cook pH for any pH axis;
   the pre-cook labels understate the cook pH by 0.2–0.4.
3. **No precursor or sulfide measurement.** Cysteine, cystine, glutathione, ribose, IMP, thiamine and
   H2S all unmeasured. The added liquid is 12 mL in every sample; only its HCl content differs.
4. **HCl adds chloride** (30–120 mmol/kg) as well as acid; ionic strength differs across the series.
5. **Response factor 1 on TIC.** Cross-compound comparisons within a column are unreliable;
   same-compound comparisons across pH are the usable quantity.
6. **Headspace at 60 °C for 2 h is itself a thermal and oxidative step** (O2-free N2 limits the
   latter). Thiol oxidation to disulfides during collection is not excluded.
7. **Identity footnotes:** several S-compounds rest on literature spectra only (MS, no LRI match):
   2-methyl-3-(methyltrithio)furan, dimethyl tri-/tetrasulphide, dipropyl trisulphide, both
   dithiolanone/dithianone, the dihydrothiophenone, "A formylmethylthiophene". MFT, FFT, the MFT/FFT
   disulfides, furfural, the pyrazines and thiazoles are MS + LRI.
8. **Shu 1985 is cited (p. 307) for "breakdown of cysteine (to give hydrogen sulphide) which occurs at
   acidic pH".** Shu 1985 measured no H2S and its largest extract was at pH 5.1, not at acid pH.
9. **Two SDs look misprinted:** 2-methyl-3-(2-furanylmethyldithio)furan "140 (1)" at pH 4.0 (CV 0.7 %
   against 25–60 % in its neighbours) — read as printed.
10. **Registry (`data/keys/compounds.yml`, checked 2026-10-27):** keyed — `2_methyl_3_furanthiol`,
    `2_furfurylthiol`, `methylpyrazine`, `2_5_dimethylpyrazine`, `2_6_dimethylpyrazine`, `furfural`,
    `hexanal`, `2_pentylfuran`, `2_methylthiophene`, `dimethyl_trisulfide`, `e_e_2_4_decadienal`.
    **Not keyed: 2-acetylthiazole** (although the engine carries it as species `ACTZ`), 4,5-dimethyl-
    thiazole, the mercaptoketones, the MFT/FFT disulfides and the thiophenones.
