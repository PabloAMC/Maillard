# US 5,760,005 (Watschenko, Kachler & Bugla, Pharmacia 1998) — EXTRACTION (N-acetylcysteine as a sugar-solution stabiliser for parenteral nutrition; one H2S number: "> 4 mg/l" from ≈ 36 g/L glucose + 32.6 g/L N-acetylcysteine heat-sterilised at 120 °C for 15 min; browning stated only as discoloured / not discoloured)

### ONE H2S FLOOR, AND IT IS NOT A STRECKER NUMBER: N-acetylcysteine has no free α-amino group, so it cannot form the Schiff base a Strecker degradation needs. The > 4 mg/L H2S (≥ 0.06 mol % of the NAC in 15 min at 120 °C) is H2S from an N-blocked cysteine in a concentrated glucose solution — desulfhydration, possibly carbonyl-assisted, not cysteine's Strecker sulfur.

## 1. Why it matters (B59a: H2S from cysteine's Strecker degradation)

It was downloaded for "H2S (> 4 mg/L) from an N-acetylcysteine + glucose solution sterilised at 120 °C for 15 min". The
patent does contain that number, once, in Comparative Example B, without method, pH, vessel or replicate. **Whether it
bears on B59a:** only indirectly. NAC is N-acetylated; the Strecker route (α-amino acid + α-dicarbonyl → Schiff base →
decarboxylation → aminoketone + Strecker aldehyde; for cysteine, mercaptoacetaldehyde + H2S) is blocked. So this is at
best a **ceiling-side reference for non-Strecker H2S** (thermolytic / carbonyl-assisted β-elimination of a cysteine thiol
in a sugar matrix), which the engine already carries as cysteine thermolysis. **Delivers for B59a: no Strecker number.**

## 3. Arithmetic (mine)

1. **Comparative B molarity.** Glucose 36 g/L / 180.16 = **199.8 mmol/L**; NAC 32.6 g/L / 163.19 = **199.8 mmol/L** — exactly
   equimolar, so "about 1:1" is a **molar** ratio (mass ratio 1.10).
2. **H2S yield.** > 4 mg/L / 34.08 = **> 0.117 mmol/L** = **> 0.059 mol % of the NAC** (and of the glucose) in 15 min at 120 °C.
   A floor (">"), single value, method unknown; if the vessel had headspace, solution H2S understates formation.
3. **Example 1 ratio.** Glucose : NAC ≈ 2220 : 4.29 mmol/L ≈ 520 : 1 (if 40 % w/v).

## Use in the model

- **B59a (Strecker H2S): nothing usable.** The only H2S number belongs to an N-acetylated cysteine, for which the Strecker
  route is chemically closed. For B59a the cited **Friedman & Molnar-Perl 1990 (JAFC 38, 1642)** is the paper to fetch: it
  heats free amino acids (and possibly cysteine) with glucose and may report measured numbers; the patent reports none of its data.
- **Possible directional use:** a one-sided check that the engine's thiol thermolysis (if it were applied to an
  N-acetylcysteine-like thiol in glucose at 120 °C, 15 min) does not predict **less** than ~0.06 mol % H2S. Only if the engine
  ever carries NAC; otherwise park it.
- **Browning:** qualitative support for "a thiol in a sugar solution suppresses later browning when amino acids are added",
  i.e. thiols scavenge reactive sugar-derived carbonyls during sterilisation. No numbers.
