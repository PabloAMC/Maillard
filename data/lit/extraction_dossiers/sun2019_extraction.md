## 1. ONE-PARAGRAPH VERDICT

This is the only one of the three K4a thiol papers that separates **reversible binding** from
**irreversible chemical loss** operationally, and it does so with an explicit, quotable definition
(§2.6). It supplies: a 5-point sealed-vial time course for four volatiles (Fig. 2, relative % only);
a 5-point open-vial time course of **free** and **total** 2-FFT in absolute µg/L with SDs and Duncan
letters (Table 1, the only real table in the paper); the reversibly-bound / irreversibly-lost
decomposition of that same time course (Fig. 3); and the requested **pH × temperature panel set**
(Fig. 4A–D) covering **pH 3, 4.5, 6, 7.5, 9 × 20, 55, 90 °C** for free 2-FFT in coffee brew,
free 2-FFT in the HHQ model, total 2-FFT in coffee brew, and total 2-FFT in the model. **There are NO
rate constants, NO half-lives, NO activation energies, NO binding constants (no K_a, K_d, no Scatchard
or Langmuir treatment), and NO % bound is ever printed as such** — the "% bound" must be derived by me
from the free/total pair. The temperature axis exists in **only one panel (Fig. 4A)** and only for
*free* 2-FFT in coffee brew; there is no temperature axis at all for total 2-FFT or for the model
system, so no Ea can be extracted for binding or for irreversible loss. Every number in Fig. 4A–D and
Fig. 5 is figure-only: the authors print in the text only the pH-3 and pH-9 endpoints of each series,
and I digitised the pH 4.5 / 6 / 7.5 interiors. Crucially, **at pH ≥ 6 in Fig. 4A the three
temperature curves are entirely superimposed and cannot be separated** — those nine cells are
recorded as a single merged band and marked unreadable per-series. All samples measured in triplicate;
errors are standard deviations; significance by Duncan's multiple range test at p < 0.05.

### 2.6 THE OPERATIONAL DEFINITIONS — reversible vs irreversible, verbatim

This is the paper's own definition, quoted in full because the task turns on it (p. 452, §3.2):

> "Total available 2-FFT can be lost by irreversible losses and reversible binding reactions
> (Charles-Bernard, Kraehenbuehl, et al., 2005; Guichard, 2002). These two losses affect the total and
> potential for free 2-FFT in coffee. As the total 2-FFT includes free 2-FFT and reversibly bound
> 2-FFT, **the difference between the total 2-FFT amount and free 2-FFT amount is defined as
> 'reversibly bound 2-FFT'**; On the other hand, irreversible losses occurred and increased during
> coffee storage. So, **the difference of total 2-FFT between original and stored coffee is defined as
> 'irreversibly lost 2-FFT'**." (emphasis mine, wording verbatim)

The equations exactly as printed (p. 453):

> "Reversibly bound 2 − FFT = Total X − Free X"
>
> "Irreversibly lost 2 − −FFT = Total O − Total X"
>
> "X: The concentration of aroma in coffee brew stored at time X min;
>  O: The concentration of aroma in original fresh coffee brew at time 0 min."

(The second equation is printed with a stray extra minus sign — "2 − −FFT" — a typesetting error;
transcribed verbatim.)

And the operational definitions of "total" and "free", verbatim from the Introduction (p. 450):

> "Cysteine addition has also been shown to reversibly release bound 2-FFT in coffee brew. This has
> been used to determinate the total: bound 2-FFT in coffee brews. Hereinafter **'total 2-FFT' refers
> to the whole available 2-FFT in the coffee brew at any time point, including both free 2-FFT and
> reversibly bound 2-FFT. 'Free 2-FFT' means the 2-FFT fraction that exists in free form in a coffee
> brew** (Sun et al., 2018)."

And the analytical realisation of "total", verbatim (p. 451, §2.8):

> "For total 2-FFT analysis, cysteine powder was added to each vial before closure at 16 g/L (Sun
> et al., 2018) so that the bound form of 2-FFT was released to the free form. With cysteine, the free
> 2-FFT currently detected by SPME-GC–MS was the amount of total 2-FFT. Aroma analysis for free 2-FFT
> in coffee brew had no cysteine addition."

The mechanistic claim behind reversibility, verbatim (p. 450):

> "Cysteine has been shown previously to release 2-FFT that is bound by coffee matrix (Darriet,
> Tominaga, Lavigne, Boidron, & Dubourdieu, 1995; Mestdagh et al., 2014). After cysteine addition,
> the bound form of 2-FFT is competitively replaced by cysteine due to its mercapto structure and high
> reducing properties. High cysteine concentration could also prevent 2-FFT from forming dimers
> (Rowe, 2009)."

And the definition of what is *irreversible*, verbatim (p. 449–450):

> "The irreversible losses of 2-FFT are presumed to be due to physical diffusion/volatile loss and
> chemical degradation reactions, such as polymerization or oxidation (Blank et al., 2002;
> Charles-Bernard, Kraehenbuehl, et al., 2005). This irreversible fraction is very hard to regenerate.
> Reversible losses are believed to be mainly through covalent bonding to non-volatile components in
> the coffee matrix, it is proposed that this 2-FFT lost due to reversible reactions could be
> subsequently released again by cysteine addition (Mestdagh, Davidek, Chaumonteuil, Folmer, & Blank,
> 2014; Müller & Hofmann, 2007; Sun et al., 2018)."

**Important consequence of these definitions for the model.** "Irreversibly lost" here is
**operationally defined as anything cysteine cannot recover**, and the paper explicitly lumps
**physical volatilisation** into it ("presumed to be due to physical diffusion/volatile loss **and**
chemical degradation"). In the *open-vial* experiment (Table 1, Fig. 3) volatilisation is deliberately
allowed. So **Fig. 3's "irreversibly lost 2-FFT" is NOT a pure chemical-degradation quantity** — it is
chemical degradation **plus** headspace loss, and the paper never separates the two. The authors say as
much: "These low (∼10%) losses suggest that **the volatilization and irreversible chemical degradation**
have a limited effect on 2-FFT loss" (p. 453) — they treat the pair as inseparable. Any model that maps
this onto a pure degradation rate will over-estimate degradation.

### 2.7 Model system (Fig. 4B, 4D)

> "The buffer solution was prepared using disodium phosphate solution (0.2 M), sodium dihydrogen
> phosphate solution (0.2 M) and sodium hydroxide solution (0.1 M) and hydrochloric acid solution
> (0.1 M) to adjust the pH to 3 or 9. An aqueous solution of hydroxyhydroquinone (HHQ) (0.004 g HHQ
> diluted in 30 mL Milipore water) was added (300 µL) into phosphate buffer solution (40 mL) and
> placed in amber vials (40 mL), the samples was then stored for 1 h in a water bath at 40 °C to allow
> incubation (Müller & Hofmann, 2007). 2-FFT (36 μg in 31.7 μL of methanol) was added into HHQ buffer
> solution. 2-FFT was reacted with HHQ for 1 h at 40 °C prior, to evaluate free and total 2-FFT by
> SPME-GC–MS." (p. 451)

[Z] Model-system concentrations: HHQ stock = 0.004 g / 30 mL = 0.1333 g L⁻¹ = **1.057 mM** (MW 126.11);
300 µL into 40 mL ⇒ **7.93 µM HHQ**. 2-FFT: 36 µg / 40 mL = **900 µg L⁻¹ = 7.88 µM** (MW 114.17).
So **HHQ : 2-FFT ≈ 1.006 : 1 (mol/mol)** — an almost exactly equimolar model system. Phosphate is
0.2 M stock; the working molarity is not separable from the text (NaOH/HCl were used to trim pH), so
**buffer molarity in the reaction: unreadable / not determinable from the paper.**

**Text/protocol inconsistency (flagged):** §2.7 says the buffer was adjusted "to pH 3 or 9", i.e. two
levels, but **Fig. 4B and 4D plot five pH levels (3, 4.5, 6, 7.5, 9)**. The Methods do not describe how
the intermediate model pH values were made. Both statements quoted; the figures are taken at face value.

Also note the model system was incubated at **40 °C**, whereas the coffee-brew free-2-FFT arm used
20/55/90 °C. **Fig. 4A and Fig. 4B are therefore not at a common temperature** and must not be
compared cell-by-cell.

### 2.9 Calibration — verbatim

> "The quantification of 2-FFT was carried out using the internal standard quantification method (Sun
> et al., 2018). 3-Heptanone (4.1 μg in 5 μL of methanol) was added into calibration solution as an
> internal standard (IS) to accommodate for instrument drift. For coffee brew, the calibration curve
> was established by adding 2-FFT (0, 1.8, 3.6, 7.2, 14.4 μg in 31.7 μL of methanol) into a
> prefabricated coffee model which had a similar matrix to coffee brew. To prepare this coffee model,
> cysteine (1.6 g) was presented into fresh coffee brew (200 mL) at 40 °C. The coffee brew was dried by
> rotary vacuum evaporation at 40 °C. The dried sample was then dissolved in 200 mL of ultra-pure
> water. This part of experiment (from adding cysteine to samples dissolved into water) was repeated
> once more to release maximum 2-FFT from the coffee brew (Sun et al., 2018). For the model
> experiment, the calibration curve was established by adding 2-FFT (0, 9, 18, 36, 44 μg in 31.7 μL of
> methanol) into the phosphate buffer solution. The concentration of 2-furfurylthiol could be
> calculated by the calibration curve made by relative peak area (peak area of standard 2-FFT to that
> of internal standard) to the concentration of standard 2-FFT (**y = 0.0055 x + 0.0381, R² = 0.998**)."
> (p. 451)

**The single fitted quantity printed in this paper: y = 0.0055x + 0.0381, R² = 0.998** [F]. It is a
calibration line, not a kinetic model. Note the paper prints **one** equation for what its own text
describes as **two different calibration curves** (coffee-brew matrix and buffer matrix) at different
spike levels — it is ambiguous which one the equation belongs to. Flagged.

Also note the IS mass is printed as **4.1 μg** in §2.7 and **4.09 μg** in §2.8 — a trivial internal
inconsistency, both quoted.

> "Approximate quantification of all volatiles for the aroma stability study (Item 2.3) were
> calculated by comparing the GC peak areas of volatiles with the peak area of 3-heptanone internal
> standard, **using a response factor of 1** to give relative concentrations (Liu, Yang, Linforth,
> Fisk, & Yang, 2019). The percentage of volatile was calculated by the comparison of their relative
> concentration after storage to their initial relative concentration in fresh coffee. All samples
> were analyzed in one run in randomized order." (p. 451)

**Fig. 2 is therefore semi-quantitative: response factor assumed = 1 for all four compounds.**

| 45 | 0.5 | 138 | 137.5 | 15 | 99.64 % | 0.36 % |
| 60 | 0.4 | 138 | 137.6 | 15 | 99.71 % | 0.29 % |

**The single most useful derived number in the paper: in fresh Robusta French-press brew at ~pH 6.2
and 40 °C, 97.8 % of the total available 2-FFT is already reversibly bound at t = 0, rising to 99.7 %
after 1 h** [Z]. The paper never prints a "% bound"; it only says "a large amount of 2-FFT was
reversibly bound" (p. 453).

Fractional losses over 1 h [Z]: free 3.4 → 0.4 µg/L = **88.2 % loss**; total 153 → 138 µg/L =
**9.8 % loss**; reversibly bound 149.6 → 137.6 = **8.0 % loss**.

The authors' own statements, verbatim (p. 452 and p. 453):
> "Over 1 h storage, the concentration of both total and free 2-FFT decreased significantly (Table 1).
> The biggest change happened within the first 30 min and then plateaued. **Total 2-FFT decreased from
> 153 to 138 μg/L (10% loss)** (p < 0.05), and **free 2-FFT decreased from 3.4 to 0.4 μg/L (88% loss)**
> over 1 h storage (p < 0.05). The total 2-FFT concentration was much higher than free 2-FFT
> concentration, indicating that a large amount of 2-FFT was reversibly bound to coffee components
> within the fresh coffee brew."

> "Over the 1 h storage period, the proportion of reversibly bound 2-FFT reduced from **149.6 to 137.1
> μg/L**). The irreversibly lost 2-FFT increased from **0 to 15.5 μg/L**. These low (∼10%) losses
> suggest that the volatilization and irreversible chemical degradation have a limited effect on 2-FFT
> loss compared to the predominant reversible binding in natural coffee brew (∼pH 6.2). Due to this, in
> natural coffee (∼pH 6.2), reversible loss is proposed to be the main reason for the loss of free
> 2-FFT, which is expected to play a significant role in the loss of the characteristic aroma of coffee

**Minor arithmetic mismatch (flagged, both quoted):** the text gives reversibly bound at 60 min as

> "This pH sensitivity can be explained by the impact on quinone. As discussed previously, during the

> inhibited and more free 2-FFT is reversibly bound."

**Circularity and structural flags for the orchestrator:**
1. **Fig. 3 is arithmetically derived from Table 1** by the authors' own two equations. Fitting both is
   double-counting the same three-replicate measurement. Use Table 1; take only the Duncan letters
   from Fig. 3.
2. **Do not treat "irreversibly lost 2-FFT" as chemical degradation.** The paper's definition
   explicitly bundles volatilisation with degradation, and the experiment that produces it is an
   *open* vial. Fig. 4C (sealed amber vials) is the cleaner irreversible-loss series.
3. **Fig. 4A and Fig. 4B are at different temperatures** (20/55/90 °C vs 40 °C). They must not be
   fitted as a single brew-vs-model contrast at matched T.
4. **Fig. 4C has no stated incubation temperature.** If the model needs one, this must be recorded as
   an assumption (40 °C is the only defensible guess) and propagated as a systematic, not folded
   into σ.
5. **The Fig. 4D pH-3 discrepancy (351 [M] vs 323 [fig])** is unresolved. Do not let a [fig] value pass
   as [M] there, and do not average them.
6. The whole paper is **one brew batch, one roast, one origin (Vietnamese Robusta, medium roast,
   L* = 41)**. Batch-to-batch matrix variability is entirely unsampled. σ from these SDs is a
   *within-batch* σ and will understate real uncertainty.
