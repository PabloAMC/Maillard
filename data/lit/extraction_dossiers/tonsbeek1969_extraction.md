## 1. Methods as they matter to a model

- **Extract.** 2 kg minced lean beef shin extracted three times with 1 L water at room temperature
  (15 min each) → ~3 L; proteins precipitated with 4 vol cold ethanol (2 °C, 1 h); concentrated at 40 °C
  to 400 mL ("The extractable material of 5 grams of beef is then contained in 1 ml").
- **Fractionation.** Bio-Rad AG 50W-X8 (H⁺): Fraction I (neutral + acidic) vs II (basic: amino acids,
  creatine; eluted with 6 N ammonia). Fraction I → Ba(OH)₂ to pH 8 then 9.3 → III (first Ba
  precipitate), IV (supernatant after ethanol), V (second precipitate). V → Katz & Comb column
  (0.05 N HCl): V-A (sugar mono-/diphosphates, phosphoric and succinic acids), V-B (5′-IMP), V-C
  (5′-GMP).
- **Screen.** Each fraction "neutralized … to pH 5.5, which is equal to the pH of an aqueous extract of
  beef", "refluxed for 2½ hours", ether-extracted 3 h; positive if UV max at 289 mμ; norfuraneol
  confirmed on a polyamide column + GLC (separating the 2,5-dimethyl homologue, furaneol). **Volumes
  and concentrations of the refluxed fractions not reported.** Buffer: none (pH adjusted with 1 N
  NaOH/HCl at start; not held).
- **Model experiments.** "Varying quantities (2 to 100 mg.)" of fraction-IV components with **5 mg
  ribose-5-phosphate in 50 ml** distilled water, pH 5.5, boiled 2.5 h. → R5P ≈ **0.43 mM** (mine, as
  free acid, MW 230); partners ≈ 0.3-15 mM (mine, for MW 130-ish). Readout: UV spectra only.
- **Phosphatase test.** Fraction V-A in Tris pH 8.8 ± 500 µg alkaline phosphatase, 5 h at 37 °C; after
  hydrolysis the precursor activity is gone and free ribose appears (paper chromatography; TMS-GLC).

## 4. Flags

1. **Purely qualitative** (+/− scale; UV screen). Detection limit not stated, so "zero" means below
   an unstated UV threshold after 3 h ether extraction.
2. **Fraction concentrations unknown**; the ribose and amino-acid levels in II + IV are whatever beef
   shin extract contains (free ribose in beef is low, sub-mM), so the II + IV negative does not prove
   free ribose + amino acids cannot make norfuraneol — only that they don't at extract levels in 2.5 h.
3. pH set to 5.5 at the start of reflux, unbuffered, not monitored.
4. "probably ribose-5-phosphate": identified via phosphatase + ribose release, not isolated.
5. Mechanism text (β-elimination of phosphate) is attributed to Peer & van den Ouweland 1968
   (Rec. Trav. Chim. 87:1017), not shown here; that paper, not this one, carries yields.

## Use in the model

- **Gap (d) norfuraneol formation / gap (c) phosphate — qualitative only.** Two constraints worth
  carrying as **sign-only checks**, not fit rows: (i) in a dilute unbuffered pH-5.5 pot, R5P alone
  makes no detectable norfuraneol in 2.5 h at 100 °C, so phosphate as an intramolecular leaving group
  does not by itself open a fast amine-free NF route in dilute solution (Peer & van den Ouweland
  report the opposite in **concentrated buffer**, i.e. buffer catalysis is the operative variable —
  consistent with Larralde 1995's phosphate general-base term and with R5P decomposing 12x faster
  than ribose at pD 7.4); (ii) norfuraneol from R5P needs a nitrogen partner, and the active ones
  are weak or non-nucleophilic amines (pyroglutamic acid has no free amine; taurine's is a
  sulfonate-zwitterion), which argues for **general acid/base or buffer-type catalysis** rather
  than Amadori chemistry.
- **Engine relevance.** The engine has no R5P/IMP species; this bears on meat-matrix NF sources (a
  ribose-5-phosphate → 1-deoxyosone shortcut), not on Hofmann's or Zhang's pots. It does **not**
  inform the pH-3 enolisation law.
- **Action:** if an R5P route is ever wanted, the quantitative source is Peer & van den Ouweland 1968,
  not this paper.
