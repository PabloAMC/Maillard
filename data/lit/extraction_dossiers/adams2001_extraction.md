## 1. What this paper answers for the model

| what the model needs | what Adams 2001 gives | tag |
|---|---|---|
| does protein consume flavour disulfides? | yes, heat-denatured ovalbumin at pH 6.7 / 8.0; native protein only at pH 8.0 (and slightly at 6.7) | [M] |
| reversibility (disulfide vs covalent) | the flavour disulfide becomes **free thiol + protein mixed disulfide** (Fig. 2 scheme [P]); free thiols detected only in the protein system (Table 3: allyl mercaptan, 1-propanethiol, 1-butanethiol, 2-furanmethanethiol) [M]. Diallyl sulfide (no S-S) does not react [M] | [M] / [P] |
| pH dependence | **pH 2: no loss** (Fig. 4, heated and unheated) [F]; pH 6.7 < pH 8.0 loss everywhere [M] → thiolate-driven [Q] | [M] / [F] |
| structure dependence | 2-furfuryl methyl disulfide and diallyl disulfide >> dipropyl ≈ dibutyl > diethyl in loss (Table 2) | [M] |
| protein SH titre | Ellman: native 0.01 SH per ovalbumin; heat-denatured 1.24 per molecule (4 free SH + 1 S-S per molecule in principle [C]) | [M] |
| a rate constant | **none printed.** Order-of-magnitude [Z]: furfuryl methyl disulfide, heat-denatured protein added-after-heating, pH 6.7, ≥ 60-90 min contact at 20-37 °C: 77 % lost → k2 ~0.06-0.09 L/(mmol·min) on 0.28 mM exposed SH (sec. 3) | [Z] |
| temperature | only "before vs after heating" (BH vs AH): BH loses more, confounded with SH exposure | [M] / [Q] |
| MFT or FFT themselves | **no.** Only 2-furfuryl methyl disulfide (gives FFT + methanethiol); no MFT species | [M] |

**Answer.** The same Reading group (mottram1996, mottram1996b) showed protein turns furyl disulfides into thiols
during SDE; this paper isolates the chemistry: it needs an ionisable protein SH (none at pH 2; more at pH 8; more when
denaturation exposes SH), it releases the flavour thiol, and unsaturated (allyl, furfuryl) disulfides react fastest.
For the engine it adds the missing direction (protein-S⁻ + R-S-S-R → P-S-S-R + R-S⁻), with a pH law shaped like
a thiolate fraction, but no constant usable at process temperature.

time is an assumption; treat as ±0.5 dex.

## 4. Mechanism evidence and its kind

- **Kind 6 (within-study factors):** pH (2.0 / 6.7 / 8.0), denaturation (native / heated), order of addition (BH /
  AH), disulfide structure (5 compounds), sulfide control (diallyl sulfide does not react). Plus a product check
  (free thiols in the protein system only, Table 3) and a reagent titre (Ellman). Reading (Mottram group) with CCFRA.
- Mechanism: thiolate-disulfide interchange (Whitesides 1983 [C]), PS⁻ + RSSR → PSSR + RS⁻, then RS⁻ + PSSP →
  RSSP + PS⁻, RS⁻ + H⁺ → RSH (Fig. 2) [P]. Protein mixed disulfides not measured [P].
- Protein-SH pKa 8.80 quoted from Damodaran 1996 [C].
- Reactivity of allyl / furfuryl disulfides attributed to unsaturation making the S-S more electrophilic [P].

## 6. Disagreements found

1. **Thiol-disulfide interchange speed vs the engine's protein constant** [Z, low weight]: Adams' furfuryl
   disulfide + denatured-ovalbumin SH runs at k2 ~0.06-0.09 L/(mmol·min) at 20-37 °C, pH 6.7 (contact time
   assumed); the engine's thiol + protein-disulfide constant is 6.05e-6 L/(mmol·min) (ambient). Different reactions
   (direction and which partner carries the thiolate), so not a contradiction; but interchange in both directions
   shares the thiolate-on-S-S step, and a 4-dex gap is a reason to revisit the bracket's provenance before the
   protein channel is ever switched on. Candidate causes: different proteins (β-lactoglobulin vs ovalbumin), the
   engine bracket is a 24 h binary endpoint, the Adams estimate assumes the contact time.
2. **"200-fold excess"** printed vs 84x (total disulfide) or 450x (per compound) from the paper's own concentrations
   [!].
3. Fig. 3 legend order (water, 6.7 AH, 8.0 AH, 6.7 BH, 8.0 BH) differs from Table 2 column order; the bars match the
   table once matched by label, no inconsistency [checked].
