# US 3,904,655 (van den Ouweland & Peer, Lever Brothers 1975) — EXTRACTION (4-hydroxy-3(2H)-furanones and maltol-type pyrones heated with H2S, Na2S, cysteine, glutathione, thioacetamide or mercaptoacetamide in water, 95-110 °C, 1.5-6 h → "roast meat" flavour; 24 examples of charges, times and a few pH values; NO product identified, NO yield, NO analytical chemistry)

### A RECIPE BOOK, NOT A MEASUREMENT: every example ends in a sensory verdict. 2-methyl-3-furanthiol (or any other product) is never named, let alone quantified. What it does give is a set of well-defined charges (mostly equimolar 50 mmol ketone + 50 mmol sulfur donor) at ~100 °C that a model could be asked to make "meaty" sulfur volatiles from — usable only as qualitative licences.

## 1. Why it matters (B59a / sulfur-lane product routes)

Downloaded as the origin of "4-hydroxy-5-methyl-3(2H)-furanone (norfuraneol) + H2S → 2-methyl-3-furanthiol (MFT)". The
patent establishes that **furanone/pyrone + an H2S source in water at ~100 °C gives a meat-like flavour within minutes to
hours**, with H2S itself, inorganic sulfides, cysteine, glutathione, thioacetamide or mercaptoacetamide as donors. It does
**not** identify MFT (or any compound), report a yield, or measure H2S release from cysteine. **Delivers for B59a: no
quantitative number; a qualitative licence for "furanone + H2S → meaty thiols" only.** (The MFT identification is in later
literature, e.g. the van den Ouweland & Peer 1975 JAFC paper on the same chemistry, which is not on disk and not cited here.)

## 2. General process conditions as claimed / described

- Ketones (claim 2 / description): 4-hydroxy-5-methyl-2,3-dihydrofuran-3-one (= **norfuraneol**, 4-hydroxy-5-methyl-3(2H)-
  furanone); 4-hydroxy-2,5-dimethyl- (= **furaneol / HDMF**); 4-hydroxy-2-methyl-5-ethyl-; 4-hydroxy-5-methyl-2-ethyl-;
  4-hydroxy-2,5-diethyl-; 4-hydroxy-2-hydroxymethyl-5-methyl-; 4-acetoxy-5-methyl-; 4-methoxy-2,5-dimethyl-; pyrones maltol
  and ethyl maltol. "The first three named ... are the most preferred."
- Temperature 0-150 °C (claim 1); preferred > 60 °C, "most preferably between about 90 °C and 110 °C", reflux at atmospheric
  pressure; superatmospheric pressure above the boiling point or when H2S gas is used.
- pH "from 2 to 10, but values between 4 and 7 are preferred".
- Time: "flavour substances are produced within a few minutes ... and continue to build up ... for several days"; at 100 °C,
  1 min to 15 h; at room temperature 3 min to 30 days; preferred 1-6 h. Preferred embodiment: reflux slightly above 100 °C for
  about 3 h.
- Ratio ketone : sulfur compound 1:50 to 50:1 by weight; claim 1: 1:10 to 10:1. Water at least equal by weight to the sulfur
  compound; preferably ≥ weight of reactants; usually ≤ 100× reactants.
- Optional: amino acids (incl. cysteine, cystine), monosaccharides (glucose, ribose, xylose, rhamnose, arabinose), fatty acids.
- Excess H2S to be removed after reaction (ventilation), else residual H2S makes the flavour objectionable.

## 4. Arithmetic (mine)

1. **Design.** Examples 2, 3, 4, 5 and 12 are all 50.0 ± 0.6 mmol ketone + 44-51 mmol donor — a deliberate equimolar
   screen at 0.08-0.5 mol/L. Cysteine examples use 1-4.7 eq of cysteine per ketone (Ex. 1: 4.1; Ex. 7: 3.5; Ex. 8: 6.4;
   Ex. 9: 0.94; Ex. 20: 1.9; Ex. 24: 4.7).
2. **Ex. 6 / 15 H2S charge:** 0.5 g H2S in 100 mL = 5 g/L (147 mmol/L); H2S solubility at 1 atm and 20 °C is ≈ 3.8 g/L
   (handbook value, not from this source) — the stated charge exceeds 1-atm solubility, so it was presumably dissolved under
   pressure or partly in the headspace; the autoclave is consistent with that.
3. **Ex. 24 cysteine:** 4.0 g in 10 mL = 400 g/L, above L-cysteine's room-temperature solubility (≈ 280 g/L, handbook) — a
   slurry at the start.
4. **Ex. 3 "110 °C" under reflux** at atmospheric pressure for an aqueous solution: the liquid cannot exceed ≈ 100-101 °C;
   110 °C is presumably the bath temperature.

## Use in the model

- **B59a (Strecker H2S): nothing quantitative.** The patent treats cysteine as an H2S source without measuring the H2S, and it
  does not say which pathway releases it.
- **Furanone + H2S product step:** a **qualitative licence** that norfuraneol / furaneol / maltol react with H2S or cysteine in
  water at 95-110 °C on a 1-6 h timescale to give meat-like sulfur aroma. If the engine adds a norfuraneol + H2S → MFT step, its
  rate must come from elsewhere (declared assumption); this patent can only be cited for the route's existence and for the
  ~hours timescale at ~100 °C (flavour "within a few minutes ... build up for several days").
- **Possible directional pot:** Ex. 20/21 (Cys 330 + Xyl 133 + norfuraneol 175 mmol/L, 95 °C, 2½ h) says that adding norfuraneol
  to a cysteine-xylose pot increases meaty character — a direction-only check on any future furanone → MFT flux, sensory not
  chemical.
