## 1. What it says about pentoses (the only lines bearing on the gaps)

p. 4-5, opening paragraph (translated faithfully; German quoted where load-bearing):

- Reacting pentoses with some amino acids **in water at 100 °C** gave, "neben anderen Produkten"
  (among other products), a sublimable substance C₅H₆O₃, assigned from UV, IR and NMR spectra as
  2,3-dihydro-4-hydroxy-5-methyl-furan-3-one (= norfuraneol, 4-hydroxy-5-methyl-3(2H)-furanone).
- "Die katalytisch wirksamen Aminosäuren lassen sich durch Acetate primärer Amine ersetzen." — the
  catalytically active amino acids can be replaced by acetates of primary amines. The scheme on
  p. 5 draws: pentose —(RNH₂, CH₃COOH)→ norfuraneol (formula 1). The amine is called **catalytic**.
- Pentose-derived compound: **λmax 286 mμ, log ε = 3.88** (water); CO band at 1690 cm⁻¹.

**NOT reported here:** which pentose(s), which amino acids, concentrations, pH, heating time,
norfuraneol yield, any time course or rate.

## 2. The glucose experiment (the paper's actual subject)

- **Charge (p. 6):** 130 g glucose (hydrate) in 700 mL water + 20 mL 40 % aqueous methylamine + 14 mL
  glacial acetic acid; "2 Std am Rückflußkühler gekocht" (refluxed 2 h, i.e. ~100 °C).
  Concentrations (mine): glucose 0.656 mol (MW 198.2) in ~734 mL ≈ **0.89 M**; methylamine ≈ 0.23 mol
  (assuming ρ ≈ 0.9 g/mL) ≈ **0.31 M**; acetic acid 0.245 mol ≈ **0.33 M**. The text (p. 5) says
  "äquimolaren Mengen Methylamin und Essigsäure" — equimolar amine and acid, **not** equimolar to
  glucose (amine : glucose ≈ 0.36). **pH not reported**; methylammonium acetate alone would sit near
  pH 7.7 at 25 °C (mine, (pKa 4.76 + 10.6)/2), with a glucose excess.
- **Work-up:** continuous extraction (Perforator) with ethyl acetate, distillation at 80 °C / 0.1 Torr,
  resublimation, recrystallisation from cyclohexane.
- **Product:** C₆H₈O₄ (144.1), mp 72-74 °C, colourless, "Karamell-Geruch" (caramel odour); λmax
  298 mμ, log ε 3.87 (water); CO 1670 cm⁻¹; CH₃ singlet 7.91 τ. Assigned (probable) as
  **2-acetyl-4,5-dihydro-3,4-dihydroxyfuran** (formula 3), NOT the hexose analogue of norfuraneol
  (formula 2, 4-hydroxy-2-hydroxymethyl-5-methyl-3(2H)-furanone) that the authors first expected.
- **Yield:** "Ausbeute 0,1 %, bezogen auf Glucose" — 0.1 % based on glucose (w/w or mol not stated;
  both ≈ 0.1-0.14 %). Isolated yield after distillation and sublimation, so a lower bound on formation.
- **Proof of structure:** diacetate (mp 59 °C, 34 % of theory); in ~0.5 N methanolic HCl, 5 min at room
  temperature → isomaltol methyl ether (2-acetyl-3-methoxyfuran, mp 98-101 °C, 46 %), identical by
  mixed mp and IR with Hodge & Nelson's compound.

## Use in the model

- **Gap (d), norfuraneol formation vs pH — not served.** The only norfuraneol content is a
  secondary, qualitative restatement: pentose + amino acid (or primary-amine acetate) in water at
  100 °C gives norfuraneol, with the amine described as catalytic. That is **consistent** with the

- **Gap (c), low-pH route — not served.** No pH is reported; the amine-acetate medium is near-neutral,

- **Glucose side-note.** A 0.1 % isolated yield of an isomaltol-type C₆ product from glucose + amine
  acetate in 2 h at 100 °C is a hexose 2,3-enolisation (1-deoxyosone) marker; it could bound a
  hexose 1-deoxyosone exit in the core lanes, but only as a loose lower bound (isolated, unbuffered,
  pH unknown).
