### *"Formation of cysteine-S-conjugates in the Maillard reaction of cysteine and xylose"*
### Xylose (~600 mmol) and cysteine·HCl·H2O (~541 mmol) refluxed as a concentrated syrup, initial pH 4.0, 100 °C, 2 h. S-Furfuryl-L-cysteine (FFT-S-Cys) and S-(2-methyl-3-furyl)-L-cysteine (MFT-S-Cys) were isolated and identified by NMR, LC-MS² and synthesis. ## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

This is an **identification paper**. A concentrated xylose/cysteine syrup (initial pH 4.0, 100 °C,
2 h, reflux) was fractionated by cation exchange and RP-18. Two fractions, each "essentially a
single compound", were matched to synthesised references by ¹H/¹³C NMR, LC retention time, UV λmax
and ESI-MS² (m/z 202 [M+H]⁺; both C8H11NO3S isomers). One is **S-furfuryl-L-cysteine (FFT-S-Cys)**,
the other **S-(2-methyl-3-furyl)-L-cysteine (MFT-S-Cys)**. **No amount of either conjugate is
reported for the Maillard pot. There is no yield, concentration, ratio to free FFT/MFT or time
course, and the isolated fraction masses are not printed.** In the only other reaction, furfuryl
alcohol (FFA) + cysteine (1 : 10, pH 4, 100 °C, 2 h), both conjugates formed, **MFT-S-Cys LC-DAD
area ≈ 2.5 % of FFT-S-Cys** ("approximately 40-times smaller"). The authors' mechanism (Fig. 5)
starts from **FFA**, not from the thiols: acid-catalysed S_N substitution of protonated FFA by
cysteine at the CH2 gives FFT-S-Cys, and attack at furan C-3 with an allylic rearrangement gives
MFT-S-Cys. They explicitly argue **against** the free thiols being the precursors ("the substitution
of FFT and MFT by cysteine i.e. of one thiol by another thiol is not straightforward"). They also
state that the fate of the conjugates is unknown: "it remains unknown to which extent formed MFT-S-Cys
and FFT-S-Cys are subsequently degraded further in the Maillard reaction, and what ratio exists
between formation and reduction." Both conjugates are odourless in water at 1.0 mg/L. When tasted
they give FFT-like and MFT-like retronasal aroma (7 panellists, data not shown), which the authors
attribute to oral β-lyases.

## 4. DOES THIS PAPER IDENTIFY OR BOUND THE MODEL'S THIOL SINK?

**The model's sink.** `ch_thiolate_loss_fft` / `ch_thiolate_loss_mft` (FFT/MFT → FRAG_C + FRAG_S,
constant `k_thiolate_loss`, first order in thiol times the thiolate fraction) is **fitted on
Kumazawa & Masuda 2003**. That paper dosed **1 ppm 2-furfurylthiol into citrate/Na2HPO4 buffer
with no cysteine, no sugar and no amino acid** and heated it at 121 °C for 10 min across pH 3–7
(`kumazawa2003_extraction.md`). `k_thiol_decay` is the shared lumped constant for the minor thiols
(MP, MP3P, MP2P, HMP, MeSH, 2-acetylthiazole).

**Verdict: NO. This paper neither identifies nor bounds the sink.** Five reasons:

1. **No amounts.** Neither conjugate is quantified in the Maillard product, and free FFT/MFT were
   not measured alongside them. There is no conjugate : free-thiol ratio from which a
   consumption fraction could be read. The only number is an LC-DAD **area** ratio (2.5 %) between
   the two conjugates in the FFA pot.
2. **The authors' route does not consume free thiol.** Fig. 5 makes both conjugates from protonated
   **furfuryl alcohol + cysteine** (cysteine thiol as nucleophile, furfuryl carbon as electrophile).
   The text says thiol-for-thiol substitution of FFT or MFT by cysteine "is not straightforward". On
   the authors' reading the conjugates are a **parallel product of the C5 precursor pool**, a
   formation-side branch competing with FFT for the furfuryl skeleton. They are not a fate of FFT/MFT
   once formed.
3. **The sink the model fits operates with no cysteine present.** Kumazawa's FFT loss happens in
   plain buffer. A cysteine-S-conjugate cannot be that loss, so this chemistry cannot be the identity
   of the *fitted* `k_thiolate_loss` regardless of mechanism. At most it could be an *additional*,
   cysteine-dependent loss in pots that contain cysteine, and nothing here measures one.
4. **The decisive control was not run.** Nobody heated FFT or MFT with cysteine (or cystine) and
   looked for m/z 202 (thioether) or m/z 234 (FFT-S-S-Cys / MFT-S-S-Cys mixed disulfide). The
   mixed disulfide, the obvious thiol + cysteine product, is not mentioned.
5. **Single condition.** One temperature (100 °C), one time (2 h), one nominal initial pH (4.0),
   one very high concentration (a syrup, ~7 mol/kg water). There are no kinetics, no Ea and no pH
   dependence.

**What it does establish (qualitative, for the model's completeness list):**
- **A non-volatile, cysteine-bound fate of the furfuryl and 2-methyl-3-furyl skeletons exists** in a
  pentose + cysteine Maillard pot. A mass balance that counts only volatile FFT/MFT plus fragments can
  therefore under-count sulfur and C5 retained in non-volatiles. **The size is unknown.**
- **Furfuryl alcohol is an intermediate in the pot** (detected, not quantified) and **is itself a
  precursor of both skeletons when cysteine is present**. That includes the 2-methyl-3-furyl
  skeleton by C-3 attack and rearrangement, which is a route to an MFT-type C–S bond that starts at
  a furfuryl carbon. The model has **no FFA species**. FFT comes from `FUR` + H2S (`r_fur_fft_hs`)
  and from `TDP`; MFT from `DDP`, `HMP` and `NF`. A future FFA node would have to carry this branch
  as a **formation-side competitor** (FFA + Cys → bound conjugate), not as a thiol sink.
- **The conjugates are flavour-relevant only in the mouth** (β-lyase release). The model's
  headspace/OAV outputs correctly ignore them.

**Consequence for repo documentation (no edit made):** `docs/validation/isotope_topology_evidence.md`
row 26 lists this paper (from the abstract, `[ABS]`) as a "**bound-thiol sink**" that "affects
free-thiol mass balance". `tasks/data_restructure_plan.md` (l. 2821) calls it "a candidate identity
for the thiol sink". On the full text, the authors' mechanism makes it a **precursor-side branch**,
not a free-thiol sink, and it cannot be the Kumazawa-fitted sink (reason 3). A more accurate label is
"cysteine-S-conjugate branch from furfuryl alcohol, unquantified (completeness gap on the formation
side)".

## 5. CHECK AGAINST THE MODEL'S THIOETHER CONJUGATION STEP (`ch_thioether_*`)

The model's step: `ch_thioether_mft` / `ch_thioether_fft` add FFT/MFT (the thiol as **nucleophile**)
to a matrix electrophile site `MELE` (a Michael acceptor), giving reversible adducts `BND` / `BND_F`
that release the thiol again (`ch_thioether_release*`). `k_thioether` is first order in thiol, and
K(T) comes from Stack et al. 2018: N-acetylcysteine + a quinone, k₁ = 496 M⁻¹ s⁻¹, k₋₁ = 88 s⁻¹ at
19.4 °C, K = 5.64 M⁻¹, ΔH = −28.5 kJ/mol.

| property | model `ch_thioether_*` (Stack 2018) | Cerny & Guntz-Dubini 2013 |
|---|---|---|
| which partner is the thiol | the **aroma thiol** (FFT/MFT) | **cysteine**; the furan is the electrophile (protonated FFA) |
| electrophile | Michael acceptor (quinone-type) | furfuryl cation / allylic system from an **alcohol** (S_N substitution) |
| product | thiol-quinone thioether, **reversible** | aryl/benzyl-type C–S thioether (S-furfuryl-Cys, S-furyl-Cys), **chemically stable**; release needs a C–S β-lyase |
| temperature | 4.6–19.4 °C measured | 100 °C |
| quantities | k₁, k₋₁, K, ΔH | none |

**Verdict:** the two are **different reactions** with opposite polarity and opposite reversibility.
This paper **neither supports nor contradicts** the Stack constants and gives no number to test them
against. It also shows that the "bound" forms of FFT/MFT in a Maillard pot need not be the reversible
`BND` reservoir. The conjugates here would **not** release free thiol on heating (no retro-reaction
is proposed or observed), so they behave as a terminal species, not as a reservoir.

## 6. CAVEATS

1. **Identification, not quantification**, throughout. No yields, and no blank (xylose alone,
   cysteine alone).
2. **Extreme concentration** (~7 mol/kg water each). This is a syrup; transferring it to dilute
   aqueous pots (10–300 mM) is not supported.
3. **pH**: initial pH 4.0 from Cys·HCl + NaOH with no buffer, and a reading that is hard to square
   with a net base excess (§2.1). No hot or final pH.
4. **Open reflux** (air) for 2 h, so oxidative chemistry is possible. Atmosphere not controlled.
5. **Mechanism is hypothetical** (the authors' word, Fig. 5 caption). The FFA model reaction shows FFA
   is *sufficient*, not that it is the route in the xylose pot.
6. The printed 13 % synthesis yield does not follow from the printed masses (7.7 % [Z]). This is
   immaterial to the Maillard result but is a data-hygiene flag.
7. Sensory data are preliminary (n = 7, "data not shown").
