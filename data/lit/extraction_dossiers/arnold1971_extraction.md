# Dwivedi & Arnold 1971 — EXTRACTION
### *"Hydrogen Sulfide from Heat Degradation of Thiamine"*
### At pH 7 and ~100 °C, 33–44 % of the thiamine that degrades in 30 min leaves as H2S; at pH 6.5 a few per cent; at pH ≤ 6 essentially none. Cysteine stops thiamine degrading; oxidants destroy it without H2S. One temperature for the kinetics, plus one autoclave series. Read 2026-10-27.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Thiamine hydrochloride (0.04 mg/mL, 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ M) in 0.1 M phosphate, heated 5–30 min in boiling
water (timed from 96 °C) or autoclaved at 120 °C, loses **≤ 2 % below pH 6 and ~14–17 % at pH 7**
in 30 min at ~100 °C, and **~51 % at pH 7, 120 °C** (Figs. 2–3). H2S follows the pH much more steeply
than thiamine loss: at ~100 °C, 30 min, **≈ 0 at pH 5.5, ≤ 3 at pH 6.0, 3–7 at pH 6.5 and 47–71 mmol
H2S per mol thiamine at pH 7.0** (Fig. 4). At pH 7 Table I puts **up to ~40 %** of the sulfur of the
*degraded* thiamine into H2S (≤ 25 % at the shorter times). At 120 °C, pH 7, H2S reaches ~78 mmol/mol
in 5 min and then **plateaus at ~88 mmol/mol** while thiamine keeps disappearing. Air, nitrogen and
sealed tubes differ little. **Ferricyanide or permanganate (1 mg/mL) destroy all thiamine in 5 min
with no H2S detected; cysteine (1 mg/mL) stops thiamine degradation completely for 30 min at pH 5–7,
and all H2S then comes from cysteine.** Thiamine added to commercial chicken broth (N2 purge, even at
pH 6.5) did not measurably raise H2S. The authors conclude thiamine is not a major H2S source in
heated foods.

## 4. DERIVED QUANTITIES [Z] (mine, not printed)

**Branching fraction f = H2S formed / thiamine degraded** (Fig. 4 ÷ Fig. 2, 30 min, ~100 °C):

| pH | open air | sealed tube | N2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.0 | ~0.2 (both terms ≤ 3 %, meaningless) | ~0.3 (same) | ~0.1 (same) |
| 6.5 | 0.050 | 0.026 | 0.031 |
| 7.0 | 0.41 | 0.34 | 0.44 |

f rises with time at pH 7 (Table I: 17–28 % at 5 min, 33–44 % at 30 min) while thiamine loss
decelerates. **H2S therefore behaves like a product of a slower secondary step, not a fixed-fraction
parallel channel.**

**Apparent first-order constants, ~100 °C** (k_loss = −ln(1 − loss)/30 min; k_THI→H2S = f · k_loss,
which assumes a parallel first-order channel):

| pH | k_loss (min⁻¹) | k_THI→H2S (min⁻¹) | k_THI→H2S (s⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7.0 | 5.0–5.8 × 10⁻³ | **1.7–2.6 × 10⁻³** | 2.9–4.3 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 6.5 | 4.6–5.5 × 10⁻³ | **1.2–2.6 × 10⁻⁴** | 2.0–4.3 × 10⁻⁶ |
| ≤ 6.0 | ≤ 6 × 10⁻⁴ | ≤ ~3 × 10⁻⁵ (floor of the figure) | ≤ 5 × 10⁻⁷ |

pH slope of k_THI→H2S between 6.5 and 7.0: **×8.5–15 per 0.5 unit, i.e. d log k/d pH ≈ 1.9–2.4**,
steeper than first order in OH⁻. The pH 6.5 points sit within ~1–2 mmol/mol of zero on the figure,
so the slope is uncertain by roughly a factor of two.

**Temperature (two points only, 120 °C autoclave vs ~100 °C sealed tube, pH 7):**
- thiamine loss, 30-min end point: k120/k100 = 0.0238/0.0050 = 4.7 → **Ea ≈ 78–95 kJ/mol**, for a
  bath temperature of 96–100 °C;
- H2S initial rate, 5-min point: 78.5/8.8 = 8.9 → **Ea ≈ 110–133 kJ/mol** (same temperature span).

Both are two-point estimates, and the second has opposite-signed biases that do not cancel. The
120 °C H2S is already near its plateau at 5 min, which understates the rate. The autoclave come-up
time is not counted, which overstates it. **Neither number is defensible as a prior.**

## 5. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**The model's need.** The sulfur lane makes H2S only from cysteine (`k_cys_h2s`, Zheng & Ho 1994,
pH-resolved first order) and from glutathione. Thiamine (THI) feeds MFT and MeSH through
`r_thi_hmp`/`r_thi_mesh` but releases no H2S, so a pentose + thiamine pot cannot form
2-furfurylthiol. The question is whether a measured `THI -> H2S` step can be built.

**Verdict: (b), a yield/branching fraction at one temperature, with pH dependence. Not (a).**
- **(b) What is usable:** at ~100 °C, 0.1 M phosphate, 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ M thiamine, 30 min, a yield of
  **47–71 mmol H2S/mol thiamine at pH 7.0** (Fig. 4/5, [F]; Table I [M] 47.9–60.9 after the time
  correction), **3–7 at pH 6.5, ≤ 3 at pH 6.0, ~0 at pH 5.5**. Per mol degraded thiamine, f ≈
  **0.34–0.44 at pH 7, 0.03–0.05 at pH 6.5**. This can be declared as a branching fraction of total
  thiamine loss: `k_thi_h2s = f(pH) × k_THI,total`. **Assumptions it needs:** (i) a measured total
  thiamine loss rate from elsewhere (this paper has only ~100 °C and one 120 °C series); (ii) f is
  time-independent, which Table I contradicts (f grows from ~0.2 to ~0.4 over 30 min); (iii) f
  transfers from 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ M thiamine to the mM loadings of flavour pots (no concentration series
  here); (iv) the cold-measured pH is the pH that matters.
- **(a) is not reachable:** one temperature plus an autoclave series whose H2S plateaus within
  5 min. My [Z] Ea values are two-point estimates with uncounted come-up time and should not be
  promoted.
- **(c) What is qualitative only:** oxidants abolish H2S while destroying thiamine; cysteine
  protects thiamine; atmosphere barely matters; in real chicken broth the thiamine contribution was
  undetectable.

**Contradictions with the current model:**
1. **"THI releases no H2S" is wrong at pH ≥ 6.5 and roughly right at pH ≤ 6.** At pH 7 a third to a
   half of the degraded thiamine's sulfur leaves as H2S. Currently `r_thi_hmp` and `r_thi_mesh` send
   all of it to HMP or MeSH. At the pH of a plant-based patty (~6) the omission is small (f and
   loss both near zero at 100 °C, 30 min), but at 120 °C, pH 6.0, Fig. 4 shows 22.5 mmol/mol, which
   is not small.
2. **Cysteine protects thiamine completely** (pH 5–7, 30 min, 100 °C, ~70:1 Cys:THI). A mass-action
   model with first-order THI decay independent of cysteine will over-degrade thiamine in Cys + THI
   pots. The protective ratio threshold is unknown; this is a 70-fold excess.
3. **H2S at 120 °C plateaus while thiamine still degrades.** That is consistent with H2S being
   consumed by thiamine degradation products in a closed tube, i.e. the THI + H2S cross-chemistry
   the model routes through HMP/norfuraneol. A `THI -> H2S` step would need its H2S sinks in place
   to reproduce this.
4. **Oxidising conditions** destroy thiamine with no H2S. The model has no oxidant term; an open,
   aerated sear is not oxidising in this sense (open air ≈ N2 here).
5. **pH sensitivity of thiamine loss itself:** negligible below pH 6 at ~100 °C for 30 min. Check
   that the declared `k_thi_hmp`/`k_thi_mesh` pH dependence is not more permissive at pH 5–6.

**Not usable:** the food-extrapolation H2S (40 ppb) is a worked example, not a measurement; the
broth experiment prints no numbers.
