## 0. Claims in brief (cols 123-126, PDF pp. 79-80)

Three independent claims (1, 14, 27), all methods, all on the same composition: a **meat replica
matrix** comprising one or more plant proteins, a sugar selected from glucose, ribose, sucrose,
fructose, xylose, maltodextrin and combinations, and at least one sulfur compound selected from
methionine, cysteine and thiamine, into which **0.01 %-5 % (by weight of the meat replica matrix) of a
non-animal heme-containing protein** is added (claim 1, "imparting a beef-like aroma") or incorporated
(claims 14 and 27, "making a meat replica matrix having a beef-like aroma"), such that on cooking "at
least two volatile compounds are generated that are associated with a beef-like aroma". Claim 14 adds the
step "obtaining a non-animal heme-containing protein".

Dependent claims, per independent claim: heme protein with at least 70 % homology to SEQ ID NO: 1 (2, 15,
28); UV-VIS spectrum substantially similar to animal myoglobin (3, 16, 29); heme protein chosen from
leghemoglobin, flavohemoglobin, Hell's gate globin I, erythrocruorin, protoglobin, cyanoglobin,
chlorocruorin, truncated hemoglobin, truncated 2/2 globin, hemoglobin (4, 17, 30); **0.4 %-1 %** heme
protein (5, 18, 31); purified heme protein (6, 24); isolated / purified plant protein (7-8, 19-20);
plant protein from rubisco, pea, lentil, other legume (9, 21, 32), pea albumins (10, 22); plant protein
1-30 % of the matrix (11, 23); plant protein soluble at >= 25 g/L at 2-32 °C, pH 3-8, NaCl 0-300 mM (12,
25); the matrix inside a taco filling, casserole, sauce, topping, soup, stew or loaf, generating the
volatiles "in substantially higher amounts" than the product without the matrix (13, 26, 33).

Against US 9,943,096 claim 1 (a flavour additive: heme protein + one of ten sugars + cysteine, cystine,
thiamine or methionine) and EP 2 943 072 B2 claim 1 (heme protein + cysteine + thiamine + glucose or
ribose): this patent claims the **replica matrix with a heme dose window**; its sugar list adds sucrose
and xylose and drops lactose, arabinose, galactose, maltose and glucose-6-phosphate; its sulfur list
drops cystine; no volatile is named in any claim.

## 1. Examples with a heated reaction

The specification has 37 examples. Most are protein isolation (Ex. 1), tissue-replica construction
(Ex. 2-14, 20-32: heat-set gels at 90-100 °C, extrusion at up to 130 °C, autoclaving at 121 °C) or
fat-release / firmness tests (Ex. 3-13, 35-37: pan ramps to 150 °C), with no volatile or sulfur
measurement. Example 34 heats pea vicilin with coconut oil at 95 °C for 15 min to test antioxidants and
lipoxygenase removal (off-flavours: GC-MS "5x reduction in overall volatiles", ">2x reduction",
C6-C7 aldehydes; Table 11 sensory descriptors only; no sulfur). The four that bear on the sulfur lane:

### Example 16 (cols 71-73, PDF pp. 53-54): tissue-replica particle size, sealed vials, 150 °C / 10 min

- **Materials.** Muscle replica as in Example 2 (col 65-66): moong bean 8S globulin and leghemoglobin,
  concentrated to "moong bean 8S globulin 61 mg/ml, and of leghemoglobin 6.5 mg/ml", cross-linked
  overnight with ~400 mg transglutaminase, final total protein 67.5 mg/ml, in 20 mM phosphate pH 7.3-7.4
  with 400 mM NaCl. Adipose replica as in Example 7 (col 67): 70 % v/v fat mixture (cocoa butter, coconut
  butter, olive oil, palm oil 1:1:1:1) emulsified with 4.2 % wt/v moong bean 8S protein and 0.4 % wt/v
  soybean lecithin, set by transglutaminase or by heating to 100 °C. Connective replica as in Example 14
  (electrospun moong bean globulin fibres). **No sugar, cysteine, thiamine or nucleotide is added**; the
  only "precursors" are the proteins, lipids and whatever the protein preparations carry.
- **Arms.** Lean:fat 85/15: (a) 2.1 g muscle + 0.9 g fat chunks 5-10 mm ("coarse mix"); (b) 2.1 g muscle
  chopped to 2-3 mm + 0.9 g fat ("fine mix"); (c) 2.1 g muscle + 0.9 g fat blended to < 1 mm ("blend");
  "muscle only" 3 g; "fat only" 3 g. Beef counterpart: 80/20 ground beef as "fine size particle mix" and
  liquid-nitrogen-powdered "very fine size particle mix" (< 1 mm), muscle-only and fat-only 3 g.
- **Heating.** "cooked in sealed glass vials at 150° C. for 10 min" (replicas and beef alike). pH of the
  cooked mixtures not stated (the replicas are built in pH 7.3-7.4 buffers).
- **Measurement.** Sensory panel (size not printed) and GC-MS "as described in Example 1" [sic: Example 1
  is protein isolation and describes no GC-MS; the cross-reference is broken]. No table; results in
  prose.

### Example 17 (cols 73-74, PDF p. 54; Table 2): heme protein added to ground chicken

Ground chicken (90 % lean, 10 % fat), strained through cheesecloth, mixed with recombinant soy
leghemoglobin or recombinant bovine myoglobin (both expressed in *E. coli*, nickel-affinity purified,
reduced with 20 mM sodium dithionite, desalted) "to a final concentration of 0.5-1.0% wt/wt";
leghemoglobin in KP (20 mM potassium phosphate pH 7.4, 100 mM NaCl), myoglobin in KP or NC (20 mM sodium
citrate pH 6.0, 100 mM NaCl); half of each heme sample bubbled with CO for 2 min. Formed into nuggets,
held overnight at 4 °C, "oven baked or pan fried at 165° C. until each nugget reached an internal
temperature of 165° C." [sic: an internal 165 °C is not reachable in a moist nugget; probably °F, not
stated]. Sensory only; no GC-MS reported for this example.

### Example 27 (col 80, PDF p. 57; Table 5): the flavor precursor mix

"A flavor precursor mix was prepared by mixing concentrated stock solutions of each additive to make a
17x solution. Table 5 contains the chemical composition of the mix and the mM concentration of each
component in the final burger. The concentrated flavor precursor mix was sterile filtered and adjusted to
pH 5.5-6.0 using NaOH, and used at a 1x concentration in burgers." Used in Example 21 (raw tissue
replica, Table 4: 6.2 % of the "17x" mix, with 26.7 % "heme protein in buffer" at 55 mg/g, so about 1.5 %
heme protein in that replica by arithmetic, mine) and Example 31 (leghemoglobin reconstituted in the 17x
mix at pH 6.0, 10 % of the burger, Table 6). No heating or measurement in Example 27 itself.

### Example 33 (cols 84-85, PDF pp. 59-60; Tables 8, 9, 10): heme + precursors in replica burgers

"Flavor precursors along with 1% Leghemoglobin were added to the muscle component of the burger replicas
as indicated in Table 8. Three replicas, one with no precursors, and two different mixtures of
precursors, along with 80:20 beef were cooked then served to a trained sensory panel". Burger
formulation is that of Examples 31-32 by context (not stated which). Basis of "1 %" not stated (the
claims use w/w of the matrix). Precursor concentrations in Table 8 are labelled "(mm)" [sic, mM];
whether they refer to the muscle component or the whole burger is not stated (Example 27 says the
Table 5 mM are "in the final burger"). pH not stated for Example 33 (Example 27 adjusts the concentrate
to 5.5-6.0; Example 32 adjusts the raw muscle to 5.8 with citric acid). Cooking method for the sensory
samples not stated.

GC-MS: "The replicas and beef sample also were analyzed by GCMS by adding 3 grams of uncooked replica or
beef into a GCMS vial. All samples were cooked at 150° C. for 3 mins, cooled to 50° C. to extract for 12
minutes using GCMS (SPME fiber sampling of headspace). A search algorithm analyzed the retention time and
mass fingerprint information to assign chemical names to peaks." This is the protocol of US 9,943,096
Example 11 word for word in substance. **Only the precursor-mix-2 replica's list is printed (Table 10),
and only those of its compounds also identified in beef.** No GC-MS list for the no-precursor replica,
for mix 1, for a heme-free replica, or for beef as such.

## 3. Findings that matter for the sulfur lane

**(a) Example 33, Table 10: what the heme + precursor replica forms.** With 1 % LegH and mix 2 (cysteine
0.83, methionine 0.67, thiamine 0.50, glucose 5.55, ribose 6.66, sucrose/fructose/xylose 2.00 mM each,
maltodextrin 0.5 %), 150 °C for 3 min in a closed SPME vial, the beef-shared peaks include ten sulfur
compounds: two thiazoles plus 2-acetylthiazole, two thiophenes, methyl-thiirane, methional and three
small sulfides. **No 2-methyl-3-furanthiol, no 2-furfurylthiol, no bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide.**
Because the table lists only compounds "also identified by GCMS in the beef samples", the absence of
MFT says only that MFT was not identified in both; it does not say the replica made none. The same
protocol on essentially the same precursor set in buffer (US 9,943,096 Table 14, Magic Mix + LegH) did
list MFT and its disulfide at class "M", so either the burger matrix, the 3 g solid sample, or the beef
filter removed them. 4-Methyl-5-thiazoleethanol (a thiamine marker in the family's Example 5) is present;
4-methylthiazole is not.

**(b) Example 16: particle size, no added precursors.** Heme-containing muscle replica (LegH 6.5 mg/ml),
fat replica and connective replica, 150 °C / 10 min in sealed vials. Text (col 72): "Analysis of GCMS
data indicated that the size of individual tissue units and the extent of their mixing within meat
tissue replicas had profound effects on the generation of aromatic compounds upon cooking." Compounds
that "appeared in mixed meat replicas, but not in individual tissue replicas" include 2-pentyl-furan,
**4-methylthiazole** ("nutty/green"), ethyl pyrazine, 2,3-dimethylpyrazine, acetic acid,
5-methyl-2-furancarboxaldehyde, butyrolactone, 2,5-dimethyl-3-(3-methylbutyl)pyrazine,
2-hydroxy-3-methyl-2-cyclopentene-1-one, 3-acetyl-1H-pyrroline, pantolactone,
1-methyl-1H-pyrrole-2-carboxaldehyde, caprolactam, 2,3-dihydro-3,5-dihydroxy-6-methyl-4H-pyran-4-one;
compounds only in individual replicas: 2,6-dimethylnonane, 3-methyl-3-hexene, pyridine, acetoin,
octanal, 1-hydroxy-2-propanone, ethenyl pyrazine. "the levels to which all of the above compounds
accumulated during cooking depended on the sizes of tissue units and how they are mixed". Sensory:
muscle replica alone "store-bought gravy, faint citrus and star anise"; adipose alone "musty, rancid and
sweet"; all but the adipose replica "burnt meat smell". Beef (col 73): the compounds that differed
between fine and very fine particle sizes include 4-methylthiazole in muscle and in fat. No levels, no
peak areas, no replicate count. 4-Methylthiazole here forms with no thiamine added (the family links
it to thiamine); its precursor in this system is not identified in the patent.

**(c) The effect of heme.** The only heme-versus-no-heme comparisons are sensory: Table 2 (chicken
nuggets, beefiness from 1-1.5 in buffer controls to 4-4.5 at 1 % heme protein, oven baked; 0.5 % gives
1.5-2.5; no spread, no n) and the description passage below. Example 33 has no heme-free arm; Example 16
has no heme-free muscle replica. Description (col 52, PDF p. 43; the same paragraphs as US 9,943,096 and
EP [0052]-[0053]): heme proteins (*A. aeolicus*, *M. infernorum*, *Glycine max*, *Hordeum vulgare*,
*Vigna radiata*) heated with cysteine and glucose "provide a significantly different profile of volatile
odorants ... than any subset of the three components when analysed by GC-MS", with increases in "furan,
acetone, thiazole, furfural, benzaldehyde, 2-pyridinecarboxaldehyde, 5-methyl-2-thiophenecarboxaldehyde,
3-methyl-2-thiophenecarboxaldehyde, 3-thiophenmethanol and decanol"; and "cysteine and glucose alone or
in the presence of iron salts such as ferrous glucanate produced a sulfurous, odor but addition of heme
proteins reduced the sulfurous odor". No conditions, concentrations or table in this patent (the family
points to its Tables 8 and 9 for these). The ferrous gluconate remark is the only non-heme iron control
in the whole family, and it is one sentence.

**(d) The effect of each precursor.** Not resolvable. Example 33 omits all precursors at once (804) and
adds three sugars at once (929 vs 767); the GC-MS covers only 929. Sensory: beefy 2.3 (none) / 3.3
(mix 1) / 4.2 (mix 2), SD 0.8-1.2, n not printed. No cysteine-, thiamine- or ribose-omission arm, no
temperature or time series.

**(e) MFT, FFT, H2S.** None is reported anywhere in this patent's data (Tables 2, 9, 10; Examples 16-17,

## 5. What it means for the model and for formulation

**For the engine.** Nothing is simulable: no concentration of any product, one temperature per
experiment (150 °C for 3 or 10 min), a solid plant-protein-fat matrix with heme, which the engine does
not model (no heme or iron catalysis; see `us9943096_extraction.md` section 3(c)). The one usable
qualitative point is negative and weak: in a heme replica burger with beef-level cysteine (0.83 mM),
thiamine (0.50 mM) and ribose (6.66 mM), MFT is not among the 135 beef-shared peaks after 3 min at
150 °C, whereas thiazoles, thiophenes and small sulfides are. That is the direction the engine predicts
for millimolar-or-less cysteine and minutes of heating (little MFT formed), but the beef filter on the
list means the patent cannot be scored against it.

**For formulation.** The claimed recipe is a plant-protein matrix with 0.4-1 % heme protein (0.01-5 %
broadly) plus a sugar and a sulfur amino acid or thiamine; the working example uses a beef-mimicking
precursor mix at 0.5-7 mM per component, with sucrose, fructose and xylose (2 mM each) as the step that
raised beefy scores from 3.3 to 4.2 (n and test not printed). Example 16's result that the volatile
profile depends on how finely fat and lean replicas are mixed is the one finding here with no
counterpart in the family; it is prose only.

## What it does not give

- No quantification: no ng/g, no peak areas, no classes, no internal standard; one presence list.
- No MFT, FFT, furyl disulfides or H2S in any data; no thiol of any kind.
- No heme-free arm in any GC-MS experiment; no equal-protein heme-free control anywhere.
- No precursor-by-precursor omission; no temperature or time series.
- No GC column, SPME fibre, panel size, replicate count or statistics; the GC-MS method reference in
  Example 16 points to an example that has none.
- No pH for Examples 16 and 33 cooking; Example 17's "internal temperature of 165° C." is as printed.
