# Dwivedi, Arnold & Libbey 1972 — EXTRACTION
### *"Chemistry of Thiamine Degradation: 4-Methyl-5-(β-Hydroxyethyl) Thiazole from Thermally Degraded Thiamine"*
### Identifies the intact thiazole as a major sulfur product of heated thiamine at pH 5–7 (TLC, IR, UV, NMR, MS). A structure proof with no yields, no kinetics and no H2S. Qualitative support for the "thiazole keeps the sulfur at pH ≤ 6" reading. Read 2026-10-27.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

USP thiamine HCl at **10 mg/mL** ([Z] 29.6 mM), brought to **pH 5.0, 6.0 and 7.0** with 1 N NaOH or
0.1 M phosphate, was heated 30 min in sealed tubes in boiling water, 30 min in open tubes in
boiling water, or 30 min sealed in the autoclave at **121 °C**. TLC showed similar patterns under
all treatments and pH values, "although the relative proportions of degradation products changed"
(not quantified). One very intense spot (F) was isolated (ether extraction, Sephadex G-10, GLC) and
proved to be **4-methyl-5-(β-hydroxyethyl)thiazole** by TLC Rf, GLC, UV, IR, ¹H NMR and MS (M⁺ 143)
against an authentic Merck sample. The authors conclude it "appears to be a major degradation
product of thiamine heated under **slightly acidic conditions**". **No yield, no rate, no pH series
in numbers, no H2S measurement, no mention of 2-methyl-3-furanthiol.**

### 3.2 Identification of F [M], printed values only

| method | printed result |
|---|---|
| Sephadex / GLC | single TLC spot, single GLC peak; IR of GLC-trapped effluent identical to authentic (no rearrangement on column) |
| UV | in water agrees with Makino & Imai 1936; in CCl4 the maximum shifts **~10 nm** to shorter wavelength, extinction unchanged (solvent effect). λmax not printed |
| IR | thin film resembles Neal 1968; in CCl4 the broad OH near **3300 cm⁻¹** decreases, C–O stretch moves **1040 → 1105 cm⁻¹**, two new peaks at **1400–1500 cm⁻¹**; interpreted as an internally hydrogen-bonded (O–H···S) form |
| ¹H NMR (CDCl3), printed on Fig. 4 | **δ 8.36** (C2-H), **2.84** (ring-CH2), **3.65** (CH2-O), **4.50** (OH), **2.22** (CH3); "not reported previously" |
| MS (Fig. 5) [F, ±3 % relative intensity] | **M⁺ 143** (~40 %), base peak **112** (100 %), 45 (~47 %), 85 (~40 %), 59 (~18 %), 125 (~10 %), minor 51, 53, 58, 69, 71; agrees with Shima et al. 1969; TMS derivative matches Amos & Neal 1970 |
| Odour | pure compound: viscous oil, **not** the pungent odour Morfee & Liska reported for their "compound D" (NaOH-adjusted pots); the phosphate-buffer fraction F **was** pungent, from an inseparable trace contaminant also present in the Merck standard |

## 4. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**Verdict: (c), qualitative only.** No yield, rate, Ea or pH-resolved number.

- **(a) Confirm/refine/contradict B50's H2S shares:** none of the three. H2S was not measured. The
  only bearing is indirect. The thiazole, which keeps thiamine's sulfur, is a **major** product at
  pH 5–7 under all three heatings, "slightly acidic conditions" being where it dominates. That is
  consistent with B50's zero share below pH 6.25. The ³⁵S balance in `dwivedi1972_extraction.md`
  makes it quantitative (≥ 96 % of degraded thiamine's S as thiazole at pH ≤ 6, 121 °C).
- **(b) pH basis:** 25 °C labels, adjusted with NaOH or phosphate; at 29.6 mM thiamine HCl the
  buffer pH may have drifted on heating. No in-situ value.
- **(c) Temperature / kinetics:** none. The three heatings (~100 °C sealed, ~100 °C open, 121 °C)
  are reported only as giving "similar patterns".
- **(d) Thiamine → MFT:** nothing. MFT is not mentioned; volatile S products are not isolated here.
- **Model consequence (same as the companion dossier):** the model has no THI → thiazole +
  pyrimidine channel and no thiazole species. This paper establishes that the channel exists and
  is major at slightly acidic pH. `dwivedi1972_extraction.md` gives its share and rate at 121 °C.
- **Identity data** (NMR shifts, M⁺ 143 / base 112) are useful only if a thiazole observable is ever
  scored from GC–MS data.
