**Answers, weighed.**
1. **This is a release-kinetics paper at 50 °C, not an equilibrium paper.** The observable is the appearance of free
   sugar, normalised to complete release (C/C_inf, C_inf = the theoretical amount). Its tau_1/2 mixes the ring's
   dissociation with re-formation and with any cysteine sink (oxidation to cystine, decomposition), which the authors
   say all act. My confidence that no K for any ring can be recovered from it: ~0.9.
2. **The pentose ring's sugar release is pH-flat between 3.16 and 4.99 at 50 °C** (28.9 vs 29.1 h). The hexose
   rings speed up x1.5-1.7 over the same step. Unbuffered IIIa is faster (19 h at initial pH 3.99), so the buffered
   values also carry a buffer-identity effect (acetate vs phosphate, concentrations not printed) or an unbuffered
   drift effect; the paper cannot separate them.
3. **Release stops in base** (hexose ring, pH 9.85, 50 °C, > 8.4 h). This agrees with the direction of the
   analogues the M1c case cites against R-rel (Karpinski, Nagasawa, Bustillo Trueba): release not favoured above
   pH ~6. It is a hexose ring and one qualitative point.

- 50 % times: runs 1-5 = 2.43 / 2.01 / 1.90 / 1.83 / 2.82 h; runs 6-8 do not reach 0.5 by 185 min (extrapolated
  3.99 / 3.39 / 3.24 h).
- Mean +- SD: runs 1-5 **2.20 +- 0.42 h**; all eight with extrapolation **2.70 +- 0.79 h**. The printed **2.5 +- 0.7 h**
  lies between, nearer the eight-run value, so the printed mean likely includes extrapolated runs; the method is not
  stated.
- The runs split into a fast group (1-4, release visible at 35 min) and a lagged group (5-8, nothing until 75-125
  min). A lag of 35-95 min at 50 °C is not first-order kinetics; candidate causes are heat-up of the sample, the
  assay floor, or a real induction. The paper does not discuss it.
- Release continues past 0.85 of the theoretical amount at ~35 mmol/L in 3 h, while the cysteine is oxidised in
  parallel [Q]. If free cysteine equalled released glucose (no cysteine loss), the glucose ring's
  K(50 °C, pH ~3) <= 0.15 / (0.85^2 x 35.3 mmol/L) = **0.006 L/mmol**. Cysteine loss makes the true free cysteine
  smaller, so this is NOT a bound; it is shown only to say the paper cannot give K. Hexose, not pentose.

### Text results [Q]
- Stability order (summary): IIIa >> IIId > IIIc > IIIb; in buffer the order "undergoes marked levelling out", but
  the hexose / arabinose gap remains.
- Polarography, 25 °C, N2: IIIa decomposes rapidly in ammonia buffer pH 9.3 and at pH 13-14 ("complete ... less than
  30 min", judged by the cysteine anodic wave). VII (2,2-dimethyl) gives the cysteine wave instantly at every pH
  1-13. IV and V: no wave at pH 1; the wave appears after ~30 min at pH 13-14 [T]. VI (N-acetyl) never gives it.
- The authors conclude an equilibrium I + II <-> III "in acidic aqueous solutions" that sets the aldehyde-form
  concentration of the sugar, disturbed by decomposition of the sulfur compounds [P].

## 6. Conflicts with existing records

1. **Karpinski 1990 (`karpinski1990_extraction.md`), the same L-arabinose ring (AThz).** Karpinski: cysteine
   release 1.8e-4 /min at pH 6.0 and 4.8e-5 /min at pH 7.4, 25 °C, argon, voltammetry. Belikov: sugar release
   4.0e-4 /min at pH 3.16 and 4.99, 50 °C [Z]. Taken at face value the two give an apparent Ea of ~25 kJ/mol over
   25 -> 50 °C (pH 6.0 vs 4.99), or ~45 kJ/mol if Karpinski's PrThz pH shape (k(4.5) / k(6.0) = 0.29) is used to move
   AThz to pH ~5 [Z]. Different observables (free sugar vs free cysteine), buffers, atmosphere; not a conflict of
   kind, recorded for whoever sets a release band.
2. **CF-076's declared release band** (conflicts register: k_r(100 °C, pH 4.5) in [2e-4, 8e-4] /min; the 2026-11-01
   run's feasible k_r(100 °C, pH 4.5) 0.9-3.2e-4 /min). Belikov's pentose ring at **50 °C**, pH 4.99, releases sugar
   at an apparent 4.0e-4 /min, which already equals or exceeds the feasible 100 °C range; any positive activation
   energy puts the 100 °C value above it. **Caveat:** Belikov's quantity is net sugar appearance in a dilute
   (39.5 mmol/L) pot with a cysteine sink, from a different pentose (L-arabinose), and k_r in CF-076 is defined in
   Kallen's bookkeeping; whether the two are the same quantity is not established. My P that this is a real
   conflict of kind once definitions are aligned: ~0.5. Not entered in the register (this branch does not edit
   `docs/reference/`).
3. **Polarography vs paper chromatography inside this paper.** IIIa "rapid decomposition" at pH 9.3 / 13-14
   (25 °C, cysteine wave) against "extremely slowly ... cannot be measured" for IIIb at pH 9.85, 50 °C (free
   glucose). Both can hold if base opens the ring to a thiol-bearing open-chain form (a Schiff base gives a cysteine-
   like wave) without freeing the sugar [Z, P ~0.4]; or the ammonia buffer transiminates. The paper does not
   resolve it.
4. **"No appreciable amounts of compounds (III)" in boiling water** [Q] against Zhang 2025b
   (`zhang2025b_extraction.md`: TTCA ~18 mmol/L at 40 + 40 mmol/L, 120 °C, LC-MS/MS) and Cao 2017
   (`cao2017_extraction.md`: TTCA 0.356 mol/L at 0.4 + 0.4 mol/L, 90 °C). Belikov gives no time, concentration or
   floor for that statement; paper chromatography with an effective floor of a few mmol/L [Z] could miss a minor
   pool. The modern measured records govern; the sentence is noted, not used.
5. **Ring-N pKa.** Belikov quotes 5.2-5.7 for sugar thiazolidines (Weitzel 1959). `radomski1989_extraction.md`
   infers 5.2-6.1 for TTCA from solution pH. Consistent; Belikov adds no measurement.

## 7. Translation ambiguities [T]

1. "condensation of hydrocarbons (I)" (p. 34): "hydrocarbons" for carbohydrates (Russian *uglevodorody* vs
   *uglevody*); context fixes it as carbohydrates. No effect on values.
2. "some of which are stable at ~20 °C or above, are rapidly heated" (p. 34): garbled clause; probably "stable at
   ~20 °C, decompose when heated rapidly". It does not say which compounds or to what temperature.
3. "the gradual increase of the constant of carbohydrates (I)" (p. 35): "constant" for content / concentration.
4. "half-period" throughout = half-life (*period poluraspada*); used here as tau_1/2.
5. "To evaluate the influence of the carbohydrate residue on the ease of the reactions were determined at constant
   pH values" (p. 34): a word is missing; read as "the half-lives were determined at constant pH in buffers".
6. "pH 13-14 ... It appeared after about 30 min at pH 13-14" (p. 35, compounds IV and V): the sentence reads that
   the wave was absent at pH 1 and appeared after ~30 min at pH 13-14; the "this was not the case ... at pH 1"
   construction is ambiguous.
7. Refs. **12 and 13** are cited (Table 1 last column; p. 35) but the printed list ends at ref. 11. The source of the
   aldehyde-form percentages cannot be checked. Values of that era came from polarography and are known to differ
   from later NMR values; not used here.
8. E_1/2 of the cysteine anodic wave printed "0.45 V" with no sign or reference electrode beyond "calomel".
9. Summary 1 writes "IIIa >> IIId > IIIc > IIIb" (stability) and the text writes "IIIa << IIId < IIIc < IIIb" (rate):
   consistent (inverse orders), not a conflict.
10. Whether the +- values in Table 1 are SDs is not stated in the translation.

## M1c case criterion (frozen bd641ae8)

The frozen case (`docs/reference/cases/M1c_thiol_supply_structure.md` on `origin/m1c-case`, sec. 4.3) names belikov1967
**decisive only if at process temperature (>= 90 °C)**, on: *K (or ring : open ratio) for a cysteine-PENTOSE ring at
>= 2 pH or >= 2 temperatures, >= 90 °C*; at 25-60 °C *a same-class band for K(pH) / K(T), by amendment*.

**Verdict: NOT MET.**
- **K or ring : open ratio: none printed** for any ring, pentose or hexose. The only percentages in Table 1 are the
  free sugars' aldehyde-form shares, cited from an unlisted ref. 12.
- **Temperature: 50 +- 0.5 °C only.** No measurement at >= 90 °C; the 70 °C rate was "difficult to measure" and
  boiling-water statements are qualitative.
- **pH: the pentose ring (IIIa, L-arabinose) has two buffered pH values (3.16, 4.99) and one unbuffered (initial
  3.99)**, but the quantity is a sugar-release half-life (28.9 / 29.1 / 19 +- 3.0 h), not K.
- **The fallback band role (K(pH) / K(T) at 25-60 °C) is also not filled**, because no K is printed at any
  temperature.

What the paper does hold, outside the case's criterion: the pentose ring's sugar release at 50 °C is pH-flat between
3.16 and 4.99 (k ratio 0.99 [Z]), and hexose-ring release is unmeasurable at pH 9.85 in 8.4 h. Under the case's rule
("a band source enters only through an amendment registered before any engine result of M1c exists"), these could
enter R-rel's n_r band only by such an amendment; this dossier does not propose one.
