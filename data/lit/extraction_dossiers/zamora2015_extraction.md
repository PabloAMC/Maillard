## 1. Why it matters

The lipid-Maillard programme (roadmap 5d) needs the split between the Strecker aldehyde and the
amine when a lipid carbonyl, not a sugar dicarbonyl, degrades the amino acid. This paper is the
corpus's only systematic table of that split: eleven lipid carbonyls (hydroperoxides, alkenal,
alkadienals, epoxyalkenals, oxoalkenals, hydroxyalkenal) at two pH values under nitrogen and under
air, all at one loading (10 µmol each) and one heat (140 C, 1 h), with PAC, PEA and BZA (the
oxidation product of PAC) each quantified against matrix-matched curves. It also prints four
apparent barriers for the 2,4-decadienal system (PEA and PAC at pH 3 and pH 6 under nitrogen,
100-170 C), the first amine-formation barriers in the corpus. For Programme 6 the value is the
mechanistic bracket: the same azomethine ylide gives aldehyde (oxidative branch) or amine
(non-oxidative branch), and the table quantifies how oxygen, pH and the carbonyl's substituents move
the branch ratio.

## 2. Methods as they matter to a model

- **Reaction mixtures (verbatim).** "mixtures of phenylalanine and the lipid derivative (10 µmol of
  each) were singly homogenized with 50-70 mesh sand (600 mg) (Aldrich), 30 µL of 0.3 M buffer, and
  80 µL of water. Samples were heated under controlled atmosphere in closed test tubes at the
  indicated times and temperatures, usually 1 h at 140 ºC." So the aqueous phase is 110 µL on 600 mg
  sand: a low-moisture slurry. Nominal concentrations if everything sits in the 110 µL: **10 µmol /
  0.110 mL = 90.9 mmol/L** phenylalanine and 90.9 mmol/L carbonyl; buffer 0.3 M × 30/110 = **81.8
  mmol/L** (sodium citrate at pH 3, sodium phosphate at pH 6). These are nominal: the lipid carbonyls
  are poorly water-soluble, so the effective phase is undefined. Atmosphere: nitrogen or air (Table 1);
  0-100 % O2 (Fig. S1/2A). Cooling 5 min room temperature, 15 min at -30 C.
- **Series on 2,4-decadienal (all 1 h, 140 C unless stated).** O2 0-100 %; pH 2.15-9 (buffers not
  itemised; citrate pH 3 and phosphate pH 6 named); DD 0-10 µmol at fixed 10 µmol Phe; water 0-200 µL
  added; time courses at 100, 120, 140, 160 and 170 C under nitrogen at pH 3 and pH 6 (Fig. 4 legend;
  x-axis ticks 10, 20, 30 min).
- **Work-up and quantification.** 20 µL internal standard (24.09 mg 2-ethylpyridine in 50 mL methanol
  → 9.64 µg = 0.0899 µmol per sample) + 1 mL methanol-water 80:20; stir 1 min; centrifuge; 700 µL
  supernatant reduced with 1 mg NaBH4 30 min (PAC → 2-phenylethanol, BZA → benzyl alcohol); 500 µL
  acetone; centrifuge; GC-MS (HP 6890 / 5973, CAM column 30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 µm, 80 C 4 min → 120 C
  at 2 C/min → 220 C at 15 C/min, EI 70 eV, m/z 28-550). Quantifier ions: IS m/z 106; PEA m/z 91; PAC
  (as 2-phenylethanol) m/z 122; BZA (as benzyl alcohol) m/z 108. **Calibration: ten-level standard
  curves (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7.5, 10 µmol) prepared in the 600 mg sand + 80 µL water + 30 µL
  pH 6 phosphate and carried through the whole procedure without heating**; r = 0.998; CV < 8 %.
  Absolute, matrix-matched, but the calibration matrix is pH 6 for all samples including the pH 3
  ones.
- **Replicates / statistics.** "mean ± SD values of, at least, three independent experiments"; ANOVA +
  Tukey, p < 0.05 (Origin 7.0).
- **Kinetic treatment (verbatim).** "The amount of 2-phenylethylamine formed increased linearly (r >
  0.98, p < 0.01) as a function of time at the different assayed temperatures ... [2-phenylethylamine]
  = [2-phenylethylamine]0 + kt ... These rate constants were used in an Arrhenius plot ... The values
  obtained for Ea were 51 kJ/mol at pH 3 and 65 kJ/mol at pH 6." Same for PAC: "74 kJ/mol at pH 3 and
  40 kJ/mol at pH 6." Benzaldehyde "produced to very low extent ... not possible to determine its
  formation Ea." The k values are in Figures S5 and S6 (not on disk); Fig. 3's ln k axis runs -3 to 1
  and the 1/T axis 0.0022-0.0026 K^-1 (111-182 C), consistent with 100-170 C and k of order 0.05-3 in
  the plotted unit (unit not stated; presumably nmol/µmol Phe per min).

### Numbers in the text (Supporting figures not on disk)

- **O2 series (DD, 140 C, 1 h).** At pH 6, > 20 % O2 "completely inhibited" PEA; at pH 3 small amounts
  of PEA persist in 100 % O2; PAC and BZA rise steeply from 0 to 20 % O2 then plateau (PAC) or keep
  rising slowly (BZA). PEA/PAC falls with O2 (Fig. 2A, 0-6 on the y-axis).
- **pH series (DD, 140 C, 1 h; atmosphere not stated in the text; the magnitudes match the nitrogen
  column of Table 1).** PEA rises linearly (r = 0.85) from 2.3 % at pH 2.15 to 2.8 % at pH 9; PAC falls
  linearly (r = -0.998) from 1.6 % at pH 2.15 to 0.4 % at pH 6.5 and is flat above; BZA falls linearly
  (r = -0.79) from 0.28 % at pH 2.15 to 0.06 % at pH 9. PEA/PAC rises linearly with pH over 2.15-9
  (r = 0.94).
- **DD amount series (0-10 µmol at 10 µmol Phe, 140 C, 1 h).** PAC and BZA linear in DD (r > 0.98 at pH
  3, r > 0.94 at pH 6); PEA saturates (large rise by 2 µmol at pH 3, plateau above 4 µmol at pH 6):
  the amine route "is catalytic in nature" (the carbonyl is regenerated), the aldehyde route consumes
  the carbonyl. PEA/PAC decreases linearly with DD (r < -0.94).
- **Water series.** pH 3: both PEA and PAC maximal at 70-80 µL added water, PEA more sensitive to
  excess water; PEA/PAC flat. pH 6: PEA highest at 0-50 µL, PAC rises to 120 µL then flat; PEA/PAC
  "decreased exponentially".
- **Time-temperature (DD, nitrogen, 100/120/140/160/170 C).** PEA and PAC linear in time at each
  temperature (r > 0.98). Ea: PEA 51 (pH 3), 65 (pH 6); PAC 74 (pH 3), 40 (pH 6) kJ/mol. PEA/PAC
  "more or less constant at each temperature but decreased with temperature at pH 3 and increased with
  temperature at pH 6" (which the barrier pairs imply: 51 < 74 at pH 3, 65 > 40 at pH 6).

## 5. Flags

1. **Low-moisture slurry, not a solution.** 110 µL aqueous on 600 mg sand; the "concentration" 90.9
   mmol/L is nominal and the lipid carbonyls are largely undissolved. Rates and yields belong to a
   surface/low-aw regime (the water series shows the yields move with 0-200 µL added water), not to the
   trunk's aqueous pots. Transfer to water needs a declared band.
2. **Barriers without rates.** The four Ea values are printed; the zero-order k(T) values are in
   Supporting figures not on disk and the unit of k is not stated. The barriers can be carried; a rate
   cannot.
3. **Barrier pairs cross.** PAC 74 (pH 3) vs 40 (pH 6) and PEA 51 vs 65: apparent barriers of lumped
   yields whose branch ratio itself changes with temperature; they are not activation energies of an
   elementary step and should be stored as apparent, pH-specific.
4. **Calibration matrix is pH 6 for every sample**, including the pH 3 citrate ones; the work-up
   (methanol extraction, NaBH4) is the same, but recovery from citrate vs phosphate is not shown.
5. **Phenylalanine alone makes product.** Under air Phe with no lipid gives PAC 3.9 (pH 3) and 21.1
   (pH 6) nmol/µmol and PEA 4.8 (pH 3 air) / 5.7 (pH 6 N2): thermal autoxidation/decarboxylation of the
   amino acid at 140 C is not negligible against the weaker carbonyls (HN, OH, ON under N2). Any
   carbonyl-attributed yield should be read net of the Phe-alone row.
6. **Yield, not mass balance.** PAC + PEA + BZA is at most 19 % of the Phe (LOOMe, pH 3, air); the fate
   of the rest (imines, pyridines, Michael adducts, polymers) is not measured, so the "split" is a
   split of the quantified products only.
7. **pH series atmosphere unstated.** The 1.6 % PAC at pH 2.15 matches the nitrogen column of Table 1
   (DD pH 3 N2 = 2.1 %), not the air column (12.7 %); I read the pH series as nitrogen, but the paper
   does not say.
8. **One heat for the table (140 C, 1 h)**: no time dependence for eleven of the twelve carbonyls;
   the BZA fraction, which comes from PAC oxidation, will move with time, so PEA/PAC at 1 h is a
   snapshot.
9. **Amine → aldehyde conversion** (ref 14, pH ≥ 6 inhibited) is part of the measured PEA/PAC and
   is not separable here.
10. Registry gaps: 2-phenylethylamine and all lipid carbonyls have no `compounds.yml` rows.
