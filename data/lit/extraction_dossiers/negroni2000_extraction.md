**Answers.**

1. **Which volatiles change with the oil:** the pyrazine PROFILE. Unsubstituted pyrazine falls ×5-6.5 and 2,5- /
   2,3-dimethylpyrazine rise ×2.4-16 from olive (monounsaturated, 9 % linoleic) to sunflower (58 % linoleic), in both
   sugars [M]. 2-methylpyrazine moves ≤ ×1.9. 2-Furanmethanol falls ×6 with xylose. No sulfur volatiles.
2. **By how much relative to no oil:** not answerable from this paper.
3. **Chemistry or partition:** a shared physical partition operator cannot move pyrazine and 2,5-DMP in opposite
   directions between three triglyceride oils of the same mass; so the oil-to-oil differences are more likely
   chemistry (lipid-derived carbonyls / radicals changing alkyl substitution) [Z, confidence ~0.7]. The authors favour
   mass transfer [P]. The absolute oil effect (oil vs none) could still be partly partition.

## 2. Conditions

- **Pot** [M]: 70 ml of 0.5 M xylose or glucose + 0.5 M lysine (equimolar; ~35 mmol each [Z]), + 25 g oil, + additive
  (BHT / tocopherol / rosemary extract 0.30 mmol, or AIBN 0.36 mmol), + 0.381 mg tetradecane (first internal
  standard). **Heated 3 h at 100 °C in a flask on a Likens-Nickerson apparatus, extracting continuously into CH2Cl2
  (200 ml)**: volatiles are stripped as they form, so the result is the cumulative strip, not a standing level.
- **pH held at 6**: "monitored and kept constant by adding diluted sodium hydroxide" [V-ish; label "pH 6"] — a manual
  pH-stat. Buffer: none.
- **Headspace / O2**: open distillation (air); not controlled [!]. Water activity: aqueous phase dilute, ~26 % oil by
  mass of the charge [Z].
- **Oils** [M]: olive (San Giorgio), canola ("zero" erucic, Zucchi), sunflower (Carapelli). Peroxide index fresh
  1.5 / 1.4 / 0.9 meq O2/kg (olive / canola / sunflower); discarded above 10. Fatty acids: olive 18.0 % palmitic,
  85.0 % oleic [sic, sums > 100 % — !], 9.0 % linoleic, 1.3 % linolenic; sunflower 19.3 % oleic, 58.2 % linoleic,
  0.34 % linolenic; canola 56 % oleic, 19.7 % linoleic, 8.9 % linolenic. Tocopherols 15.7 / 34.9 / 54.7 mg/100 ml.
- **Analysis** [M]: concentrated to 1 ml, pentadecane second IS; GC-MS (Shimadzu QP-5000, SPB-1701), quantified
  against tetradecane with correction factors; n ≥ 4.

## 4. Mechanism evidence and its kind

- **Within-study factor** (kind 6): oil identity at fixed oil mass, pH held, same sugar and amine; radical
  modulators as a second factor. End products only; no labelling, no intermediate.
- **Chemistry vs partition [Z]:** the three oils are triglycerides of similar polarity, so their oil / water partition
  coefficients for a given pyrazine should differ little (my assumption; not measured here). A partition or
  mass-transfer operator would lower the more lipophilic 2,5-DMP (log Kow ~0.6-1.0, assumed) more than pyrazine
  (~−0.2); instead the most unsaturated oil gives the MOST 2,5-DMP and the LEAST pyrazine, in both sugars. That pattern
  fits a chemical supply of C-fragments / alkyl groups from oxidising linoleate (or radical chemistry), not partition.
  Against chemistry: sunflower's fresh peroxide value is the LOWEST (0.9) and AIBN (a radical source) lowers rather than
  raises 2,5-DMP; the authors' viscosity argument is untested [P].
- Lab: Milano (Arnoldi), EU FAIR consortium; one paper here.

## 5. Bearing on open questions

- **E7 (lipid lane is a direct sum; MFT / FFT unchanged with lipid in the engine): LEAVES the thiol claim UNCHANGED**
  (no sulfur). For the pyrazine branch it **SUPPORTS "lipid is not inert"** in the specific form "oil IDENTITY changes
  pyrazine substitution ×5-16 in opposite directions" — an effect a pure partition operator cannot give. It cannot
  size the oil-vs-none effect.
- **CF-031 (lipid lowers / raises thiols): LEAVES UNCHANGED** (no thiols). The cluster-4 direction "electrophile load
  (oxidation) drives the falls" gets an indirect analogue: the most linoleic oil moves the profile most; but the
  fresh PV ordering runs the other way (sunflower lowest), so "oxidation load" would have to be what forms during the
  3 h at 100 °C under air [Z].
- **F30 lipid crossover / F19 pyrazines** (atlas): a new lab with a within-study oil-identity factor at pH 6
  (held), 100 °C, for pyrazines; F30's tag "lipid unsaturation / oxidation" gains a non-thiol example.
- **F21 / F20:** TMP appears with canola / sunflower and not olive-xylose [M] — an extra alkylpyrazine supply tied to
  the PUFA oils.

## 6. Disagreements found

1. **Phospholipid vs triglyceride direction on pyrazines.** F30's Reading cell (egg lecithin, 0.2 M phosphate pH
   5.7-6.2: methylpyrazine lowered ×2.1, other pyrazines ×1.0-1.5) vs here: the PUFA oil RAISES methyl- and
   dimethylpyrazines (×1.4-16 over olive) and lowers only unsubstituted pyrazine. Not a clean conflict (Reading
   compares lipid vs none; here oil vs oil), but the sign on 2-MP / DMP differs. Causes: lipid class (phospholipid
   amine / interface vs triglyceride), amine (Gly / Lys / Cys vs Lys), extraction (SDE during heating here).
2. **Internal [!]:** Table 6 (glucose + canola) lists tocopherol 6.21 / 3.30 and rosemary 5.99 / 3.030 for pyrazine /
   2-MP; Table 8 and Fig. 2 give the same numbers with rosemary and tocopherol swapped. The reference column agrees.
   Olive's fatty-acid composition sums to > 100 % (palmitic 18.0 + oleic 85.0 ...). Footnotes print "standard errors
   (P < 0.5%)", which is not a defined statistic.
3. **Year:** the stem says 2000; the article is JAFC 2001, 49:439 (web 12 Dec 2000). The DOI is right.
