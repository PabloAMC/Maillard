## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

This is a GSH **glycation** paper (same group and system as `sun2019b_extraction.md`). At atmospheric
pressure, 80 °C, initial pH 7.4, GSH 100 mM + xylose 200 mM accumulate at most **8.44 % ARP** (of GSH)
[M], while **8.26 % of GSH is oxidised to GSSG in 150 min** [M]. Vacuum dehydration (25 mbar)
pushes ARP to **67.98 % in 15 min at 80 °C**, with 3-DX 6.49 % and 1-DX 2.77 % [M]. Under SDR, GSH
loss equals xylose loss and is first order in GSH (R² 0.93–0.995) [M]; ARP + 3-DX + 1-DX account for
> 96 % of initial GSH [M] — i.e. **no detectable non-glycation GSH loss at 50–80 °C within 60 min**.
Table 2 gives a fitted scheme GSH + Xyl → GX (k1, 2nd order, **Ea 95.99 kJ/mol**) → ARP (k2, zero
order, **79.35**) → 3-DX (k3, **110.5**) and → 1-DX (k4, **124.3 kJ/mol**) [M]. GSH loss at 80 °C (SDR)
is 0.1306 min⁻¹ at pH 5.4 and 0.2159 min⁻¹ at pH 9.4 [M]. Browning (A420) rises steeply with pH and
temperature (Table 1).

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Model context.** GSH → H2S only (`r_gsh_h2s`); no GSH glycation, no GSH-ARP, no GSH oxidation. The
model has pentose → Amadori / deoxyosone chemistry for free amino acids. Nothing here was run against
the model.

1. **GSH glycation by xylose (the dominant GSH sink with a pentose at ≤ 90 °C, usable as a test, not
   as a constant).** With 2:1 xylose, GSH is consumed only as fast as xylose, into an ARP that then
   fragments to 3-DX ≫ 1-DX. Table 2's Ea values (GX formation 96, ARP formation 79, 3-DX 110,
   1-DX 124 kJ/mol) are the only Arrhenius parameters for GSH-sugar chemistry in the corpus.
   **But they are fitted under SDR** (25 mbar, water continuously removed, so concentrations and
   a_w change with time) and k2–k4 are zero-order mmol/L/min constants — they **cannot be transplanted**
   into a constant-volume aqueous mass-action scheme. Under atmospheric pressure ARP stays ≤ 8.4 %
   (cf. `sun2019b_extraction.md`: max 8.6 % at 90 °C, 60 min, pH 7.0) — consistent across the two papers.
2. **Mass balance (usable, important):** at 50–80 °C under SDR, ARP + 3-DX + 1-DX > 96 % of GSH lost,
   so **no H2S-forming GSH degradation is visible below 90 °C on a 1 h scale** — consistent with
   Zheng & Ho's small H2S constants at 80–90 °C (`zheng1994_extraction.md`: 3.3 × 10⁻⁵ and 7.7 × 10⁻⁵
   min⁻¹ at pH 7) and with GSH glycation outcompeting desulfuration when sugar is present.
3. **GSH oxidation in air at 80 °C: ~5.8 × 10⁻⁴ min⁻¹** (8.26 % in 150 min). With `lu2019`'s ~1.8 × 10⁻³
   min⁻¹ at 110 °C it bounds the GSSG channel, but both depend on unreported headspace O2; use only
   as an order of magnitude.
4. **pH (direction):** GSH glycation 0.13 (pH 5.4) → 0.22 min⁻¹ (pH 9.4) at 80 °C SDR; A420 at pH 9.4 vs
   7.4 is 17× higher at 15 min and 1.6× at 60 min; 1-DX lowest at pH 9.4 (fragmentation). Not transferable as constants.
5. **Not usable:** anything about H2S, MFT, FFT or volatiles (none measured).

**Caveats, collected.** SDR is a non-standard, evaporating reactor; concentrations reported after
re-dilution to 50 mL; atmospheric vessel and headspace not described; initial pH only; "R²" printed for
correlation coefficients; the 7.05× / 8.44 % inconsistency; Fig. 5 small panels (±2–3 mM).
