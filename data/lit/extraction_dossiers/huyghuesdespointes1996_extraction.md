# Huyghues-Despointes & Yaylayan 1996 — EXTRACTION
### *"Retro-Aldol and Redox Reactions of Amadori Compounds: Mechanistic Studies with Variously Labeled D-[13C]Glucose"*
### **Pyrolysis-GC-MS** (quartz tube, 250 °C, 20 s) of L-proline with D-glucose labelled at C-1 ... C-6 (one position each), and of the proline / morpholine Amadori compounds: carbon origin of the C3 product 1-(1'-pyrrolidinyl)-2-propanone (2), the C4 product 1-(1'-pyrrolidinyl)-2-butanone (4) and N-acetylpyrrolidine (acetic acid). Pathway shares assigned from the labels (Scheme 1). **Answer.** The paper is labelling evidence for **where the carbons go** when a proline Amadori compound fragments
under pyrolysis: C3 units split ~55 : 45 between the upper and lower glucose halves (consistent with a C3-C4 retro-aldol
of the ARP [P]), C2 / C4 units mostly from a C2-C3 cleavage after carbonyl migration [P], and acetic acid mostly from
C-1/C-2. It shows qualitatively (product identity by GC-MS) that formic acid reduces an iminium (Leuckart-Wallach) and
that pyruvaldehyde alone at 100 °C gives 1,2-propanediol (18 %), acetic acid (30 %) and acetol (7 % of peak area), i.e.
**redox without an added oxidant** [M as area %, mechanism P]. It gives **no rate, no O2 contrast and no aqueous
condition** — so for the 100-140 °C aqueous model it is a structural prior at most (confidence ~0.3 that the pathway
shares transfer to a primary-amine aqueous pot), and it does not move the redox row beyond SINGLE.

## 3. Quantitative results

**Table 4, label in 2 (1-(1'-pyrrolidinyl)-2-propanone)** [M] (checked on a 300 dpi render): [1-13C]: 55 % labelled at
the methyl, 45 % unlabelled; [2-13C]: 55 % at the carbonyl, 45 % unlabelled; [3-13C]: 55 % **drawn at the methyl**,
45 % unlabelled; [4-13C]: 30 % methyl + 15 % N-CH2, 55 % unlabelled; [5-13C]: 45 % carbonyl, 55 % unlabelled;
[6-13C]: 15 % methyl + 30 % N-CH2, 55 % unlabelled. Net: 55 % C1-C3, 45 % C4-C6 (C-4 and C-6 scrambled between the two
ends, the triose isomerisation the authors invoke [Q]). **[!] The [3-13C] row cannot be right as drawn:** C-1 and C-3
cannot both become the methyl of one C3 unit, and nothing from C1-C3 would then reach the N-CH2. Scheme 1 numbers 2 as
C-3 on N, C-2 carbonyl, C-1 methyl, and Table 3's [3-13C] spectrum has m/z 85 (pyrrolidinyl-CH2⁺ + 1) at 100 vs 84 at
99, i.e. the label on the N-CH2 [Z]. Read row 3 as "55 % at N-CH2".

**Table 5, label in 4 (1-(1'-pyrrolidinyl)-2-butanone)** [M], unlabelled share per labelled glucose: C-1 73 %, C-2
74 %, C-3 5 %, C-4 7 %, C-5 30 %, C-6 26 %. So 4 carries C-3 and C-4 ~95 % of the time, C-5/C-6 ~70 %, C-1/C-2 ~27 %
[Z].

**Table 6, label in N-acetylpyrrolidine** [M], labelled share: C-1 73 %, C-2 73 %, C-3 5 %, C-4 5 %, C-5 20 %, C-6 20 %.

**Scheme 1 pathway shares** [M as printed, assignment P]: A (RA[3,4] of the ARP): 2 at 55 % from 7, 45 % from free
glyceraldehyde (30 % + 15 % by end); D (carbonyl migration to the 4-hexosulose, RA[2,3]): 70 % of C2 / C4 products,
4 split 45 % / 25 % by end; B (1,4-dideoxy-2,3-hexodiulose, RA[4,5]): 5 %; C (1-DG, RA[4,5]): 20-25 %; acetyl C1-C2
70 %, C5-C6 25-30 %.

**Table 2, morpholine Amadori, 250 °C, 20 s (GC area %)** [M]: acetic acid 21.6, morpholine 32.6,
1-(N-morpholino)-2-propanone 10.1, 2-hydroxy-1-(N-morpholino)-1-buten-3-one 10.1, γ-pyranone 1.9,
1,2-(N,N-dimorpholino)-1-propene 1.9, acetol 0.7, 2,3-butanedione 0.5.

**Redox side experiments** [M / Q]: pyruvaldehyde, 100 °C, 8 min: 40 peaks; 1,2-propanediol 18 %, acetic acid 30 %,
acetol 7 %, 2,4-dimethyl-1,3-dioxolane 7 % (areas). Acetol + pyrrolidine (1 : 1, 100 °C, 10 min): 2 is the major
product, the Heyns product 9 only traces; 1 : 2 raises the dipyrrolidinyl-propene 10 ×6. Pyruvaldehyde + pyrrolidine +
formic acid gives 2 as the major product; trimeric glyoxal at 250 °C gives formic acid and CO2. Cited energies for
enediol / dicarbonyl double proton transfer (113.8 vs 149.7 kcal/mol, Tachibana 1989) [C].

## 4. Mechanism evidence and its kind (Yaylayan laboratory, McGill)

- **Isotope labelling (kind 1)** of three product classes from six singly labelled glucoses; the pathway shares are
  inferences from label patterns, and the authors warn that "more than one mechanism can be compatible" [Q].
- **Fed intermediates (kind 2), qualitative:** 7 -> 2 on pyrolysis; pyruvaldehyde + proline / pyrrolidine -> 2; acetol
  + pyrrolidine -> 2 (and 10).
- **Proposed, not shown [P]:** carbonyl migration to the 4-hexosulose; every reduction step ("[H]" in Scheme 1); the
  three redox mechanisms as the operative reductants in Maillard systems; acetic acid via oxidation of glycolaldehyde.
- Not shown: any O2 role (or its absence), any rate, any aqueous behaviour.

## 6. Disagreements found

1. **With the cited Tressl 1993 (not on disk) [C]:** from [1-13C]glucose + proline (heated mixture), 2 is 60 %
   unlabelled / 40 % labelled at C-3; here 45 % unlabelled / 55 % labelled. Candidate causes: heated solution vs
   250 °C pyrolysis (the authors' own two-maxima temperature curve says routes change with temperature), and an
   unprinted label-share procedure. A rough check on the molecular ions of Table 3 (m/z 128 / 127, natural 13C
   subtracted) gives ~45 % labelled for [1-13C] [Z], closer to Tressl; the M⁺ is ≤ 2.6 % relative intensity, so this
   check is weak. Flag for a second reader before any label share is keyed.
2. Internal [!]: Table 4's [3-13C] row draws the label on the methyl (same as [1-13C]); Scheme 1 and the Table 3 m/z 85
   ion put it on the N-CH2 (sec. 3). A drawing error, but a keyer copying the table would key an impossible pattern.
3. Internal [!]: Table 6 assigns 5 % of N-acetylpyrrolidine to C-3 / C-4, which Scheme 1's acetyl shares (70 + 25-30 %)
   do not carry.
