## 1. Methods

- **Food.** A freshly prepared stewed beef vegetable gravy (BVG) and stewed pork vegetable gravy (PVG). Beef
  and pork top round from a butcher; carrots, leeks, celery roots, onions, lard and iodized salt from a local
  market (p. 13122, Materials). **The recipe, cooking time and temperature are not printed here**; the paper
  says the gravies "were prepared as described recently" (ref 1 = Christlbauer & Schieberle 2009, JAFC 57:9114,
  the AEDA paper, not on disk). Gravy fat content 2 % by weight (p. 13126, Aroma Recombination).
- **Selection.** 38 odorants with high FD factors in the 2009 AEDA (p. 13127 text).
- **Quantitation.** Stable isotope dilution assays: 5-350 g gravy spiked with labelled standards, stirred
  overnight in diethyl ether, SAFE, acidic/neutral-basic split, concentrated to ~250 µL (p. 13125). HRGC-MS (ion
  trap, CI with methanol) on DB-FFAP for the acids; two-dimensional HRGC-MS (DB-FFAP then DB-1701 or the
  reverse) for the rest. Selected ions and response factors in **Table 1 (p. 13125)**; three new labelled
  standards synthesised: [2H2]-(E)-2-undecenal (RF 0.88), [2H8]-12-methyltridecanal (RF 0.72),
  [2H2]-δ-octalactone (RF 1.0) (p. 13127 text). 2- vs 3-methylbutanoic acid split by EI mass traces m/z 74 and 60.
  Concentrations are **means of triplicates differing by not more than ±10 %** (Table 2 footnote a).
- **OAVs.** Concentration (µg/kg gravy) divided by the **orthonasal odour threshold in water (µg/L)**
  (Table 3 header and footnote a, p. 13127). Thresholds newly determined in this study unless footnoted;
  footnotes c-f cite earlier water thresholds (refs 28, 26, 29, 12). Water was chosen as "the main ingredient of
  both gravies" (p. 13127 text). No oil or gravy-matrix thresholds were used.
- **Sensory.** Panel of **16 experienced assessors** (p. 13126). Eight descriptors, each trained with a
  reference compound in water at 50× its threshold: seasoning-like (3-hydroxy-4,5-dimethyl-2(5H)-furanone,
  sotolon), fatty ((E,E)-2,4-decadienal), roasty (2-acetyl-1-pyrroline), tallowy (12-methyltridecanal), cooked
  vegetable-like (3-(methylthio)propanal), meaty (bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide), caramel-like
  (4-hydroxy-2,5-dimethyl-3(2H)-furanone), sweaty (butanoic acid). 20 g gravy in covered glass vessels
  (i.d. 40 mm, 45 mL) equilibrated 60 °C for 30 min. Intensities on a "seven-point linear scale from 0.0 to 3.0";
  results of three sessions averaged; single assessors differed by no more than 0.5 scale points.
- **Recombinate.** Minor odorants from an ethanol stock (1 mL into ~800 mL tap water), major ones (acetic acid,
  butanoic acid, HDMF) added in ethanol, 20 g deodorised sunflower oil, made up to 1000 mL with tap water
  (2 % fat). Equilibrated 60 °C, 30 min. Profiled like the gravies; overall similarity rated separately on a
  0-3 scale (p. 13126).
- **Omission.** One reduced model per gravy: **all compounds with OAV < 1 removed together** (butanoic,
  pentanoic, 2- and 3-methylbutanoic acids, (E)-β-damascenone, vanillin, phenylacetic acid, 4-methylphenol,
  3-methylindole, δ-octalactone, γ-octalactone, γ-nonalactone; for PVG also 12-methyltridecanal). Triangle
  tests against the complete model; significance α computed per ref 27 (p. 13126). **No single-compound
  omissions were run.**

What the text states (pp. 13128-13129): the BVG model gave "a very good similarity" to the gravy, with the
same fatty and caramel-like intensities and nearly identical spicy, roasty and tallowy intensities. Only
"meaty" was rated slightly higher in the gravy, and vegetable-like the reverse. For PVG, meaty, spicy and
roasty were somewhat higher in the gravy, and fatty and vegetable-like higher in the model; the overall
aroma was "in good similarity". **The numeric similarity scores (0-3 scale) are not printed.** The tallowy
note is visibly higher in beef than pork in both gravy and model (read from graph), in line with
12-methyltridecanal.

Which compounds went into the recombinate is stated two ways: the methods and Results say "all 38
odorants in their natural amounts" (p. 13128), while the abstract says the models contained "all odorants
with OAVs > 1". The omission design (removing the OAV < 1 set from "the entire aroma models") supports the
38-compound reading.

### 2c. Omission (p. 13129)

There was one triangle test per gravy: the full model against the model without its OAV < 1 compounds.
Both differences were "statistically insignificant (α > 5%)". The data are not shown: no panel count for
the triangle tests and no number of correct answers are printed. **Single-odorant omissions, for MMP,
12-MTD or decadienal, were not done**, so the paper does not show by test that any one compound is
necessary.

## 3. What it means for the model and for formulation

**Target profile.** At OAV level, a stewed beef-and-vegetable gravy is dominated by three groups, in this
order:
1. **A vegetable thiol.** 3-mercapto-2-methylpentan-1-ol, from onion and leek, is the top odorant in both
   gravies. A plant-based dish cooked with onion and leek gets it from the same source.
2. **Lipid-oxidation aldehydes.** (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (E,Z)-2,6-nonadienal, (E)-2-decenal,
   (E)-2-undecenal, (E)-2-nonenal, (E,E)-2,4-nonadienal, 1-octen-3-one and hexanal. The authors attribute
   the low Maillard OAVs and the dominance of these aldehydes to the "low-cooking" (stewing) process.
3. **Beef's species marker.** 12-methyltridecanal (tallowy), OAV 3600 in beef and below LOD in pork. The
   paper ties it to beef plasmalogens. No plant protein, oil or vegetable here makes it.

Maillard odorants (methional, HDMF, 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline, sotolon, DMTS) are present at OAV 7-69. No
alkylpyrazine was among the key odorants, and the "meaty" reference compound
bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide was below its LOD.

For a plant-based "stewed beef" analogue, the paper implies:
- the vegetables carry the top note;
- the meat-specific gap is mainly **12-methyltridecanal** plus the **balance of lipid aldehydes**: beef
  favours (E,E)-2,4-decadienal, (E)-2-decenal and (E)-2-undecenal, while pork gives (E,Z)-2,4-decadienal
  at OAV 1300;
- MFT-type meaty thiols are not what defines this dish at OAV level.

No odorant was tested individually for necessity (§2c), so these are OAV-ranked candidates, not proven
requirements.

**What the kinetic model emits** (grep of `src/kinetic_core/species*.py`, 2026-10-09):
- *Emitted as species:*
  - methional (`MTAL`)
  - HDMF (`DMHF`)
  - 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (`AP`)
  - hexanal (`HEXANAL`) and trans,trans-2,4-decadienal (`DECADIENAL`), both in the lipid lane from
    linoleate hydroperoxides
  - acetic acid (`AA`)
  - bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide (`MFTD`)
  - its monomer MFT
- *Not emitted:*
  - 3-mercapto-2-methylpentan-1-ol (a vegetable thiol, outside the lanes)
  - 12-methyltridecanal (a plasmalogen product; no lane)
  - (E,Z)-2,6-nonadienal, (E)-2-decenal, (E)-2-undecenal, (E,E)-2,4-nonadienal, 1-octen-3-one, octanal
  - (E,Z)-2,4-decadienal (only the E,E isomer is carried)
  - (E)-2-nonenal: named as an unquantified Hock co-product in `species_lipid.py`, and listed in the live
    `engine.unrepresented_compounds` of `results/validation/core_prediction_uncertainty.json`
  - sotolon (declared out of scope in `sulfur.py`)
  - dimethyl trisulfide (refused explicitly: the methionine chain carries no H2S, per
    `parameters_methionine.py` / `engine.py`)
  - the methoxypyrazines, β-ionone, phenylethanal and the phenols/lactones

So the model covers the Maillard minority of this profile and two of its lipid aldehydes. It cannot speak
to the top two odorants, nor to most of the beef-versus-pork difference.

## What it does not give

- The gravy recipe, cooking time or temperature (they are in the 2009 paper, ref 1, not on disk).
- Any apportioning of the lipid aldehydes between meat fat, the lard and the vegetables. Lard was bought for
  both gravies; the recipe that says how much is in ref 1.
- Numeric similarity scores, triangle-test panel size, or counts of correct answers.
- Single-compound omission tests.
- Thresholds in oil or in a gravy matrix: all OAVs use water thresholds, while the gravy carries 2 % fat.
- Precursor data (cysteine, ribose, thiamine, plasmalogen content) and any kinetics.
