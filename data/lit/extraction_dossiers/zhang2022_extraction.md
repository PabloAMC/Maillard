## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

This is an **oil-water two-phase xylose/cysteine pot** (120 °C, 90 min, initial pH 7.2) and its only
kinetic-grade numbers are end-point water-phase measurements: **pH falls from 7.2 to 4.72 without oil and
to 4.33-4.68 with oil; xylose left 28.9 mg/mL (no oil) vs 23.5-28.6 mg/mL; cysteine left 14.1 mg/mL vs
8.0-12.4 mg/mL** [F]. Oils make the water phase consume more xylose and cysteine (most with ENTH corn oil:
−19 % xylose, −43 % cysteine relative to no oil) and brown more (A420). The printed reactant amount
("0.4 mol" each in 10 mL) is impossible; the residuals are consistent with ~0.4 mol/L each (flag 1). The
volatile results are HS-SPME semi-quant with a response factor of 1, given per compound only in the
supplement; the main text says FFT and MFT "were formed in all oil-MRPs" and were highest with coconut oil,
and that **no pyrazines formed from xylose + cysteine** (only peanut oil carried its own). Thiol-class totals
in the oil phase are ~100-4000 ng/mL vs ~5 ng/mL in the oil-free water phase [F], but oil- and water-phase
headspace numbers are not on a common basis (flag 3).

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Can anchor (weakly):**
- **End-point acidification of a xylose/cysteine pot:** pH 7.2 -> 4.72 after 90 min at 120 °C, sealed, no
  buffer (only NaOH). A check on the engine's pH-drift term for an unbuffered Cys/Xyl pot, if the initial
  concentration is fixed (flag 1). One time point.
- **End-point reactant residuals** (xylose ~48 %, cysteine ~29 % left under the 0.4 M reading) as a soft
  check on Cys/Xyl consumption at 120 °C. The cysteine assay is not specific (flag 2), so the cysteine
  residual is an upper bound on free cysteine.
- **Sign of the oil effect on the water phase:** every oil lowers residual cysteine; all but raw coconut
  (28.6, letter "ab" vs control "a") lower residual xylose; pH falls by 0.04-0.39 (corn ENTH not
  significant). The effect is larger with the more unsaturated and with pre-oxidised (ENTH) oils. Mechanism claimed: oil-derived
  carbonyls consume amino/H2S and partition of lipophilic volatiles into the oil. Qualitative support for
  lipid-carbonyl coupling to the sulfur lane; no rate.
- **Null on pyrazines from Cys/Xyl** at 120 °C, pH 7.2 -> 4.7 (consistent with cysteine suppressing
  pyrazines).

**Cannot anchor:**
- **No MFT/FFT numbers** in the paper body; the supplement (S1-S5) is not on disk. Even with it, the values
  are HS-SPME with a response factor of 1.
- **No time course**, one temperature, end-point only.
- **Two-phase partitioning:** the engine is a single aqueous phase; the oil phase holds most volatiles, and
  oil-phase vs water-phase headspace ng/mL are not comparable (flag 3).

**Caveats / flags:**
1. [!] **Reactant amount impossible as printed.** "0.4 mol" of cysteine (48 g) and xylose (60 g) cannot
   dissolve in 10 mL. "0.4 mmol" (40 mM: xylose 6.0, cysteine 4.8 mg/mL) is contradicted by the measured
   residuals (28.9 and 14.1 mg/mL in the control). The residuals fit **~0.4 mol/L each** (xylose 60 mg/mL,
   cysteine 48 mg/mL), my interpretation at ~70 % confidence; ask the authors before using absolute
   concentrations. Total liquid volume in the 50-mL tube is also not stated.
2. [!] **Assay specificity.** Cysteine by phosphotungstic-acid reduction responds to any reducing thiol
   (MFT, FFT, mercaptoacetaldehyde, possibly H2S); xylose by phloroglucinol responds to pentoses and
   furfural-forming pentose derivatives. Both are "reactant-like" pools, not the free reactant.
3. [!] **Semi-quantitation basis differs by phase:** 3 mL oil vs 4 mL water, same IS spike, RF 1. Headspace
   partition from oil vs water differs by orders of magnitude for lipophilic compounds, so "oil phase
