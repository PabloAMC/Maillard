## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

This is an **oil-water two-phase xylose/cysteine pot** (120 °C, 90 min, initial pH 7.2) and its only
kinetic-grade numbers are end-point water-phase measurements: **pH falls from 7.2 to 4.72 without oil and
to 4.33-4.68 with oil; xylose left 28.9 mg/mL (no oil) vs 23.5-28.6 mg/mL; cysteine left 14.1 mg/mL vs
8.0-12.4 mg/mL** [F]. Oils make the water phase consume more xylose and cysteine (most with ENTH corn oil:
−19 % xylose, −43 % cysteine relative to no oil) and brown more (A420). The printed reactant amount
("0.4 mol" each in 10 mL) is impossible; the residuals are consistent with ~0.4 mol/L each (flag 1). The
volatile results are HS-SPME semi-quant with a response factor of 1, given per compound only in the
supplement; the main text says FFT and MFT "were formed in all oil-MRPs" and were highest with coconut oil,
and that **no pyrazines formed from xylose + cysteine** (only peanut oil carried its own). Thiol-class totals
in the oil phase are ~100-4000 ng/mL vs ~5 ng/mL in the oil-free water phase [F], but oil- and water-phase
headspace numbers are not on a common basis (flag 3).

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Can anchor (weakly):**
- **End-point acidification of a xylose/cysteine pot:** pH 7.2 -> 4.72 after 90 min at 120 °C, sealed, no
  buffer (only NaOH). A check on the engine's pH-drift term for an unbuffered Cys/Xyl pot, if the initial
  concentration is fixed (flag 1). One time point.
- **End-point reactant residuals** (xylose ~48 %, cysteine ~29 % left under the 0.4 M reading) as a soft
  check on Cys/Xyl consumption at 120 °C. The cysteine assay is not specific (flag 2), so the cysteine
  residual is an upper bound on free cysteine.
- **Sign of the oil effect on the water phase:** every oil lowers residual cysteine; all but raw coconut
  (28.6, letter "ab" vs control "a") lower residual xylose; pH falls by 0.04-0.39 (corn ENTH not
  significant). The effect is larger with the more unsaturated and with pre-oxidised (ENTH) oils. Mechanism claimed: oil-derived
  carbonyls consume amino/H2S and partition of lipophilic volatiles into the oil. Qualitative support for
  lipid-carbonyl coupling to the sulfur lane; no rate.
- **Null on pyrazines from Cys/Xyl** at 120 °C, pH 7.2 -> 4.7 (consistent with cysteine suppressing
  pyrazines).

**Cannot anchor:**
- **No MFT/FFT numbers** in the paper body; the supplement (S1-S5) is not on disk. Even with it, the values
  are HS-SPME with a response factor of 1.
- **No time course**, one temperature, end-point only.
- **Two-phase partitioning:** the engine is a single aqueous phase; the oil phase holds most volatiles, and
  oil-phase vs water-phase headspace ng/mL are not comparable (flag 3).

**Caveats / flags:**
1. [!] **Reactant amount impossible as printed.** "0.4 mol" of cysteine (48 g) and xylose (60 g) cannot
   dissolve in 10 mL. "0.4 mmol" (40 mM: xylose 6.0, cysteine 4.8 mg/mL) is contradicted by the measured
   residuals (28.9 and 14.1 mg/mL in the control). The residuals fit **~0.4 mol/L each** (xylose 60 mg/mL,
   cysteine 48 mg/mL), my interpretation at ~70 % confidence; ask the authors before using absolute
   concentrations. Total liquid volume in the 50-mL tube is also not stated.
2. [!] **Assay specificity.** Cysteine by phosphotungstic-acid reduction responds to any reducing thiol
   (MFT, FFT, mercaptoacetaldehyde, possibly H2S); xylose by phloroglucinol responds to pentoses and
   furfural-forming pentose derivatives. Both are "reactant-like" pools, not the free reactant.
3. [!] **Semi-quantitation basis differs by phase:** 3 mL oil vs 4 mL water, same IS spike, RF 1. Headspace
   partition from oil vs water differs by orders of magnitude for lipophilic compounds, so "oil phase
   provided the major volatiles" is partly an artefact of the method. Compare within a phase only.
4. [!] Section 2.5.2 says "For D-xylose determination" twice; the second paragraph is the cysteine assay.

FFT -0.03 to +0.81 dex, MFT -0.45 to +0.68 dex. **No oil suppresses free FFT in the water phase beyond x0.93
(palm RM), and only two arms have less free MFT than the control: olive RM (0.12, x0.86, within noise) and ENTH
coconut (0.05, x0.36).** The oxidised (ENTH) oil gives *more*

x1.4; corn x0.86, coconut x0.09). Furfural is up x3.3-15.8 in every oil water phase.

**But the MFT disulfide moves the other way.** The control holds 20x more bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide
(2.81) than free MFT (0.14); with oil it is undetected in all five RM water phases and 0.57-2.32 in the EM

0.12, coconut 0.87, palm 0.19 (**x1/3.4 to x1/25**); EM corn 1.74, peanut 2.99, olive 1.10, coconut 1.45, palm
0.84 (x1/1.0 to x1/3.5) [Z]. So "MFT suppression" in this study depends on whether the disulfide is counted.

**(b) Oil phase: raw vs oxidised (ENTH), within a phase** [Z]: FFT ENTH/raw corn 1.10, peanut 0.85, olive 0.89,
coconut 0.71, palm 0.80; thiol subtotal 0.63, 0.94, 0.77, 0.63, 0.57; MFT corn – -> 0.54, peanut – -> 10.18,
olive 13.79 -> –, coconut 17.76 -> 3.39, palm – / –. The ENTH oxidation is mild (PV <= 4.28 mmol/kg, MDA < 0.25
mg/100 g), and it is confounded with lipase hydrolysis (free fatty acids).

**(c) Between oils, oil phase:** FFT coconut 2141 > palm 1407 > peanut 1158 > olive 642 > corn 53 (Raw-MR; the
thiol subtotals rank the same). Against raw-oil iodine value (coconut 12, palm 52, olive 87, peanut 92, corn 99)
Spearman rho = -0.9 (n = 5; two-sided p ~0.08) [Z]. Corn, the most PUFA-rich oil (60 % C18:2), holds 40x less FFT
than coconut in its oil phase.

**(d) Whole system, nominal** (4 vol oil + 1 vol water per vol of aqueous; assumes the phase volumes are
unchanged, not stated) [Z]: FFT x50-57 (corn), x860-1000 (peanut), x500-560 (olive), x1300-1850 (coconut),

and 1,2-dichlorobenzene (log Kow 3.4) is far more retained by oil than MFT/FFT (log Kow ~1-2, my estimate), so
Cv = (Pv/Pi) Ci inflates the oil-phase numbers, by my order-of-magnitude estimate 10-100x (low confidence).
Even deflated by 100x the FFT totals stay >= the control for four of five oils.

**Capture vs partition (4:1 oil : aqueous).** If the oil only partitioned the thiols and formation were
unchanged, the water phase would fall by 1/(1 + 4 K_oil/w); for K_oil/w ~5-100 that is **x1/20 to x1/400**. The
water phase instead *rises* for free FFT and stays within x0.4-4.8 for free MFT, so (i) **partition alone is
ruled out** as the dominant effect on free thiols, and (ii) the oil systems must *form* at least as much FFT/MFT
as the control, and probably more (consistent with the larger cysteine and xylose consumption, lower pH and
higher furfural in Fig. 2 / S1-S5). Signatures that do fit partition: the lipophilic MFT disulfide disappears
from the water phase; apparent oil/water FFT ratios of 36-430 for four oils. Signatures that fit **chemical
capture inside the oil**: the oil-phase thiol ranking against unsaturation (c), the ENTH x0.57-0.95 (b), MFT
undetected in 5/10 oil phases while present in their water phases (at any K > 10 it should be well above the
oil-phase detection seen elsewhere), and corn's anomalous oil/water FFT ratio of 2.6 vs 36-430 (two long-chain
triglycerides, corn and peanut, cannot differ 75x in a true partition coefficient). Capture here is a 1-40x
between-oil effect inside the oil phase, not a suppression relative to oil-free cooking. **Reading: not
consistent with a net capture-driven suppression; partition accounts for the disulfide only; the net oil effect
on free MFT/FFT is neutral to positive.** Confidence ~60 % in that sign for FFT (no SD; HS-SPME across
matrices), ~40 % for MFT (near-LOD values, disulfide ambiguity).
