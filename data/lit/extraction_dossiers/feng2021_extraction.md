## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Table 2 gives methylglyoxal at 120 °C rising **2.49 → 2.62 → 3.63 → 3.74 → 4.11 µg/mL at 60 / 90 /
120 / 150 / 180 min** (RSD 4-10 %, n = 3), and at 120 min rising **1.66 → 2.76 → 3.63 → 3.99 →
4.34 µg/mL from 100 to 140 °C** [M]. Glyoxal is 2-7× higher (4.22 → 27.64 µg/mL over time; 11.74 →
31.25 µg/mL over temperature) and diacetyl ≤ 1.19 µg/mL (nd at 100 °C and at 60-90 min). [Z] If the
unit is µg of the free dicarbonyl per mL of reaction product, methylglyoxal is 35-57 µM along the
time ladder and 23-60 µM along the temperature ladder; **if it is µg of quinoxaline** (the calibration
footnote says x is "the concentration of quinoxaline derivatives"), it is half that (17-29 µM and
12-30 µM). The xylose molarity is not stated (the hydrolysate's volume is not given); a declared
estimate of ~0.55-0.6 M xylose puts methylglyoxal at ~0.01 mol % of xylose. The pot also contains a
corn-peptide hydrolysate of unstated nitrogen content, so it is a matrix pot, not a model pot.

## 2. SYSTEM AND CONDITIONS [M], quoted

- **Hydrolysate (sec. 2.2):** "80 g of corn gluten meal [70 % protein] with the distilled water in a
  weight ratio of 1:10, ... heated at 95 °C for 30 min ... adjusted to pH 8.0", alcalase 1:100,
  180 min, then flavourzyme 1:100, 120 min; 100 °C 10 min inactivation; centrifuged 2100 × g, 20 min;
  supernatant stored at −20 °C. **Peptide / free-amino-acid content of the supernatant: not stated.**
- **Pot (sec. 2.3):** "The mixture was composed of corn peptide hydrolysate (40 g), xylose (4 g) and
  cysteine (6.4 g), and mixed by magnetic stirring. The pH was adjusted to 7.4 and heated in an oil
  bath at 100, 110, 120, 130 and 140 °C for 120 min while stirring. The reaction time intervals of 60,
  90, 120, 150 and 180 min at 120 °C were also conducted." "The MRPs preparation was performed in
  triplicate."
- **[Z] Amounts:** xylose 4 g = 26.6 mmol; L-cysteine 6.4 g = 52.8 mmol (Cys : Xyl = 1.98). If 40 g
  hydrolysate ≈ 40 mL and the solids add ~6-8 mL, xylose ≈ 0.55-0.6 M and cysteine ≈ 1.1-1.2 M
  **[!]** (assumption; not in the paper). Base used for the pH 7.4 adjustment: not stated (NaOH is
  listed among the chemicals).
- **Vessel:** not stated. 130-140 °C in an oil bath requires a closed or pressure vessel, but none is
  named; "while stirring". Heat-up and quench: not stated.
- **pH after heating:** not measured.

## 3. ANALYTICAL METHOD [M]

- **Derivatisation (sec. 2.8):** "MRPs (2 mL) were added with 3, 4-hexanedione (internal standard,
  9.60 μg/mL, 40 μL) and derivatization solution (2 mL). The mixture was further incubated in dark
  for 2 h" (OPD, after Gensberger 2013 / Feng 2017). OPD concentration and pH of the derivatisation
  solution: not stated. **Post-heating derivatisation**, no trapping agent in the pot.
- **HPLC-PAD:** Waters, SunFire C18 150 × 4.6 mm, 5 µm; water / methanol gradient, 1 mL/min, 30 °C;
  315 nm. Retention: GO 25.4, MGO 31.5, DA 37.8, 3,4-hexanedione 45.2 min.
- **Calibration (Table 1):** internal-standard ratio curves (y = area ratio analyte / IS);
  GO 0.40-25.54 µg/mL, y = 0.3216x − 0.1023, R² 0.998; MGO 0.31-20.00 µg/mL, y = 0.2662x − 0.0952,
  R² 0.999; DA 0.08-9.80 µg/mL, y = 0.3438x − 0.0005, R² 0.999; "x was the concentration of quinoxaline
  derivatives (μg/mL)". Standards from Sigma GO, MGO, DA. "no matrix effect in MRPs" (no recovery
  numbers given). LOD / LOQ: not stated.
- **Ledger class: calibrated** (external curves with an internal standard; the IS is not an isotopologue).
- **[!] Unit ambiguity (two factors of ~2):** (i) the calibration footnote expresses x as quinoxaline
  concentration, while Table 2's heading is "Concentration ... μg/mL" of GO / MGO / DA; (ii) the MRP is
  diluted 2 mL → ~4.04 mL by the derivatisation step, and the paper does not say whether Table 2 is per
  mL of MRP or of the derivatised mixture. The most natural reading (standards of the free dicarbonyls
  carried through the same derivatisation, result per mL of MRP) is used as the primary [Z] below.

## 5. USE IN THE MODEL

1. **A second methylglyoxal-from-xylose/cysteine data set** with a time ladder (60-180 min) and a
   temperature ladder (100-140 °C) from printed means with RSD. It is the only corpus set to 140 °C
   for MGO in a xylose-cysteine pot. It is a check, not a fit row, until the xylose molarity is fixed
   by a declared assumption (sec. 2) and the unit ambiguity (sec. 3) is decided.
2. **Directional checks that need no molarity:** MGO saturates early (60 → 180 min, ×1.65) while GO
   keeps rising (×6.5); both rise with temperature and flatten above 120-130 °C; diacetyl appears only
   from 110 °C / 120 min.
3. **Matrix caveat:** the corn-peptide hydrolysate supplies free amino acids and peptides at unknown
   concentration; MGO and GO are scavenged by amines, so the pot is matrix-confounded.

## 6. WHAT THE PAPER DOES NOT STATE THAT A KINETIC MODEL NEEDS

1. Total volume / mass balance → xylose and cysteine molarity.
2. Hydrolysate composition (protein, free amino acids, degree of hydrolysis).
3. Vessel (open / closed), headspace, heat-up and quench.
4. pH after heating; base used.
5. OPD concentration and derivatisation pH; whether results are per mL of MRP or of derivatised mix;
   whether the calibration is in dicarbonyl or quinoxaline mass.
6. LOD / LOQ (diacetyl "nd" entries), recovery.
