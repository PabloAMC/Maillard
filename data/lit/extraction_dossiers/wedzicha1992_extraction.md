## 1. Methods

- **Preparation (p. 361).** Glucose 0.1-1.0 M + glycine 0.1-1.0 M, **initial pH 5.5, no buffer named**,
  heated at **55 °C or 90 °C**. Non-dialysable fraction, Mr > 12 000, as in Wedzicha & Kaputo 1987;
  checked free of low-MW carbonyls (DNPH-TLC) and by a radiochemical test. Ultrafiltration sub-fractions
  12-30 k, 30-100 k, > 100 k (PM30, XM100A).
- **Microanalysis (p. 361).** C, H, N; coefficients of variation **2.5, 3.1, 2.9 %** from 10 preparations
  (method reported previously).
- **Stoichiometry (p. 363).** Melanoidin written as
  C(la+pb-x) H(ma+qb-2y) O(na+rb-2x-y) N(b), with glucose l, m, n = 6, 12, 6 and glycine p, q, r = 2, 5, 2;
  with N = b = 1: **C - 2 = 6a - x; H - 5 = 12a - 2y; O - 2 = 6a - 2x - y**, solved for a (glucose per N),
  x (CO2 per N) and y (H2O per N). This form assumes the Strecker aldehyde (formaldehyde) is retained in
  the polymer, one of two limiting cases; the other (HCHO lost) changes x and y not at all and a by at most
  +0.06 (at x = 0.38), "in proportion to the value of x" otherwise (p. 364).
- **pH drift.** Final pH printed: Table 2 runs 3.69 (55 °C) and 3.71 (90 °C); Table 3 runs 4.88, 4.39,
  4.12, 4.19, 4.41 in row order.

Because N = 1, **the printed C column is the atomic C/N ratio**: 6.6 to 9.3. Check (derived here):
6a + 2 - x reproduces every printed C to ± 0.02. The text cites these y/a values as "the data in Table 3";
they are Table 5's (a slip in the paper).

**Derived here, per row:** sugar carbon per N = 6a; glycine carbon per N = 2 - x; decarboxylated share of
incorporated glycine = x. Equimolar 1.0 : 1.0 row: 6.36 + 1.75 = 8.1, decarboxylated 25 %. 1.0 : 0.5 row:
7.50 + 1.82 = 9.3. Glycine-excess 0.1 : 0.9 row: 4.68 + 1.92 = **6.6, a = 0.78 < 1**. Propagated
microanalysis error on C/N ≈ √(2.5² + 2.9²) = 3.8 %, about ± 0.3 at C/N 8 (derived here; the paper
propagates none, and x, a small difference of large numbers, carries more).

**Text (p. 363-364, 366).** Decarboxylation "could be as great as 38 %" of incorporated glycine (0-38 %
across preparations); none detectable at 0.9 : 0.1. "With the exception of the experiment in which the
concentration of glycine far exceeded that of glucose, the stoichiometry of our polymers is in the range of
four to five molecules of glucose for every four amino acid molecules", insensitive to decarboxylation.
Dehydration close to 3 mol H2O per mol glucose. The three Mr fractions have similar stoichiometry. The
earlier paper's product (1.0 M glucose + 0.5 M glycine, pH 5.5, 90 °C, 22 h) is cited as 5 glucose : 4
glycine, 3 mol H2O, 0.17 mol CO2 (p. 359, 362); this matches Table 5's 1.0 : 0.5 row (a 1.25, x 0.18).

## 3. What it means for the model

**The live value.** The melanoidin repeat unit is a code constant, not a fitted coordinate:
`MELANOIDIN_REPEAT_UNIT_CARBON = 8`, `MELANOIDIN_REPEAT_UNIT_NITROGEN = 1`
(`src/kinetic_core/species.py` lines 350-351; anchor Martins & van Boekel 2005 Table 2 step 9,
"3-DG + Gly -> melanoidins", 3-DG C6 + intact glycine C2). It has no entry in
`core_prediction_uncertainty.json`. The trunk's predicted pool C/N of **8.42 to 9.94** is the figure
recorded in the comments of the same file (lines 369-444, Martins conditions); it was not re-run here.

**Against the unit.** At 90 °C, equimolar, this paper gives C/N 8.1 with a = 1.06 and 25 % of the glycine
decarboxylated: numerically on the unit's 8 : 1, but built differently (6.36 sugar C + 1.75 glycine C per
N, not 6 + 2). The glycine-excess row breaks the unit's floor of 8 outright (6.6, with fewer than one
glucose per nitrogen, which a 1 : 1 unit cannot express). Reactant ratio alone moves C/N from 6.6 to 9.3
at one temperature.

**Lining up the Leeds temperatures (same laboratory, same sugar and amine, initial pH 5.5):**

| T | source | charge | glucose per N, a | decarboxylated share of glycine | C/N |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 55 °C | this paper | 1.0 M Glc + 0.5 M Gly | **no composition printed** | — | — |
| 70 °C | Mundt & Wedzicha 2004 (dossier Table 1 and radiochemistry) | 0.25 M + 0.25 M, 0.2 M acetate pH 5.5 | 1/0.951 = 1.05 (derived) | 0.70 (14C) | 7.64 ± 0.21 |
| 90 °C | this paper, Table 5 | 1.0 M + 1.0 M, unbuffered, final pH 4.12 | 1.06 | 0.25 (from composition) | 8.1 |
| 90 °C | this paper, Table 5 (and the 1987 paper) | 1.0 M + 0.5 M, final pH 4.19 | 1.25 | 0.18 | 9.3 |

**On "sugar : amine ratio rises with temperature".** Between 70 and 90 °C at equimolar charge, **glucose
per nitrogen does not move (1.05 against 1.06)**. The C/N rise from 7.64 to 8.1 (about 1.2 combined
standard deviations, derived here) comes entirely from the glycine side: 70 % against 25 % decarboxylated.
That decarboxylation contrast is itself across two methods (14C labels against an elemental solve) and two
concentrations (0.25 against 1.0 M) and buffered against unbuffered, so it is weak. So this paper does
**not support** a rising sugar : amine ratio in the 70-90 °C window, and it **does not contradict** the
claim above 100 °C (Martins 2003 C/N 11-19 at 100-120 °C; Mohsin 2018 13-21 at 130-200 °C), which it never
reaches. What it adds is a confound the cross-study series must control: at one temperature, reactant
ratio moves a from 0.78 to 1.27 and C/N from 6.6 to 9.3, as wide as the 70-to-120 °C spread. Only
equimolar rows should enter the temperature series; Martins (0.2 M + 0.2 M) and Mundt qualify, Mohsin
(solvent-free) needs checking.

**Can it pin the unit?** It cannot pin a temperature-dependent ratio (one temperature with composition).
It does pin, at 90 °C, that a fixed C8N1 unit with intact glycine is the wrong structure even where its
C/N happens to agree.

## What it does not give

- Any elemental composition at 55 °C (the abstract's "all the melanoidins had similar ... stoichiometry"
  rests on 90 °C data only); any temperature series of composition.
- A measured CO2 or formaldehyde yield; x is solved from composition under a stated limiting assumption.
- Fraction of glucose or glycine converted to the Mr > 12 000 pool at 90 °C (only mg yields from 30 mL);
  buffer identity; any time series of composition.
- Any amino acid other than glycine; any protein.
