# Ohnishi & Shibamoto 1984 — EXTRACTION
### *"Volatile Compounds from Heated Beef Fat and Beef Fat with Glycine"*
### Rendered beef kidney fat (10.20 g) heated alone or with glycine (9.95 g; also fat/glycine 10:1 and 2:1) in a sealed pressurised bottle, **200 °C / 4 h, no added water**; simultaneous steam distillation / dichloromethane extraction; GC-FID / FTD and GC-MS on OV-101 and Carbowax 20M; **peak area % of the total chromatogram (Table I), single runs, no replication stated**. 143 compounds. With glycine, the saturated aldehydes fall in area share (**nonanal 7.47 → 0.54 %, hexanal 0.58 → 0.24 %, heptanal and decanal 0.90 / 4.16 → < 0.01 %**) while 2-butyl- and 2-pentylpyridine appear (6.77 / 3.03 % at 1:1). An amine (NH3) sink for lipid aldehydes in a dry, fat-continuous, 200 °C system, in composition terms only. Read 2026-11-15.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Heating beef fat at 200 °C for 4 h gives n-alkanes, n-alkenes, n-aldehydes, 2-ketones, n-alcohols,
n-alkylcyclohexanes and γ-lactones, explained by RCOO·, RCH2O·, R· and RO· radicals from the
triglyceride (Fig. 1). Adding glycine 1:1 (w/w) adds ten N-compounds, chiefly **2-butylpyridine (6.77 %)
and 2-pentylpyridine (3.03 %)**, N-alkylacetamides and N-methylpyrrole, but no pyrazines; the mixture
smelled "unpleasant rather than the expected cooked flavor". The 2-alkylpyridine yield falls steeply with
less glycine (2-butylpyridine 6.77 / 0.98 / 0.03 % at 1:1 / 2:1 / 10:1; Table II). The authors attribute
the lower nonanal and decanal shares with glycine to their conversion, with ammonia from glycine, into
2-butyl- and 2-pentylpyridine. The carbonyl amounts were "significantly low (ca. 1-nonanal = 17 mg)"; the
sample this refers to is not stated.

## 4. DERIVED [Z] — glycine 1:1 / fat alone, area-share ratios (dex)

| compound | log10 ratio |
|---|---:|
| hexanal | **−0.38** |
| nonanal | **−1.14** |
| heptanal | ≤ −1.95 (to < 0.01 %) |
| decanal | ≤ −2.62 (to < 0.01 %) |
| undecanal | −0.01 |
| 2-hexenal | +1.59 |

**These are composition shares, not amounts.** Adding 10 g of glycine adds a large set of new peaks
(N-compounds 10+ %, 6-methylheptanol, ketones) and changes the denominator; the extraction yield and the
total area are not reported, so a share falling from 7.47 % to 0.54 % does not establish an absolute
14× loss. The pattern is selective, though: undecanal is flat (−0.01 dex) while nonanal and decanal (the
proposed pyridine precursors) fall by ≥ 1 dex, and 2-hexenal rises. A dilution artefact alone would move
all shares together.

## 5. USE IN THIS REPO

**Question it was downloaded for (L1 scoping):** can an amino acid (via NH3 or the amine itself) act as a
sink for lipid aldehydes during heating, and by how much?

- **Mechanism pointer: yes, an NH3 → 2-alkylpyridine sink.** Saturated C9/C10 aldehydes are drawn down
  selectively and 2-butyl / 2-pentylpyridine appear in proportion to glycine dose (Table II). This is the
  same chemistry as the 2-pentylpyridine from 2,4-decadienal + NH3 in `mottram1989` and `farmer1989`, where
  **cysteine gives > 10× more 2-pentylpyridine than glycine** because its Strecker degradation releases NH3
  with H2S. A cysteine-specific NH3 supply is one more reason the sulfur lever can lower dienals.
- **Hexanal: −0.38 dex in share, sign only.** Not a concentration ratio; the denominator moves.
- **Distance from the P3 pot: very far.** Dry, fat-continuous, 200 °C, 4 h, glycine at ~50 % of the
  mass, no water and no pH control, versus a 20 min, 140 °C aqueous pot at pH 6–7 with 15 % pea isolate.
  At 200 °C in a dry melt, thermal radical scission of saturated chains (alkanes, alkylcyclohexanes,
  2-ketones) dominates, which is not the autoxidation route of P3.
- **P3 levers: none tested.** No sugar, no sulfur, no pH. Glycine is not a P3 lever.
- **Not usable for a declared band.** At most it corroborates the direction "free amino acid present →
  saturated aldehydes lower, alkylpyridines up", already in the L1 note's sink structure.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Area %, single runs, no replication or dispersion.** Ratios in §4 are compositional.
2. **OCR layer**: several cells are garbled; the rows in §3.1 were read from the page image.
3. **Identity of "6-methylheptan-2-one", "6-methylheptanol" and the iso-alcohols** is by library match
   and Kovats index; some carry "c" (no MS). Not used here.
4. **Glycine Strecker products almost absent** (acetaldehyde 0.01 %); the authors note Strecker
   degradation "may not have occurred sufficiently", i.e. the NH3 source in this dry melt is not
   established.
