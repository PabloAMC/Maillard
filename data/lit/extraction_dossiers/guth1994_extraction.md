## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

In stewed beef juice the sulfur odorants are **methanethiol 300 µg/kg (OAV 1560, the single most
important odorant; omitting it destroys the aroma)**, **methional 13 µg/kg (OAV 65)**,
**2-furfurylthiol 0.5 µg/kg (OAV 4)** and 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline 1.0 µg/kg (OAV 1, sensorially
irrelevant). **2-Methyl-3-furanthiol does not appear anywhere in the paper** — not in AEDA (16
odorants), not in static headspace (15 odorants), not quantified; nor do bis(2-methyl-3-furyl)
disulfide or the disulfide of FFT. Dimethyl trisulfide is at FD 1. The lipid aldehydes are hexanal
72 µg/kg (OAV 7, omission not noticed) and (E,E)-2,4-decadienal 12 µg/kg (OAV 60). The juice is
dominated in mass by acetic acid (200 mg/kg), Furaneol (8 mg/kg), acetaldehyde (6.4 mg/kg) and
butyric acid (6.1 mg/kg). A 15-odorant recombinate in a pH 5.7 coconut-oil/water emulsion matched
the juice (similarity 3.0 of 3).

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**The model's need.** Target levels in a real cooked beef for the odorants the engine predicts, as
the "what a meat analogue must reach" reference.

**Can anchor (as an external real-food level, not a benchmark pot):**

| engine output | stewed beef juice | provenance | strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| FFT | **0.5 µg/kg** (4.4 nmol/kg) | SIDA, d-FFT, duplicate | good analytically; one beef, one cook |
| methional | **13 µg/kg** (125 nmol/kg) | SIDA, d-methional | good |
| hexanal | **72 µg/kg** (0.72 µmol/kg) | SIDA, d-hexanal, calibration factor 0.73 | good |
| 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline | 1.0 µg/kg | SIDA | good (not 2-acetylthiazole) |
| butane-2,3-dione | 46 µg/kg | SIDA (headspace) | good |
| methanethiol | 300 µg/kg | SIDA after pH 7.5 derivatisation | the derivatisation at pH 7.5 for 13 h can release bound/labile MeSH; probably an upper estimate |
| **MFT** | **no number** | not detected by AEDA (FD ≥ 1 screen) or headspace | **absence, not zero** |

- **FFT : methional ≈ 1 : 26 by mass (1 : 28 molar).** A within-food ratio; usable as a soft target
  for a beef-like product if the engine predicts both in the same matrix.
- **MFT absence is informative but weak.** The screen is AEDA from the juice (not the meat) and
  headspace at 40 °C. MFT has a very low threshold (about an order of magnitude below FFT's in most
  compilations, e.g. 0.0004 vs 0.005 µg/kg in `fan2018_extraction.md` Table 5), so being
  missed at FD 1 suggests a low level in this juice, but no upper bound can be computed: the
  extraction recovery of MFT (prone to oxidative loss to its disulfide, cf.
  `hofmann1996_extraction.md`) is unknown here. Do not encode "MFT = 0 in beef".

**Cannot anchor:**
- **No kinetics, no precursor data** (no cysteine, ribose, thiamine, fat composition). The
  juice-to-meat partition is unknown; the solid meat was not analysed.
- **No pH** for the juice; "pH 5.7" is the sensory model, not a measurement.
- **The thermal history is unusual:** an open sear (10 min, temperature unstated) followed by a
  4-h braise in a sealed pan in a 200 °C oven. The liquid is near 100 °C but the pan walls above
  the liquid line are not; Maillard products from the sear dissolve into the juice. Not a
  temperature-defined pot.
- Duplicates only; no SD.
- **Concentrations are in the juice** (800 g decanted from 2 kg meat + 1 L water + 50 g oil); they
  are not per kg meat.

**Caveats:**
- [!] OCR of this scan is unreliable; use this dossier's raster-checked numbers, not a text grep.
- [!] Table 4 OAV of methanethiol (1560) is 4 % above conc/threshold (1500); immaterial.
- Hexanal's omission was not noticed by the panel (Table 6, 3.0): in this beef the lipid aldehyde
  load is sensorially minor, unlike Fan 2018's chicken broth (`fan2018_extraction.md`).
