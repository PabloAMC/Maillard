## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**DFG is in the paper: it is compound 1, N-(1-deoxy-D-fructos-1-yl)glycine.** It was synthesised,
crystallised (mp 146.5–147.5 °C dec) and titrated. Its **amine pKa2 = 8.18 ± 0.01** and **carboxyl
pKa1 = 2.20**, against **9.60 and 2.33 for glycine** measured in the same system. The conditions are
**25.0 ± 0.1 °C, I = 0.2 M KNO3**, glass electrode, PSEQUAD fit. The constants are **concentration
(stoichiometric) constants on a p[H] scale**: the electrode was calibrated against log[H⁺] in the same
medium. Fructosylation lowers the amine pKa by ~1.4–1.5 units in all six compounds. The carboxyl pKa
drops by ~0.1–0.2 only when the carboxyl is α or β to the amine (compounds 1 and 2). **No temperature
dependence, no ionisation enthalpy and no other ionic strength** are reported. Two earlier literature
values for fructose-glycine are quoted without method: **8.4 (Anet 1959) and 8.21 (Röper 1987)**.

## 6. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Can anchor:**
- **DFG amine pKa at 25 °C: 8.18 ± 0.01**, a concentration constant at I = 0.2 M (KNO3), p[H] scale. This is
  the direct measurement the speciation law needs. No analogue is required, because DFG itself was
  titrated. Two independent older values (8.4, 8.21) bracket it.
- **DFG carboxyl pKa: 2.20** (25 °C, I = 0.2 M). This is irrelevant above pH ~4: the carboxylate is fully
  deprotonated across the Maillard-relevant pH range.
- **Structural transferability:** the ~1.4–1.6 unit amine-pKa drop on fructosylation is uniform across six
  amines. For other Amadori compounds (e.g. fructosyl-lysine ε-amine: 9.02 for the N^α-formyl model 6), the
  parent amine pKa minus ~1.5 is a measured-class rule at 25 °C.

**Cannot anchor:**
- **Temperature dependence / ionisation enthalpy: not in this paper.** One temperature only. Any ΔH used
  to carry 8.18 from 25 °C to 90–145 °C must be declared from elsewhere. The closest structural analogue
  in Table 6, with a well-characterised ΔH in the literature, is **tricine** (R = (HOCH2)3C–, pKa 8.13),
  and that number is not in this paper. Glycine (9.60) is the parent but has a different amine environment.
- **Thermodynamic (I → 0) constant:** not given. [Z] Glycine's amine pKa rises from 9.60 (I = 0.2) to
  ~9.78 at I = 0 in the standard literature, so DFG's thermodynamic pKa2 is plausibly ~8.3–8.35. This is an
  analogy, not a measurement.
- **Scale mismatch [!]:** the constants are on a concentration scale (p[H] = −log[H⁺]), but the pH in
  kinetic papers is an activity-scale electrode reading. At I = 0.2 M the difference is ~0.1 unit
  (−log γH±). Declare which scale the model's pH is on before combining.
- Nothing about Amadori **degradation** rates. This is a synthesis and characterisation paper.

## 7. CAVEATS

1. DOI not printed; reconstructed from the PDF's PII metadata (§0).
2. Scanned PDF with a bad OCR layer; every number above was re-read from the raster.
3. The ± values are fit statistics from PSEQUAD over ≥ 2 titration series, not between-laboratory
   uncertainty. The calibration (e ≈ −0.995, C ≈ 0.05) contributes a systematic ~0.01–0.05.
4. KNO3 medium at I = 0.2 M is not food-like and not phosphate. Specific-ion effects on the DFG amine are
   expected to be small but are untested here.
