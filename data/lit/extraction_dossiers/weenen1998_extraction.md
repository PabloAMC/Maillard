## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**Which deoxyosone gives methylglyoxal, and how:** Scheme 1 (from a 1-13C-glucose experiment) draws
methylglyoxal (pyruvaldehyde) from **both** deoxyosones by **retro-aldolisation (RA)**: the
3-deoxyosone (favoured under "acidic" conditions) gives 13C-CHO-labelled methylglyoxal + glyceraldehyde;
the 1-deoxyosone ("neutral or alkaline") gives 13CH3-labelled methylglyoxal + the C3 counterpart; the
2,4-tautomer of the 1-deoxyosone gives hydroxyacetone by **β-cleavage**. Text: "The formation of
pyruvaldehyde can be explained as resulting from retro-aldolization of 1- and 3-deoxyglycosones".
Glyoxal "cannot be explained as originating from deoxyglycosones, nor from Amadori rearrangement
products"; it is drawn from the aldose / imine by retro-aldolisation then oxidation (Scheme 5).
**Pentoses:** no pentose mechanism is drawn; the text only notes that xylose gave "even higher yields
of pyruvaldehyde" than 3-deoxyglucosone, hypothesising that the 1- and 3-deoxyxylosones
"possibly give retroaldolization more readily, because they are less stable" (fewer stabilising
bicyclic hemiketals). **Measured pentose number [D]:** xylose + alanine (pH 8, 100 °C, 1 h, OPD-trapped)
→ **methylglyoxal ≈ 81 µg**, glyoxal ≈ 27 µg, diacetyl ≈ 28 µg, 2,3-pentanedione ≈ 42 µg; xylose
without amine → methylglyoxal ≈ 17 µg, glyoxal ≈ 62 µg. The basis of "µg" (whole pot or aliquot) is
not stated, so these are **relative** amounts (sugar-to-sugar, amine vs no amine), not yields.

## 2. SYSTEM AND CONDITIONS [M], quoted (p. 398)

"Various carbohydrates (0.55 M) and the Amadori rearrangement product of glucose and alanine (0.55 M)
were reacted with cyclohexyl amine (0.6 M), alanine (0.6 M) and without amine/amino acid, in an aqueous
phosphate buffer solution (30 ml, 0.33 M, pH 8) and n-butanol (15 ml) containing 4-methylquinoline as
internal standard, at 100 °C for 1 h (Weenen and Apeldoorn, 1996). Four α-dicarbonyl substances were
observed as volatile quinoxaline derivatives: glyoxal, pyruvaldehyde, diacetyl and 2,3-pentanedione."

- 1,2-diaminobenzene: "used as a trapping agent, which selectively traps α-dicarbonyl-containing
  substances" — i.e. **in situ during heating** **[!]** (amount not stated).
- Two-phase pot (aqueous phosphate + n-butanol); vessel, headspace, pH after heating: not stated.
- Fig. 1 = "with alanine" (the cyclohexylamine results are not plotted); Fig. 2 = no amine.
- [Z] 0.55 M × 30 mL = 16.5 mmol sugar if the 0.55 M refers to the aqueous phase.

## 5. MECHANISM DETAIL (Schemes) [M]

- **Scheme 1:** "Formation of pyruvaldehyde, glyceraldhyde and hydroxyacetone from 1-13C-glucose."
  ARP → (acidic) 3-deoxyosone → RA → 13C-labelled methylglyoxal (label on the aldehyde carbon);
  ARP → (neutral/alkaline) 1-deoxyosone → RA → methylglyoxal (label on CH3); 1-deoxyosone ⇌ 2,4-dione
  tautomer → β-cleavage → 13CH3-hydroxyacetone. Basis: label position in products from 1-13C-glucose
  (Weenen & Tjan 1994 with cysteamine trapping; NMR).
- **Hydroxyacetone:** formed from glucose and fructose "but not from 3-deoxyglucosone", hence from the
  1-deoxyosone via its 2,4-dicarbonyl isomer and β-cleavage.
- **Scheme 5:** glyoxal from hexose by RA + oxidation (not from deoxyosones).
- **Scheme 6:** diacetyl from a 1-deoxyglycosone isomer; **Scheme 7:** 2,3-pentanedione by aldol of
  diacetyl + formaldehyde.
- **Scheme 8:** sugar, ARP/HRP and deoxyglycosones all fragment "by retroaldolisation or β-cleavage";
  "Direct cleavage of Amadori rearrangement products can, however, not be ruled out."

**For the pentose route DPO → HA + MGO (engine `r_dpo_c2c3`):** Weenen supplies the hexose analogue
(1-deoxyglucosone → MGO + C3 by RA) and the hypothesis that pentose deoxyosones retro-aldolise more
readily. He does not draw or measure the C5 split, and never mentions glycolaldehyde as its partner.
Smuda & Glomb 2013 (smuda2013 dossier) later list the hexose 1-deoxyosone RA as "results disproven by
Voigt" and retro-aldolisation of dicarbonyls as "largely hypothetical".
