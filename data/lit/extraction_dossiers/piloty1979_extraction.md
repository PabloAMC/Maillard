## 1. What this paper answers for the model

The question: "say what it measures".

**What it measures:** the **% of each free amino acid lost** after 3 h with a dicarbonyl (or sugar), by ion-exchange
amino-acid analysis (Biocal 201; mean error ±3 % [M]). **It does not measure Strecker aldehydes, CO2, aminoketones or
pyrazines**; the authors say the method cannot tell "to what extent the Strecker degradation itself takes place" [V,
translated], only the first step (carbinolamine / Schiff base formation) for amino acids without other functional
groups [Q].

**Answers.**

1. **It measures amino-acid consumption by α-dicarbonyls (and sugars) in a competing mixture, as single 3 h
   end-points**, under 0.4 M phosphate. A ranking and a set of condition contrasts, not Strecker yields.
2. **Its most useful number for the engine:** with MGO at 100 °C the loss of Gly, Ala, Leu, Phe is **pH-flat from 4.5 to
   7** (rate ratio pH 4.52 / 6.97 ≈ 1.0-1.2 [Z, from −ln(1 − x)]), and at 150 °C it is highest at pH 4.5.
3. **Side reactions are large at ≥ 150 °C**: Glu cyclises (blank 53-100 %), Thr / Ser split to Gly (negative Gly and Ala
   "losses" at 200 °C), Arg hydrolyses in alkali [Q/M]. Only the 60-100 °C rows and the non-functional amino acids
   (Gly, Ala, Leu, Ile, Phe) are clean measures of the dicarbonyl reaction.

## 2. Conditions

- **Standard** (Table 1) [M]: dicarbonyl 390 mmol/l + amino-acid mixture 390 mmol/l (13 × 30 mmol/l), in **0.4 M sodium
  phosphate buffer**, 3 h, 100 °C; pH 6.9 (diacetyl) / 6.1 (MGO). Temperature series: 60, 100, 150, 200 °C (same
  concentrations). pH series: dicarbonyl 312 mmol/l + amino acids 208 mmol/l (16 mmol/l each [Z]), pH 4.52 / 6.97 /
  9.50, 100 and 150 °C. Gly-Ala: 300 / 300 and 30 / 30 mmol/l, 100 °C, pH 6.7 / 7.1. Glucose / fructose: as standard,
  pH 6.9.
- **pH**: label (buffer pH at preparation); no measured drift, no temperature correction [!].
- **Vessel / O2**: not stated (aliquots taken for the analyser); 150-200 °C implies closed vessels [Z] [!].
- **Reagents**: amino acids p.A. (Merck); diacetyl 99.5 %; MGO 40 % aqueous (Roth); Avicel cellulose.
- **Analysis**: Biocal 201 amino-acid analyser (Beckman M 82 / Aminex A 5 resin), peak height × half-width; mean error
  ±3 %. Replicates: not stated.

## 4. Mechanism evidence and its kind

- **Fed intermediate** (kind 2: the dicarbonyl is supplied, no sugar) + **within-study factors** (kind 6: pH,
  temperature, dicarbonyl concentration, amino-acid structure, water). Response is substrate loss only; no product
  identified, no mass balance.
- The Strecker scheme (Abb. 1) and the reading that α-substituents and side-chain functional groups, not the pK of the
  α-amino group, set reactivity are [Q/P]. The pH reversal between 100 and 150 °C is proposed to come from Schiff-base
  formation and decarboxylation gaining weight at lower pH and higher temperature [P].
- Lab: TU Berlin (Baltes); one paper (Part II not on disk).

## 5. Bearing on open questions

- **E4 / F16-c (the pyrazine-cascade pH law sits on the Strecker STEP, ×0.091 at pH 5 vs 6.8): SUPPORTS the remaining
  E4 claim** (the step law is wrong) from a **new laboratory**: MGO + Gly / Ala / Leu / Phe consumption at 100 °C is flat
  (×1.0-1.2 faster at pH 4.52 than 6.97) and at 150 °C clearly faster at pH 4.52. The engine's GO / MGO + Gly →
  aminoketone step at ×0.091 would predict Ala loss of ~3 % at pH 4.5 where 32 % is measured [Z, first-order transfer
  of the pH-6.97 extent]. Caveats: consumption includes non-Strecker adducts; 0.4 M phosphate; a mixture; label pH.
  Weight: moderate (P ~0.6 that it bears on the step as the engine defines it).
- **F16 amino-acid identity** ("Ile > Leu > Phe > Met with MGO", DFA): here, by consumption, Phe ≥ Leu > Ile ≈ Ala at
  100 °C (33 / 26 / 18 / 17 %) — a different order (sec. 6).
- **Hub / sugar identity:** glucose consumes amino acids faster than fructose at 100 °C, pH 6.9, in 0.4 M phosphate
  [M] (bears on the F29 tag "fructose > glucose" only indirectly: that tag is for acrylamide).
- **E5:** every pot is in 0.4 M phosphate; no buffer contrast. **CF-011, CF-031, E7:** not touched.

## 6. Disagreements found

1. **Amino-acid order with MGO.** DFA (F16 atlas summary): Strecker aldehyde yield Ile > Leu > Phe > Met. Here, by
   consumption at 100 °C: Phe 33 > Leu 26 > Ile 18 (pH 6.1). Causes: response (loss vs aldehyde yield — Ile may react
   less but give the aldehyde more cleanly), mixture competition, 0.4 M phosphate, and this paper's ±3 % error on
   losses of 15-35 %.
2. **pH direction with MGO.** Hofmann (F16#3): fed-dicarbonyl PA peaks near pH 5 and falls ×2 to pH 9. Here Phe loss
   with MGO at 100 °C is flat 4.5-9.5 (45 / 46 / 49 %). Not a conflict in direction for the engine (both reject ×0.091),
   but loss does not show Hofmann's fall to pH 9.
3. **Table 3 vs Table 4 at 150 °C** [!]: MGO + Leu is 42 % at "normal" conditions (pH 6.1, 390 / 390 mM) and 87 % at
   pH 4.52 (312 / 208 mM). Different loadings and pH, so not a contradiction, but the two tables must not be pooled.
4. **[!]** The 200 °C rows have negative "losses" (net formation of Gly, Ala from Thr / Ser) and blanks up to 100 %:
   unusable for the dicarbonyl step.
