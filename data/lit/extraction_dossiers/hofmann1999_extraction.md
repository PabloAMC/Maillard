## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

In a refluxed xylose/L-alanine pot (≈1.5 M each, pH 7.0 phosphate), **methylglyoxal rises
monotonically from 25 µg/mmol xylose at 5 min to 120 µg/mmol at 180 min [D; 120 also M] = 0.35 →
1.67 µmol/mmol (0.17 mol %) [Z]**; glucose gives almost the same late value (117 µg/mmol at 180 min,
1.62 µmol/mmol) but is ~3× slower at 10 min. **Glycolaldehyde from xylose peaks at 10 min at
7-8 µg/mmol (0.12-0.13 µmol/mmol) [D, and Z from Table 3], then falls to a plateau of ~3 µg/mmol
(0.05 µmol/mmol) from 40 to 180 min**; from glucose it rises slowly to ~18 µg/mmol (0.30 µmol/mmol)
by 120 min. Glyoxal is the earliest fragment from both sugars (xylose: 59 µg/mmol at 5 min [M],
~1.0 µmol/mmol, gone to ~1.5 µg/mmol by 40 min). 3-Deoxypentosone and 1-deoxypentosone peak at
10 min at 1260 and 610 µg/mmol [M] (~0.95 % and ~0.46 %), then plateau at ~4.6 and ~1.5 µmol/mmol.
In xylose, at every time point, **glycolaldehyde is 5-37× below methylglyoxal on a molar basis
[Z]** (the ratio falls with time); the pot does not show the two C2 + C3 fragments accumulating 1:1.
Glycolaldehyde is also the most potent browning precursor tested (CD factor 1024, Table 1), so a low
standing level is consistent with fast consumption; the paper does not measure its loss rate.

## 2. SYSTEM AND CONDITIONS [M], quoted

- **Pots (Materials and methods):** "Solutions of 6 mmol xylose or 6 mmol glucose in 4 ml phosphate
  buffer (0.5 mol/l, pH 7.0) were refluxed in the presence of 6 mmol L-alanine for the reaction
  times given in Figs. 3–6." (Figs. 4, 5, 9, 10 are the time courses.)
- **[Z] Concentrations:** 6 mmol in 4 mL buffer = 1.5 mol/L nominal for sugar and for alanine;
  the dissolved solids (0.90 g xylose or 1.08 g glucose, 0.53 g alanine) raise the volume, so the
  true molarity is lower, roughly 1.1-1.3 M **[!]** (final volume not stated). Phosphate 0.5 M in
  the buffer volume. Molar ratio 1:1.
- **Temperature:** "refluxed"; the boiling point of the concentrated solution is not stated
  (**[Z]** expect ≈ 101-103 °C at ambient pressure). Open to air through the condenser; no inert gas.
- **Stop:** results text: "the reaction was stopped by rapid cooling on ice".
- **pH:** 7.0 is the buffer's initial pH; no pH during or after heating reported.
- **Sampling:** each analytical method takes "A quarter of the reaction mixture" (osones; C2-C4
  dicarbonyls) or the whole cooled mixture (hydroxycarbonyls; furans). **[!]** Whether each time
  point is a separate pot is implied (quarters for different analyses) but not stated.
- **Replicates:** not stated; no error bars.
- **Table 1 browning-ability pots:** "Mixtures of carbohydrate degradation products (1 mmol) and
  L-alanine (1 mmol), dissolved in 5 ml phosphate buffer (0.5 mol/l, pH 7.0), were refluxed for
  15 min" (partly from ref. [4], Hofmann, Bors & Stettmeier 1999 JAFC 47:379).

## 3. ANALYTICAL METHODS [M]

**No trapping agent is present during heating** — all derivatisation follows cooling. The paper's
stated reason: in-situ trapping "leads to an accumulation of the derivatized target compounds".

1. **2-Osuloses and deoxyosones** (quarter of pot + 1 mL water + 2 mL 1 mol/L 1,2-diaminobenzene in
   methanol, 3 h at 30 °C): RP-HPLC (Hypersil RP-18, acetonitrile/ammonium formate pH 3.5),
   UV 320 nm; "quantified by using the synthesized reference compounds as external standards"
   (quinoxalines of 2-xylosulose, 2-glucosulose, 3-deoxy- and 1-deoxy-osones synthesised).
   → **calibrated** (external, authentic derivatives).
2. **Glyoxal, methylglyoxal, butane-2,3-dione** (quarter of pot, diluted, pH 6.5, [13C4]-butane-2,3-dione
   in methanol, 2 mL 1 mmol/L 1,2-diaminobenzene, 3 h at 30 °C, ether extraction, silica clean-up):
   GC-MS (CI, isobutane) on the quinoxalines; response factors from 1:3-3:1 mixtures: glyoxal 0.97,
   2-oxopropanal 0.97, butane-2,3-dione 1.00. The author calls this SIDA. **[!]** The internal standard
   is an isotopologue only of diacetyl; for glyoxal and methylglyoxal it is a labelled homologue with
   measured response factors → ledger class **calibrated** for GO / MGO, **sida** for diacetyl.
   **[!]** Only 2 µmol OPD (2 mL of 1 mmol/L) is added to a quarter of the pot, which at 10 min holds
   ~2 µmol of C2-C4 dicarbonyls and ~23 µmol of xylose-derived osones [Z: (9.5 + 4.7 + 1.2) µmol/mmol
   × 1.5 mmol xylose per quarter, Fig. 5]. If the osones compete for OPD, the reagent is limiting in
   the early xylose samples and GO / MGO / DA there are lower bounds. The osone method uses 2 mmol OPD
   (1 mol/L), so it is not affected. Not discussed in the paper.
3. **Glycolaldehyde, hydroxy-2-propanone, 3-hydroxy-2-butanone** (cooled pot, [13C4]-3-hydroxy-2-butanone,
   4 mmol ethoxamine·HCl, pH 7.5, 3 h at 30 °C; pH 6.0; ether extraction): GC-MS-CI of the
   syn/anti-O-ethyloximes, m/z 104 (glycolaldehyde), 118, 132, 136 (IS). Response factors:
   glycolaldehyde 0.96, hydroxy-2-propanone 0.99, 2-hydroxy-3-butanone 1.00. **[!]** Isotopologue only
   for acetoin → **calibrated** for glycolaldehyde and hydroxyacetone.
4. **Furfural, HMF**: thiophene-2-carboxaldehyde internal standard, ether extraction, MS-CI
   (m/z 97, 127, 113), response factors 0.97 and 0.94 → **calibrated**.
5. **Colour**: visual colour-dilution (CD) factor, triangle test against tap water.

**Units:** every concentration is **µg per mmol of starting sugar**. [Z] µmol/mmol = (µg/mmol) / M;
M: glyoxal 58.04, methylglyoxal 72.06, diacetyl 86.09, glycolaldehyde 60.05, hydroxyacetone 74.08,
acetoin 88.11, furfural 96.08, HMF 126.11, deoxypentosones 132.12, 2-pentosulose 148.11,
deoxyhexosones 162.14, 2-hexosulose 178.14. The paper's own yields check this unit: "1260 and
610 µg/mmol xylose corresponding to a yield of about 1.0% and 0.5%" (1260/132.1 = 9.5 µmol/mmol ✓).
µmol/mmol × ~1.2-1.5 ≈ mmol/L in the pot [Z].

## 5. USE IN THE MODEL

Engine names: methylglyoxal `MGO`, glycolaldehyde `HA` (`species_sulfur.py`, "hydroxyacetaldehyde
(the C2 fragment)"), hydroxyacetone `ACETOL`, furfural `FUR`, 1-deoxypentosone `DPO`, glyoxal `GO`,
diacetyl `DA`. The engine's `r_dpo_c2c3` (DPO → HA + MGO, one step, 1:1) is the reaction this pot
bears on.

1. **Glycolaldehyde (HA) observable — the only one in the corpus.** A pentose/amino-acid pot with HA
   at 5 (Table 3), 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, 120, 180 min, plateau ~0.05 µmol/mmol from 40 min. Together with
   the browning ability (CD 1024, the highest measured) it is the only measured handle on an HA sink.
   It does not give an HA loss rate directly; a sink constant would be a fit to this curve.
2. **Methylglyoxal from a pentose, second laboratory (DFA Garching, not Jiangnan).** Xylose + alanine,
   7 points 5-180 min (plus Table 3's 60-min value), calibrated. Different amine (alanine, not
   cysteine) and very high concentration (~1.2-1.5 M), so it constrains the amine-independent / Ala
   arm of MGO formation, not the cysteine pots directly.
3. **HA : MGO stoichiometry.** Measured molar ratio 0.19-0.20 at 5-10 min falling to 0.03 at 180 min.
   This is an observed standing ratio; whether it is compatible with a 1:1 source depends on the HA and
   MGO sinks, which the paper does not measure.
4. **Deoxypentosone pools.** 3-DP and 1-DP time courses (Fig. 5), the latter the engine's `DPO`;
   peak at 10 min then a flat plateau from 40 min (a plateau, not a decay to zero, at 4.6 and 1.5
   µmol/mmol). A plateau after 40 min with sugar still abundant is a quasi-steady state, so the
   curve constrains the ratio of formation to loss, not each separately.
5. **Hexose vs pentose contrast** at identical conditions: same late MGO (1.62 vs 1.67 µmol/mmol at
   180 min), xylose ~3× faster at 10 min; HA 6× higher from glucose at 120-180 min.
6. **Hydroxyacetone and furfural** (xylose), HMF (glucose), glyoxal (both) — calibrated time courses.
7. **Not usable for colour**: CD factors are visual dilution thresholds, not an absorbance.

## 6. WHAT THE PAPER DOES NOT STATE THAT A KINETIC MODEL NEEDS

1. Reflux temperature of the 1.5 M solution; heat-up time to reflux; whether t = 0 is cold start.
2. Final volume (solids raise it), so true molarity; water loss under reflux (condenser assumed).
3. pH during or after heating (0.5 M phosphate buffers strongly, but 1.5 M alanine and acid
   formation over 3 h are not checked).
4. Whether each time point is an independent pot; replicates; precision.
5. Headspace / air access (open reflux: O2 available; relevant to the 2-osuloses, which the author
   attributes to metal-catalysed oxidation).
6. Detection limits; recovery of the ether extractions (the 13C4 standards correct only for the
   compound they are the isotopologue of).
7. Sugar and alanine consumption (not measured).

## 7. FLAGS

1. **[!]** Concentration ~1.2-1.5 M sugar and alanine: far above the 10-100 mM pots of the corpus;
   activity and water-activity effects are not small.
2. **[!]** Reflux, temperature not stated (≈ 100-103 °C).
3. **[!]** "SIDA" is isotopologue SIDA only for diacetyl and acetoin; GO, MGO, glycolaldehyde and
   hydroxyacetone use labelled homologues with measured response factors (0.96-0.99) → calibrated.
4. **[!]** Diacetyl: figure and BPA tables disagree by 1.4-3.6× (sec. 4.6).
5. **[!]** Fig. 10 has no glycolaldehyde marker at 5 min and no methylglyoxal marker at 60/80 min
   (the curve crosses the axis break); Table 3 supplies 5- and 60-min values.
6. No replicates, no error bars, pH start only.
7. Registry: paper absent from `data/keys/papers.yml`.
