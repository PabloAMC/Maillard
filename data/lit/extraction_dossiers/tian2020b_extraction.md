## 4. THE THRESHOLD METHOD — complete transcription **[M]**

| item | value as printed |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | **ASTM-E1432** (ASTM International, 2011a) — ⭐ **explicitly NOT ASTM-E679-04** |
| **Task** | **3-AFC (3-alternative forced choice)**, **ten presentations** |
| **Series** | **dilution factor 2.0**, descending; panelists start at the highest concentration, *"expected to correspond to **2 concentration steps above the estimated thresholds**"* |
| **Rule** | *"If a panelist could recognize the aroma of the same sample **each time**, the sample of the next lower concentration was tested"* |
| **Threshold definition** | *"the concentration at which the **correct detection probability was 50 %**"* |
| **Psychometric function** | *"a sigmoid curve **y = 1/[1 + e^(−λx)]**, where λ is a constant parameter"* |
| **Chance correction** | **`P = (3 × p − 1)/2`**, where p = proportion correct, P = chance-corrected proportion; *"**1/3 for 3-AFC**"* |
| **Replication** | *"All experiments were performed in **triplicate**."* |
| **Panel** | **45 panelists (25 men, 20 women, age 35 ± 10 yr)**, selected from **150 candidates** including yogurt researchers; discriminative selection **3× per week over 2 weeks**; then trained on detection, recognition and identification of yogurt flavours |
| Presentation | individual **brown glass bottles**, random 3-digit codes, controlled room temperature, **~5 min per session** |

**⭐ The panel (n = 45, screened from 150) is by a wide margin the largest in the corpus's
threshold set** — cf. 10 (Brewer 1995), 16 (Vega 1994), 15 (Tian 2019/2020). **And the chance
correction is explicit and its formula is printed.** Both are strengths worth registering.

Method note: *"The concentration ranges were divided into **9 logarithmically spaced

| compound | 1 | 2 | **3 (= the threshold)** | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Diacetyl** | 2.71 | 3.84 | **5.43** | 7.67 | 10.9 | 15.4 | 21.71 | 30.7 | 43.4 |
| **Acetaldehyde** | 7.69 | 10.9 | **15.4** | 21.8 | 30.8 | 43.5 | 61.5 | 87.0 | 123 |
| **Acetoin** | 14.5 | 20.5 | **29.08** | 41.0 | 57.9 | 82.0 | 116 | 164 | 232 |

**[Z] The series ratio is √2 = 1.414 at every step** (2.71 × 1.414 = 3.83 ✔; 5.43 × 1.414 =
7.68 ✔; 43.4/2.71 = 16.0 = 2⁴ over 8 steps ✔). **⇒ These are half-log₂ steps, and position 3
is by construction the measured threshold from Table 2.** *(Acetoin's position 3 prints
**29.08** while Table 2 prints **29.0** — a trivial rounding difference. Diacetyl's position 7
prints **21.71** where the √2 series requires 21.7; likewise trivial.)*

⚠️ **[!] "mg/L, vol/vol" IS A UNIT CONTRADICTION.** `mg/L` is mass-per-volume; `vol/vol` is
dimensionless. **The Table 2 thresholds are stated as `mg/L` only and the arithmetic is
internally consistent on that basis; take mg/L and discard "vol/vol".** *(If the doses were
truly volumetric, the diacetyl figures would need multiplying by ρ = 0.99, acetaldehyde by
0.788 and acetoin by 1.01 — i.e. acetaldehyde's threshold would fall to 12.1 mg/L and its ratio
to 110×. **The 1,810×/139× the paper itself quotes are consistent with the mass reading.**)*

## 7. THE SYNERGY RESULTS — Feller's model and the σ-τ plot **[M]**

### 7a. Feller's additive model — Figure 1 **[M]**
Three binary pairs; the actual detection probability exceeded the theoretical additive
prediction in every pair.
> *"The **ratios of the actual and theoretical detection thresholds** ranged from a minimum
> factor of **1.81 for the acetaldehyde and acetoin pair** (Figure 1c) to **2.31 for the diacetyl
> and acetoin pair** (Figure 1b). This suggested that, **due to the similar aromas of diacetyl
> and acetoin**, this pair may yield the strongest synergistic effects … all 3 were **carbonyl
> compounds**, and thus **structural similarities** may have led to these synergistic effects."*

| pair | **actual / theoretical threshold ratio** |
|---|---:|
| **diacetyl + acetoin** | **2.31** (strongest) |
| diacetyl + acetaldehyde | *between* |
| **acetaldehyde + acetoin** | **1.81** (weakest) |

**⇒ ⭐ MEASURED BINARY SYNERGY OF 1.8–2.3× ON THE THRESHOLD ITSELF, IN A DAIRY MATRIX, BETWEEN
THREE CARBONYLS. Register `binary_carbonyl_synergy_in_dairy_matrix: 1.81–2.31× threshold
reduction [M]`. Any repo OAV summation that treats these compounds as independent
under-predicts the perceived intensity by ~2×** — the same order as, and consistent with, the
super-additivity `k2_matrix_and_thresholds.md` §B.6 records for protein + mucin binding.

### 7b. The σ-τ diagram — Figure 2 **[M]**
Definitions as printed: **τ = I_A/(I_A + I_B)** (the intensity share of one unmixed component)
and **σ = I_mix/(I_A + I_B)** (the interaction level). Categories: σ > 1 superadditive;
σ = 1 perfectly additive; σ < 1 low-additive, subdivided into partially additive / compromise /
subtractive (Frijters, 1987). Hyper-additivity threshold used: **σ > 1.05**.

> *"most points were located in the **region of partial addition** … Of the **72 mixtures, 5 were
> located in the region of hyper-additivity (σ > 1.05)**, whereas only … τ values of **0.472,
> 0.753, and 0.313** (Figure 2b panel 1) and **0.716, 0.454, and 0.752** (Figure 2b panel 2). The
> corresponding pairs of **diacetyl and acetoin** concentrations were **4.00 to 16.0, 4.00 to
> 32.0, and 16.0 to 32.0 mg/L** … For the pairs of **acetaldehyde and acetoin** (Figure 2c),
> **1 mixture (τ = 0.765 / 0.334) was classified in the region of hyper-additivity**, and
> **one mixture (τ = 0.792 / 0.152) was classified in the region of perfect additivity**."*

> *"**hyper-additivity was observed frequently when the concentrations of the aroma compound
> pairs were at the threshold level**. This finding was consistent with the results reported by
> Wu et al. (2012) that **flavor is enhanced when the odorants are added at threshold levels**."*

**⇒ [Z] 5 of 72 mixtures (7 %) are hyper-additive, and they cluster at threshold-level
concentrations. Synergy is a near-threshold phenomenon here, not a general one. Register
`synergy_regime: near_threshold_only`.**

### 7c. The optimal ratios [M]

> **1:2 for the acetaldehyde and acetoin pair** … For the diacetyl and acetoin pair,
