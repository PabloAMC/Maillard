## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**This is the cleanest dose series in the three-paper set: four phosphate concentrations at a fixed
and *verified* pH.** 0.1 M glucose + 0.1 M glycine in phosphate or citrate buffer at 0.02, 0.05,
0.2 and 0.5 M, initial pH 7.02 ± 0.02, 25 °C, 160 days. The measured pH stayed within **6.86–7.05**
in phosphate and **7.01–7.20** in citrate over 142 days (Table 2). **Pseudo-zero-order glycine loss
rises from 0.00055 to 0.0033 M/month across 0.02→0.5 M phosphate (×6 for ×25 in concentration).**
Browning is zero at 0.02 and 0.05 M, 0.017 OD/month at 0.2 M and 0.076 at 0.5 M, after a lag of
roughly 55–60 days. **Citrate at every concentration gives rates "not significantly different
from zero" for both responses.** When 0.2 M citrate is added to 0.25 M phosphate (0.2 M
glucose/glycine, 37 °C), it slightly *raises* the rates (Fig. 4), so citrate does not inhibit; it
simply does not catalyse at 25 °C. **There is no unbuffered control.** The buffer-free rate k0 has
to be extrapolated. A straight line through the four phosphate rates fits within the 95 % CL and
gives **κ ≈ 10–11 M⁻¹ (1σ range ~7–17 M⁻¹) for glycine loss at pH 7.0, 25 °C** [Z]. A power law
with exponent 0.56 fits the same four points equally well, so "linear with intercept" and
"sub-linear without intercept" cannot be told apart.

## 4. DERIVED QUANTITIES [Z] (mine, not printed)

### 4.1 Dose response of glycine loss (pH 7.0, 25 °C, 0.1 M Glc/Gly)

Fit of rate = a + b·[P] to the four phosphate rates:

| fit | a = k0 extrapolated (M/month) | b (M/month per M) | κ = b/a (M⁻¹) | χ² (2 dof) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| weighted by the 95 % CL (σ = CL/1.96) | 0.00055 ± 0.00017 | 0.0062 ± 0.0009 | **11.3** (1σ ~7–17) | 1.4 |
| unweighted | 0.00056 | 0.0056 | **10.2** | — |

- **Linear with intercept fits within error** (residuals ≤ 0.4σ except the 0.5 M point at 0.75σ).
- **A power law with no intercept fits equally well**: rate ∝ [P]^**0.56**. Four points with these
  CLs cannot separate "linear with a buffer-free rate k0" from "sub-linear with k0 ≈ 0".
- **[!] The intercept is not a measured buffer-free rate.** It equals the 0.02 M phosphate rate
  within error, and the citrate rates (non-detects) are formally *below* it. If citrate is
  non-catalytic and k0 really were 0.00055 M/month, the 0.5 M citrate rate should have been
  ~0.00055 M/month, about the size of the 0.02 M phosphate CL, which is plausibly undetectable.
  So the citrate zeros do not rule the intercept out. They do put **k0 at the detection floor**, so
  κ is uncertain by roughly ×2 in either direction.
- **Increment form (independent of k0):** the phosphate-catalysed increment is
  **0.0062 ± 0.0009 M month⁻¹ per M phosphate**. As a third-order constant over [Glc][Gly] =
  0.01 M²: **0.62 M⁻² month⁻¹ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ M⁻² s⁻¹** (pH 7.0, 25 °C). This is the robust quantity.
- Second-order apparent constants (rate/[Glc][Gly]): **0.33 M⁻¹ month⁻¹ = 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ M⁻¹ s⁻¹** at
  0.5 M phosphate; **0.055 M⁻¹ month⁻¹ = 2.1 × 10⁻⁸ M⁻¹ s⁻¹** for the extrapolated k0.

### 4.2 Dose response of browning (post-lag zero-order slope)

0.017 → 0.076 OD/month for 0.2 → 0.5 M: ×4.5 for ×2.5, i.e. **apparent order ≈ 1.6 in phosphate,
super-linear**, with zero at ≤ 0.05 M. A threshold or lag-limited response like this cannot be
written as k0(1 + κ[P]) with one κ: k0 is 0 within error and the slope steepens. The super-linearity
is what a chain of two or more phosphate-accelerated steps would give (cf. Potman 1989 order
1.3 in sugar loss; Rizzi 2004 A420 ∝ t^2.6 in phosphate).

### 4.3 Citrate

- **25 °C, pH 7:** κ_citrate ≈ 0, i.e. not resolvable above the detection floor at any
  concentration up to 0.5 M.
- **37 °C add-back:** the glycine-rate ratio is 1.14 [F]. Writing k = k0(1 + κP·0.25 + κC·0.2) and
  taking κP ≈ 10 M⁻¹ gives **κC ≈ 0.14·(1 + 2.5)/0.2 ≈ 2.5 M⁻¹**, with a 0–5 M⁻¹ range from the
  ±12 % slope read error. **This is ≤ 1/4 of phosphate per mole, and it is confounded by the large
  ionic-strength step that 0.2 M citrate adds.**
- Citrate at pH 7.0 is ~80 % citrate³⁻ (pKa3 ≈ 6.4). So the inactive carboxylate here is the
  trianion. This contradicts Rizzi 2004 (0.1 M citrate catalytic at ~100 °C); see §6.

### 4.4 Buffer species and ionic strength (not controlled)

At pH 7.0, 25 °C, with apparent pKa2 ≈ 6.8–6.95 at these ionic strengths (Davies), phosphate is
**~55–60 % HPO4²⁻ / 40–45 % H2PO4⁻**, the same split at every concentration because pH is fixed.
**This paper cannot say which phosphate species catalyses.** Ionic strength rises from **~0.04 M
(0.02 M phosphate) to ~1.1 M (0.5 M phosphate)**, so κ here absorbs any salt effect. Na⁺ vs K⁺ is
unknown.

### 4.5 Temperature (one comparison; weak)

At 37 °C with 0.25 M phosphate and 0.2 M Glc/Gly, glycine loss is 0.0017 M/day = 0.052 M/month →
**k2 ≈ 1.3 M⁻¹ month⁻¹**. At 25 °C, interpolated at 0.25 M phosphate, it is 0.0021 M/month over
0.01 M² → **0.21 M⁻¹ month⁻¹**. The ratio ≈ 6 gives **Ea ≈ 115 kJ/mol**. This is a two-point
estimate with different reactant concentrations, a different phosphate:reactant ratio, an
unreported pH at 37 °C and a digitised slope. **Do not promote it.**

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**The model's need:** a general buffer-catalysis multiplier k_obs = k0·(1 + κ·[buffer]) on Maillard
steps, with κ per buffer species and the step it acts on.

**Can anchor (best use):**
1. **Phosphate κ on amine loss (sugar + amine → early adduct, as glycine disappearance) at pH 7.0,
   25 °C: κ ≈ 10 M⁻¹ (1σ 7–17).** This is the only one of the three papers with a 4-point dose
   series at a *measured, held* pH and without a hot-pH problem. It is a hold-out-quality
   constraint on the *ratio* k(0.5 M)/k(0.02 M) = 6.0 (95 % CL range roughly 2.5–∞, because
   the 0.02 M rate's CL nearly reaches zero). The ratio k(0.5 M)/k(0.2 M) = 1.74 ± 0.5 is tighter.
2. **Absolute catalysed increment:** 2.4 × 10⁻⁷ M⁻² s⁻¹ (third order, [Glc][Gly][P]) at 25 °C,
   pH 7.0. It is usable as a low-temperature anchor if the model writes catalysis as an additive
   third-order term instead of a multiplier, which avoids the poorly known k0.
3. **Citrate (trianion) κ ≈ 0 at 25 °C, pH 7** (≤ ~5 M⁻¹ at 37 °C). A model that assigns a
   generic carboxylate κ comparable to phosphate fails this.
4. **Browning's dose response is super-linear (order ≈ 1.6) with a lag of ~55–60 d at 25 °C that
   does not shift with phosphate.** This is a qualitative shape check, not a κ.

**Cannot anchor:**
- **The step.** Glycine loss is *net* amine loss (glycosylamine + Amadori formation minus glycine
  regenerated). Neither Amadori product nor sugar loss is measured, so κ cannot be split between
  glycosylamine formation and the Amadori rearrangement. Bell's mechanism figure (Fig. 3:
  bifunctional H2PO4⁻/HPO4²⁻ proton shuttle in carbinolamine dehydration) is a proposal with no
  data behind it.
- **Species attribution** (H2PO4⁻ vs HPO4²⁻): single pH.
- **Temperature transfer:** 25 °C only (one 37 °C add-back). Cooking-temperature κ must come from
  elsewhere (Potman, Rizzi), and κ need not be temperature-independent.
- **Buffer-free k0:** not measured. It is extrapolated to the detection floor.

## 6. CAVEATS AND FLAGS

1. **[!] No replicate samples**: one vial per time point. The CLs come from the regression scatter
   alone.
2. **[!] Ionic strength co-varies with buffer** (0.04 → 1.1 M). κ is "phosphate + salt" and may
   overstate the specific catalytic effect, or understate it if salt slows the reaction.
3. **[!] Sodium azide 0.2 %** (31 mM N3⁻) is present in every vial; its effect is not tested.
4. **[!] Citrate "0" values are non-detects without a stated floor.** Do not encode them as a
   measured zero rate; encode them as an upper bound at roughly the 0.02 M phosphate rate.
5. **Contradiction with Rizzi 2004**, where 0.1 M citrate at ~100 °C, pH 7.3 gave 7.6× bis-tris
   browning (xylose/β-alanine). The two are reconcilable only if carboxylate catalysis has a much
   higher activation energy than phosphate catalysis, or if the Rizzi signal is sugar-only
   degradation that is negligible at 25 °C. Bell's own explanation (Saunders & Jervis: pH 3 and
   121 °C "probably changed the mechanism") is speculation.
6. Bell cites Potman & van Wijk 1989 for glucose loss rising with phosphate at pH 5.6, 100 °C, and
   says "the high temperature used makes the reported pH value questionable." The same objection
   applies to Rizzi 2004 and is absent here: this is the only paper of the three with pH measured
   at the reaction temperature.
