## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Equimolar 0.1 M sugar + amino acid in distilled water, "pH was set to 9" (Table 1 also 3, 5, 7), heated
in swing-top bottles in an oven at 100–160 °C for 2 h, cooled under running water. α-Dicarbonyls by
derivatisation with o-phenylenediamine at pH 12 (2 h) to quinoxalines, ethyl-acetate extraction and
GC-NPD against 0.5–100 µg/mL standards. **Glucose–lysine (Table 1):** GO 0.36–16.78, MGO trace–50.81,
DA N.D.–2.27 µg/mL; dicarbonyls rise with temperature and are highest at pH 9, where **MGO jumps from
5.79 (pH 7) to 50.81 µg/mL (pH 9) at 160 °C** (0.71 mol % of glucose). **Sugar × amino acid at 160 °C,
pH 9 (Table 2):** GO 5.92–39.10, MGO 3.66–151.88, DA 1.10–6.12 µg/mL; **cysteine gives the lowest
dicarbonyls with every sugar**, fructose–threonine the highest MGO. Volatiles (pyrazines, 2-acetylthiazole,
benzaldehyde) are reported only as peak-area ratios to methyl cinnamate. For the trunk: **GO, MGO and DA
for glucose–lysine at 100–140 °C and pH 5–7 are the nearest to the trunk's envelope**, but the amine is
lysine not glycine, the pH is unbuffered and unverified after heating, time is not stated for Table 1,
and 160 °C/pH 9 is far outside the trunk's measured window (pH 5.5–6.8, Martins 2003/2005 at 100–120 °C).

### 4.3 Shape features of Table 1 worth a test

- **Temperature at pH 7 (the pH nearest the trunk's reference 6.8):** GO 9.5 → 16.7 → 33.1 → 272 µM,
  MGO 3.7 → 1.5 → 4.3 → 80 µM, DA N.D. → 8.8 → 10.0 → 4.8 µM for 100 → 120 → 140 → 160 °C at (presumably)
  2 h. GO rises ×29 over 100–160 °C; MGO is **non-monotone** between 100 and 140 °C (all three values
  < 5 µM, i.e. near the 0.5 µg/mL lowest calibrator ≈ 7 µM — below it, Flag 3).
- **pH at 160 °C:** MGO 8.6 → 49 → 80 → 705 µM (pH 3 → 9): ×8.8 from pH 7 to 9. GO peaks at pH 7.
- **pH 9 step at 140 °C:** GO ×8.7, MGO ×50 versus pH 7.
- **DA** stays within 4–26 µM everywhere, with no clean temperature or pH trend.
- **pH 3** gives non-trivial GO/MGO/DA at 120–160 °C: in an unbuffered glucose–lysine solution at pH 3
  the Maillard amine is mostly protonated, so caramelisation/fragmentation chemistry dominates there.

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**The trunk's relevant state.** `species.py` carries `MGO` (measured in the trunk's fit corpus),
`GO` and `DA` (declared **not measured** in the fit corpus; `TRUNK_ONLY_KEYS`), and `TDG`
(3-deoxyglucosone). The trunk's kinetic constants are Martins & van Boekel glucose–**glycine**, 0.1 M
phosphate, pH 6.8 reference, with a declared pH term measured only on **pH 5.5–6.8**
(`trunk_conditions.PH_MEASURED_WINDOW`) at 100/120 °C, and a declared a_w term.

**3-Deoxyglucosone: not measured here.** Only GO, MGO, DA quinoxalines were calibrated; 3-DG's
quinoxaline is not mentioned. Nothing for `TDG`.

**Candidate hold-outs, ranked by how close to the trunk's envelope they sit:**

| rank | cells | why usable | why weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Table 1, **pH 5 and pH 7 at 100 °C and 120 °C** (GO, MGO; DA at 120 °C) | temperature inside the trunk's measured range; pH 5/7 bracket 5.5–6.8; absolute µg/mL with SD | **lysine, not glycine** (two amines, different reactivity — `species.py` warns the rates do not transfer); **unbuffered**, pH set before heating only (an unbuffered glucose–amine solution acidifies on heating, so the "pH 7" cells probably react at a lower and falling pH); time for Table 1 not stated (2 h assumed); **MGO values 0.11–0.27 µg/mL are below the lowest calibrator (0.5 µg/mL)** |
| 2 | Table 1, pH 5 and 7 at 140 °C | GO/DA above the lowest calibrator | temperature outside Martins' 100–120 °C (extrapolation in T); same amine/pH caveats; MGO 0.31–0.69 µg/mL at/below calibration floor |
| 3 | Table 1, 160 °C, any pH; all pH 9 cells; Table 2 | large, well-quantified signals | 160 °C in an oven-heated swing-top bottle (internal pressure ≈ 6 bar from water alone; seal integrity unknown); **pH 9 and pH 3 are outside every measured pH window in the trunk**; Table 2 adds fructose (a trunk species), maltose, lactose, serine, threonine and cysteine (not trunk amines) |

**Directional/shape claims that could be tested without trusting absolute levels:**

- **D1 (temperature, glucose–lysine, pH 7, 100 → 120 °C):** GO rises (9.5 → 16.7 µM, ×1.8); MGO is
  flat/near the floor. Within the trunk's temperature span; pH 7 is just above the 6.8 reference.
- **D2 (pH 5 → 7 at 100/120 °C):** GO rises modestly (×1.2, ×1.1); MGO at the floor. Compatible in sign
  with the trunk's declared "every DFG-consuming step is faster at pH 6.8 than 5.5", but the GO effect
  is small and the amine differs.
- **D3 (amine identity, 160 °C pH 9):** cysteine gives the lowest GO + MGO with every sugar (0.17–0.51
  mol % vs 0.6–2.3 for Lys/Ser/Thr). Qualitatively consistent with thiol scavenging of dicarbonyls; the
  sulfur lane is not built for glucose + cysteine at pH 9, 160 °C, so this is context, not a test.
- **D4 (sugar, 160 °C pH 9):** fructose ≥ glucose for MGO with Lys/Ser/Thr (×1.3–2.8), and the
  disaccharides make much less MGO but comparable GO. The trunk carries fructose as glucose's isomer;
  an "MGO from fructose > from glucose at high pH" claim is outside the trunk's pH window.

**What it cannot anchor:** any rate constant (one time point per condition, no time course), any
activation energy (temperature series at one time, with unbuffered and unmeasured pH, oven come-up not
counted), any pH term (unbuffered, pH not re-measured), the volatiles (PAR only; no calibration for
pyrazines, which the trunk's `PZ`/`DMP` species would need in mol), and anything about 3-DG. **None of
these cells should enter a fit**; at most rank-1 cells could become declared out-of-corpus hold-outs
with an explicit "lysine-for-glycine, unbuffered" caveat, and the model's own envelope would likely
(correctly) refuse rank-3 cells.

**Artefact risk specific to the assay (Flag 2):** derivatising a sugar-containing reaction mixture
with o-phenylenediamine **at pH 12 for 2 h at room temperature** can generate GO/MGO/DA from unreacted
sugar and Amadori products during the derivatisation itself (alkaline enolisation/retro-aldol).
Residual sugar is largest where conversion was least (100–120 °C cells), so **the low-temperature
values are the most exposed to an upward artefact** — exactly the cells closest to the trunk.

## 6. FLAGS [!]

1. "Equimolar solutions (0.1 M)" — final concentration 0.1 M each or 0.05 M each after mixing is not
   stated.
2. **Derivatisation at pH 12 for 2 h** in the presence of sugar: possible formation of α-DCs during
   the assay; no blank (sugar alone, unheated) reported.
3. **Values below the lowest calibrator** (0.5 µg/mL): every MGO at 100–120 °C except 120 °C/pH 3 and
   pH 9; several DA values; reported without LOD/LOQ.
4. **Table 1 heating time not stated**; the 160 °C/pH 9 row equals Table 2's Glu + Lys (MGO SD 0.59
   vs 0.60), consistent with the same 2 h run.
5. Text: "In the Glu–Thr model system, 2-ethyl-3,5-dimethylpyrazine was the highest detected with peak
   area ratio 0.7304" — the table assigns 0.7304 to **2-ethyl-3,6-dimethylpyrazine**; the table sum
   (1.5351) supports the table.
6. Text: Lac–Thr 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine "0.04961"; table 0.4961, and the table sum 0.5224 supports the
   table.
7. Text lists "2,3-dimethyl-5-methylpyrazine" (a misnomer for 2,3,5-trimethylpyrazine) and five "sweet"
   compounds (5-methylfurfural, furaneol, homofuraneol, norfuraneol) that were **never detected** in any
   system.
8. Unbuffered pH, set once before heating; no post-heating pH; acid/base not named.
9. Swing-top bottles at 160 °C in an oven: vessel volume, headspace, seal and come-up time unknown;
   volatile α-DCs (DA especially) could be lost through the seal.
10. The pH 3 cells (all temperatures) are dominated by sugar degradation, not amine chemistry.
11. L-Norvaline is listed among the chemicals and never used (perhaps an unused internal standard).
12. The abstract says reactions "were performed at 160 °C and pH 9" (true only for Table 2).
