## 1. ⭐ THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**Two activation energies: 28 kJ/mol (acrylamide + benzyl mercaptan) and 30 kJ/mol
(acrylamide + N-acetylcysteine), both from a six-to-eight-point temperature series over
80–180 °C at pH 7 under nitrogen.** They are **the smallest measured activation energies in
the corpus's electrophile–nucleophile set** and the authors draw the correct conclusion from
that themselves [Q]: *"The activation energy of this reaction is **quite low (28–30 kJ/mol)**.
Therefore, it is expected to be produced **to a high extent at relatively low temperatures**."*

**Two further results make the paper worth more than its two numbers:**
1. **⭐ THE ADDUCT IS IRREVERSIBLE, AND IT IS TESTED, NOT ASSERTED.** The synthesized adduct
   3-(benzylthio)propanamide was heated **alone at 180 °C for 0–35 min under nitrogen** and
   yielded *"only trace amounts"* of acrylamide (Fig. 3C). **This is a real reverse-reaction
   experiment with a real negative result — the kind of test that
   `meynier2004_extraction.md` §4a and `shepelev2024_extraction.md` §3d both record as
   entirely absent from the aldehyde–protein literature.**
2. **⭐ IT IS THE EXACT COUNTERPART TO `zamora2010`.** Same lab, same year, same electrophile,
   same silica-gel/a_w 0.95 method, same 180 °C reference — but an **amine** nucleophile
   instead of a thiol. **The amine adduct IS reversible (Eₐ_reverse = 52 kJ/mol); the thiol
   adduct is not.** Together the two papers give the corpus its only matched
   reversible/irreversible pair for Michael chemistry (`k6b_adduct_kinetics_synthesis.md` §4).

## 3. ⭐⭐ THE ACTIVATION ENERGIES **[M]**

### 3a. The rate law and the temperature set
> *"acrylamide disappearance **increased linearly as a function of time** when 0.2 µmol of
> acrylamide and 20 µmol of benzyl mercaptan were heated **under nitrogen at temperatures from
> 80 to 180 °C**. By employing the **disappearance rates obtained from the slopes of the lines
> in Fig. 3A**, it was possible to determine the activation energy (Eₐ) … by means of an
> **Arrhenius plot (Fig. 4)**."*

**Temperatures assayed (Fig. 3 caption, verbatim):**
**180, 160, 140, 120, 110, 100, 90 and 80 °C** — ⭐ **EIGHT temperatures.**
*(The densest temperature series in the Instituto de la Grasa Eₐ set: cf. 4 points in
`hidalgo1993`, 4–7 in `zamora2013`, 4 in `zamora2010`.)*

### 3b. **THE VALUES**

| system | **Eₐ (kJ/mol)** | conditions |
|---|---:|---|
| **acrylamide + benzyl mercaptan** | **28** | pH 7, N₂, 80–180 °C, 0.2 : 20 µmol, a_w 0.95, silica gel |
| **acrylamide + N-acetylcysteine** | **30** | identical |

Verbatim: *"The activation energy (Eₐ) for the disappearance of acrylamide in the presence of
benzyl mercaptan was **28 kJ/mol**."* … *"The activation energy (Eₐ) for the disappearance of
acrylamide in the presence of N-acetylcysteine was **30 kJ/mol**, **very similar** to the above
described."*

⚠️ **[!] BOTH VALUES ARE PRINTED AS BARE INTEGERS with no decimal, no R², no standard error and
no confidence interval.** Treat as **28 ± 3** and **30 ± 3**. The abstract's *"28–30 kJ/mol"* is
a range across two nucleophiles, **not** an uncertainty interval.

⚠️ **[!] THE OBSERVABLE IS ACRYLAMIDE *DISAPPEARANCE*, NOT ADDUCT *APPEARANCE*.** Under
nitrogen the two coincide (*"mercaptans react very rapidly with acrylamide producing the
corresponding addition product"*, and the adduct is the only new GC-MS peak). **Under air they
do not** — §5 shows a radical channel consumes acrylamide **without** producing the adduct.
**⇒ The 28–30 kJ/mol is valid only for the anaerobic Michael channel.
Register `atmosphere: N2_REQUIRED`.**

### 3c. **[Z] What 28–30 kJ/mol means numerically**
Acceleration from 25 °C: **80 °C → 6.9× · 140 °C → 22× · 180 °C → 44×** (at Eₐ = 29).
**Q₁₀ near 300 K ≈ 1.4.**
**⇒ This is a reaction that is only weakly temperature-sensitive — which is exactly the
authors' own conclusion, and it puts thiol-Michael chemistry in the same low band as
`shepelev2024`'s aldehyde–protein adduction (15–20) and well below the browning /
Strecker band (50–67).**

## 4. ⭐⭐ THE REVERSIBILITY TEST — a real experiment with a real negative **[M]**

> §3.2, verbatim: *"**The addition of the benzyl mercaptan to acrylamide was not reversible.**
> Thus, **only trace amounts of acrylamide were formed** when **0.2 µmol of
> 3-(benzylthio)propanamide was heated under nitrogen at 180 °C for 0–35 min** (Fig. 3C)."*

And in the Discussion, with the explicit contrast:
> *"**Differently to the adduct produced between acrylamide and amino compounds** (Zamora,
> Delgado, & Hidalgo, 2009), **this reaction is not reversible under the assayed conditions**
> and only trace amounts of acrylamide were found when 3-(benzylthio)propanamide was heated
> under nitrogen at 180 °C for 35 min."*

**⇒ ⭐⭐ THE PROTOCOL IS THE TEMPLATE THE ALDEHYDE–PROTEIN LITERATURE HAS NEVER RUN:
synthesize and purify the adduct → heat it alone under the same conditions → quantify the
released electrophile against a labelled internal standard.** Three sentences of method; a
definitive answer. **`meynier2004_extraction.md` §4a, `anantharamkrishnan2020b_extraction.md`
§5d and `shepelev2024_extraction.md` §3d all record the same gap for aldehyde–protein adducts,
and this paper shows exactly what would close it.** Register as the **methodological
prescription** attached to that standing null.

⚠️ **Its limit, stated by the authors: "under the assayed conditions" — anhydrous-ish silica at
a_w 0.95, pH 7, N₂, 180 °C, 35 min. A thioether C–S bond is genuinely robust; a Schiff base
C=N is not. The IRREVERSIBILITY RESULT IS SPECIFIC TO THE THIOETHER AND MUST NOT BE
TRANSFERRED TO AN ALDEHYDE–AMINE ADDUCT.** `zamora2010_extraction.md` shows the amine analogue
of *the very same electrophile* behaving oppositely.

## 5. THE OXYGEN RESULT — a second channel, and an antioxidant inversion **[M][Q]**

### 5a. What air does
> *"when the nitrogen … was replaced by air, a **significant increase in acrylamide
> disappearance** was observed (Fig. 5). This increase was **not a consequence of the
> destruction of acrylamide by the oxygen** because the presence, or not, of oxygen **did not
> have a significant role in acrylamide losses when benzyl mercaptan was absent**."*
> *"When an acrylamide/benzyl mercaptan reaction mixture was heated **under air**, … **the
> addition product was not observed** by GC-MS and a **new major compound** was observed …
> identified as **dibenzyl sulfide**."*
> *"the produced dibenzyl sulfide was **not** the responsible for the acrylamide disappearance
> because when acrylamide/**dibenzyl sulfide** mixtures were heated under either nitrogen or
> air, **acrylamide losses were much reduced** (Fig. 5)."*

**Mechanism proposed [C]:** *"The oxidation of a mercaptan to its corresponding disulfide may
involve the formation of a **thiolyl radical** (Wallace, Schriesheim, & Bartok, 1963).
Therefore, and in addition to the dimerization of this radical …, **this radical might also
react with acrylamide**."*

### 5b. ⭐ The antioxidant experiment — Figure 5 **[M]**
Conditions: **0.2 µmol acrylamide + 20 µmol mercaptan + 0–2 µmol gallic acid, 10 min at 180 °C,
pH 7.** Gallic acid levels: **GA1 = 0.5 µmol, GA2 = 1 µmol, GA3 = 2 µmol.**
*"Values are mean ± SD for, at least, two independent experiments."*

> *"**In the presence of nitrogen, acrylamide disappearance remained constant** … However,
> **in the presence of air, acrylamide disappearance decreased** and there was an **inverse
> correlation (r = 0.996, p = 0.0037)** between acrylamide disappearance and the amount of
> gallic acid added … In fact, **in the presence of 2 µmol of gallic acid, acrylamide
> disappearance in the presence of benzyl mercaptan was identical when either nitrogen or air
> was present**."*

**⇒ ⭐⭐ A MEASURED, QUANTIFIED, COUNTER-INTUITIVE RESULT: AN ANTIOXIDANT *PRESERVES*
ACRYLAMIDE.** The authors state it plainly [Q]: *"antioxidants are able to **inhibit the
formation of thiolyl radicals** and, therefore, **protect acrylamide** … This effect **may, to
some extent, counteract the inhibition of acrylamide formation described for some antioxidants**
and may explain that antioxidants can exhibit **different effects depending on the food
composition**."* **Register `gallic_acid_effect_on_acrylamide: PRESERVES_under_air (r = 0.996,
p = 0.0037), NULL_under_nitrogen [M]`. A repo module that treats antioxidants as monotonically
acrylamide-reducing is contradicted by direct measurement.**

## 6. THE OTHER MEASURED DEPENDENCES **[M]**

### 6a. Mercaptan concentration — Figure 1 (N₂, 30 min, 180 °C, pH 7)
- **Benzyl mercaptan:** *"only **15 %** of the initial acrylamide remained when **0.2 µmol** of acrylamide was heated in the presence of **20 µmol** of benzyl mercaptan"* ⇒ **85 % eliminated at a 100 : 1 nucleophile excess.**
- **N-acetylcysteine:** *"seemed to be **more reactive** than benzyl mercaptan and **almost 50 % of acrylamide disappeared** after heating … in the presence of **1 µmol** of N-acetylcysteine."* ⇒ **50 % at a 5 : 1 excess — a ~20× potency advantage at low dose.**
- But at **20 µmol** both are *"very similar"* — **the ranking depends on dose and collapses at saturation.** [!]

### 6b. pH — Figure 2 (N₂, 30 min, 180 °C, 0.2 : 20 µmol)
- **Benzyl mercaptan:** *"acrylamide **decreased as the pH increased from pH 2.15 to 6**, when sodium citrate buffer was used. A **lower decrease** was observed when the sodium citrate buffer was **replaced by sodium phosphate**. In fact, an **analogous disappearance** … at pH either **7 or 8**."*
- **N-acetylcysteine, with numbers:** *"Acrylamide disappearance increased **from 79 % to 87 %** when pH increased **from pH 2.15 to 6**, and this value increased to **90 %** when pH increased to **8**."*

**⚠️ [!] THE BUFFER IDENTITY, NOT ONLY THE pH, MOVES THE RESULT** — the authors say so
explicitly and the figure distinguishes open (citrate) from filled (phosphate) symbols at the
overlapping pH 6. **The identical hazard as `hidalgo1993_extraction.md` §2's pH 7.5→8 dip.
Register `buffer_confound: citrate_vs_phosphate_at_pH6` on both papers.**

**⭐ Direction: base-favoured, consistent with thiolate (not thiol) being the nucleophile** —
the same conclusion Sauerland et al. 2021 reached for acrolein (`research_round4_nulls.md`
§A.2: *"a linear inverse correlation for acrolein with the thiol pKₐ indicates that the
thiolate anion is the reactive species"*). **Two labs, two electrophiles, one mechanism.**
