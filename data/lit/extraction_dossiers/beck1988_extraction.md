# Beck, Ledl & Severin 1988 (Carbohydr. Res. 177:240) — EXTRACTION
### *"Formation of 1-deoxy-D-erythro-2,3-hexodiulose from Amadori compounds"* (a 4-page Note)
### Glucose Amadori compounds of a SECONDARY amine (1-deoxy-1-piperidino-D-fructose, 0.5 M; tertiary N at C-1) and a PRIMARY alkylamine (1-deoxy-1-propylamino-D-fructose, 0.1 M; secondary N at C-1, as in amino-acid Amadori compounds) heated 10 h under reflux in phosphate buffer at pH 7 or 4.5 WITH o-phenylenediamine (2 equiv) present from the start: the 1-deoxyglucosone (1-DG) quinoxaline isolated and characterised for the first time; 1-DG : 3-DG quinoxaline ratio **20 : 1 at pH 7 and 8 : 5 at pH 4.5** (GLC peak areas of the triacetates). **IN-SITU trapping, so a cumulative FLUX split; two pH points; hexose; no amino acid Amadori compound; no time course; buffer concentration not printed.**

## 1. What this paper answers for the model

| model need | what Beck 1988 gives | tag |
|---|---|---|
| (a) 1-DG / 3-DG split as FLUX or level | **Flux (cumulative).** OPD is present from t = 0 (2 equiv), so each deoxyosone is trapped as it forms; the ratio of quinoxalines after 10 h is the integrated formation split -- provided OPD out-competes each osone's other fates. 1-deoxy-1-propylamino-D-fructose: **1-DG : 3-DG = 20 : 1 (pH 7), 8 : 5 (pH 4.5)** | [M] |
| (b) pH law of the split | two points: log10(1-DG / 3-DG) falls from 1.30 to 0.20, i.e. **~0.44 dex per pH unit** between pH 4.5 and 7 [Z]. **Answers, weighed.**
1. **This is the cleanest flux-split pH contrast on disk for a 1,2- vs 2,3-enolisation** (in-situ trap, isomeric
   derivatives, one Amadori compound, two pH values). It says the formation split itself moves ~12x between pH 4.5
   and 7 toward 1-DG (confidence that the direction is right ~0.9; that the size transfers to a pentose amino-acid
   ARP in water at 100-140 °C ~0.3). For the hub this supports H3 ("the enolisation split itself shifts toward 1-DX at
   high pH") as a FLUX effect, against the reading of prereg sec. 20 that the joint passes put the split "near off" and
   carry the pH law in 3-DX REMOVAL. Both can contribute; Beck shows the split term is not negligible in the hexose /
   alkylamine case.
2. **Caveats that limit transfer:** (i) a propylamine (no carboxylate) and a piperidine Amadori compound, not an amino
   acid one; (ii) OPD at 0.2-1.0 M is itself an amine nucleophile and base present throughout -- it could alter the
   enolisation it is meant to report (untested here) [!]; (iii) phosphate concentration unprinted -- phosphate
   catalyses Amadori breakdown (zyzak1995) and may itself shift the split; (iv) the 10 h reflux far outlasts the ARP, so
   the ratio is end-point cumulative, not initial; (v) no replicates, no response factors.
3. **Secondary vs primary parent amine** is a real within-study structural factor: the piperidine Amadori compound
   (a TERTIARY amine at C-1) goes to 1-DG even at pH 4.5 [Q]. The propylamine one has a SECONDARY amine at C-1, as every
   amino-acid Amadori compound does, so it -- not the piperidino case -- is the structural analogue of the model's
   ARPs (it lacks only the carboxylate) [Z]. The N-substituent, not only the pH, sets the split.

## 6. Disagreements found

1. **Secondary citations drop the conditions.** blank1997, martins2003b and apriyantono1993 quote 20 : 1 / 8 : 5 as "the"
   1-DG : 3-DG ratio of Amadori degradation; it is for 1-deoxy-1-propylamino-D-fructose (an alkylamine, no carboxylate),
   reflux 10 h with OPD present, GLC peak areas. A conditions correction, not a numerical one.
2. **Flux split (here) vs standing levels (Ho network).** At pH 7 the formation flux favours the 1-deoxyosone 20 : 1,
   while the Jiangnan standing levels at "pH 7" favour 3-DX ~1.3-22 : 1 (wei2022 1.3-2.2, hou2026 2.6-11, zhou2024b 2.6-9.7, zhou2022 5-22). Not a
   contradiction -- a 1-deoxyosone that lives far shorter than the 3-deoxyosone (as every lifetime bound on disk says)
   inverts the ratio between flux and level -- but it is the same inversion desclaux2006's fitted 50 : 1 flux split
   shows against the standing levels (evidence map 2.1), now with an in-situ measurement behind the flux side
   (hexose / alkylamine, so transfer-laden). Candidate causes for any residual gap: sugar (hexose vs pentose), amine
   (alkylamine vs amino acid), OPD's presence, and the pH drift of the unbuffered Jiangnan pots (hou2026 measures pH
   5 -> 3.5, where Beck's own pH 4.5 split is already only 8 : 5).
3. **Engine:** the split carries a pH law only through the B7 switches; the shipped `dpo_share` (CR-12) rests on a
   standing-level ratio (prereg sec. 2) -- Beck is a flux datum of the kind that split should be anchored on, with the
   transfer caveats above.
