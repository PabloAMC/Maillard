## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Three beef samples were each minced (500 g) and boiled with 500 mL water under reflux for 90 min. The broth was
treated with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (DNPH) in conc. HCl to remove carbonyls. Volatiles were stripped
into HgCl2 traps, and the mercury precipitate was regenerated with HCl/oxalate and run on four packed GC
columns of different polarity. Compounds were assigned only by Kováts index (±3 units against the
authors' own index library). **29 sulfur compounds are "identified" (a separate peak on ≥2 columns) and 10
are "tentative"**: 17 thiols, 8 sulfides, 7 di/trisulfides and 7 heterocycles. Another 9 are "possible"
(overlapping on all columns). **2-Furfurylthiol (FFT), methanethiol, 1,2-ethanedithiol, 1,4-butanedithiol
and dimethyl trisulfide are assigned in all three samples.** 2-Methyl-3-furanthiol (MFT), its
disulfides, methional, H2S and thiazoles do not appear. The method could not have shown them either,
because it only assigns compounds already in the index library. The only number is the mass of mercury
precipitate, **20–25 mg per kg meat**.

## 2. METHODS [M] (pp. 237–238)

| item | as printed |
|---|---|
| Samples | A = *Longissimus dorsi*, 2-year-old cattle (Moscow 1973); B = *L. dorsi*, 1-year-old bull (Potsdam 1975); C = shoulder meat, 3-year-old cattle (Potsdam 1974) |
| Cooking | **500 g minced meat + 500 mL cold distilled water**, rest 30 min, brought to the boil, **boiled 90 min under reflux**; broth filtered warm |
| Carbonyl removal | **5 mL saturated DNPH in conc. HCl** added to the broth, left overnight ("the sharp odour notes disappeared") |
| Stripping, A and B | 500 mL broth, N2 stream, **paraffin bath 120–130 °C**, until 75 % of the volume had distilled; two traps, each 2 mL 6 % HgCl2 in ethanol + 2 mL saturated Na acetate |
| Stripping, C | vacuum stripping into HgCl2 traps cooled with dry ice/acetone |
| Precipitate | **20–25 mg Hg precipitate per kg meat**, for all samples |
| Regeneration | 25 mg precipitate + 25 mL solvent (A: benzene and hexadecane; B: hexadecane and cyclohexane; C: hexadecane), 20 mL conc. HCl + 2 mL 10 % K oxalate, sealed capillary, **boiling water 3 min**; upper layer injected |
| GC | Pye-Unicam 104, FID; glass 210 × 0.4 cm packed columns; 5 % Apiezon M, 10 % OV-17, 10 % Triton X-305, or 10 % PEG; **isothermal 130 °C**; N2 carrier |
| Identification | Kováts index within **3 units** of the reference compound. "++" = separate peak on ≥2 columns; "+" = separate on one column, overlapped on the others; "(+)" = overlapped on all columns. **No mass spectrometry, no sniffing** |

### 3.2 Other content [M]

- **Fig. 1 (sample B, Apiezon M):** 26 peaks, 13 of them "not identified". Peak 9 is furfuryl thiol. Peak 7 is
  2-methylthiophene and dimethyl sulfide co-eluting. Dimethyl sulfide is named only in this legend,
  not in Table 3 [!].
- **Table 1:** compound counts from the literature (beef 309, chicken 163, pork liver 179, all meat
  flavour compounds 530). **Table 2:** sulfur compounds in heated meat (68) versus cysteine/methionine/
  thiamine model systems (79). Both are counts from other papers, not measurements.
- **Odour [Q]:** the regenerated precipitates "form meaty flavour notes", which are "not stable" and
  change after a short time.
- **The authors' own caveat [Q]:** "secondary reactions" in the DNPH/HgCl2 system are possible, so
  mechanistic interpretation is "very speculative".

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Verdict: qualitative presence only. Nothing kinetic and nothing quantitative.**

- **Can support, qualitatively:** **FFT forms in plain boiled beef** (aqueous, ~100 °C, 90 min, no
  added sugar or cysteine). It is assigned "++" in all three samples. This is consistent with the
  sulfur lane producing FFT from endogenous pentose and cysteine in a boiled-meat pot. FFT is not
  starred in Table 3, so the authors treat it as new to meat, and their Table 2 lists only one "sulfur
  containing furan compound" in heated meat. Methanethiol and dimethyl trisulfide are also assigned in all three samples, which supports the
  presence of the methionine → MeSH → DMDS/DMTS branch in boiled meat.
- **Cannot anchor:** any concentration, yield, rate or pot score. No amounts are printed, and
  20–25 mg Hg precipitate/kg meat is not convertible to a compound.
- **Absence is not evidence:** MFT, bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide, methional and H2S are missing.
  Only compounds already in the authors' Kováts library could be assigned. H2S would be trapped as
  HgS and is not reported. Carbonyls such as methional were removed with DNPH before stripping.
  **Do not read "no MFT" as a negative for any lane.**

**Caveats [!].**
1. **Identification is weak by modern standards:** packed columns, retention index only (±3 units),
   no MS, no odour port. Several assignments are implausible as meat volatiles and are probably
   mismatches or artefacts: tert-butyl thiophenes, tert-butyl/aryl thiophenols, vinyl phenyl sulfide,
   di-tert-butyl disulfide, and long-chain alkanethiols or dithiols with no obvious precursor. They
   recur unevenly across samples. Benzene and cyclohexane solvents were used in regeneration.
2. **Strong acid plus DNPH overnight, a 120–130 °C bath, and a 3-min HCl regeneration at 100 °C**
   add their own chemistry, so the profile is not that of the cooked broth.
3. Three animals of different ages, cuts and years, with sample C vacuum-stripped. Sample-to-sample
   differences cannot be separated from method differences.
4. A later Part II (model systems) is announced in the text but is not in the corpus.
