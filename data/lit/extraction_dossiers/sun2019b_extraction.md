## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

In water at an initial pH 7.0, 81 mmol/L GSH with 162 mmol/L xylose forms its Amadori compound
**N-(1-deoxy-D-xylulos-1-yl)-glutathione (MGX, [M+H]⁺ 440)** only slowly and to a low ceiling: **yield
(MGX / GSH charged) ~7 % at 20 min and a maximum of 8.6 % at 90 °C / 60 min; 8.0 % at 100 °C / 40 min;
7.3 % at 80 °C / 120 min**, then a slow decline (to 5.7–7.5 % at 180 min). Removing water (25 mbar, 10 min
rotary evaporation) raises the yield to **40 % with no prior heating and 55.5 % after 50 min at 90 °C**.
Heated at 5 mmol/L, pH 7.0, 105 °C, purified MGX makes volatiles faster than GSH + xylose (total
43.6 vs ~6 ng/mL at 80 min). At 80 min the MGX pot holds **FFT 1.02 ng/mL** (≈ 1.8 × 10⁻⁶ mol/mol MGX)
and **no MFT**; the volatiles are 87 % furfural. The Cys-xylose Amadori compound gives no FFT but more
thiazoles. No GSH, H₂S, pH-drift or MGX-decay measurement during the 105 °C heating.

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Model context.** `GSH` is a sulfide source only (`r_gsh_h2s`, Zheng & Ho 1994); no GSH-Amadori
species. Pentose + amine → Amadori is carried for free Cys (Cys-Amadori; `cao2017_extraction.md`,
`zhang2025b_extraction.md`), not for GSH. Nothing here was run against the model.

**(1) GSH glycation in solution is a few-per-cent pool (usable, semi-quantitative).** At 81 mM GSH /
162 mM xylose, water, pH 7.0 start, the Amadori compound reaches **7–9 % of GSH within 20–60 min at
90–100 °C, and plateaus/declines** (formation ≈ degradation). This bounds a GSH + pentose → GSH-ARP
branch: the steady pool is ≤ ~9 % of GSH, reached in ≤ 20–40 min at 90–100 °C [Z: an initial-rate
estimate at 90 °C, 7.0 % in 20 min with 162 mM xylose, gives k₂ ≈ −ln(0.93)/(20 min × 0.162 M) ≈
0.022 M⁻¹ min⁻¹ as a *net* rate — a lower bound on the forward constant, since the pool is already
turning over; the uncontrolled pH drift (no buffer, an acidic peptide + organic acids) makes it
indicative only]. Consistent with `wang2020_extraction.md` (glucose-GSH-Amadori ≤ 0.3 % and transient at
140 °C) and `du2025_extraction.md` (GSH-Amadori decaying ~0.1–0.2 min⁻¹ at 145 °C): **in a wet pot,
glycation is not where most glutathione goes.** Only drying (vacuum, Fig. 5B) pushes it to 40–55 %, a
low-water-activity regime the model does not cover.

**(2) MFT/FFT from the GSH-pentose Amadori (direction test).** Purified MGX at 5 mM, pH 7.0, 105 °C,
80 min gives **FFT ~1.8 × 10⁻⁶ mol/mol and no detectable MFT**; FFT is absent from Cys-, Gly- and
Glu-xylose ARPs under the same conditions. If the model gains a GSH-ARP species, its decomposition should
favour furfural (87 % of volatiles) + some FFT, not MFT. The MFT-free result also fits
`wang2012_extraction.md` (GSH + xylose: MFT carbon is not xylose-derived) and `zhou2023c_extraction.md`
(MFT from Ala-xylose ARP + GSH is ~1/18 of the Cys case).

**(3) Not usable.** Absolute volatile amounts as fit targets (RF = 1, IS volume unstated, no standards,
MS-only IDs, a mis-summed subtotal); the MRP curve (pre-reacted mixture of unknown composition); A420
(Fig. 1); the GX volatile curve as a GSH-thermolysis rate (no H₂S or sulfur volatiles itemised for GX).

**Caveats, collected.**
1. Unbuffered water; only the starting pH (7.0) is set; drift unmeasured.
2. Early-View PDF ("Vol. 0"); final pagination from Crossref.
3. Yields relative to GSH charged; MGX decomposition products and residual GSH not measured.
4. 80 °C series has missing 40/60 min points; Fig. 1 time axis disagrees with its caption.
5. Two co-eluting thiophenecarboxaldehyde assignments.
