## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Lipid aldehydes enter Maillard chemistry by **four routes**, which the review takes from Farmer & Mottram 1990:
(1) reaction with the amino group of cysteine and with the **NH3** released by its Strecker degradation;
(2) the ethanolamine amine of PE reacting with sugar-derived carbonyls; (3) lipid free radicals acting on the
Maillard reaction; (4) **H2S** adding to hydroxy and carbonyl lipid products. The products carry a ≥ C4 alkyl
chain: 2-alkylthiophenes, 2-pentylthiapyran and alkanethiols (H2S + 2,4-dienals / 2-enals), 2-pentylpyridine
(2,4-decadienal + NH3), 2-alkylpyrroles (2-enals + NH3), long-chain alkylpyrazines (aldehyde + dihydropyrazine),
trithiolanes, dithiazines and thiadiazines (saturated aldehydes + H2S + NH3). For the levers, the review
makes three points. **Cysteine is the strong NH3/H2S source:** 2-pentylpyridine with lecithin is about 20× and 100×
higher with cysteine than with glycine or lysine (Whitfield 1988). **Adding amino acid + ribose to lecithin
raises the 2-alkylfurans** 3–6× over lecithin alone. The authors call this surprising, because Maillard products are
supposed to be antioxidants, and they point to Maillard free radicals instead. **pH is nearly unstudied** (the
conclusion calls it a gap). The one pH result is that furyl/thienyl disulfides fall steeply from pH 4.5 to 6.5.
**Hexanal, nonanal and decadienal are never measured as outcomes of a lever here.** The only hexanal statement is
mechanistic: 2,4-decadienal → hexanal by retro-aldol in water (Josephson & Lindsay 1987), and hexanal is lost
to 2-pentylpyridine when glutathione replaces cysteine (Zhang & Ho 1989).

### 2.4 2,4-Decadienal + cysteine vs glutathione (Zhang & Ho 1989; the cysteine-forms-hexanal rival)

- System as printed: 2,4-decadienal + cysteine or glutathione, water, **pH 7.5, 180 °C, 1 h**, closed cylinder [M].
- In water, 2,4-decadienal undergoes hydration of the α,β double bond and **retro-aldol to hexanal + acetaldehyde**
  (Josephson & Lindsay 1987, *J. Food Sci.* 52:1186). Hexanal self-condenses to 2-butyl-2-octenal [Q].
- With glutathione, more 2-pentylpyridine forms "at the expense of hexanal". The proposed reason is a Schiff base between
  decadienal and GSH's amino group, which leaves less decadienal for retro-aldol. Cysteine instead breaks down quickly to
  acetaldehyde + NH3 + H2S (Shu et al. 1985 pH paper), so the products shift to thiadiazines and dithiazines [Q].
- Cysteine degraded alone gives four times as much volatile as glutathione (Zhang, Chien & Ho 1988).
- **The review does not print the hexanal numbers.** They are in `zhang1989` (on disk). This review's Table 6 is heterocycles only.

### 2.7 pH [Q]

- **Furyl and thienyl disulfides from cysteine + ribose fall rapidly as pH rises from 4.5 to 6.5 and are absent at
  pH 6.5** (Farmer & Mottram 1990, Flavour Sci. Technol.). Offered as a link between meat pH and flavour.
- Cysteine thermal degradation depends on pH (Shu, Hagedorn, Mookherjee & Ho 1985, *J. Agric. Food Chem.* 33:442; `shu1985`).
- **Conclusion (p. 53):** the effect of pH on Maillard–lipid interactions "has received little attention" and needs
  study. The review gives no pH dependence for any lipid aldehyde or alkylfuran.

### 2.8 Sugar identity [Q]

No study in the review compares sugars in a lipid-containing pot. The Reading systems use ribose; Ledl & Severin use
xylose; Arnoldi uses fructose; Bruechert uses glucose. The sugar-side contributions named are the furans,
cyclopentenones and α-dicarbonyls (Fig. 2). The review also notes that **sugar–lipid-aldehyde reactions (acetals) and
sugar-fragment + lipid-carbonyl reactions had not been studied** (Sections IV.D–E).

## 4. WHERE THE REVIEW SAYS THE DIRECTION DEPENDS ON CONDITIONS

1. **Maillard products and lipid oxidation:** antioxidant in the literature (Lingnert & Eriksson 1981), yet
   alkylfurans **rise** 3–6× with amino acid + ribose in Whitfield 1988. Free-radical promotion is proposed and called
   unresolved.
2. **Lipid and pyrazine yield:** corn oil **raised** pyrazines in zein systems (Huang 1987; radicals) but **lowered**
   them in corn meal and gluten (Bruechert 1989; physical barrier). Water content and temperature moved yields in the
   same study.
3. **Lipid class:** PE / beef PL suppress MFT and thiophenethiols most; triglyceride suppresses bicyclic S compounds
   most; PC makes most lipid-chain products. Fatty-acid profile and head-group amine both matter.
4. **Chemistry vs partition:** quenching may be reaction or solubility in the lipid phase (conclusion, p. 53).
5. **Amine identity:** cysteine (Strecker NH3 and H2S) vs glycine / lysine changes the product family (pyridines,
   thiophenes); glutathione vs cysteine shifts decadienal between pyridine and retro-aldol hexanal (Zhang & Ho 1989).
6. **pH:** disulfides from cys/ribose fall to nil between pH 4.5 and 6.5; otherwise unstudied.

## 5. USE IN THIS REPO

**Status: secondary evidence.** Every item below is a mechanism pointer that names a primary study. None of it is a
measurement for fitting or for setting a band. Where the review reprints numbers (Tables 6, 9, 12, 13), take them from
the primary dossier (`whitfield1988`, `farmer1990`, `zhang1989`), not from here.

**Hold-out note.** farmer1992 and elmore2002 are designated hold-outs (their dossiers §5; farmer1992 also
appears as rows FAR92-01/02 in FIT_HOLDOUT_DECLARATION). This review is 1992 and **cites neither**, so it carries no restatement of
their numbers. Its Reading-group numbers come from the companion studies (Whitfield 1988, Farmer 1989, Farmer &
Mottram 1990, Mottram & Salter 1989). Those share farmer1992's lab, lipids, buffer and 140 °C / 1 h pot. A band set
from them would be **near-duplicate information** to the farmer1992 test, so keep that dependence in mind before using
them to set what farmer1992 scores.

   Lingnert papers for the query.
