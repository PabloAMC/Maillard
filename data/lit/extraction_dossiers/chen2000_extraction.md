## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

A roasting-temperature (180 °C, 2 h) unbuffered ribose/cysteine pot at two starting pHs, ± equimolar urea.
Without urea, **2-furfurylthiol is the largest single volatile at pH 5 (1.55 mg/g ribose)** and falls ~7× at
pH 8.5 (0.22); 2-methylthiophene, 2-methyltetrahydrothiophen-3-one, 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolane and
(at pH 5) 2/3-mercaptopropanoic acid are also abundant. **MFT is not listed** (only an unassigned
"methylthiofuran", 0.35 / 0.04). Urea removes most thiophenes and all thiols and produces pyrazines
(methylpyrazine 3.18 / 3.80 mg/g ribose), attributed to NH3 released from urea competing with H2S. The pH
drifts strongly: 5 → 3.86 and 8.5 → 6.05 without urea; with urea 5 → 7.43 and 8.5 → 8.35.

## 4. DERIVED [Z]

- **FFT molar yield** (FFT 114.17 g/mol; ribose 150.13 g/mol → 1 g = 6.66 mmol): pH 5: 1.55 mg/g = 13.6 µmol
  per 6.66 mmol = **2.0 × 10⁻³ mol/mol ribose (0.20 mol %)**; pH 8.5: **2.9 × 10⁻⁴ (0.029 mol %)**.
  Hofmann 1998 aqueous 145 °C/20 min pH 5 (1:3 Cys:R): 1.06 × 10⁻⁵ — Chen pH 5 is **~190× higher**
  (180 °C, 2 h, 1:1 Cys:R, unbuffered drift to pH 3.9). Plausible direction (FFT accumulates at high T/long
  time; cf. `schieberle2000` 100 °C time course), but the magnitude rests on an uncalibrated FID/tridecane
  ratio after solvent extraction and K-D concentration.
- **pH 5 / pH 8.5 FFT ratio: 7.0** (initial pH), with final pH 3.86 / 6.05. Hofmann pH 5 / pH 7: 10.1.
- **Sibling reproducibility** (`chen2002_extraction.md`, same lab, same nominal pot): FFT pH 5 **1.55 vs
  0.895** mg/g (×1.7); pH 8.5 **0.22 vs 0.318** (×0.69); final pH 3.86/6.05 vs 4.01/4.96. MFT absent here,
  0.083/0.040 there. Between-paper spread is therefore ~×1.7 on FFT and pH-endpoint differences of up to
  1.1 units for nominally identical pots.

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

- **FFT at 180 °C / 2 h, pH 5 → 3.86 and pH 8.5 → 6.05 (unbuffered, 100 mM Cys + 100 mM ribose
  presumed):** a **candidate HOLD-OUT row of low quality** — absolute in name (mg/g ribose) but
  semi-quantitative (no response factor, no recovery, no labelled standard; Schieberle 1996 shows plain
  solvent extraction can destroy MFT entirely). It is a **high-temperature extrapolation test** (180 °C;
  the core's Hofmann anchors are 100–145 °C) and the measured final pH lets the `ph_state` charge balance
  be checked. **Not benchmark quality** for levels; the within-study pH ratio (7×) is the more defensible
  quantity, and the two-paper spread (§4) should be carried as its uncertainty.
- **MFT: no row** (not reported; "methylthiofuran" is unassigned — it could be 2-methyl-3-furanthiol, a
  methylthio-furan, or 2-methyl-3-(methylthio)furan; do not key it).
- **Urea arm:** NH3 donor competing with H2S — a pyrazine/thiazole switch. Outside the engine's
  scope (no urea species); qualitative evidence that an ammonia source suppresses thiol formation.
- **Urea in meat** (beef 41 mg/100 g ≈ 6.9 mM in water-phase terms [Z, assuming 75 % water]) is a matrix
  composition fact, not a kinetic one.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **"0.01 mol of L-cysteine and D-ribose"** — total or each, not stated.
2. **Unbuffered**; pH drifts 1.1–2.5 units; urea arms drift *upwards* (NH3 release).
3. **No response factors**; FID-vs-tridecane only; duplicates without SD.
4. **"Methylthiofuran"** unassigned isomer.
5. **Fig. 1 legend "10 mM urea"** vs Methods "100 mM"; figure not digitised.
6. Table 3 diacetyl columns labelled "mg/g of acetoin".
7. Table 1 SDs (±0.011 mg/100 g on a 41 mg/100 g mean, 0.03 %) are implausibly tight for a meat assay.
