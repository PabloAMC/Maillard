## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Equimolar (~0.005 mol in 100 mL, ≈ 50 mmol/L) cysteine and glutathione, each alone in water at pH
7.5, 180 °C for 1 h in a sealed cylinder. **Total identified volatiles: cysteine 3.84 g/mol, glutathione
0.82 g/mol [M] (≈ 4.7×).** In moles [Z]: 26.6 vs 5.4 mmol volatile per mol precursor; in sulfur: **40 vs
16 mmol S per mol precursor S (4.0 % vs 1.6 % of the precursor sulfur ends up in identified
volatiles)**. Cysteine's volatiles are dominated by acetaldehyde + H2S + NH3 condensates
(2,4,6-trimethylperhydro-1,3,5-thiadiazine 1257 mg/mol, thialdine 1467 mg/mol: 70.9 % of total);
glutathione's by the syn/anti 3,5-dimethyl-1,2,4-trithiolanes (515 mg/mol, 63.2 %) and polysulfides,
with only thiazoles (9 % of total) carrying N. The authors read this as **"glutathione releases
hydrogen sulfide more easily than ammonia"** [Q] and the degradation of glutathione as "much milder"
than cysteine's [Q]. Neither H2S nor residual precursor was measured. **No MFT or FFT** in either
pot (no sugar present).

So **glutathione makes ~2.8× more trithiolane per mol than cysteine** although it makes 4.7× less
total volatile: in the GSH pot, H2S meets an aldehyde (acetaldehyde) without NH3; in the Cys pot NH3
and acetaldehyde both come from the cysteine itself and divert H2S into thiadiazine/dithiazines.
Where the acetaldehyde in the GSH pot comes from (the Cys residue after desulfuration, or
fragmentation of the γ-Glu/Gly) is not addressed [Q].

## 4. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Model context.** GSH has one reaction, `r_gsh_h2s` (GSH → H2S + FRAG_C(10) + FRAG_N(3),
`k_gsh_h2s` from Zheng & Ho 1994; `sulfur.py`, `parameters_sulfur.py`). Nothing here was run against
the model.

1. **No kinetics.** One temperature, one time, no residual GSH/Cys, no H2S. No rate constant or Ea can
   be taken from this paper.
2. **Direction test (usable, qualitative): GSH is a poorer source of S-volatiles than free cysteine at
   180 °C, by ~2.5× in sulfur and ~4.7× in mass.** Compare `zheng1997_extraction.md` (Furaneol +
   GSH ≈ ¼ of the Cys sulfur-volatile total at 130 °C) and `zhou2023c_extraction.md` (MFT 18× lower
   from GSH than Cys, FFT 1.3×). All three say the same thing: GSH sulfur is less available than free
   Cys sulfur.
3. **Ammonia vs H2S release (qualitative):** in GSH the amine is a peptide-bound γ-Glu α-NH2, not
   free; the absence of thiadiazine/dithiazines says little free NH3 forms in 1 h at 180 °C. The
   model's `r_gsh_h2s` puts FRAG_N(3) out with H2S; this paper argues against free NH3 from GSH on
   this timescale (but it measures neither species).
4. **Not usable:** the mg/mol values as absolute yields (FID response factor 1 vs a single IS, SDE
   recovery unknown, single run, no SD); any pH dependence (start pH only).

**Caveats.** Mole misprint (0.05 for 0.005 mol) for both precursors; air headspace; 180 °C is far above
the range of every kinetic dataset on GSH in the corpus (Zheng & Ho 1994: 80–110 °C; Zhou 2023c:
120 °C; Lu 2019: 110 °C); SDE at 100 °C for hours itself makes artefacts; no replicates.
