## 1. Why it matters

This is the fed-intermediate paper for Programme 6's proline branch. It measures, by stable isotope
dilution, the molar yield of AP from 1-pyrroline + MGO as a function of the MGO : pyrroline ratio and
of wet vs dry heating, the yield of ATHP from 1-pyrroline + hydroxyacetone as a function of pH, the
yield of ATHP from the isolated intermediate HOP as a function of pH, and the AP : ATHP split when
proline itself is fed with 4-400 mmol/L MGO. Under the repository's rule these are fitted quantities
(fed_intermediate_yield) for a step "1-pyrroline + MGO -> AP" and "1-pyrroline + acetol -> ATHP", with
Blank 2003's sugar + proline levels as the validation. The 13C-labelling tables fix the atom bookkeeping:
ATHP takes three sugar carbons (two in the acetyl, one in the ring) and AP mainly two (77 %) with a
19 % three-carbon isotopomer.

## 2. Methods as they matter to a model

- **Fed pots (Tables 4, 7, 8):** 1-pyrroline 10 µmol (690 µg) in **5 mL 0.5 M phosphate** ->
  **2 mmol/L**; partner 10 µmol (2 mmol/L) or 50 µmol (10 mmol/L); **30 min at 100 C** ("boiling");
  vessel closure not stated except for Table 5 ("closed vessel"). Table 4 varies pH 3 / 5 / 7 / 9 at
  1:1; Table 7 varies MGO at pH 7; expt 3 of Table 7 has 50 µmol pyrroline (10 mmol/L) with 10 µmol
  MGO; expt 4 is dry: 10 + 10 µmol on 2.7 g silica gel with 300 µL 0.1 M phosphate pH 7, **5 min at
  180 C**.
- **HOP pots (Table 5):** N-Boc-HOP 1 µmol deprotected with trifluoroacetic acid (5 µmol in 100 µL
  CH2Cl2), then 5 mL 0.5 M phosphate -> **0.2 mmol/L HOP**, 30 min 100 C, closed vessel, pH 3 / 5 / 7 / 9.
- **Proline pots (Table 9):** proline 2 mmol + MGO 0.02 / 0.2 / 2.0 mmol in 5 mL 0.5 M phosphate pH 7
  -> **proline 400 mmol/L; MGO 4 / 40 / 400 mmol/L** (MGO : Pro = 0.01 / 0.1 / 1); 30 min 100 C.
- **Labelling pots (Tables 3, 6):** proline 2 mmol + [13C6]glucose 1 mmol on silica gel (2.7-3 g)
  with 300 µL 0.1 M phosphate pH 7, **10 min at 160 C**, closed vials.
- **AMTHP synthesis (text):** 2-methyl-1-pyrroline 5 mmol + hydroxyacetone 50 mmol in 500 mL 0.5 M
  phosphate pH 7 (**10 + 100 mmol/L**), reflux 2 h -> AMTHP "3 mol %; data not shown".
- **Quantification:** volatile fraction by sublimation in vacuo; **stable isotope dilution** with
  [2H]-AP and [2H]-ATHP (Schieberle 1995); HRGC on FFAP and SE-54, MS EI 70 eV. 1-Pyrroline was
  generated by oxidative decarboxylation of L-proline (Yoshikawa 1965; Schieberle 1990) — its purity
  is not stated (flag 3).
- **Yield basis:** "%" is molar on the 10 µmol limiting reagent: 10 µmol AP = 1111.4 µg; 10 µmol ATHP =
  1251.7 µg; 1 µmol ATHP = 125.17 µg. Recomputed below; Table 8's printed percentages do not follow
  this basis (flag 1).

**What the paper says the step is.** (a) ATHP: the enol of hydroxyacetone adds at C-2 of 1-pyrroline
-> HOP -> ring opening to 5,6-dioxoheptylamine -> Schiff closure to ATHP (ring enlargement proven by
AMTHP: the 2-methyl of the pyrroline becomes the 3-methyl of the tetrahydropyridine). (b) AP: hydrated
MGO adds at C-2 of 1-pyrroline -> 2-(1,2-dioxopropyl)pyrrolidine -> air oxidation, hydration,
rearrangement, decarboxylation -> 2-acetylpyrrolidine -> oxidation to AP; the aldehyde carbon of MGO
leaves as CO2 (main route, M+2); a minor route loses C-2 of the pyrroline (M+3). Both need an oxidant
(air). (c) Which odorant forms is set by the MGO : amino-acid ratio (Table 9): abundant free amino
acid converts MGO by Strecker to acetol (and 1-pyrroline), so ATHP wins; excess MGO acylates
1-pyrroline directly, so AP wins.

**What a proline step would take from here.** Two fed constants at 100 C, pH 7, 0.5 M phosphate
(AP ~9e-4, ATHP ~1.4e-4 L/(mmol·min) as bilinear lower bounds), one pH ladder each (Table 4 for ATHP;
none for AP), no temperature ladder, and the Table 9 split as the within-study shape of the branching
against MGO : proline. The 1-pyrroline supply (Strecker of proline) is the unmeasured part, exactly as
the dicarbonyl supply is for B18.

## 5. Flags

1. **Table 8's printed yields use the wrong base** (µg AP / ~830 µg). Recomputed: 4.8 % (1-pyrroline)
   and 3.6 % (2-methyl-1-pyrroline). Table 7's percentages are on the correct base.
2. **Table 8 does not state the MGO charge**; the agreement 4.8 vs 5.3 % with Table 7 expt 2 suggests
   10 µmol but that is an inference.
3. **1-Pyrroline purity is unstated.** It was made by oxidative decarboxylation of proline and is known
   to trimerise; "10 µmol, 690 µg" is a nominal charge. All yields per pyrroline inherit this.
4. **The rate law is not bilinear across Table 7.** Fivefold MGO raises AP 5.4x (bilinear), but fivefold
   1-pyrroline at fixed MGO LOWERS AP per MGO 16x. Either 1-pyrroline consumes itself (trimer) at
   10 mmol/L or MGO is diverted; a step written as k [pyrroline] [MGO] must carry this as a caveat or
   include a pyrroline self-sink.
5. **Single time point (30 min) everywhere**; no time series, no temperature ladder in water (the only
   second temperature is the dry 180 C run).
6. **Closed vs open vessel stated only for Table 5.** AP and ATHP are volatile; the other pots may
   have lost some product at 100 C.
7. **0.5 M phosphate** throughout (Blank 2003 used 0.2 M); phosphate catalysis is common to both.
8. **Table 9's 400 mmol/L MGO** arm adds a substantial volume of 40 % MGO solution to the 5 mL; the
   concentrations for that arm are nominal.
9. **AMTHP yield "3 mol %, data not shown"** and the ">30 % ATHP from HOP" of the abstract is the pH 9
   value (35 %); at pH 7 it is 23 %.
10. **Oxidant.** Both routes to AP need an oxidation (2-acetylpyrrolidine -> AP); air in a 5 mL pot is
   not limiting but an oxygen-free extrusion barrel might be. Nothing here measures that.
