**Answers.**

1. **Nothing on pyrazines; it does not distinguish P1x / P1y from P2b.**
2. **Free ammonia is the dominant N donor when it is available** [M]: glutamine's pyridine N is ~97-99.5 % amide-derived;
   asparagine's ~47 %. The other half for Asn is not shown to be the alpha-amine reacting directly: Asn releases NH3
   from both the amide and the alpha-amino group (Sohn & Ho 1995 [C]), so "alpha-amino groups can contribute" [Q]
   is not separated from "alpha-amine → free NH3 → pyridine" [!]. For EX-052 this is the analogue fact behind P2b's
   "N label g from its own source": at 180 °C, free NH3 can out-compete amino-group N for ring closure. It says nothing
   about pyrazines' aminoketone N.
3. Glutamic acid (1.3 % NH3 release, cited) still gives ~320 mg/mol vs glycine ~45, so a non-NH3 route exists [F] / [C].
