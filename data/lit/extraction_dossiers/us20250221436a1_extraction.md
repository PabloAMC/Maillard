## 1. Methods

**Reaction (MRP) preparation.**
- Example 01 (PDF p. 24, [0277]-[0282], Table 1): 4.5 g amino acid + 3 g xylose + 3.5 g buffer, in an
  oven at 100 °C. Buffers: pH 4.4 = 8.82 ml 0.2 M Na2HPO4 + 11.18 ml 0.1 M citric acid; pH 8.0 = 19.45 ml
  + 0.55 ml. Samples 01-04 cysteine + xylose (pH 4.4 / 8.0, 1.5 or 2 h); 05-06 methionine + xylose
  (pH 1.5 and 0.5 as printed, 1.5 and 0.5 h). Methionine pH values are as printed; the buffers listed
  cannot make them.
- Example 03 (PDF p. 25, [0291]-[0296], Table 3) and Example 05 (PDF pp. 26-27, [0304]-[0309], Table 5):
  "equimolar amounts of amino compound and xylose, and buffer solution of the same mass as the xylose",
  pH 4.4, in a 4-neck flask with mixer and reflux; 100 °C in an electric heating mantle, 120 °C in a
  silicone-oil bath; timer started when the set temperature is reached. Absolute masses, concentrations
  and the thiamine salt form (free base or hydrochloride) are **not printed**.

**Application to the patty and cooking** (Examples 02, 04, 06; PDF pp. 24-27). MRP (2 ml in Example 02;
1 or 2 ml in Example 04; 1 ml in Example 06) added to one 113 g Beyond Burger patty (Beyond Meat,
plant-based; lot Z1B01 116917906, produced 2021-06-18), homogenised by mixing with a spoon, then fried
3 minutes per side in a frying pan with oil. Control: the plain patty. Example 04 and 06 panellists were
trained on American Meat Science Association attribute standards and given a McDonald's Beijing beef
patty as reference. Panel: 6 people (Example 02) and "6 trained people" (Examples 04, 06, the same six).

## 3. What it means for the model and for formulation

**(a) The thiamine row** (`results/validation/cultivated_tissue_invariance_prereg.md` sections 10-11:
thiamine comparable to cysteine as an MFT source at 100 °C, negligible at 140 °C). This patent is the
closest thing to a two-temperature thiamine-versus-cysteine comparison seen so far, but it is not the
test section 10 asks for. Differences: thiamine and cysteine are **alternatives** (each with xylose, no
cysteine in the thiamine pot), not additions to a meat-like base; they are at near-molar, equimolar
loadings, not beef level; pH 4.4, not 5.6-6.0; 100 vs 120 °C (not 140) with different times (1.5 h vs
0.5 h); and MFT was measured only at 100 °C, by Area-%. What it shows:
- At 100 °C / 1.5 h the cysteine pot gives free MFT (12.35 %) and its disulfide (25.67 %); the thiamine
  pot gives no listed free MFT and only 0.73 % disulfide in a chromatogram swamped by furfural. On MFT
  itself, thiamine is **not** comparable to cysteine here. This leans against the row, with the caveat
  that section 10's claim is about restoring beef-level thiamine on top of cysteine, where the engine's
  thiamine branch competes with a sugar route that is short of sulfide, a situation this experiment
  does not create.
- In the cooked patty, the thiamine MRPs score as meaty as the cysteine MRPs at 100 °C (Table 06: 4.00 vs
  4.23 overall, 3.83 vs 3.50 red meat aroma) and do not collapse at 120 °C (red meat aroma 3.75 vs 3.00
  for cysteine; overall 3.42 vs 4.03). Sensory carries no compound identity, so this is not evidence on
  MFT; it says only that thiamine-derived reaction flavour is not negligible in a plant matrix at 120 °C.
- Net: no change to the about-0.3 probability in section 10. The decisive beef-level, isotope-dilution
  experiment there is still the test.

**A route the engine lacks.** In the thiamine-only pot the largest sulfur volatile is **bis(2-furfuryl)
disulfide (6.07 %)**, the FFT dimer, with no cysteine present. In the engine H2S is produced by one
reaction only, cysteine to H2S (`r_cys_h2s`, `src/kinetic_core/sulfur.py`); FFT forms from furfural + H2S
(`r_fur_fft`); thiamine's two reactions go to 5-hydroxy-3-mercapto-2-pentanone (then MFT) and to
methanethiol. So the engine predicts zero FFT from xylose + thiamine, while this patent (one
chromatogram, library-matched) and the general literature on thiamine degradation releasing H2S say
otherwise. Worth a backlog note: thiamine as an H2S/FFT source, at least in pentose systems at high
thiamine. At beef thiamine levels it is likely small.

**(b) Cysteine and ribose (here xylose) as MFT/FFT sources.** Sample 18 is direct, if semi-quantitative,
support for the engine's main sulfur route: a pentose with cysteine at 100 °C, pH 4.4, gives MFT, FFT and
their disulfides as dominant sulfur volatiles, with MFT (12.35 %) above FFT (3.99 %) and the MFT disulfide
largest of all (25.67 %). The large disulfide share says that, in an open-flask hour-scale reaction with
air, much of the MFT ends as dimer; the engine gates dimerisation on an explicit oxidant pool, so a
fully aerobic MRP preparation is a regime it must be told about. Xylose is not ribose, and the engine's
claim is ribose-specific; both are pentoses and react alike to first order, which is an assumption here,
not something this patent tests.

**(c) Heme.** None used. The patent shows a heme-free route to meaty aroma in a plant-based patty: a
pre-reacted pentose + cysteine (or thiamine) flavour added at about 1 ml per 113 g and fried. The engine
has no heme or iron catalysis (`grep -i "heme\|hemin\|leghemoglobin\|iron" src/kinetic_core/*.py`: 19
hits, all inside the word "Scheme"; none as whole words), which is no handicap for this system. The
commercial patty used (Beyond Burger) contains no heme to my knowledge; its composition is not given in
the patent.

**Formulation.** Practical reading for a plant-based product: make the Maillard flavour outside the
matrix (pentose + cysteine, pH ~4.4, 100 °C, ~1-1.5 h), then dose it low (about 1 % v/w; 2 ml per 113 g
was worse than 1 ml in all three matched pairs) and cook. Thiamine + xylose is a cheaper, cysteine-free alternative
that the panel liked as much at 100 °C, with a different volatile profile (furfural and furfuryl
disulfide, little MFT), so it is a complement to cysteine rather than a substitute for MFT. All of this
rests on one panel of six with no statistics and one chromatogram per sample.

## What it does not give

- No absolute amounts: Area-% only, one injection per sample, no internal standard, sample mass not
  printed; Area-% cannot be compared between samples 18 and 19.
- No GC-MS of the 120 °C MRPs (samples 16, 17) and none of any cooked patty, so nothing on what survives
  frying or on the temperature dependence of MFT from thiamine.
- No reactant masses or concentrations for Examples 03 and 05 (only "equimolar" and buffer mass = xylose
  mass), no thiamine salt form, no final pH after reaction.
- No randomisation or serving order, no statistics, no plain-patty control in Example 06.
- No mixed cysteine + thiamine arm, no ribose, no heme.
