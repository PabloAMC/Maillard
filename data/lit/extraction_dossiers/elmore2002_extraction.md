# Elmore, Campo, Enser & Mottram 2002 — EXTRACTION
### *"Effect of Lipid Composition on Meat-like Model Systems Containing Cysteine, Ribose, and Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids"*
### 25 mM cysteine + 25 mM ribose in pyrophosphate pH 5.5, 140 °C / 30 min, with and without 25 mM methyl linoleate (18:2n-6) or methyl α-linolenate (18:3n-3). Adding PUFA ester cuts MFT 807 → 58 (18:2) → trace (18:3), i.e. 14× to > 80×, while FFT barely moves with 18:2 (92 → 87). Lipid aldehydes are consumed and H2S-lipid products (thiapyrans, alkylthiophenes) appear. A lipid-driven sulfur/thiol sink, measured at one pH and one time. Read 2026-10-27.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

Five pots, each **0.5 mmol of each reagent in 20 mL pyrophosphate buffer pH 5.5** (= 25 mM each),
sealed in 100-mL Duran bottles and heated **140 °C / 30 min**: (1) Cys + ribose; (2) Cys + ribose +
methyl linoleate; (3) Cys + ribose + methyl linolenate; (4) methyl linoleate; (5) methyl linolenate.
Headspace by two-fibre SPME at room temperature, GC-MS vs 100 ng dichlorobenzene, response factor 1,
four replicates. Of the 33 cysteine/ribose compounds (Table 1) **none increased with 18:2n-6; only
2-formylthiophene and 2-ethylthiophene increased with 18:3n-3**. **MFT 807 (±508) → 58 (±71) → tr**,
**bis(2-methyl-3-furyl) disulfide 202 → tr → ND**, **2-methyl-3-thiophenethiol 233 → tr → tr**,
**3-thiophenethiol 149 → 10 → ND**, **FFT 92 → 87 → 17**. Lipid side (Table 2): Cys + ribose remove
the C3–C5 2-alkenals of linolenate (2-butenal 497 → ND, 2-pentenal 437 → tr, 2,4-heptadienal 3083 →
297) and raise furans and alcohols (2-pentylfuran 315 → 1736 with linoleate; 2-ethylfuran 915 → 1912
with linolenate). New H2S–lipid products appear (Table 3: 2-ethyl-(2H)-thiapyran 469, 3-formyl-
thiophenes). The authors' reading: H2S from cysteine reacts with lipid degradation products, "which
reduces its availability to react with Maillard reaction-derived compounds".

## 4. DERIVED QUANTITIES [Z]

**Suppression of cysteine/ribose thiols by 25 mM PUFA ester** (tr censored at 10, − at 2):

| compound | Cys+Rib | + 18:2 | fold drop | + 18:3 | fold drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MFT** | 807 | 58 | **14×** | tr | **> 80×** |
| **FFT** | 92 | 87 | **1.06× (none)** | 17 | **5.4×** |
| 2-methyl-3-thiophenethiol | 233 | tr | > 23× | tr | > 23× |
| 3-thiophenethiol | 149 | 10 | 15× | − | > 75× |
| bis(2-methyl-3-furanyl) disulfide | 202 | tr | > 20× | − | > 100× |
| dihydrothienothiophene (1329) | 1006 | 47 | 21× | 56 | 18× |
| 2-methylthiophene | 443 | 83 | 5.3× | 54 | 8.2× |

**MFT/FFT ratio:** 8.8 (Cys+Rib) → 0.67 (+18:2) → < 0.6 (+18:3). Linoleate leaves FFT intact and
removes > 90 % of MFT.

**Lipid aldehydes removed by Cys + ribose** (linolenate pot): (E)-2-butenal 497 → ND (> 250×),
2-propenal 132 → ND, (E)-2-pentenal 437 → tr (> 44×), (E,E)-2,4-heptadienal 3083 → 297 (10×),
(E,Z)-2,4-heptadienal 646 → 43 (15×), (E,E)-2,4-hexadienal 143 → 30 (4.8×); linoleate pot: hexanal
644 → 379 (1.7×, SD > mean), pentanal 72 → 11 (6.5×). Saturated aldehydes are lost far less than
2-enals and 2,4-dienals.

**Oxidant supply [Z]:** 80 mL of air headspace in each bottle ≈ 16.8 mL O2 ≈ **0.7 mmol O2**, more
than the 0.5 mmol of each reagent. These are oxygen-rich pots; disulfide formation and lipid
oxidation are not oxygen-limited.

**Dispersion:** CV of MFT is 63 % (Cys+Rib) and 122 % (+18:2); bis(2-methyl-3-furanyl) disulfide CV
105 %; 2-pentylfuran (+18:2) 73 %; (E)-2-octenal (+18:2) 133 %. Only differences larger than ~3×
are meaningful.

## 5. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

- **(a) Levels: no.** Headspace SPME at room temperature, TIC response factor 1, normalisation "per
  100 mL of reaction mixture" for a 20 mL pot (Flag 1), CVs often > 50 %.
- **(b) A measured competing sulfur/thiol sink — directional, one condition.** At 25 mM each,
  pH 5.5, 140 °C, 30 min, in air:
  1. 25 mM methyl linoleate cuts MFT **~14×** and leaves FFT unchanged; methyl linolenate cuts MFT
     **> 80×** and FFT **~5×**.
  2. The same additions consume the lipid **2-alkenals and 2,4-alkadienals** (10× to > 250×) and
     create H2S/mercaptoacetaldehyde adducts (2-alkyl-(2H)-thiapyrans, 2-alkylthiophenes,
     2-alkyl-3-formylthiophenes).
  For the model this is a **lipid-lane → sulfur-lane coupling test**: if the lipid lane makes enals/
  dienals from PUFA, a pot with PUFA should lose MFT preferentially to FFT. The **MFT-selective**
  suppression is the discriminating feature: a mechanism that only scavenges free H2S would hit both
  thiols; one that consumes MFT itself (thiol–enal Michael addition) or its specific precursors
  (1,4-dideoxypentosone, norfuraneol) would be MFT-selective.
- **(c) What it cannot be: the unidentified thiol sink in lipid-free pentose + cysteine pots.** This
  sink needs added PUFA; it says nothing about the sink seen without lipid. It does show that an
  enal-type electrophile is a potent enough thiol sink to remove > 90 % of MFT at 25 mM.
- **(d) pH: none.** One pH (5.5, pyrophosphate), not re-measured after heating.
- **(e) Cysteine/H2S kinetics: none.** No H2S, no cysteine, no time course.
- **(f) Lipid-side shape, usable as hold-out direction for the lipid lane:** Maillard reactants
  **lower** 2-alkenals/2,4-alkadienals and **raise** 2-alkylfurans, alcohols and methyl esters
  (methyl octanoate 764 → 3189 with linoleate; the authors' "cysteine and ribose increased the rate
  of lipid oxidation"). Note this matters for hexanal-type predictions: hexanal falls only 1.7× (within
  the SD) with Cys + ribose.

## 6. FLAGS

1. **Normalisation mismatch.** Each pot is 20 mL, but every quantity is "ng in the headspace of
   100 mL of reaction mixture". Either the values were scaled ×5 from a 20 mL pot, or the headspace
   sampled was not the 20 mL pot. Not resolvable from the paper; ratios between pots are unaffected.
2. **Room-temperature SPME of a two-phase pot.** The methyl-ester oil phase (≈ 147 mg per 20 mL)
   **dissolves lipophilic volatiles and lowers their headspace concentration**. Part of the "drop"
   of MFT and the thiophenes when lipid is added could be partitioning into the oil, not chemistry.
   The authors do not test this. FFT surviving with linoleate argues against a pure partition
   artefact for that compound but not for the more lipophilic thienothiophenes.
3. **Ester vs free fatty acid, and oil phase.** Methyl esters, not triacylglycerols or phospholipids,
   at a 1:1 molar ratio to cysteine — far above meat's free PUFA. Transfer to a food matrix is not
   direct.
4. **Air headspace** (≈ 0.7 mmol O2, Section 4). Oxidation conditions are not those of a vacuum-packed
   or anoxic cook.
5. **Table 1 has no lipid-only columns**, so a cysteine/ribose compound that a heated ester could
   also make (the authors assign three to both groups) cannot be checked against the ester blank
   there; Table 2 carries the ester blanks for the lipid products.
6. **Large CVs** (Section 4). The 18:2 MFT value 58 (±71) is statistically indistinguishable from
   trace.
7. **Identity**: thienothiophene isomers and several disulfides are library/literature spectra only;
   MFT, FFT, 2-methylthiophene, the 2-enals and dienals are MS + LRI. 2-methyl-3-formylthiophene and
   2-ethyl-3-formylthiophene are called tentative in the text but carry "MS + LRI" in Table 3.
8. **Registry (`data/keys/compounds.yml`)**: `2_methyl_3_furanthiol`, `2_furfurylthiol`, `hexanal`,
   `2_pentylfuran`, `2_methylthiophene`, `e_e_2_4_heptadienal`, `e_e_2_4_decadienal` keyed; 2-ethylfuran,
   the C3–C5 enals, thiapyrans and formylthiophenes not keyed.
