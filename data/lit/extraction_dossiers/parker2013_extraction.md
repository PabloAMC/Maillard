## 1. Why it matters

Programme 6 needs primaries, not reviews. This review is Reading's own map of where the kinetic
numbers for Strecker aldehydes, pyrazines and the small dicarbonyls sit: the Reineccius set (1989-1995,
PZO rates and Ea in aqueous buffers, 75-150 C), Wedzicha's three-step glucose-glycine model, Martins
and Brands (van Boekel), Jousse 2002, Desclaux 2006 (xylose-glycine dicarbonyls at 80-120 C), Low
2006-2007 and Balagiannis 2009-2010 (Strecker aldehydes in potato cakes and meat extracts), Koutsidis
2008 (pyrazines and acrylamide in low-moisture starch), and the milk sulfur model of de Wit 2008. Its
verdicts matter for the wave's design: the Strecker aldehydes peak and then fall (Chan 1994, Low
2006), so a PZO rate is an early-time rate; and in a food extract the Strecker rate is independent of
the free amino-acid concentration because peptide and protein amines are in excess (Balagiannis
2009), which is the regime the isolate programme (5d) will meet.

**What the wave should take from the map.** Of the primaries that carry per-amino-acid Strecker
rates at two or more temperatures, the on-disk set is: Chan 1994 (ref 15: methional only, 75-115 C,
`chan1994_extraction.md`) and, for the Leu/Ile pair, Huang 2017 (`huang2017_extraction.md`, not cited
by this review) and Balagiannis 2010 (ref 62, one temperature). NOT on disk and highest priority for
Programme 6: **Chan & Reineccius 1994 ref 16** (3-methylbutanal and phenylacetaldehyde PZO rates and
Ea at pH 6/7/8, 75-115 C, the source of the "Ea 80.4 kJ/mol for 3-MB" that Huang 2017 quotes),
**Cremer & Eichner 2000 ref 26** (2-/3-methylbutanal and 2-methylpropanal Ea 115-124 kJ/mol,
low-moisture, 70-110 C), **Balagiannis 2009 ref 59** (Leu/Ile → methylbutanals at 120/130/140 C with
barriers), **Desclaux 2006 ref 55** (the only tabulated glyoxal / methylglyoxal / 2,3-butanedione /
2,3-pentanedione time courses at 80/100/120 C in water, pH 6, the "dicarbonyl supply" the B18 report
names as the missing piece), **Low 2007 ref 58** (alkylpyrazines with added glycine in a potato
system), **Koutsidis 2008 ref 41** (pyrazines with fed glyoxal / methylglyoxal at 160 C) and
**Jusino 1997 ref 24** (first-order pyrazine kinetics 80-120 C in starch).

## 5. Flags

1. **Review; no number in it is admissible as a rate or barrier.** Every primary it lists has to be
   read itself; this dossier is the reading list with disk status, nothing more.
2. **Two Chan & Reineccius 1994 chapters.** The corpus holds the sulfur chapter (ref 15). The
   Strecker-aldehyde chapter (ref 16, RSC volume pp. 131-139) is a different work and is not on disk;
   `chan1994_extraction.md` must not be cited for 3-methylbutanal or phenylacetaldehyde kinetics.
3. **Two Leahy & Reineccius 1989 chapters**, both on disk: ref 20 (amino acid and sugar type, pH 9) =
   `leahy1989_extraction.md`; ref 14 (pH and aw, milk powder) = `leahy1989a_extraction.md`.
4. **Three Balagiannis items**: ref 23 (Weurman 2010 proceedings, not on disk), ref 59 (JAFC 2009,
   not on disk), ref 62 (ACS 2010 book chapter, on disk as `balagiannis2010`). The on-disk
   `balagiannis2015` is a later review chapter and is not cited here.
5. **"Glu-Arg" (Huang 1989)** is ambiguous in the review's Table 1 (glucose-arginine by the paper's
   title "amino acid-glucose systems"); the entry is left as printed.
6. **"752"** in Table 1 (Vazquez-Landaverde) is "75" with a footnote marker; read as 75 C.
7. **The review's own verdicts** that matter for the wave design — Strecker aldehydes peak then fall
   (PZO fits are early-time), and the Strecker rate in a protein-rich extract is independent of free
   amino acids — are the authors' readings of refs 16, 23 and 59, of which only ref 62's companion is
   on disk.
