# Rizzi 2004 — EXTRACTION
### *"Role of Phosphate and Carboxylate Ions in Maillard Browning"*
### At ~100 °C, initial pH 7.3, 0.10 M phosphate raises 80-min A420 3–100× over 0.10 M bis-tris for eleven sugars with β-alanine, and browns pentoses and galactose even without amine. Halving phosphate (0.10 → 0.05 M) cuts ribose-only A420 ~6×. Citrate, succinate and acetate also catalyse, at about 1/2, 1/3 and 1/8 of phosphate's A420 effect. Read 2026-10-27.

## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**This is a buffer-*type* comparison at one concentration (0.10 M), not a dose series.** The one
exception is a two-point phosphate dose on ribose alone. Sugars (0.10 M) ± β-alanine (0.033 M) were
refluxed (~100 °C) in 0.10 M buffer, initial pH 7.3 ± 0.04, and A420 was read at 80 min. pH was
**not held**: phosphate runs drifted −0.03 to −0.86, carboxylate runs −0.78 to −1.33, bis-tris
≤ 0.17. **Phosphate beats bis-tris for every sugar** (xylose/β-ala 0.837 vs 0.050; ribose/β-ala
1.17 vs 0.061). **Phosphate also browns sugars with no amine at all** (ribose 0.262, xylose 0.150,
galactose 0.041 vs ≤ 0.003 in bis-tris) and fragments ribose to glyoxal, methylglyoxal, diacetyl and
2-oxobutanal, trapped as quinoxalines. None form in bis-tris. **Ribose-only dose: 0 → 0.05 →
0.10 M phosphate gives A420 0.003 → 0.045 → 0.262**, strongly super-linear in A420. Carboxylates
catalyse too: xylose/β-ala in succinate 0.249, citrate 0.379, bisulfite/sulfite 0.191; ribose/β-ala
in acetate 0.15. EDTA leaves 88 % of the phosphate browning and 50 µM Cu²⁺ adds nothing, so the
effect is not trace-metal catalysis. **Figure 1 shows that the phosphate effect grows with time.**
At 10 min, A420 in phosphate ≈ bis-tris (×1.2); at 80 min it is ×17. So an end-point A420 ratio is
not a rate-constant ratio. **κ can be bounded only through an assumed browning order: phosphate
κ ≈ 20–90 M⁻¹ (on A420 at 80 min, ~100 °C), citrate ≈ 12–38, succinate ≈ 9–25** [Z]. All rest on
one concentration.

[Z] Local log–log slopes (A ∝ tⁿ): bis-tris **n ≈ 1.2–1.4**, roughly constant (1.18, 1.34, 1.30);

on one step.

## 4. DERIVED QUANTITIES [Z]

### 4.1 Why an A420 ratio is not a κ

The model wants k_buffer/k_ref for a step. Rizzi gives A420 at one time, and Fig. 1 shows the
P/BT ratio climbing from 1.2 to 17.7 between 10 and 80 min. If buffer acted as a single multiplier
f on every rate, A(t) = g(f·t) and A_P(t)/A_BT(t) ≈ fⁿ, where n is the local log–log slope of g. I
bound f with the two observed shapes: **n = 1.3** (bis-tris curve), which gives an upper κ, and
**n = 2.6** (the phosphate curve's mean slope over 10–80 min), which gives a lower κ.
κ = (f − 1)/[buffer], with [buffer] = 0.10 M.

| system (80 min, ~100 °C, initial pH ~7.1–7.8) | A420 ratio R vs bis-tris | f = R^(1/2.6) | f = R^(1/1.3) | **κ range (M⁻¹)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xylose/β-ala, phosphate | 16.7 | 2.95 | 8.7 | **20–77** |
| ribose/β-ala, phosphate | 19.2 | 3.11 | 9.7 | **21–87** |
| xylose/β-ala, citrate | 7.6 | 2.18 | 4.8 | **12–38** |
| xylose/β-ala, succinate | 5.0 | 1.86 | 3.45 | **9–25** |
| xylose/β-ala, bisulfite/sulfite | 3.8 | 1.67 | 2.8 | 7–18 (a carbonyl trap; not a catalysis estimate) |
| ribose/β-ala, acetate | 2.5 | 1.42 | 2.0 | 4–10 (pH 7.8 start, −1.33 drift) |

A **time-equivalence** estimate for xylose/β-ala in phosphate falls inside that range. The
phosphate curve reaches the bis-tris 80-min A420 (0.048) at ≈ 34 min, so f ≈ 80/34 = 2.35 and
**κ ≈ 13.5 M⁻¹**. Reading the curves at earlier times gives smaller f, down to f ≈ 1.2 at 10 min
(κ ≈ 2). **Best reading: phosphate κ on A420-forming chemistry is of order 10–90 M⁻¹ at ~100 °C.
It is not one number, because the effect builds up over the reaction.**

**Rank order per mole at 0.10 M, ~pH 7.2, ~100 °C (independent of n):** phosphate > citrate
(~0.45× phosphate's A420) > succinate (~0.30×) > sulfite > acetate > bis-tris (reference).

### 4.2 The only dose series (ribose, no amine)

| [phosphate] (M) | A420, 80 min | ratio vs bis-tris |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (0.1 M bis-tris) | 0.003 (Table 1; at noise, ±20 %+) | 1 |
| 0.05 (in 0.10 M bis-tris) | 0.045 | 15 |
| 0.10 (phosphate only) | 0.262 | 87 |

- In A420 terms doubling phosphate gives ×5.8, an **apparent order of 2.5**: super-linear.
- If A ∝ (k t)^2.6 (the xylose/β-ala phosphate curve shape), k(0.10)/k(0.05) = 5.8^(1/2.6) =
  **1.97**, i.e. **first order in phosphate at the rate-constant level**. That rests on borrowing
  the shape from a different system [!].
- For sugar-only browning the bis-tris reference is at the noise floor, so **k0 ≈ 0 and
  κ = (f − 1)/[P] is undefined (→ ∞)**. **This channel should be modelled as an additive,
  phosphate-dependent sugar degradation (rate ∝ k_P·[P]·[sugar]), not as a multiplier on an
  existing step.**
- **[!] Confounds:** the 0.05 M run also contains 0.10 M bis-tris and the 0.10 M run does not;
  ionic strength differs (~0.2 vs ~0.3 M); buffer capacity at 0.05 M is lower, so pH drift was
  presumably larger (ΔpH not given for 0.05 M).
- Glycerophosphate (0.05 M) gives 0.026, about 0.6× of inorganic phosphate at 0.05 M. A phosphate
  monoester, which has only one ionisable OH left at pH 7, still catalyses.

### 4.3 pH drift direction

Phosphate runs became more acidic than bis-tris runs (e.g. xylose/β-ala −0.50 vs −0.09). Lower pH
slows amine–carbonyl chemistry, so **the phosphate A420 ratios are, if anything, *under*-estimates
of the fixed-pH catalytic effect.** Carboxylate runs drifted more (−0.78 to −0.87), so their ratios
are under-estimated more. The author: phosphate effects "at nearly constant pH (cf. xylose,
galactose, and fructose reactions, Table 1)" show it acts "independently of hydrogen and/or
hydroxide ion concentration".

## 5. WHAT THIS CAN AND CANNOT ANCHOR IN THE MODEL

**Can anchor:**
1. **Phosphate opens a sugar-only degradation channel at neutral pH and ~100 °C** that is
   essentially absent without it (ribose 0.262 vs 0.003; xylose 0.150 vs 0.003; galactose 0.041 vs
   0.001). It yields C2/C3 dicarbonyls (MGO > glyoxal ≫ 2-oxobutanal > diacetyl, by peak area)
   and 1-deoxypentosone (~3.3 % molar as isolated quinoxalines). **This is the single most
   important finding for the κ design:** part of the "phosphate catalysis" of browning is not
   amine chemistry at all, so a κ fitted on browning or on sugar loss with amine present (Potman
   1989) conflates the two. A holdout check: *a model with phosphate acting only on amine steps
   predicts no A420 for ribose + phosphate without amine; Rizzi measures 0.26 at 80 min.*
2. **Carboxylate buffers catalyse at ~100 °C** (citrate ≈ 0.45×, succinate ≈ 0.3× phosphate in
   80-min A420 at 0.1 M), against Bell 1997's null at 25 °C. **If one generic κ_carboxylate is
   used, it must be temperature-dependent or the two papers cannot both be met.**
3. **The rank order** phosphate > citrate > succinate > acetate ≈ sulfite > bis-tris can serve as
   a qualitative ordering check.
4. **No trace-metal confound** (EDTA 88 %, Cu²⁺ nil).

**Cannot anchor:**
- **A κ value with less than a factor ~4 uncertainty.** There is one concentration, end-point
  A420, an unstated browning order, and the ratio grows with time.
- **The species.** At pH 7.3 phosphate is mostly HPO4²⁻. The author favours an "XO2⁻" motif shared
  by H2PO4⁻ and carboxylates but states "Our data does not exclude" HPO4²⁻.
- **The step.** Only A420 and sugar-only dicarbonyls are measured. There is no amine loss, no sugar
  loss and no Amadori product.
- **Temperature dependence**: one temperature, and "ca. 100–105 °C" at that.

## 6. CAVEATS AND FLAGS

1. **[!] "Semilogarithmic" vs log–log Figure 1**, and "exponential" growth that the figure does not
   show. The numbers in the figure agree with Table 1 at 80 min, so the data are consistent; only
   the description is wrong.
2. **[!] Initial pH 7.3 is inconsistent with the stated equimolar Na2HPO4/NaH2PO4 recipe** (~6.85),
   and the pH measurement temperature is unstated. The pH at reflux will differ (bis-tris dpKa/dT
   ≈ −0.017 K⁻¹ ⇒ ~1.3 units lower at 100 °C; phosphate nearly temperature-flat), so **the "same
   initial pH" in the two buffers is not the same pH at 100 °C**. At reflux the bis-tris reference
   is probably ~1 unit more acidic than the phosphate run. Part of the P/BT ratio could be a hot-pH
   artefact, which runs the opposite way to §4.3.
3. **[!] Bis-tris is a tertiary amine**, so not a Maillard amine, but a possible general base. The
   "inert" reference is assumed, not shown.
4. **[!] Dilution:** whether A420 values are corrected for the 1:4 dilution is not stated, which
   matters only for absolute comparison with other papers, not for ratios.
5. **Single runs** for most entries; ±3–20 % stated precision, worst at low A420, i.e. exactly the
   bis-tris denominators.
6. **Mechanism (Fig. 4)** — nucleophilic addition of XO2⁻ to the sugar carbonyl followed by
   intramolecular proton abstraction (enolisation → aldose/ketose isomerisation; dehydration →
   3-deoxyosone) — is proposed, not tested.
