## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

**Is 1-deoxypentos-2,3-diulose → methylglyoxal + glycolaldehyde admissible, and by what route?** The
review does not discuss any pentose deoxyosone. The only route that could give MGO + glycolaldehyde
from a 1-deoxypentosone is **retro-aldol cleavage** (C3-C4), the C5 analogue of Table 1's row
"1-deoxyglucosone → methylglyoxal + glyceraldehyde", whose basis is Weenen's 1-13C-glucose labelling
and whose comment is **"results disproven by Voigt (43)"**: "degradation incubations of independently
synthesized 1-deoxyglucosone led to extremely mismatched amounts of methylglyoxal and glyceraldehyde.
Thus, under the tested reaction conditions retro-aldolization seems to be very unlikely to explain the
formation of methylglyoxal from 1-deoxyglucosone." General verdict: "there is, to the best of our
knowledge, no persuasive experimental proof for the retro-aldol reaction of dicarbonyls. Thus,
fragmentation routes based on this mechanism remain largely hypothetical." The four other mechanisms
cannot yield MGO + glycolaldehyde from a 1-deoxypentosone: α-cleavages give a carboxylic acid + an
aldehyde (hydrolytic, judged "nonexistent") or two acids (oxidative); β-cleavages need a
2,4-dicarbonyl and give a carboxylic acid (or amide) + an α-hydroxycarbonyl / enediol. [Z] Applied to
the 2,4-tautomer of a 1-deoxypentosone by direct analogy with Table 4 / Fig. 6 (sec. 3), β-cleavage
gives acetic acid + a C3 enediol, or hydroxyacetone + glycolic acid; neither pair is
methylglyoxal + glycolaldehyde. So **the route is admissible only as the retro-aldol reaction the authors call
largely hypothetical and contradicted for the hexose analogue; the review gives no measured pentose
fragment yield.** It names β-dicarbonyl cleavage as "the almost unrivaled fragmentation pathway"
quantitatively.

## 2. THE FIVE MECHANISMS AND THEIR EVIDENCE [M]

| mechanism | bond / products | evidence cited | review's verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) retro-aldol (Table 1, Fig. 2) | Cα-Cβ next to a carbonyl of a β-hydroxy-carbonyl → shorter hydroxy-carbonyls / dicarbonyls | 1-DG → 1-deoxythreosone + glycolaldehyde (hypothetical, Hodge 1953); **1-DG → MGO + glyceraldehyde (Weenen 1998 1-13C; "disproven by Voigt")**; **3-DG → MGO + glyceraldehyde (Thornalley 1999; "only 0.18 mol-% methylglyoxal")**; 2,5-tautomer of 1-DG → acetol + 3-hydroxypyruvaldehyde (Yaylayan & Keyhani 2000 labelling; "quantification of counterparts not performed"); 1,4-dideoxyglucosone → diacetyl + glycolaldehyde (Pfeifer & Kroh 2010; disproven: neither detected from synthesised 1,4-dideoxyglucosone, Smuda & Glomb 2011) | "largely hypothetical"; "needs to be challenged" |
| (ii) hydrolytic α-dicarbonyl (Table 2, Figs. 3-4) | C-C between the two carbonyls → acid + aldehyde | γ-pyranone → lactic acid + glyceraldehyde (Kim & Baltes 1996); 3-DG → formic acid + 2-deoxypentose (Brands & van Boekel 2001; furfuryl alcohol ~1000× short of formic acid); Ginz 2000 | **"nonexistent"** (2,3-pentanedione gives acetic + propanoic acid, not propanal / acetaldehyde, Davidek 2006) |
| (iii) oxidative α-dicarbonyl (Table 3, Fig. 5) | O2 / peroxide attack at C2 or C3, Baeyer-Villiger → two acids | 1-DG → acetic acid + erythronic acid (13C, 18O2; Davidek 2006); 2,3-diketogulonic acid → oxalic + threonic acid | established; "minor, but important" |
| (iv) hydrolytic β-dicarbonyl (Table 4, Fig. 6) | retro-Claisen of a 2,4-dicarbonyl → acid + α-hydroxy-carbonyl / enediol | 2,4-tautomer of 1-DG → acetol + glyceric acid (Hayami 1961; Weenen 1-13C); → acetic acid + erythrulose (Davidek, Voigt); **maltosone → formic acid + 3-deoxypentosone, 16 mol %** (Smuda & Glomb 2011) | "the almost unrivaled fragmentation pathway" |
| (v) amine-induced β-dicarbonyl (Table 5, Fig. 7) | as (iv) with Lys-NH2 → amide-AGE + α-hydroxy-carbonyl | 1-DG → Nε-acetyl-lysine + erythrulose; 2,3-diketogulonic acid → Nε-oxalyl-lysine + erythrulose | established (Glomb lab) |

(C1-C3), as 1-DG gives glyceric acid + acetol. Neither produces methylglyoxal or glycolaldehyde.

## 4. USE IN THE MODEL

1. **Admissibility of `r_dpo_c2c3` (DPO → HA + MGO, retro-aldol):** the review gives no evidence
   for or against the pentose split specifically; for the hexose analogue it reports the retro-aldol
   MGO route as contradicted by mismatched counterpart amounts (Voigt 2010, numbers not given in the
   review) and the 3-DG route as tiny (0.18 mol %). A model that keeps the pentose split must carry it
   as an assumption, and the counterpart test (HA vs MGO amounts) is the discriminating observable;
   hofmann1999 is the corpus's only pot that measures both.
2. **Competing routes the model may lack:** β-dicarbonyl cleavage of the 1-deoxyosone to acetic acid
   (and, with lysine, amide-AGEs) is named quantitatively dominant; oxidative α-cleavage needs O2.
3. **Primary papers worth reading for numbers** (none is in `data/articles/` or has a dossier as of
   2026-10-30): Voigt,
   Smuda, Pfahler & Glomb 2010 JAFC 58:5685 (1-DG fragment amounts, ± O2); Thornalley 1999 (3-DG → MGO
   conditions); Davidek 2006 JAFC 54:6667 / 6677; Davidek 2008 Ann NY Acad Sci 1126:241 (acetic acid
   yields in **pentose** systems).
