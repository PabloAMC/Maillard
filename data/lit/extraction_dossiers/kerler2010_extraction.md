## 3. The pH 3-4 claim checked against its sources (arithmetic mine) [Z]

- **Hofmann & Schieberle 1998a** (J. Agric. Food Chem. 46, 235-241; dossier `hofmann1998_reconciliation.md`,
  Table 2): Cys 3.3 mmol + carbohydrate 10 mmol in 100 mL 0.5 M phosphate, 145 °C, 20 min, pH 3.0 / 5.0 / 7.0.
  - ribose: MFT **55.3 / 19.8 / 2.5 µg**, FFT 22.9 / 12.1 / 1.2 → pH 3 beats pH 5 by **2.8× (MFT), 1.9× (FFT)**;
    pH 5 beats pH 7 by 7.9× and 10×.
  - glucose: MFT 0.3 / **1.9** / 0.4; FFT 0.7 / **2.8** / 0.6 → **optimum pH 5**, pH 3 is 6× (MFT) worse than pH 5.
  - rhamnose: MFT 0.1 / **0.8** / 0.1 → optimum pH 5.
  - No pH 4 point. So "pH 3-4" is true for ribose (only pH 3 tested below 5) and false for hexoses.
- **Madruga & Mottram 1995** (J. Sci. Food Agric. 68, 305-310; dossier `madruga1995_extraction.md`): minced
  beef acidified to pH 5.6 / 5.0 / 4.5 / 4.0, 140 °C, 30 min; MFT rises ≥ 65× from pH 5.6 to 4.0 (lowest pH
  tested 4.0; no pH 3). FFT does not follow the same trend (per that dossier).
- **Meynier & Mottram 1995** (not cited by Kerler for this claim; `meynier1995_extraction.md`): ribose +
  cysteine pH 4.5-6.5, MFT collapses > 152× from 4.5 to 6.5 — monotone, no optimum resolved (no pH < 4.5).

**Reading:** the measured law is "MFT/FFT formation falls steeply from pH ~4.5 to 7 with pentoses (×8-150);
below pH 4.5 data exist only at pH 3 (ribose, Hofmann, 2.8× above pH 5) and pH 4.0 (meat, Madruga)". There
is no measured optimum *at* 3-4; there is a monotone rise down to the lowest pH tested for ribose, and a pH-5
optimum for hexoses. Kerler's sentence over-generalises.
