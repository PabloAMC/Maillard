## 1. Why it matters

Programme 6 wants the Strecker step per amino acid with the carbonyl partner named. This paper's
partner is the α-keto acid: a carbonyl that is itself an amino-acid product (transamination or
lipid-carbonyl oxidation) and that hands its own nitrogen back as a new amino acid, so the
"Strecker" here is a transamination-decarboxylation that regenerates an amino acid rather than an
aminoketone. It matters to the lipid-Maillard programme (roadmap 5d) as the delayed route: oxidised
lipid → α-keto acid → Strecker aldehyde of a second amino acid after the lipid is gone. It carries one
printed barrier (38.2 kJ/mol, Phe + glyoxylic acid, pH 3, 80-140 C) and a set of within-study
orderings across eleven α-keto acids; and it states that no phenylethylamine was formed with any
α-keto acid, which is the aldehyde/amine split for this carbonyl class.

## 2. Methods as they matter to a model

- **Phe / α-keto acid mixtures (Section 2.2).** "A solution of phenylalanine (20 µmol) and the α-keto
  acid (0-20 µmol) in 320 µl of 0.3 M buffer (sodium citrate for pH 2.15-6, sodium phosphate for pH
  6-8 and pH 11-12, and sodium borate for pH 8-10) was heated at 37-140 C in closed test tubes for 1 h
  (in most experiments)." Concentrations: 20 µmol / 0.320 mL = **62.5 mmol/L phenylalanine**; the
  α-keto acid **0 to 62.5 mmol/L** (equimolar, 62.5 mmol/L, in Fig. 1 and Figs. 2A, 2C, 2D). Buffer
  0.3 mol/L. Cooling 5 min at room temperature, 10 min at -20 C.
- **Conditions per figure.** Fig. 1 (eleven α-keto acids + OHP + control): 100 C, 1 h, pH 3,
  equimolar. Fig. 2A (pH 2.15-12): 100 C, 1 h. Fig. 2B (0-20 µmol GlyKA): pH 3, 100 C 1 h or 37 C 6
  days. Fig. 2C (0-100 % O2): pH 3, 100 C, 1 h. Fig. 2D (time and temperature): pH 3, 140, 120, 100
  and 80 C; the times are not stated in the text.
- **In situ route (Section 2.3).** Glycine 20 µmol + 4,5-epoxy-2-decenal 20 µmol (62.5 mmol/L each) in
  320 µL 0.3 M citrate pH 3, 37 or 60 C, 2 days; then phenylalanine 20 µmol added (a further 62.5
  mmol/L, nominal, if the volume is unchanged) and 3 more days at the same temperature. Controls:
  glycine heated alone. The epoxyalkenal was made by epoxidation of (E,E)-2,4-decadienal with mCPBA.
- **Phenylacetaldehyde quantification (2.4).** NaBH4 (~1 mg, 10 min) reduces PAC to 2-phenylethanol;
  acetone (300 µL) quenches; 30 µL internal standard (cis-3-nonenol, 15 µL in 25 mL ethanol); GC-MS
  (HP 6890 / Agilent 5973, HP5-MS 30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 µm, 40 → 240 C at 5 C/min then 300 C at 10
  C/min, EI 70 eV); SIM m/z 122 (quantifier) and 91 for 2-phenylethanol, m/z 81 (quantifier), 95, 124
  for the IS. **Calibration: eight-level standard curves of the aldehyde prepared in the 320 µL of
  buffer and carried through the whole procedure**; r > 0.98; CV < 10 %. Absolute, matrix-matched.
- **Amino acids and epoxyalkenal (2.5).** Methyl chloroformate derivatisation (Villas-Bôas 2003,
  modified), IS octanoic acid; GC-MS SIM (glycine derivative m/z 88; phenylalanine derivative m/z 162;
  epoxydecenal m/z 68; IS methyl octanoate m/z 74); six-level matrix-matched curves, r > 0.99, CV < 10 %.
- **Glyoxylic acid (2.6).** Dansylhydrazine derivatisation (TfOH catalyst, 25 C 1 h), IS 2-oxovaleric
  acid; LC-MS/MS (API 2000, ESI+, MRM 322 → 236.1 quantifier, 322 → 171.1, 322 → 156.2; IS 364 →
  236.1); five-level matrix-matched curves, r > 0.99, CV < 15 %.
- **Replicates / statistics.** "mean ± SD values of, at least, four independent experiments"; ANOVA
  + Tukey, p < 0.05 (Origin 7.0).
- **Kinetic treatment (verbatim).** "Phenylacetaldehyde concentration also increased linearly as a
  function of time and temperature between 80 and 140 C ... Reaction rates at different temperatures
  were calculated by using this equation: [phenylacetaldehyde] = [phenylacetaldehyde]0 + kt where
  [phenylacetaldehyde]0 represents the intercept, k is the rate constant and t is time. These rate
  constants were used in an Arrhenius plot ... From the slope of the obtained line (r = -0.9999,
  p < 0.0001), the Ea was determined. Ea was 38.2 kJ/mol." The k values themselves are NOT printed.

## 5. Flags

1. **One barrier, no rates.** 38.2 kJ/mol is printed; the four zero-order k values behind it and every
   PAC level are figure-only. The repository can carry the barrier and the orderings, not a rate.
2. **The barrier is low and the mechanism is not the dicarbonyl Strecker.** 38 kJ/mol at pH 3 with an
   α-keto acid whose reaction is a transamination that returns an amino acid; the authors themselves
   note PAC forms at 37 C (6 days gives 70 % of 100 C / 1 h). It should not be pooled with
   dicarbonyl-driven Strecker barriers (Zhou 2024's 100-112 kJ/mol lumped, Cremer & Eichner's
   115-124) as the same step.
3. **Zero-order in time over what interval?** The times of Fig. 2D are not stated; "linearly" is the
   authors' claim. With 62.5 mmol/L reactants and yields that are (from the Zamora 2015 analogue)
   at most a few per cent, the reactants are effectively constant, so "zero order" is an initial-rate
   statement, as in Zhou 2024.
4. **pH series crosses three buffers** (citrate ≤ 6, phosphate 6-8 and 11-12, borate 8-10) at
   0.3 mol/L; buffer catalysis is confounded with pH, and the decline above pH 9 is in borate/phosphate.
5. **Closed tubes, 320 µL, headspace unknown**; PAC (bp 195 C) is not very volatile, but the 140 C
   runs are above the boiling point of the aqueous buffer, so pressure and partitioning are unstated.
6. **Amine null is a detection statement**, not a quantified limit: "was not observed" with the
   PAC method (which monitors 2-phenylethanol); the amine assay of Zamora 2015 (m/z 91 after the
   same work-up) is not said to have been applied here.
7. **In situ experiment is semi-quantitative**: the PAC formed at 60 C exceeded the GlyKA formed,
   which the authors attribute to other glycine-epoxyalkenal products; the route "lipid → α-keto
   acid → second Strecker aldehyde" is shown to exist but its share is not measured.
8. **Ordering statistics**: LeuKA and IleKA "did not produce a significant increase"; OHP, a
   non-conjugated 6-oxo acid, did. So "α-keto acid" is not a clean reactivity class; the carbonyl's
   electrophilicity matters more than the conjugated carboxyl.
9. Registry gaps: glyoxylic and pyruvic acids and 4,5-epoxy-2-decenal have no `compounds.yml` rows.
