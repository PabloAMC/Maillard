## 1. Why it cannot be scored

**Every volatile number in this paper is a peak area.** Figure 4's caption is explicit: "Mean peak
areas (counts per second; n = 3) of five indicator compounds". There is **no internal standard, no
external standard and no calibration curve** for volatiles — standards were used only to confirm
identity. Hexanal, 2-pentylfuran, methylpyrazine, furfural and 2,5-dimethylpyrazine appear **only in
Figure 4**, as peak areas, with no printed values. The repository refuses to score a peak area as a
concentration, so none of it is taken.

**And there are no volatile data for unextruded bran at all** — verbatim, "Volatile compounds **of the
extrudates** were analysed". The unprocessed and processed material never appear side by side for any
volatile.

## 3. The relative statements, which are all this paper gives the model

Verbatim, and all ratios rather than values:

- "The amounts of volatile Maillard products in coarse bran extrudates produced at low water content
  (13 and 16 %) were **up to 40 times higher** than those amounts in the extrudate produced at 140 °C
  and 22 % water."
- "the levels of the two indicator pyrazines were **up to four times higher** in the extrudate
  produced at 13 % water than in the extrudate produced at 16 % water".
- for the fine bran, "The formation of volatile oxidation products was in general **one-third**
  compared to the extrusion series with the coarse rye bran."

That is a **water-activity ordering on Maillard product formation in a real extruder**, and it points
the same way the model does: less water, more pyrazine. It supports a directional claim and nothing
more.

## 4. Storage losses (quantified, and the only rates in the paper)

α-Tocopherol loss over 10 weeks at 40 °C: 68–76 % at 80, 100 and 140 °C, about 90 % at 120 °C (22 %
water); 11 and 32 % at 13 and 16 % water against 84 and 78 % at 22 and 30 % water (coarse, 120 °C);
4 and 7 % against 25 and 65 % for the fine bran. These are percentage losses over a fixed interval at
one temperature — **not rate constants**, and a rate cannot be regressed from them without the figure.

## 5. Verdict

A cereal bran rather than a legume protein, volatiles as peak areas, no unextruded volatile baseline.
It contributes one directional claim about water and pyrazines and is otherwise recorded as read.
