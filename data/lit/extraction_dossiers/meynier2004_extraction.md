### 4a. ⚠️⚠️ **[!] THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT METHODOLOGICAL AMBIGUITY IN THIS PAPER**

The NaBH₄ step, verbatim (§2.7, p. 682):

> *"Solutions were treated with **40 mM NaBH₄ at 37 °C for 15 min** (Alaiz & Giron, 1994)
> **to prevent the reduction of aldehyde groups during the acid hydrolysis**."*

**As printed, that sentence is chemically incoherent: sodium borohydride is a reducing agent
and cannot "prevent reduction".** The cited protocol (Alaiz & Giron 1994, *JAFC* 42, 2094) is
the standard **borohydride trapping** step: NaBH₄ reduces the labile **imine (Schiff base)
C=N to a stable secondary amine**, so that the adduct **survives** the subsequent acid
hydrolysis instead of reverting to free lysine. **The intended sentence is almost certainly
"to prevent the *reversion* of aldehyde adducts during the acid hydrolysis" — the exact
opposite of what the printed word "reduction" conveys.**

**This matters enormously, because it decides what Fig. 6 measures:**

| reading | what the Lys/His loss counts | consequence for the repo |
|---|---|---|
| **(i) NaBH₄ traps Schiff bases** *(the standard protocol, and what Alaiz & Giron actually did — this wave's preferred reading)* | **Schiff bases + Michael adducts + cross-links = TOTAL COVALENT ENGAGEMENT, reversible imines included** | Fig. 6 is an **UPPER bound** on the truly irreversible pool. The imine fraction would release hexanal on dilution or on warming. |
| **(ii) the sentence read literally** | only adducts that survive 6 N HCl **unaided** = irreversible only | Fig. 6 is a **clean irreversible measurement**, and the imine fraction is invisible on top of it |

**⇒ REGISTER FIG. 6 AS `covalent_total_NaBH4_trapped` AND TREAT IT AS AN UPPER BOUND ON THE
IRREVERSIBLE SINK.** Reading (i) is chemically forced and is what the cited method does; but
because the paper's own sentence says the opposite, **no row derived from Fig. 6 may be
labelled "irreversible" without this flag riding on it.**

**This ambiguity does not weaken the k₂ derivation** — §8 derives a *covalent-engagement* rate,
which is the quantity the repo needs for a sink term. It weakens only the claim that the sink
is one-way. **On that question this paper is exactly as silent as the adduct survey was
(`anantharamkrishnan2020b_extraction.md` §5d, X-5): both assert irreversibility; neither
tests it.** ⇒ **The corpus still has ZERO measured reversibility data on an aldehyde–protein
covalent adduct.** That gap survives this wave.

### 8f. **The assumption chain — every step that could move this by ≥ 2×** [!]
1. **⚠️ THE DOMINANT ONE: for hexanal, the signal is inside the method's own noise (§5c).**

3. **One aldehyde per lost residue.** Cross-links consume one aldehyde across two residues,

4. **The NaBH₄ ambiguity (§4a).** Under the standard reading, Fig. 6 counts trapped Schiff

6. **Second-order kinetics with no carbinolamine pre-equilibrium**, and all Lys/His treated as

   disordered one (Na-CN); note the **caseinate rates come out 1.5–3.7× faster than the whey

7. **Mean residue MW 113 g mol⁻¹** and the 0.96 denominator correction — each ±10 %.
8. **The pH drifts to ≈6.2–6.3** at 4 g L⁻¹ (§6e) and the system is unbuffered; carbonyl–amine

9. **One temperature. Eₐ is not measurable from this paper.**
10. **The "30 g L⁻¹ powder vs protein" ambiguity (§3b [!])** — affects extents by +8/+22 %,

**⭐ But note the STRUCTURAL asymmetry, which is genuinely new and which the repo should
internalize.** Reversible binding is **capped**: its suppression factor is fixed by a partition
constant, so it can never exceed a single-digit-to-low-tens factor at any protein level. The
covalent sink is **uncapped in extent** — lysine is in 360–36 000× excess over food-relevant
hexanal (§8e), so depletion is exponential in time and *can* in principle reach any factor.
**The covalent channel is therefore the only mechanism in the corpus with the right functional
form to produce two-to-four-order suppression. It simply is not fast enough at 20 °C.**
**⇒ The right question is no longer "is the mechanism big enough?" but "is it fast enough at
process temperature?" — which turns entirely on an activation energy nobody has measured.**

## 12. ⭐ VERDICT — **DOES THIS CLOSE OR SIZE THE COVALENT-SINK CHANNEL?**

| question | **verdict** |
|---|---|
| **Is this the expected paper?** | **YES — every bibliographic field matches.** |

The brackets overlap at k₂ ≈ 6×10⁻⁶ – 1.3×10⁻⁵ M⁻¹ s⁻¹ and t½ ≈ 37–74 days.** K4d's is a lower bound, this is an upper bound, and they meet. **First two-source agreement in the corpus on this channel.** |
| **⭐ Does covalent + reversible close the hexanal matrix gap?** | **NO — not at ambient temperature, and this is now a computed result rather than a judgement. Reversible 25.4 % + covalent 0.06 % over a threshold-panel timescale = 25.5 % of the 1 304× log shift. Even a month of storage reaches only ≈40 %. `k2_matrix_and_thresholds.md` §B.4's conclusion stands unamended.** |
| **Is there any corner where covalent DOES close it?** | **YES, one: process temperature with Eₐ ≳ 70 kJ mol⁻¹, where 36× arrives in 36 min – 2.2 h at 140 °C. ### **⇒ THE HONEST ONE-LINE SUMMARY**
**The adduct survey turned the covalent hexanal sink from "a mechanism that might not apply" into
"a mechanism that applies, of unknown size". This paper turns it into "a mechanism that applies,
whose ambient size is bracketed to within half an order of magnitude, and which is demonstrably
too slow at ambient — by ~250× in required rate — to explain the matrix gap." That is progress
of exactly the kind that closes an investigation rather than one that closes a gap. The gap
stays open, and it now has a single named blocker: the activation energy.**
