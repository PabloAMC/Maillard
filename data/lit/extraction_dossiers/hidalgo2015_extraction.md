## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH ANSWER

The review maps the lipid-carbonyl → amino-acid network onto one scheme (Fig. 11). Imine A (amino
acid + lipid carbonyl) rearranges three ways. Loss of the carboxyl proton gives imine B → **Strecker
aldehyde** (Fig. 1). Loss of the α-proton gives imine C → **α-keto acid** (Fig. 4). A 5-oxazolidinone →
azomethine ylide gives imine D → **amine**, or imine B (Fig. 6). Downstream: α-keto acid → Strecker
aldehyde or the C(n−2) aldehyde; amine → Strecker aldehyde or the vinylogous olefin (styrene,
acrylamide); olefin + nucleophiles → adducts. Hydroperoxides add a free-radical route (Fig. 3).
Acid pH favours aldehyde, keto-acid and amine formation; neutral or basic pH favours olefin
elimination and nucleophile addition. Oxygen favours Strecker aldehydes and suppresses olefins. Water
activity matters most for the elimination step. **The only numbers are: 13 activation energies
(Table 1, all for Phe, Asn, PEA, 3-aminopropionamide or acrylamide, two cited as "unpublished"),
two amine-to-carbonyl yields (benzylamine 4.3 %, 2-phenylglycine methyl ester 49 %), and Grosch's
"< 5 mol %" volatiles.** For the model's question: **the review cites no primary study that
quantifies 3-methylbutanal, 2-methylbutanal, 2-methylpropanal or methional from a lipid oxidation
product.** It never names leucine, isoleucine, valine or methionine as lipid-Strecker substrates.
Methionine appears only in the title of a cited carbohydrate pyrolysis paper. The generality to
"amino acids" is asserted, not tested.

## 2. METHODS

A narrative review with no new experiments and no search protocol. Its evidence base is
essentially the authors' own 2004–2012 model-system papers (closed tubes, pH 3 citrate, 37–180 °C,
phenylalanine as the probe amino acid, GC-MS quantification), plus mechanistic literature (Chu &
Yaylayan; Tsuge; Aurelio; Granvogl) and acrylamide literature.

## 4. WHAT THE MODEL CAN TAKE FROM IT

**The question: does the review cite any primary study that quantifies a Strecker aldehyde from
leucine, isoleucine, valine or methionine produced by a lipid oxidation product?** **No.** Every
quantified lipid-Strecker study it cites uses **phenylalanine** (PAC as the read-out), or asparagine
via the amine/acrylamide route. Its one methionine citation (Yaylayan & Keyhani 2001) is a
methionine/glucose pyrolysis paper. No yield, temperature or pH is printed for any non-Phe
Strecker aldehyde.

**Does the review say whether phenylalanine results transfer to other amino acids?** Only by
assertion and only qualitatively:
- The title, the abstract and Figs 1/4/6/11 are written for "amino acids" with a generic R. The
  conclusions (p. 18) say amino acids "are easily degraded by lipid oxidation products in a similar
  way to carbohydrates".
- Against transfer: p. 10–11 says the amine/aldehyde split "will depend on the amino acid and the
  lipid-derived reactive carbonyl", citing unpublished work. Pp. 16–17 say "both amino acids and
  carbonyls usually play a significant role in the activation energy", with Phe → PEA vs Asn → APA
  as the example. Pp. 6–7 make the α-carbon substituents decide the keto-acid route.
- So the authors' own position is that **Phe-derived numbers do not transfer without an
  amino-acid-specific correction**, and they print none.

**Usable content:**
1. **Table 1 as an index** of Ea values already held as primary dossiers (Zamora 2013, 2010;
   Hidalgo 2010b). The three entries with no primary dossier yet are Phe → PEA 54 (Zamora 2012a,
   not on disk), PEA → PAC 55/54 (Zamora 2012b, not on disk) and Phe → phenylpyruvate 48/49–51
   ("unpublished" here; the 37–60 °C chemistry is in Zamora 2006b, not on disk). Cite the primaries,
   not the review.
2. **Network topology** (Fig. 11) for any future lipid-amino-acid lane. Branches: aldehyde / α-keto
   acid / amine from one imine; amine → aldehyde recycling; C(n−2) aldehyde under O2. The relevant
   C(n−2) products for meat-aroma amino acids would be 2-methylpropanal from leucine and
   methylthio-acetaldehyde from methionine. This is mechanism, no rates.
3. **Directional claims** that can be panel rows: acid pH favours lipid-driven Strecker aldehydes;
   O2 favours aldehydes over amines and suppresses olefins. These are already supported with
   numbers by Zamora 2008/2015.

**Verdict for the model:** the review closes the search *within the Seville group's cited
work*. A lipid-driven Strecker step for leucine or methionine **cannot be built from measured
numbers** from this source. The best available is the Phe constant (Zamora 2013/2015) applied to
Leu/Met as a **declared assumption with a wide band**, supported indirectly by Hidalgo 2016's
competition data. A measured row needs a primary paper outside this review that reports
3-methylbutanal or methional from a lipid carbonyl or hydroperoxide.
