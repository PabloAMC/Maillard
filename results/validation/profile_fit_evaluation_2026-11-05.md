## Findings

- One evaluation: R0 3.92 s serial -> 2.28 / 1.67 / 1.18 s at 2 / 4 / 8 workers (efficiency 0.86 / 0.59 / 0.41); C1 9.86 s -> 5.42 / 3.22 / 2.92 s (0.91 / 0.77 / 0.42; C1's other W=4 repeats 3.88 - 3.96 s at load ~3).
- Stragglers are NOT what limits an evaluation. R0's 38 serial tasks: median 0.068 s, the slowest (b60:hofmann_pentose_pH5, 0.46 s) 12 % of the total, the slowest 10 60 %. At W=4 the systems phase is 90 % busy and within 4 % of a longest-first schedule (1.24 vs 1.20 s; lower bound 1.19 s). C1's route phase at W=4 is 89 % busy (longest first: 1.82 -> 1.66 s); only at W=8 does order matter (1.54 -> 1.19 s).
- What does limit it: (a) every task runs slower when several run at once -- R0's pot-time sum is 3.59 s serial, 4.77 s at W=4 (+33 %), 5.50 s at W=8 (+53 %), with CPU time = wall time, i.e. slower cores (4 performance + 6 efficiency cores, background load 2 - 3), not waiting; (b) the one flux budget (cerny_ternary, 0.31 s: it re-integrates the pot at the engine's default rtol 1e-9, not the fit's 1e-5) runs alone after the systems, a second barrier worth 20 % of R0's wall at W=4 and 28 % at W=8.
- Pool overhead is negligible: fork + first task 9 - 24 ms, tail 3 - 8 ms per pool call (<= 2 % of an evaluation, < 0.1 % of a Jacobian); results pickle in < 0.3 ms (3 - 117 kB). A persistent pool would buy ~1 %.
- The finite-difference Jacobian is the fit's cost (~90 % of its wall: R0 s0's 161 evaluations are ~8 Jacobians of 18 points + ~17 single evaluations). Its points are equal-sized (CV 4 - 14 %) and the pool is 92 % (R0, 18 points on 4 workers: the 5th round half empty) to 99 % (C1, 20 points) busy. The wall follows the per-point slow-down: R0's point takes 3.9 s alone, 4.8 / 6.3 / 7.3 / 8.3 s with 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 running; the Jacobian takes 43.5 / 30.5 / 22.1 / 22.2 s. C1: 74.8 s (W=4), 63.5 s (W=5, 4 full rounds), 54.3 s (W=8). The machine delivers ~3.5 - 4 serial-equivalents however many workers; OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 changes nothing (30.46 vs 30.46 s).
- The '~25 % busy' of M1's fits: their records give a serial-equivalent utilisation of 37 - 57 % (evaluations x the serial evaluation time / (seconds x 4 workers)). The deficit is the per-core slowdown -- R0's and C1's lanes ran together, 8 processes, each point ~2.1x slower than alone -- plus R0's half-empty last round; it is not workers idling behind a straggler.
- Inside a pot, single process (timer-wrapped functions; the same in the slow and the typical pot): ~52 % is Python building the rate constants (sulfur_rate_constants_at re-derives all 132 from the parameter registry at every RHS and Jacobian call, 127 us), ~30 % the two pH solves per call (in-situ and the 25 C label pH, 30 us each: 17 us the numba kernel, 13 us the Python setup), 4 - 6 % mass action, 2 - 3 % the analytic Jacobian, ~10 % LSODA's Fortran, solve_ivp's per-step Python and the closures. hofmann_pentose_pH5: 900 steps, 1443 RHS, 218 Jacobian calls; whitfield_nf_cys (typical): 116 steps, 231 RHS, 0 Jacobians (LSODA stays in Adams mode); the slowest route pot: 940 steps, 1729 RHS, 354 Jacobians.
- The fixed-point coupling at R0's optimum, W=4: 14.6 s against 1.67 s inline (8.8x slower); its flux budget alone is 3.0 s.
- Nothing was implemented: no change that is small and bit-identical reaches 1.5x. The best one, memoising the pure functions of temperature, is bit-identical and gives 1.12 - 1.14x per pot, 1.13x (R0) / 1.17x (C1) per serial evaluation. The large factors (compiled constants + pH, exact gradients) change numbers at the rounding level and belong to the rewrite (W3).
